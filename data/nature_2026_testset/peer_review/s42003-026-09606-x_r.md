Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
For the Authors 
The work by Ote et al. describes how the overexpression of a RND system leads to antimicrobial resistance and transition
metal sensitivity in Caulobacter vibrioides. The last phenotype seems to emerge from the metabolic burden yielded by
hyperactivity of the RND. The authors propose that overexpression of the tripartite system creates ultrastructure defects in
the cell envelope. As no differences in metal accumulation neither in ROS production are observed between overexpressing
and wild type strains, they conclude that membrane stability is the main factor for transition metal sensitivity. The events
leading to cell envelope integrity defects are not clear. This is an interesting paper which opens a possible therapeutic
window to counteract antimicrobial resistance mechanisms mediated by RND activity. 
Comments 
The authors explore possible mechanisms leading to transition metal sensitivity in an RND overexpressing strain. In the
frame of the proposed hypothesis the fact that several transition metals induced toxicity with no intracellular accumulation
points to ROS accumulation as the main factor explaining the sensitivity phenotype. However, this is not found either. 
-My main concern is that form the two of the triggering events causing transition metals sensitivity, no one seems to be key.
Perhaps the title could focus on cell envelope integrity rather than in metals. 
-A figure including a working model could aid the reader to understand the events analyzed here. Importantly, copper is
mainly used to demonstrate and exemplify metal toxicity. To me this seems misleading. 
-In order to strengthen the work a condition complementing the membrane functon(adding Ca, Mg to the external media) or
mimicking disruption of the PMF (gramicidin) could be tested. 
-If PMF is depleted in mutant strain, how it become resistant to antibiotics? 
-In the abstract authors mention that “motility is key for transition metal resistance”. It is not clear throughout the work the
basis for this assertion. 
-Discussion section should be re-written focusing on the analyses and interpretation of the data presented in former
sections.

(Remarks to the Author) 
In the manuscript number COMMSBIO-25-7075 the authors analyse the physiological burden of efflux pump overexpression
in Caulobacter vibrioides, due to the deletion of the transcriptional regulator TipR, and investigate the involved molecular
mechanism. The increased expression of the AcrAB2NodT pump resulted in a greater sensitivity of C. vibrioides to Cu-
stress, due to the impairment of the cell membranes, caused mainly by the depletion of the proton motive force and reduced
ATP levels. 
The manuscript is very interesting, since it adds novel information about the effects of efflux pumps activity and expression
on the bacterial physiology and suggests putative novel strategies to counteract antibiotic resistance. The experimental
design encompasses different, complementary and confirmatory techniques, providing strong and convincing evidence of
the reported data. The adopted protocols are described in detail and allow the study replication. The obtained results are
well commented in the discussion. There are, however, some revisions that should be performed to further improve the
clarity and the quality of the paper: 
• Please add more information on C. vibrioides and the reasons for its selection as model microorganism in the present
study; 
• Please clarify the sentences in lines 183-187: “Live/dead staining by flow cytometry revealed that the ΔR mutant shows a
WT level of propidium iodide (PI) uptake under Cu stress, while a AcrAB2NodT impaired efflux mutant (ΔR acrB*, see
section below) displayed elevated PI uptake under both control and Cu stress conditions (Fig. 3D). This data indicates that
high AcrAB2NodT activity in the ΔR mutant triggers cell envelope defects with increased membrane permeabilization”. This
description seems to suggest that the potentiated efflux does not cause an increase of the PI entrance into the cells,
indicating membrane permeabilization, while efflux impairment does, in contrast to what assessed as final comment to these
data. Please clarify or rephrase the sentence; 
• Please specify in the text when the ΔpcoAB (Figure 1D) and ΔpleC strains (Figure 5B) were used as controls. 
• Did the authors consider testing the ΔR strain sensitivity to Cu in sessile cultures? Biofilms are extremely important for the
survival of microorganisms in the environment and are well known for showing an increase of efflux pumps expression and
activity. A simple biofilm disruption assay by crystal violet staining might provide information on the putative increase Cu
sensitivity of C. vibrioides even in the sessile lifestyle and further corroborate the reported results. 
After solving these revisions, the manuscript can be considered for publication.
