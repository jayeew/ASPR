Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This is an excellent study in a mouse model demonstrating that a parenteral prime followed by a nasally boost with a
yersinia vaccine is an effective immunization approach against Yersinia pestis. The data, although not easy to assimilate on
first viewing, and are mostly comprehensive and convincing. 
Specific comments 
1) The authors have provided good data on the mechanism of protection based on experiments involving FTY720 treatment
and transfer studies with TRM and/or BRM cells. However, the relative role of humoral and cellar immunity in protection is
still not clear. The FTY studies do not discriminate between T cells and B cells, whereas the transfer studies suggest that T
cells are key, but that there may also be a role for B cells. This raises two questions. A) Is the role of B cell to produce
protective antibodies or perhaps to act as APC for the T cells. This could be addressed by antibody transfer experiments,
and B) what is the role for the lung TRM cells. They appear to secrete both IFN-γ and IL-17. Are both cytokines required, and
if so, what do they do, stimulate macrophages and neutrophils? This could be addressed with cytokine blocking studies. 
2) The authors have addressed the long-term efficacy of the vaccine by delaying the period between parenteral priming and
nasal boosting. What is the durability of the protection if mice are challenge at longer interval after the nasal immunization? 
3) The CFU counts are given for one time point. It would be much more convincing to give a time course of bacterial
clearance (i.e. CFU counts at multiple time points). 
4) The IgA responses do not appear to benefit by the presence of OMV in the vaccine, whereas other immune response do.
Do the authors have any explanation for this? 
5) Is the somewhat reduced efficacy of the i.m. vaccine when absorbed to alum due to the induction of IL-10 or Th2
responses with alum. Is the addition of alum the best way to reduce the reactogenicity of a vaccine? 
6) The manuscript should provide a better description of the various vaccine components, without having to refer to previous
papers from this group. 
7) Some of the data points/lines in the figures, in particular on the survival curves are very difficult to distinguish because
they are with overlapping. It would be good to nudge the data points to improve the clarity of the figure. 
8) The first figure in the manuscript is very similar to previous published data from the same group and might be better shown
as supplemental information. 
9) The study has employed male and female mice. Were there any sex difference in the immune responses or protective
efficacy of the vaccines?

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript by Majumder et al. explores the "prime-spike" immunization strategy for pneumonic plague, combining
intramuscular (IM) OMV46-LcrV/Alum vaccination with intranasal (IN) LcrV boosting. The study is of significant interest as it
addresses important aspects of plague vaccine development, including safety, efficacy, and immune responses. However,
there are several areas that require further clarification and improvement to enhance the quality and impact of the research,
providing valuable insights into the development of effective immunization strategies against pneumonic plague. 
Major Concerns: 
1. While the study shows that the "prime-spike" approach increases lung resident memory B and T cells, the underlying
cellular and molecular pathways remain unclear. For example, it is not clear how the IN LcrV booster specifically promotes
the differentiation and retention of these resident memory cells. Studies could be included to investigate signaling pathways
involved in the activation and trafficking of lymphocytes to the lungs, such as chemokine - receptor interactions. 
2. Although the production of IFN - γ, IL - 17A, and IL - 4 by lung TRM cells is enhanced, the regulatory mechanisms of these
cytokine responses are not well-defined. Experiments to explore how the "prime-spike" strategy modulates cytokine-related
gene expression, epigenetic changes, or transcription factor activities would strengthen the mechanistic understanding. 
3. The manuscript does not comprehensively compare the "prime-spike" strategy with currently available or experimental
plague vaccines in terms of efficacy, safety, and immunogenicity. A more in-depth comparison would help to better position
this new strategy in the field of plague vaccine development. 
Minor Concerns: 
1. The study mainly uses Swiss Webster mice, C57/BL6 mice, and Hfe
⁻⁻
 mice. While these models are valuable, the results
may not be directly applicable to other species, including humans. This should be mentioned in the discussion section to
indicated for future studies, including additional experiments in larger animal models, such as non - human primates, to
better evaluate the potential of this immunization strategy in a more translational context. 
2. In addition to immunological aspects, the cost - effectiveness and practical feasibility of the "prime-spike" strategy should
be discussed. This includes considerations such as the cost of vaccine production, administration routes, and the potential
for large - scale implementation. 
3. The manuscript does not mention the power calculation of the statistical analysis for the experiments. Reporting the power
calculation would help readers assess the reliability of the statistical results and the sufficiency of the sample size. 
4. Some of the data in the figures could be presented more clearly. For example, in some graphs, the error bars are not well-
labeled, making it difficult to interpret the variability of the data. 
5. The antibody analysis methods could be further detailed. For example, the specificity and sensitivity of the ELISA assays
used to measure anti-LcrV antibody titers should be described in more detail to ensure the accuracy of the results. 
6. The methods for cell sorting and transfer could be improved. The purity of the sorted BRM and TRM cells should be
reported, and the potential impact of the sorting process on cell function should be discussed.
