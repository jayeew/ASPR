Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
The authors present new geochemical and genomic data on biological N2 fixation from a sulfate- and molybdenum-depleted
stratified lake. The results suggest that N2 fixation in cyanobacteria remains active even at <1nM Mo levels, indicating that
this metabolism may not have been Mo-limited in the Archean ocean, which may have experienced similar Mo depletion.
The authors propose that low sulfate levels may facilitate molybdate uptake into cells. 
The conclusions of this work are profound and help address a major conundrum in Precambrian biogeochemistry, i.e. the
antiquity of Mo-based N2 fixation despite vanishingly low Mo levels in the early oceans. The manuscript is well written and
based on state-of-the-art methodologies. My only major comment is that cyanobacteria may not have been the main N2-
fixers in the Archean (see line comment with references below). However, this should just require some rewording of the
text. It is quite possible that the findings from this work are relevant for other microbial phyla as well. Perhaps this point could
be addressed by adding a few sentences discussing how conserved the Mo-uptake enzymes are across phyla. If the same
enzymes appear in many other organisms, then they may express similar sensitivities to Mo and sulfate supplies. 
Line comments:

* What are the major claims of the paper? 
The question of how the nitrogen fixing enzyme, nitrogenase, could first evolve in its Mo-based form within the extremely low
Mo environments of the Archean eon is enigmatic. This paper uses N fixation, Mo, and gene sequencing from Lake Deming,
a modern lake with extremely low Mo and low sulfate waters, as an analog to the Archean ocean to constrain the metal
dependency of Archean nitrogen fixation. Using N fixation measurements and Mo additions, the authors show Lake Deming
N fixation is not Mo limited at < 1nM Mo ambient concentrations. This is surprising because 1 nM Mo is considered to be
extremely low Mo based on modern day oceanic concentrations (100 nM). Metagenome and metatranscriptome sequences
demonstrate the use of Mo nitrogenase in this system, with no evidence for Mo-free alternative nitrogenases. The data
indicate modern N fixers that rely only on Mo nitrogenase are highly adapted for Mo uptake in a system with very low Mo. By
comparing Lake Deming’s sulfate concentration (very low) with those in studies documenting Mo limited N fixation at higher
Mo and higher sulfate conditions, the authors propose the Mo concentration threshold for Mo limitation of N fixation will
depend on the sulfate context. Given the low sulfate levels of Archean oceans, Mo at nanomolar and below levels in the
Archean should not be considered “low” for diazotrophs. 
* Are these claims novel, and will they be of interest to others in the community? If the conclusions are not original, it would
be helpful if you could provide relevant references. 
As far as I know, these are the first data that show Mo replete N fixation at sub nM Mo concentrations. The work adds to the
growing literature that indicate Mo limitation of diazotrophy in aquatic systems is very difficult to achieve. 
* Is the work convincing, and if not, what further evidence would be required to strengthen the conclusions? 
There are relatively few replicate incubations (N=3 for + Mo and 3 for – Mo for each of two layers) for the strongest claims of
the paper on the Mo limitation of N fixation. Is it possible that N fixation could have been Mo limited but was not detected
because Mo addition at the 75 nM level led to some toxicity – the N fixation rates decreased a bit in both layers with the +
75nM Mo experiments. 
Could a 10 nM Mo addition have yielded a positive effect on N fixation rates? 
The DNA data came from August 2023, but the incubations demonstrating Mo replete N fixation were performed in May
2024. Are there DNA and RNA data from the actual incubations themselves? How well do the OMICS data reflect the
microbes in incubation bottles ? For RNA data from May 2024, were there only Mo nitrogenase transcripts? 
The results for
RNA focused on metagenome assembled genomes, but what about broader analyses of nitrogenase transcripts? 
How likely is it that the Mo sensitivity of Archean diazotrophs would equal that of modern N fixers using solely the Mo
nitrogenase in a system with barely detecteable Mo? Could the findings simply reflect that N fixers in Lake Deming having
evolved a super efficient Mo uptake system in an environment with extremely low Mo, rather than indicating something
fundamental about early N fixation? This gets at the role of Mo uptake vs sulfate competition. 
Sulfate context of Mo – N fixation relationships : A central proposal of the paper could be strengthened by incubations in
which sulfate addition leads to Mo limited N fixation. Culture experiments with N fixing cyanobacteria in the low sulfate and
low Mo regimes expected for the Archean would strengthen the claims of the key role of sulfate in modulating the threshold
for “high” vs “low” Mo. 
* On a more subjective note, do you feel that the paper will influence thinking in the field in terms of either conceptual
understanding or technological capability? Please feel free to raise any further questions and concerns about the paper. 
The papers improves our conceptual understanding of micronutrient limitation of a key process for life by arguing for more
nuanced, context dependent framework for definitions of “high” vs “low”. I am in strong support of this type of holistic thinking.
