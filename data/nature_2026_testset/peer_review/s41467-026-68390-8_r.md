Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
It has long been recognized that adding solvents can enhance ball-milling efficiency, yet the underlying microscopic
mechanism has remained elusive. In this study, the authors revealed that surface electronic redistribution as the origin of
high mechanical nanosizing effect. Specifically, the formation of a Mgδ+-Mgδ− dipole structure induced by solvent
adsorption plays a key role. It is interesting that the mechanism is applicable to Mg-based alloys, a well-known class of
ductile materials, leading to a 54-fold improvement in mechanical nanosizing efficiency. The increased surface area of the
processed Mg-Ni-Y alloys results in exceptional hydrogen storage kinetics, enabling complete hydrogen release in 3 min at
300 °C. Additionally, the authors employ a polymer coating technique to address surface sensitivity caused by ball-milling,
further enhancing material stability. The manuscript is well-organized and this method holds potential application for the
hydrogen science community. It also provides valuable microscopic insights into affordable and scalable nanofabrication
methods. However, some improvements are needed to justify publication in Nature Communications. My comments are
listed below.

organic materials. It effectively improved the ball-milling efficiency, promoted nano-confinement, and prevented the oxidation

part.

Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this report, the authors present a nanosized Mg-Ni-Y alloy synthesized via a solvent-mediated milling process,
demonstrating enhanced hydrogen storage performance. Compared to dry milling, THF-mediated milling yields smaller Mg-
based alloy particles with improved hydrogen absorption and desorption properties. However, solvent-mediated milling is a
well-established method for particle size reduction (e.g., doi.org/10.1002/er.7046, doi.org/10.1007/s12598-025-03330-x,
doi.org/10.1039/C3TA01332D, etc.), and the performance reported here is not particularly exceptional. Although the authors
include a comparative chart in Fig. 3h, there are numerous previous studies that report comparable or even superior
performance. In addition, the authors claim that THF-adsorption leads to the charge redistribution on Mg surface, promoting
the nanosize effect; however, the data provided are insufficient to substantiate this claim. 
While it is not necessary for a study to demonstrate record-breaking results, in the absence of a novel synthetic strategy or
innovative structural design, I do not believe this work meets the standards for publication in Nature Communications. 
1. Although the authors claim that solvent-free milling results in only a moderate size reduction, other reports have
demonstrated a significantly greater decrease in particle size. (ex. doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhydene.2020.09.251,
doi.org/10.1016/j.jallcom.2009.11.154, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jallcom.2004.08.102). 
2. For the particle size characterization, additional high-resolution SEM images are needed, as Figure 1a lacks sufficient
clarity to support accurate size estimation. While the mean particle diameter is reported as 813 nm, the resolution of the
provided image is too low to justify this calculation. Moreover, the standard deviation should also be reported. Based on
Figure 1a, the sample appears to exhibit a broad size distribution, with many larger particles clearly visible—contrary to the
histogram presented in the image. In particular, the claim that the particles are "monodisperse" (line 95) is not supported by
the data and should be reconsidered. 
3. Please provide detailed experimental conditions for the dry ball-milling process. In ball-milling, parameters such as the
size and type of milling balls can significantly influence the resulting particle characteristics. 
4. In the Mg 1s XPS results, only the metallic Mg peak was observed, despite the well-known tendency of Mg to form a thin
oxide layer—even under ultra-high vacuum conditions—due to its instantaneous reaction with trace amounts of oxygen. It is
recommended that the Mg 2p spectrum be provided, as it is more sensitive to detecting surface oxides. Furthermore, the
authors claim that THF adsorption induces a change in the charge distribution on the Mg surface. If THF adsorption indeed
affects the surface chemistry, the presence of at least a partial oxide layer should be detectable to support this claim. 
5. 
PCT isotherms, along with the corresponding thermodynamic parameters, should be measured to properly characterize
the hydrogen storage properties. This type of analysis is essential for studies involving metal hydride-based hydrogen
storage materials.

The major finding of this work lies in elucidating the nano-size mechanism of 
solvent-assisted ball milling. Despite the wide recognition of the metallurgy and nano-
material community on the effect of some solvents during ball-milling, the microscopic 
mechanism of solvent has remained unsolved. The knowledge gap has forces solvent 
selection to rely on trial-and-error, which is highly time and resource consuming. In this 
submission, we discovered that the electron re-distribution caused by the solvent (THF 
in our study) adsorption is the key factor that modifies surface mechanical properties 
of Mg and its alloys. As shown in the Figure R1-2a, the THF adsorption on Mg lattice 
gives rise to the Mg δ+-Mgδ− dipole structure, and such ionic nature should enhance 
surface mechanical properties. The enhanced surface mechanical properties are indeed 
evinced utilizing Vickers hardness test. As shown in Figure R1-2b, the notable 
enhancement of Vickers hardness was identified by THF adsorption. The THF-sprayed 
surface achieved an  average microhardness of 103.13 HV , representing 22 % 
enhancement compared to the untreated surface (84.58 HV). These results indicate that 
the Mg surface becomes less ductile and thus more susceptible to mechanical impact, 
leading to enhanced  ball-milling efficiency. These findings provide a microscopic 
insight into optimizing ball-milling parameters.
