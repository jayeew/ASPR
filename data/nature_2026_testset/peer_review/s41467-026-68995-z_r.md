Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript presents a well-constructed spatiotemporal atlas of cerebrovascular development in zebrafish that will
greatly address the gap in this field. The 3D vascular construction really impressed me, as it plays a pivotal role in
constructing the spatiotemporal map of vascular development. Also, the spatiotemporal atlas reveals the CapEC enrichment
alternation and dynamic expression module of transporter and tight junction pathway. Finally, three CapEC-enriched genes
were recruited to validate their contribution to the establishment of the blood brain barrier. The resource is thoughtfully
designed and of long-term significance to the field, qualifing the publications for Nature Communications. Yet it remains
some revise to improve clarity and interpretability. Also, I have some concerns about the methods. All the comments are
listed below. 
Major comments 
1. The spatiotemporal atlas was constructed within three larval stages, which are 3, 6, and 11 dpf, respectively. I am
wondering why the authors selected the three stages, are these stages special developmental stages to the zebrafish? Is
there any solid and reliable reference mentioning these three stages? Please cite the reference. 
2. The authors devoted considerable effort to constructing the 3D vascular atlas. They measured vascular length and
segments during three stages. Are these quantitative measurements necessary? Do they help in constructing the
subsequent spatiotemporal atlas? Are there other possible quantitative measurements, I don’t know, such as vessel
thickness? If it is feasible. 
3. Single-cell transcriptomic analysis identified six types of ECs. The marker gene information of these ECs should be
included in the supplementary tables, such as pseudobulk and fold change. In addition, based on the expression levels of
the marker genes, angiogenic ECs appear to be similar to the other ECs, which may suggest that this cluster of ECs are
progenitor cells of other ECs. The authors should add more analysis to discuss this cluster, as they may be related to
vasculogenic events. 
4. These HEGs in Figure 3A should be analyzed for their correlations across different species to verify their conservation.
The results should be presented in the manuscript. 
5. The spatial transcriptome profiles expression of 36 genes during different periods. What criteria were used to select these
genes? The changes of these genes across different stages should be present and discussed. 
6. This is very appealing, as the authors have generated a whole-brain transcriptome of endothelial cell-specific genes
based on the 3D vascular network. However, for the vascular localization in series zebrafish sections, more detailed
explanations are needed. For example, the source of the vascular annotation image in Figure 4B. The Results section
should also provide a clearer description of the relevant process and key steps. 
7. In figure 4C, the cell proportion of AngECs in different vessels is low when compared to other ECs, which is inconsistent
with the cell proportions from single cell analysis. Please explain this discrepancy.

9. Cell denstiy in different regions of cerebral vasculature is dynamic throughout three stages, which is interesting to me.

detailed information of cell density to each vessel should be presented in the supplemental table. 
10. Why do not present the cell density of angEC? Is it due to the consideration of figure arrangement or because the results

11. The authors validated the reliability of expression patterns in spatiotemporal transcriptomic data with a transgenic

2. The number of significance analyses in figure 3B should remain consistent with those in figure 3C and 3D.

Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript by Li et al is a resource paper which utilizes single-cell transcriptomics, 3D vascular reconstruction and in
situ sequencing to describe zebrafish brain vascular development. The authors have identified 6 endothelial subtypes,
which show a conserved transcriptional signature with mammalian cells. They also have performed some functional assays,
which preliminarily implicate 3 new genes in vascular patterning and blod-brain barrier (BBB) integrity. 
Overall, this study provides new and detailed information on brain endothelial cell transcriptomics using state-of-the-art
approaches, and potentially could be a very useful resource for the scientific community. 
However, the biggest limitation is
that the spatial transcriptomic approach using improved in situ sequencing (IISS) approach is not very convincing, and the
results do not convincingly demonstrate distinct spatial localization of different endothelial cell subtypes. This approach or
analysis may need an improvement for it to be a useful resource. Specific comments: 
1. As noted above, IISS approach aims to detect spatial localization of different marker genes that can be assigned to
distince brain endothelial cell populations. However, the results in Fig. 4 show largely overlapping expression of many cell
markers. For example, CapEC, AEC and VECs show very similar enrichment patterns (Fig. 4D and E), all of them enriched
in central arteries, including MoCtA. The approach fails to detect distinct expression patterns, which might be expected for
arterial and venous cells, for example. It seems that this could be an artifact of the methods used rather than the true
expression pattern. To verify this, the authors could perform hybridization chain reaction (HCR) using top marker genes for
each cell population and verify if these expression patterns match their IISS results. 
2. While the authors have performed very detailed description of zebrafish brain vascular anatomy at 3-11 dpf, this
description is not entirely new and builds upon several previous studies (perhaps less detailed), such as Isogai et al, Dev
Biol 2001, and others. When describing 3D cerebrovascular atlas, the authors need to acknowledge clearly what has been
done in the previous studies, and how their approach goes beyond what has been previously described. 
3. Please note how many endothelial cells were identified at each time point in scRNA-seq analysis. From the Fig. 2A it
appears that there were many more cells identified at 3 dpf stage compared to 6 and 11 dpf; it is unclear why. There may be
very few cells (only 10-15) identified in some cell populations at 6 and 11 dpf (such as CapECs), which may limit the
transcriptional profile analysis; this needs to be acknowledged. 
4. Please move the legend in Fig. 2C (the inset box which shows percent expressed and average expression) outside of the
data panel. It currently obstructs the view of dot plot expression for MEC, VEC and AngEC populations. 
5. It is difficult to read the gene list in Fig. S2. Either the figure resolution can be improved, or the list can be presented in a
separate supplementary table. 
6. Please clarify how the proportion of endothelial cells in different vessels was determined in Fig. 4C. scRNA-seq analysis

identified AngECs as the most abundant cell type, and CapEC make a relatively small proportion. However, Fig. 4C shows
CapECs as the most abundant cell type, and there is only a small proportion of AngECs. Why is there such a discrepancy
between the two approaches? 
7. Morpholino knockdown experiments (Fig. 5H-I and S9F-H) currently have no specificity controls and show data that are
very preliminary. Ideal verification would be to generate genetic mutants. At the very least, the authors should show that F0
CRISPR / Cas9 knockout of the same genes produces similar phenotypes to the morpholino knockdown. 
8. The authors state that F0 knockdown of si:ch73-86n18.1 produced erythrocyte extravasation, arguing that this
demonstrates role in BBB development (Fig. S8C). The results here show a hemorrhage, likely due to vessel rupture, which
is different from BBB formation. In defective BBB vessels may still be intact and may not necessarily show hemorrhages.
Please include “n”, how many embryos were analyzed and how many showed hemorrhages. Also, please include in the
methods the sequence of gRNA (or gRNAs) used for targeting, how was gRNA made (in vitro transcription or synthetic) and
specify what was the mutagenesis success rate based on DNA sequencing, restriction analysis, or another appropriate
method. 
9. It is very difficult to see any color in Figure S7, orange, green, purple or blue.

7. In figure 4C, the cell proportion of AngECs in different vessels is low when compared 
to other ECs, which is inconsistent with the cell proportions from single cell analysis.

Response: 
We appreciate the reviewer’s insightful comment, which allows us to highlight a key 
biological insight. The apparent discrepancy in AngEC proportions between the single-
cell and IISS datasets actually underscores the high transcriptional similarity between 
AngECs and CapECs. Our single-cell data initially revealed shared marker genes 
between these subtypes. The IISS technology, with its more focused gene panel, lacks 
the resolution to perfectly distinguish these highly similar populations, leading to some 
AngECs being annotated as CapECs. This observation is consistent with recent 
literature [6] that describes hybrid populations like “angiogenic capillary ECs”, 
supporting the concept of a developmental continuum. This observation clarifies that

the differences in proportions among these endothelial subtypes should be interpreted 
as evidence of their close relationship, rather than as a contradiction.
8. The efficiency of in-situ sequencing should be presented in the manuscript and 
discussing the specific advantages of performing this method for cerebral vasculature.
Response:
We thank the reviewer for this suggestion. We have now included the sequencing 
efficiency metrics for our IISS analysis in the revised manuscript (Lines 216-220, 355-
364).
Briefly, we performed IISS on serial sections of zebrafish embryos at 3, 6, and 11 dpf, 
targeting a panel of 36 candidate genes. The analysis yielded robust data: at 3 dpf, 28 
slices comprising 8,769 cells and 1,970 vascular regions, with 45,993 transcript counts; 
at 6 dpf, 25 slices with 6,873 cells, 1,258 regions, and 30,919 counts; and at 11 dpf, 25 
slices with 5,425 cells, 1,924 regions, and 33,118 counts. These data demonstrate the 
high efficiency and depth of our spatial transcriptomic profiling.
Regarding its specific advantages for cerebral vasculature research, IISS is uniquely 
powerful. First, its high sensitivity and specificity, achieved via rolling circle
