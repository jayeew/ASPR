Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
General Point: 
This manuscript investigates a novel glycolysis-driven signaling pathway¡ªLactate-PCAF-H3K18la-CXCL1¡ªthat promotes
neutrophil infiltration and immune evasion in pancreatic cancer. The authors combine bioinformatics analysis, CUT&Tag
profiling, and in vivo models to propose that PCAF functions as a histone lactyltransferase catalyzing H3K18la, thereby
transcriptionally activating CXCL1 expression. The study also suggests a combinatorial therapeutic strategy using PCAF
inhibition and anti-PD-1 immunotherapy. The topic is highly timely and relevant, given the current focus on metabolic-
epigenetic crosstalk and its implications for immunotherapy resistance in tumors such as PDAC. 
Despite these strengths, the manuscript has several notable methodological weaknesses. Key experimental details are
either missing or insufficiently described, particularly in the immunological depletion assays and CXCL1 rescue
experiments. Additionally, the role of PCAF is likely overstated, as the supporting biochemical evidence is limited. These
issues undermine the central conclusions and should be addressed to enhance the rigor and clarity of the study. 
Major Points: 
1. The manuscript consistently refers to ¡°lysine lactylation (Kla)¡± without distinguishing between its two stereoisomeric
forms: L-lactyllysine (KL-la) and D-lactyllysine (KD-la). Recent studies have clearly identified KL-la as the dominant
intracellular form, originating from L-lactate through enzymatic processes, while KD-la arises from methylglyoxal
detoxification via D-lactoylglutathione and is largely non-enzymatic [PMID: 39030363]. This distinction is essential for
correctly interpreting the role of glycolysis and LDH in histone L-lactylation. The stereospecificity of the H3K18L-la antibody
used in the study must be explicitly stated in the Methods section, and all references to "Kla" should be revised accordingly. 
2. Figure 1A-C: The criteria used to define "high glycolysis" based on ssGSEA scores are not detailed. Specifically, the list
of glycolysis-related genes used for enrichment scoring is not provided, nor is a reference cited. These omissions hinder
reproducibility and raise concerns about the validity of the inferred glycolysis¨Cimmune correlations. Please include
references to prior studies substantiating your method used in this paper. 
3. The manuscript states that CD11b+Ly6G+ neutrophils and CD8+ T cells were depleted in vivo using specific antibodies
but provides no details in the Methods section. The dosing regimen, administration route, depletion efficiency validation, and
relevant references are all missing. This lack of information undermines the credibility of key in vivo conclusions. Please
include references to prior studies substantiating your method used in this paper. 
4. Fig. 2J, 2K, 4P: The term ¡°rescued by recombinant CXCL1 protein¡± is vague. The source, concentration, method of
administration, and whether protein expression was validated (e.g., via immunoblot) are not reported. Without these
methodological details, the experimental design is not interpretable. Please include references to prior studies
substantiating your method used in this paper. 
5. The text refers generically to ¡°LDH knockdown,¡± without specifying whether LDHA, LDHB, or both were targeted. Given
that LDHA and LDHB have different substrate specificities and cellular functions, this ambiguity must be resolved throughout
the manuscript. 
6. Multiple studies, including recent biochemical evidence, identify p300/CBP and GCN5 as the primary H3K18la writers.
The claim that PCAF is the key lactyltransferase in PDAC contradicts these findings. Moreover, unpublished data from other
labs indicate that PCAF has little to no in vitro histone lactylation activity. The molecular docking and Co-IP evidence
provided here are not sufficient to support the enzymatic function of PCAF. The authors should remove Figure 4A and
temper conclusions from Figure 4B, which lacks controls to demonstrate specificity for H3K18la over other acyl
modifications. 
7. The IP experiment using H3K18la antibody is potentially confounded by coexisting acyl modifications (e.g., acetylation or

other lactylation sites) and cannot specifically demonstrate a direct interaction between PCAF and H3K18la. Additional
controls would be necessary to support the conclusion. 
Minor Points: 
1. Statements such as ¡°promotes the polarization of tumor-associated macrophages (TAM), and inhibits NK and effector T
cells (Teffs)¡± are not supported by citations. Please include references to prior studies substantiating these claims. 
2. The sentence ¡°Several histone acetyltransferases function as lactyltransferases¡± should be made more precise by
naming the specific enzymes (e.g., p300, CBP, GCN5) and providing appropriate citations. 
Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Zhang et al, identified a novel mechanism by which the aerobic glycolysis-induced Lactate-PCAF-H3K18la-CXCL1 pathway
mediate neutrophil infiltration and immunosuppression in TME. They also developed a potential therapeutic strategy for
pancreatic cancer. While the findings are intriguing, several major concerns should be addressed to strengthen the
manuscript: 
MAJOR POINTS: 
How do the author define neutrophils in fig1A and immune cell in fig.1B-C? This is unclear. 
Tumors treated with 2DG grow more slowly, and immune cell infiltration was analyzed at day 15, when tumor sizes differ
significantly. Since immune infiltration is known to correlate with tumor size, a more rigorous and size-matched approach is
necessary to draw valid conclusions about immune cell dynamics. 
Given that T cells rely on glucose for their function and survival, the authors should explain why T cells appear unaffected by
2DG treatment in vivo. Additional data or discussion is needed to address this apparent discrepancy. 
The study would benefit from the use of more physiologically relevant models, such as orthotopic pancreatic tumor models,
to better validate the proposed mechanism. 
The rationale behind the experimental design and selection of assays in Figures 1M–Q should be clearly explained to help
readers understand their relevance to the central hypothesis and the interpretation of the results. 
Glycolysis-driven Kla is important for the immunosuppressive activity of intratumoral neutrophils/macrophages as well as for
the function of T cells, but the authors did not address and discuss this critical point in their study. Since 2DG may affect both
tumor and immune cells, the authors should analyze Kla levels and functional consequences in immune cells to substantiate
the claim that the PCAF–H3K18la–CXCL1 pathway in tumor cells is the primary mechanism. 
As bromosporine may influence both lysine acetylation (Kac) and lysine lactylation (Kla), the authors should delineate the
relative contributions of Kla versus Kac to chemokine expression, both in vitro and in vivo
