Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Multi-Organoid Loop Cerebral Connectoids Exhibit Enhanced Neuronal Network 
Dynamics and Sequence-Specific Entrainment. 
Summary: 
Building neuronal networks in vitro is necessary for understanding brain function and disease mechanisms, yet existing
system lack the structural complexity needed for physiological relevant network dynamics. In this work, the authors
developed loop-connectoids, cerebral organoids linked by axon bundles embedded in a microfluid device (PDMS chip), to
form modular network. They compared electrical activity across different configurations, finding that larger networks
produced more complex dynamics. Increasing connectivity shifted towards near criticality. Through pharmacological
modulation, the authors demonstrated excitatory and inhibitory contributions, and through optogenetic approach they
showed a sequence of activation 
1. The authors clearly showed: 
a. Connecting multiple organoids introduces a hierarchy of network synchronization. 
b. Enriched network structure and function. 
c. Emergence of near critical dynamics in connectoids 
d. Blocking glutamatergic receptors reduced burst activity, confirming excitatory transmission as the main driver of organoid
network activity. In contrast, GABAergic inhibition shaped temporal complexity, with bicuculline increasing burst frequency
but reducing variability, indicating inhibition is essential for maintaining complex dynamics. 
e. Functional modulability and plasticity of connectoids through optogenetic approach 
General comments: 
2. The authors showed that developing modular and increasingly higher-order network structures resulted in more complex
functional dynamics, moving closer to the criticality observed in the human brain. 
3. The authors are aware of the latest methodologies for building increasingly complex networks (e.g. assembloids and
connecting long distance organoids via axon bundles). Yet, most of this work has been done with two organoids, including
the author’s own previous studies. Here, they expand on this body of research by constructing loops of three and four
organoids, using a microfluidics device that guides axon bundle formation, thus the formation of loops. This approach
provides a methodology that reduces technical variability. 
4. A limitation noted by the authors is that the loop-connectoids were generated from healthy iPSC lines. Nonetheless, a
human brain model that better recapitulates higher network dynamics and interregional connections helps advance our
understanding of functionality at a baseline level. From this baseline, a more reliable foundation for studying
pathophysiology can be stablished. 
5. The statement “The PDMS chip can be removed, and the organoids can be taken out of the device without damaging the
bundled axon structure” raises questions. I would assume that removing the chip would allow greater interconnectivity,
though it would disrupt the highly organized loop. How would the electrophysiology signature change with more long-rage
interconnections? 
Would additional loops emerge? Would such interconnectivity drive higher functional dynamics and
criticality? Please expand on this point in the discussion section

Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This study presents an innovative model, "loop connectoids," by connecting multiple organoids in a loop structure to analyze
their neural network activity. The authors convincingly demonstrate that as the number of connections increases, the network
activity becomes more complex and approaches a critical state. To further strengthen the manuscript's core claims and
improve its clarity, we recommend addressing the following points. 
[Major comments] 
Major-1. 
The basis for the term "cerebral organoid" is not fully supported by the provided evidence. A key feature of conventional
cerebral organoids, such as rosette structures or cortical layering, is not clearly observed. This suggests the model might be
closer to a "cerebral cell spheroid." To improve the manuscript's clarity, the authors should either provide a more robust
discussion to justify the use of the term "organoid" or consider a more accurate terminology. 
Major-2. 
The claim that the increased complexity of neural signals with more connected organoids is simply due to an increased
number of neurons cannot be ruled out. To clearly demonstrate that the long-range connections themselves are what
influence the neural activity, it would be beneficial to include comparative data from a 2D neuronal culture system with an
equivalent number of cells. 
Major-3. 
The manuscript claims that loop connectoids exhibit more complex and synchronized neural activity compared to single or
two-connected organoids. It specifically highlights unique features of the three- and four-connected models, such as the
emergence of hub nodes and increased inter-burst interval variability. However, it is not clear whether these characteristics
arise from the functional advantages of the loop structure itself or are simply a result of an increased number of neurons and
connections. The authors mention both "additional feedback loops" and "increased external input" in the Discussion but
provide no experimental evidence to distinguish between these possibilities. To substantiate the true advantage of the loop
connectoids, additional data comparing them to a linear model with the same number of organoids and connections is
needed to demonstrate the emergent properties unique to the loop structure. This would provide crucial qualitative evidence
beyond simple quantitative increases. 
Major-4. 
A more in-depth discussion on the changes in synchronized activity following treatment with excitatory/inhibitory drugs
(glutamate/GABA) is needed to enhance the manuscript's depth. Please strengthen the discussion on the physiological
significance of the results and highlight how the loop connectoids' response to these drugs differs from that of conventional
models. 
[Minor comments] 
Minor-1. 
The Results section is currently presented as a single block, making the flow of the narrative difficult to follow. To improve
readability, please organize the results into sub-chapters corresponding to each experiment. 
Minor-2. 
The justification for selecting the STTC (Spike Time Tiling Coefficient) threshold of 0.3 is insufficient. Please provide a more
detailed explanation for this choice. 
Minor-3. 
The phrase "reminiscent of hierarchical brain network" in the Results section (page 5) is a subjective expression that is not
suitable for scientific writing. Unless this claim can be backed by clear quantitative or qualitative evidence, it should be
removed. Alternatively, strengthen the text to explicitly state how the model mimics hierarchical brain networks by providing
more direct evidence.

Minor-4. 
Throughout the manuscript, academic terms such as in vitro and in vivo should be consistently italicized. 
Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors reported the generation of cerebral connectoids using a microfluidic devices made of PDMS silicone with the
aim to investigate the neuronal network response when more than two organoids are reciprocally connected with axonal
bundles. Pharmacological-induced response and optogenetic stimulation were also involved to better study the organoids
activity. 
The work appears a follow-up of their own previous published works in 2019 and 2024, in which the authors already
reported the capability of organoids to extend axons one to each other and how this mutual communication promotes
functionality of organoids neuronal network. The novelty element in the current work is the presence of three- and four-
membered loop cerebral connectoids. As expected, the more organoids are connected, the more the activity increases. This
was demonstrated by electrophysiological recordings as well as by optogenetic stimulation and pharmacological
administration which revealed the contribution of inhibitory and excitatory synaptic components to the activity of the
organoids. 
Major comments: 
1. The generated organoids are made of healthy iPSCs and even the authors recognized that the involvement of patient-
derived iPSCs would increase the relevance and novelty of the work. Indeed, the study of neural circuitry in cerebral
organoid model of neuronal disorders is still lacking. Adding the presence of at least one organoid made of malfunctioning
neuronal cells would be of interest to understand how neuronal networks is interrupted in presence of neuronal defect. 
2. The authors reported a comprehensive electrophysiological characterization of connectoids showing the establishment
and development of neuronal network activity. However, it would be of great interest to actually see the building up of neural
connection within the connected organoids. To this aim, I suggest the authors perform analysis like i) 3D imaging
reconstruction to visual entirely the MAP2+ network inside the organoids ii) the use of transsynaptic neuronal tracer i.e.,
rabies virus to see connected neurons by synaptic communication. 
3. Pharmacological administration revealed the presence of inhibitory and excitatory synapses. Could the authors provide
immunostaining of specific pre and post excitatory and inhibitory components? 
4. Overall, it is almost completely missing the phenotype characterization of the organoids. The authors provide a brief
description of “organoids after 92 days of differentiation” which were stained for the cortical layer marker Ctip2 and FOXG1,
the astrocyte marker GFAP and neuron specific TUJ1. Staining for the neural progenitor cells (SOX2+) and upper-layer
neurons (SATB2+) are also reported. However, it would be of interest to observe any differences in organization and relative
expression abundance of those markers in single organoid compared to connectoids. Does the communication between
organoids influence the spatial organization of cells and even their maturation at different stages? 
5. Is the “92 days of differentiation” the timing at which the electrophysiological characterization is performed? In the material
and method section the authors reported that “cerebral organoid at day 28 were placed into each of the holes of the culture
device using wide-bore pipette tip. Axons from the organoid start extending on the surface. Given the physical restrictions of
the PDMS culture chip, axons can only elongate along the channel and spontaneously assemble into a bundle after 2
weeks in culture”. At which time point are the recordings performed? This is not clear from the manuscript. Do the authors
perform a time course analysis? Which is the rationale for choosing a certain time point over another? I would clearly state at
which day the analysis were performed. This detail should be present also for the optogenetic and pharmacological assays. 
6. The generation of connectoids allow to better “recapitulate long distance axonal connections between distant brain
regions in more physiological and structurally relevant manner”. The authors do not provide any specific brain-regional
phenotype characterization of the generated organoids. The possibility to study connection between organoids with different
patterned phenotype (e.g. hippocampal and cortical) would be of great interest. If the analyzed connectoids are only made,
lets’ say, of cortical cells, this should be clearly stated in the manuscript. 
Minor comments 
Fig.S1: images in S1A and S1B should be improved in quality. Are those z-stack images of sliced organoids? Is it reported
only a section? Or is it a whole-mount staining? There are very few nuclei identified by Hoechst, especially in S1B.
Moreover, the Tuj1 staining seems to be not so specific. There is a lot of background. Fig.S1C: it would be nice to see also
in this case a lower magnification of the stained organoid to appreciate the region to which the “layers” belong. 
Fig.S4: the title of this figure legend is not appropriate.

Major-2. 
The claim that the increased complexity of neural signals with more connected organoids is simply due 
to an increased number of neurons cannot be ruled out. To clearly demonstrate that the long -range 
connections themselves are what influence the neural activity, it would be beneficial to include 
comparative data from a 2D neuronal culture system with an equivalent number of cells. 
Response: We thank the reviewer for this important comment. We agree that the increased complexity 
of neural activity in multi -organoid networks could, in principle, reflect the higher number of neurons 
rather than the addition of long -range inter -organoid connections. However, i t would be technically 
challenging to culture a 2D neuronal network with an equivalent total number of cells to the four-organoid 
configuration (which exceeds 800,000 cells) using the MEA device with limited surface area, as the cell 
density and coverage would not be comparable. Therefore, normalization by cell number per area would 
be required for a fair comparison. At the same time, previous studies using 2D neuronal cultures have 
provided useful insights into this question. For instance, Sato et al. (Front Neurosci., 16:943310, 2023) 
demonstrated that modular connections of 2D primary neurons generated higher dynamical richness, 
functional complexity and higher modularity compared to plain,  random, unstructured 2D cultures , 
highlighting the importance of long range connections and modular architecture of the system. Similarly, 
Habibey et al. (Biosens Bioelectron. 1;261:116518, 2024 ) reported increased network metrics in 64 
compartmentalized neuronal cultures connected via channels compared to randomly distributed plain 
2D networks. These findings support the idea that structured long -range connections  and modular 
architecture, even in 2D systems, can significantly enhance network dynamics beyond what is expected 
from cell number alone.  In our previous work ( Osaki et al., Nat Commun 15, 2945, 2024 ), we directly 
compared fused organoids (increased total cell number without defined inter-organoid connections) with 
two organoids connected via axon bundles  and observed that the increase in network complexity and 
activity occurred only in the axon-connected configuration. This suggests that long-range connections, 
rather than cell number, are the key drivers of enhanced neural dynamics. Interestingly, we would like 
to add that the increase in activity in our current study is not simply linear with the number of connected 
organoids, implying that the observed network properties likely arise from the establishment of reciprocal 
and hierarchical inter -organoid connections rat her than a mere summation of neuronal populations. 
Based on these considerations, we have rephrased and expanded the discussion in the revised 
manuscript to clarify this point  on page 10, line 252 – 260 as “This recurrent activity across multiple 
reciprocal connections may underlie the observed prolongation of burst duration and enhancement of 
temporal complexity through a feedback-driven amplification mechanism. Similar effects were found in 
studies where 2D neural clusters connected through thin channels were compared with random, 
unstructured 2D cultures. Structured networks were found to have higher dynamical richness 18, 
functional complexity 19 and increased network metrics 20. Furthermore, previous work with organoids 
demonstrated increased neur al activity when organoids were connected via axon bundles, whereas 
simply fusing organoids with same number of neurons did not produce such effects, highlighting the 
critical role of long-range inter-organoid connections16.”.

The justification for selecting the STTC (Spike Time Tiling Coefficient) threshold of 0.3 is insufficient. 
Please provide a more detailed explanation for this choice. 
Response: STTC values around 0.35 are often interpreted as indicating a moderate-to-strong functional 
correlation between spike trains, whereas lower values typically reflect weak or noise-level correlations. 
Importantly, STTC is bounded between –1 and 1, and values around 0.3 represent a  meaningful 
deviation from random or uncorrelated spiking, while avoiding detections that can arise at very low 
thresholds. In addition, we tested a range of thresholds (0. 0–1.0) and found that the trend in network 
structures differences was preserved across the different thresholds which we added as a new figure 
in S12B. We selected 0.3 as a balance point that reduces false positive  connections with lower 
thresholds and false negative s with higher thresholds which is visualized in the representative new 
figure in S12A. We have added explanation about the threshold into the manuscript on page 14, line 
436 - 438 as “Network parameters were quantified across thresholds from 0.0 - 1.0 (Fig. S12). To 
balance false positive inter organoid connections with lower thresholds and false negatives with higher 
thresholds (Fig. S12A) electrode pairs with STTC weights higher than 0.3 were used to generate 
connectivity map and were binarized to create the adjacency matrix.”
