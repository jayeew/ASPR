Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This study developed a new material of Ni single-atom anchored twisted SnS2 nanosheets (Ni-TSnS2), which was used in
photocatalytic reduction of CO2 for ethane (C2H6) synthesis. Through a combination of spectroscopic characterizations and
theoretical calculations, the authors systematically demonstrate that the twist angle structure induces spin-orbit coupling in
SnS2, leading to spin-momentum locking. This spin-momentum locking effect prolongs the lifetime of photogenerated
charge carriers during photocatalysis, thereby facilitating the kinetics of CO2 hydrogenation. In addition, in-situ electron
paramagnetic resonance (EPR) results reveal that the Ni single atoms anchored on the angular substrate also exhibit strong
spin-orbit coupling, which induces the formation of methyl radicals. Then, these radicals subsequently participate in chain
reactions to efficiently produce ethane. Generally, this work is of high quality with clear presentation and deep mechanism
discussion. Therefore, this work can be published in Nature Communications after some revisions, and detailed comments
are listed below: 
1. In order to comprehensively evaluate the catalytic performance, AQY and STC results should be provided. 
2. The authors need to further explain the statement of “Quadrupole moment splitting serves as a key indicator of symmetry
breaking”, so that readers can understand the principle of this representation of data and underlying mechanism of
quadrupole moment splitting. 
3. The authors claim that “Fig. 3g reveals that τ2 in Ni-TSnS2 and TSnS2 is extended by factors of more than 43 and 21
respectively compared to SnS2”. The author should provide the value of τ2. 
4. In recent years, radical-mediated CO2 reduction mechanisms have attracted increasing attention. For instance, Basak et
al. (Nat. Catal., 2025, 8, 794-803) reported that CO2 reduction at Ni(I) sites involved single-electron transfer and radical
processes. I suggest that the authors compare their findings with this reference to further refine the discussion of radical
reaction mechanisms presented in this work. 
5. During ethane (C2H6) photosynthesis, the ∙CH3-to-C2H6 radical chain reaction mentioned in this study raises an
important question: does this reaction pathway exist in natural systems? Please clarify whether such a reaction occurs in
nature and, if so, describe its natural occurrence and context. 
6. In this study, single-electron transfer (SET) at Ni sites of Ni-SnS2 leads to formation of form methyl radicals (∙CH3), which
is found as the primary intermediate during CO2 reduction. The authors should provide solid evidences for formation of
∙CH3. 
7. The authors should illustrate the most important reaction or rate-limiting step for formation of ethane in this system. Why
aren’t other reduction products such as CH4 and CO generated? 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript proposes an intriguing strategy to induce spontaneous long-range spin–orbit coupling (SOC) in SnS2
through moiré engineering, and to achieve highly selective photocatalytic CO2-to-C2H6 conversion using single-atom Ni
sites with C2v coordination. A comprehensive set of experimental characterizations combined with DFT calculations
provides a relatively complete evidence chain. Overall, the manuscript is well-structured, methodologically rich, and
presents innovative conclusions. Therefore, I believe the manuscript might be published after addressing the following
issues.

1. The authors emphasize the effect of spontaneous long-range spin pinning for photocatalysis. How to define long-range? It
would also be helpful to discuss how the performance of catalysts with short-range orders compared to those exhibiting
long-range orders. 
2. The Ni EXAFS spectra of Ni-TSnS2 should be compared with suitable reference compounds, such as Nickel sulfide or
Nickel oxide, to clarify the coordination environment of Ni. 
3. The EDS mapping images would benefit from showing a larger area to capture an entire nanosheet, ensuring that the
elemental distribution is representative of the material rather than localized regions. In addition, for comparison, AC-STEM
image of the Ni-free TSnS2 sample should be provided to better illustrate the Ni incorporation. 
4. Although the observed g-shift in the EPR spectra can be interpreted as evidence of changes in local spin or orbital
contributions, the authors should clarify how alternative factors, such as variations in chemical state, coordination
environment, or local fields induced by surface adsorption, have been excluded. 
5. In Supplementary Fig. 8, the x-axis should be consistent for better comparison. 
6. Why does the distribution of C2H6 fragment peak look different between reactions under 12CO2 and 13CO2, given that
both show a main signal at m/z = 30? 
7. According to the experimental details, the photocatalytic experiments were carried out without sacrificial agents, meaning
that H2O oxidation occurred. The production rate of oxidation products should also be well investigated to demonstrate the
occurrence of overall reaction. Besides, GC spectra for standards and reaction samples should be presented. 
8. It is noted that increasing Ni loading appears to decrease catalytic performance. The authors should discuss possible
reasons for this phenomenon. 
9. The detection of DMPO-∙CH

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This work finished by Liu et al. presents a novel twist engineering for synthesis of SnS2 nanosheets (Ni-TSnS2), and strong
spin-orbit coupling (SOC) was observed after Ni single-atom anchored on Ni-TSnS2. The developed material exhibits
spontaneous long-range spin-momentum locking (LRSL) due to SOC, and enhances its reactivity and stability for
photocatalytic reduction of CO2 for ethane (C2H6) synthesis. The authors deeply explain the SOC effect for enhanced spin-
polarized electron transfer and specific radical (∙CH3) formation. This work gives new insights into CO2 photocatalytic
reduction by twist material with consideration of SOC and long-range spin-momentum locking. I recommend its acceptance
after further consider the following suggestions: 
(1) Material characterizations including TEM and selected area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern obtained for TSnS2 and
Ni-TSnS2 present twist angle of 30° between adjacent SnS2 layers. Can the twist angle be adjusted through CVD synthesis
condition, and why 30° is selected in this study? 
(2) For Ni-TSnS2 with strong SOC effects, the authors claim the anchored C2v symmetric Ni sites are the primary reason for
this SOC effect (based on XMCD results), then what is the role of twist engineering in SOC? What is the mechanism for
improved SOC after twist engineering and then Ni deposition? 
(3) Based on Mössbauer spectra analysis, the quadrupole splitting of TSnS2 and Ni-TSnS2 is 0.88 and 0.92 mm s-1,
respectively, so is it a big improvement for this value after Ni deposition for TSnS2? 
(4) The relationship between SOC and LRSL for Ni-TSnS2 should be more clearly explained, and is the strong SOC a
prerequisite for long-range spin-momentum locking? 
(5) DRIFTS analysis indicates that CO2 reduction to C2H6 on Ni-TSnS2 proceeds through an unconventional reaction
pathway, and the specific transformation pathway or key step should be explained with consideration of SOC effect. Why
can ∙CH3 be produced in a large amount but not other radicals or other CORR pathways? 
(6) According to UV-vis DRS results, both SnS2 and Ni-TSnS2 have conduction band (CB) potentials suitable for CO, CH4,
and C2H6 production through CO2 reduction. Then, what is the key reason for inhibition of other reactions for formation of
CO and CH4? 
(7) The authors also should explain the key mechanism for the high stability of developed Ni-TSnS2 for CO2 photocatalytic
reduction.

Line 155-162: A non-zero quadrupole splitting (QS) arises from the interaction between the nucleus 
and an asymmetric electric field gradient (EFG) at the atomic site. In a perfectly symmetric crystal 
lattice ( e.g., ideal octahedral or tetrahedral coordination), the EFG is zero 9. Thus, the observed 
quadrupole splitting in Ni -TSnS2 indicates a breakdown of this local symmetry around the Sn atom. 
This symmetry breaking is attributed to  twist engineering induced strain , which creates an uneven 
distribution of electron density, i.e. the asymmetric EFG. Therefore, the presence of quadrupole 
splitting is a key indicator confirming successful local symmetry breaking induced by Ni anchoring.
