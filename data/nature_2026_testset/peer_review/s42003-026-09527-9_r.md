Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript provides seeks to understand the metabolic changes in the brain across lifespan of C57Bl/6 mice including
cognitive endpoints and glucose, amino acid and purine metabolic intermediates. 
While there are distinct differences that
are observed during the lifespan of these mice, the overall analysis of the data needs major revisions with many caveats that
could affect interpretation. Hence conclusions based on the current analyses may be overstated. Overall, the study is
descriptive and correlational without mechanistic insights. 
Majority of the figures need to be reorganized to represent the statistics that were conducted. 
Major changes: 
1) Figure 1C shows increasing latency with age. However, it is also well known that the movement velocity of mice
decreases with age – which would thus show increased latency to target. Please show velocity or other measures in these
mice to conclude that it is a cognitively driven outcome and not a peripheral impediment. 
2) Figure 1E-F are represented as line graphs for glucose metabolism at the three timepoints of injections. This is
misleading as these are static levels at those timepoints for each group and not a continuous assessment. Hence the graphs
need to be drawn as histograms for each injection. Since statistics performed were between groups for each timepoint,
conclusions need to be adjusted to refer to static levels rather than a rate of change from timepoints. 
3) To conclude glucose metabolism is altered in the brain, please validate that the injected radiolabeled glucose was
metabolized equally across the various groups (i.e, that the level of radiolabeled glucose delivered to the brain is
comparable). Levels in blood/liver would be beneficial. 
4) It is also unclear how the data for C13 labeled metabolites are normalized. 
5) In Figure 3 the statistics does not represent the graphing provided. It states that repeated measures ANOVA was used but
the data are represented over different age groups that is not repeated measures. These are different animals at each age
and hence the line graphs across ages is not accurate for the type of data being represented. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Long et al., reported metabolic profiles of murine hippocampus and cortex in mice. By using female and male mice at 5
developmental stages, authors investigated age-dependent sex dimorphism in cognitive function. The results suggest a
similar cognitive decline initiation at 16mo for both females and males but sex differences afterwards. Authors also report
pathways that are associated with sex dimorphism in cognitive function. The ‘Methods’ session needs extremely careful
revision. I have the following comments: 
1. Throughout the manuscript, there is no information about how many mice were used in the Barnes maze test. How many
mice in each sex and how many at each age? 
- Similarly, line 249, what does ‘each group’ mean? Numbers of females and males are also missing 
- RT-PCR, again, how many mice? 
- Authors need to revise throughout the manuscript to add the numbers of samples in each experiment.
