Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The present work describes a copper-catalyzed modular synthesis of α-oxy carbonyl compounds via a three-component
reaction of I(III)/P(V)-hybrid ylides with carboxylic acids as oxygenating reagents and heteroatom nucleophiles. In this study,
the I/P-hybrid ylides are activated by a copper catalyst to form a copper carbenoid, which undergoes intramolecular oxygen-
atom attack to generate a P(V)-oxirene intermediate, proposed to be the key species in the reaction pathway. Site-selective
attack of carboxylates on the oxirene intermediate induces ring opening to afford acyl phosphonium salts, which
subsequently react with heteroatom nucleophiles to furnish various α-oxy carbonyl compounds. Two types of nucleophiles—
carboxylates and other heteroatom nucleophiles—react in a site-selective and sequential manner, enabling controlled
formation of the desired α-oxy carbonyl products. 
The method exhibits a broad substrate scope and excellent functional group compatibility, and the three-component design
allows for diverse product synthesis. A wide range of aliphatic and aromatic carboxylic acids can be employed as
oxygenating reagents. Regarding heteroatom nucleophiles, alcohols, amines, and even thiols are compatible. The
successful synthesis of polypeptides containing α-oxy carbonyl motifs further highlights the synthetic utility of the method.
Mechanistic investigations suggest that the reaction proceeds through an oxygen-atom transfer process rather than a Wolff
rearrangement. Overall, the method offers a new and practical approach for the diverse synthesis of an important class of α-
oxy carbonyl compounds. The manuscript is well written and presents compelling results; however, I believe that revisions
are necessary to address the following point before it can be considered for publication in Nature Communications. 
The formation of the proposed P(V)-oxirene intermediate is somewhat questionable. An alternative pathway could involve
nucleophilic attack by an external carboxylate at the carbonyl carbon of Int-1, followed by oxygen-atom attack on the copper
carbenoid. A convincing explanation for why the intramolecular oxygen-atom attack would predominate to form such a highly
strained oxirene intermediate should be provided. This issue is critical, as it directly impacts the mechanistic foundation of
the present study.

(Remarks to the Author) 
Review for Nature Communications manuscript NCOMMS-25-86531-T 
In this manuscript, S. Huang, D. Duan, J. Luo, J. Chen, and Z. Wang disclose a protocol for the Cu-catalyzed
multicomponent synthesis of α-oxy and α-amino carbonyls from IIII/PV-hybrid ylides, carboxylic acids, and O- or N-based
nucleophiles. 
This work builds on the group’s previous research on phosphorous-containing ylides as reagents for
accessing oxazoles (Chem. Sci. 2024, 15, 6515; Synlett 2024, 35, 1153). 
Significantly, this contribution leverages the
unique reactivity of unprecedented PV-oxirene intermediates, generated via Cu-carbenes from thoughtfully designed IIII/PV-
hybrid ylides. 
The synthetic utility of the method is illustrated through preparation of various biologically relevant molecules
and highlighted as an efficient strategy for carboxylic acid homologation and 1,2-carbonyl transposition. 
Featuring good
isolated yields, a robust substrate scope, and synthetically useful products, this method represents a promising modular
approach for accessing α-oxy and α-amino carbonyls. 
Appropriate citations are included, spectral evidence of synthetic
compounds is provided, and relevant experiments were conducted to support the proposed reaction mechanism. 
Overall,
this is a well-written manuscript communicating an impactful synthetic transformation supported by solid experimental
results. 
Further improvement can be achieved by addressing the following: 
1) What is the rationale for why Na2CO3 is the preferred base for N-nucleophiles while Cs2CO3 is optimal for O-
nucleophiles (lines 88–89)? 
2) The reaction appears tolerant of sterics in the nucleophile and carboxylic acid substrates, but are bulky 3° R1 substituents
(like t-Bu) on the IIII/PV-hybrid ylide tolerated? 
Conversely, does the reaction work if R1 = H? 
3) The authors prepare the acyl phosphonium salt from the corresponding acyl chloride and PPh3, and subsequently access
product 121 (Figure 5E, bottom) as support for the formation of an intermediate like int-5 in the reaction mechanism (Figure
5G). 
Since this intermediate is isolable and observable by 1H and 31P NMR, can it also be observed under the standard
reaction conditions? 
For instance, if the reaction in the top half of Figure 5E is monitored by NMR, can this intermediate be
observed as it forms? 
Alternatively, if only 1 equiv of carboxylic acid and no additional nucleophile is used, can the initial
product (i.e., the acyl phosphonium) be isolated? 
4) For the deuterium-labeling studies, what length of relaxation delay (d1) was used on the spectrometer to acquire the 1H
NMR spectrum? 
Noting this in the SI would be helpful to confirm that a long enough relaxation delay was used to obtain an
accurate value for % deuterium incorporation. 
5) Is MS fragmentation data or 13C NMR data available for product 18O-7? 
The GC-MS data confirms the label was
transferred from the labeled ylide to the product but doesn’t necessarily prove the location of the label in the product.
Considering the novelty of the proposed PV-oxirene intermediate, providing stronger evidence for its formation would be
ideal and would enhance the usefulness of the 18O-labeling study. 
6) For the mechanistic studies in Figure 5, why was Cu(MeCN)4PF6 used as the catalyst when CuCN was found to be the
optimal catalyst (Table 1) and used in the substrate scope experiments (Figures 2–3)? 
Additionally, addressing the following writing and presentation issues is recommended: 
1) Line 27: “Readily transformation” should be “Ready transformation”. 
2) Figure 1B, top line of right side: “tradictional reactivity” should be “traditional reactivity”. 
3) Table 1: Consider moving Entry 17 elsewhere, such as Figure 2, since the reaction doesn’t fit the scheme shown in Table
1 (different nucleophile and different product). 
4) Line 103: “double bonds containing substrates” should be rephrased as “double bond-containing substrates” or
“substrates containing double bonds”. 
5) Lines 109–111: Rephrase the sentence containing “…high chemoselectivity for substrates containing common
carbenophiles rather than double bonds…” for clarity. 
It sounds like the authors mean to say the substrates reacted with
carbenes instead of double bonds, but it’s somewhat ambiguous. 
6) Figure 2: Typo in product 19. 
“Pn” should be “Ph” based on the structure in the SI. 
7) Lines 142–143: Change the period at the end of Line 142 to a comma to avoid starting Line 143 with a sentence fragment.
8) Figure 3: Headings are missing from the second two sections, depicting O-Nucleophiles and S/Se-Nucleophiles. 
9) Reference #19: publication year should be 1983, not 2002. 
10) SI p. S67: Figure S5. Caption compound “d-199” should be “d-119”. 
11) SI p. S72: The experimental procedure for preparing 121 from 123 should be written in passive voice, past tense, rather
than imperative tense.
