Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript presents a thorough study on a TADF-active macrocycle, CPCQ, and its behavior in various host–guest and
gaseous environments. The authors combine experimental and computational analysis to show that CPCQ exhibits guest-
dependent exciplex formation, tunable TADF properties, and reversible optical sensing toward gases such as O2 and H2.
The work bridges supramolecular chemistry and photophysics, and suggests CPCQ as a promising molecular platform for
optical sensing. Notably, the authors claim superradiant emission under H2 exposure. 
However, several mechanistic claims, especially regarding superradiance and the detailed origin of the gas response, are
not yet sufficiently supported and require substantial clarification. The manuscript may be considered after the following
points are addressed: 
1. The claim that H2 exposure induces “superradiance” in CPCQ is intriguing but not conclusively supported by the
presented data. The observed photophysical changes such as enhanced emission intensity, increased PLQY (~0.93),
higher radiative rate (kr), and spectral narrowing, are consistent with a more efficient, LE-like emissive state, but do not
uniquely demonstrate phase-coherent superradiant emission. The reported ~4.5-fold enhancement in emission intensity
could alternatively arise from hydrogen-induced conformational stabilization, reduced non-radiative decay, or changes in the
local dielectric environment, all of which can increase kr without invoking coherent radiative coupling. Interpreting this
enhancement through the idealized N2 superradiance model (N=2) seems overextended, as such scaling assumes
identical, phase-locked emitters with negligible energetic disorder and dephasing, conditions unlikely in a solution-phase
TADF macrocycle. Moreover, no direct experimental or theoretical evidence for coherent emission or excitonic coupling
among the donor–acceptor units is presented. Could the authors clarify how they distinguish the proposed superradiance
from these alternative mechanisms? Have they considered time-resolved anisotropy or temperature-dependent PL
measurements to provide more direct support for coherent radiative coupling? 
2. The authors propose that O2 quenches emission and suppresses TADF, whereas H2 enhances emission and also
suppresses TADF but yields a bright LE-like state. The mechanistic rationale remains somewhat speculative. Does O2 form
any weakly bound adduct or hydrogen-bonding interaction with CPCQ (in the ground or excited state)? For H2, is there any
evidence for physisorption or preferential localization inside the cavity, beyond the global diffusion analysis? DFT-level
electronic-structure calculations of CPCQ with and without bound gas molecules could help clarify how O2 and H2 perturb
the CT/LE states at the orbital level. 
3. There are many minor things to be fixed, e.g., “Vanderwal’s” → “van der Waals” and harmonize spelling like “sulfur” vs
“sulphur”; “behavior” vs “behaviour”. Avoid repeating full compound names once abbreviations are defined. Ensure the units
of energy are included in Table 1. 
4. The manuscript states that CPCQ’s performance “stems from increased local triplet (LE) density near the lowest singlet
CT state, made possible by both through-bond and through-space CT.” This phrasing is somewhat ambiguous. Could the
authors clarify how through-bond and through-space charge-transfer pathways contribute to an increase in LE character?
Typically, such coupling is expected to enhance CT–LE mixing rather than LE population. Do the authors mean that stronger
D–A electronic coupling increases the LE contribution to the S1/T1 wavefunctions, thereby facilitating RISC? 
5. The manuscript states that “TPN and PN bind tightly, whereas 1,4-DIB does not.” However, the computed binding energy
is –10.2 kcal/mol, which, while weaker than TPN/PN, is not negligibly small and still corresponds to appreciable host–guest
interaction. Could the authors clarify the relative scale or criteria used to categorize binding as “strong” or “weak”? 
6. In the rate expression, the radiative decay rate is said to depend on the square of the “…dipole moment, μ.” This should be
specified as the transition dipole moment associated with the S1 → S0 emission process, not a static dipole moment, to
avoid confusion.

7. The authors assign the red-shifted emission in CPCQ⊃TPN and CPCQ⊃PN to exciplex formation. Have they quantified
the CT character (e.g., via frontier-orbital analysis, natural transition orbitals, or related excited-state descriptors)? Such
analysis would help distinguish exciplex emission from possible conformational or aggregation-induced shifts. 
8. The manuscript infers efficient RISC (kRISC = 2.68 × 107 s-1), but ΔEST is not explicitly reported. Providing the
corresponding energy gap values would be useful. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This communication reports optoelectronic sensing of analytes by the previously-reported macrocycle named CPCQ which
displays thermally activated delayed fluorescence (as the authors acknowledge “introduced in our earlier report (Fig. 1).51”
Ref 51 includes the basic photophysics of CPCQ (PLQY, lifetime RISC data etc). The authors suggest that “superradiance-
type behaviour” is induced by hydrogen gas. MALDI, molecular dynamics and fluorescence lifetime analysis are the main
tools used for the sensing studies. 
The data appear to be scientifically sound and of general interest in the field of host-guest optoelectronic sensing and
modulation of properties. Overall, I do not find sufficient novelty or impact for publication in Communications Chemistry.
Within the Nature family of journals, the work could be better suited to Scientific Reports. Note: I am not an expert in
superradiance behavior, which is suggested on several occasions to be occurring in this communication – so maybe I am
missing something special here? 
I have the following specific points for the authors attention. 
1. Page 1 states “Unlike conventional emitters, CPCQ modulates its emission through guest inclusion and gas exposure.” I
find this rather misleading in implying something special about CPCQ. There are many previous examples of luminescent
macrocycles that modulate their emission through guest inclusion or gas exposure. For examples: Sun, ZH, et al, Advances
in Chiral Macrocycles: Molecular Design and Applications, Chem. Eur. J. 2025, DOI 10.1002/chem.202404217; Wang, K, A
pH-dependent fluorescent and colouring macrocycle for the high selective detection of arginine in pure water, Dyes and
Pigments, 2021, DOI 10.1016/j.dyepig.2021.109203. 
2. The authors cite refs 52-57 concerning cavities of TADF molecules. Additionally, for modulation of TADF by an analyte:
Wang, JQ, Supramolecular TADF Materials and Highly Selective Detection of 2-Cyanopyrazine, Angew Chem. Int. Ed 2025,
DOI 10.1002/anie.202514366. 
3. A review of TADF macrocycles could also be cited in the introduction: Quanyou Feng, et al., Adv. Funct. Mater. 2024, 34,
2312622. 
4. Conclusion states: “These findings establish CPCQ as …. offering a promising foundation for the development of next-
generation materials in photosensitization and gas sensing technologies.” Why might CPCQ be more promising than many
other readily-available luminescent molecules for sensory applications? 
5. Minor point: SI pages 5, 6. The synthesis from methylcalix[4]resorcinarene and characterization of CPCQ shown here has
already been reported in detail in SI of ref. 51: J. Phys. Chem. Lett. 16, 7848-7853 (2025), so there is no need to duplicate
the data here.
