Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript " A Fully Degradable Triboelectric Vagus Nerve Stimulator for Attenuating Cardiac Remodeling and Heart
Failure at Different Stages" presents the development of a bioresorbable, self-powered vagus nerve stimulation (VNS)
platform using a triboelectric nanogenerator (BTENG). The device integrates fully degradable materials and enables low-
level electrical stimulation without an external power source, effectively attenuating cardiac remodeling and dysfunction in
pressure-overload mouse models. The system shows biocompatibility, on-demand biodegradation via ultrasound, and
therapeutic efficacy across different disease stages. This study addresses an important unmet need in the field of
bioelectronic medicine and offers a technically innovative solution toward battery-free, transient neuromodulation. 
While the study presents a promising biodegradable neuromodulation platform, several aspects would benefit from further
clarification. These include quantitative stimulation parameters (e.g., charge density), post-degradation safety validation, and
demonstration of in vivo self-powered operation. In addition, confirming BTENG function after the onset of degradation and
providing basic thermal safety data during ultrasound exposure would strengthen the manuscript. This work possesses
sufficient completeness and novelty, meeting the standard of journal publishing standards; thus I would recommend this
manuscript for possible publication in nature communications after major revision. 
The drawbacks are listed below 
Comment 1: 
The system presented in this study offers a highly convincing approach by utilizing ultrasound to simultaneously trigger
device degradation. To further support the validity and biomedical applicability of this concept, it is recommended to cite
recent research (DOI: 10.1002/adma.202307194). This research demonstrates a representative example of using ultrasound
both as an energy source for TENG operation and as a degradation trigger in biomedical applications, which could help
reinforce the credibility of the strategy proposed in the present work. 
Comment 2: 
The use of 1
 
MHz, 3
 
W/cm² ultrasound for device degradation raises safety concerns due to potential tissue heating.
However, no data are provided to confirm tissue safety. The authors should include supporting experiments such as local
temperature measurements, histological analysis (e.g., H&E, TUNEL), and inflammatory marker expression (e.g., IL-6, TNF-
α) to validate the safety of this approach. 
Comment 3: 
The BTENG output is presented only as peak-to-peak current, without sufficient quantitative characterization of the
stimulation waveform. However, for evaluating neural stimulation efficacy, key parameters such as charge density are
essential. Including such metrics would offer a clearer understanding of the stimulation conditions and allow meaningful
comparison with conventional neuromodulation approaches. 
Comment 4: 
While the study demonstrates the device’s degradation profile over time, there is no experimental data evaluating the
biological safety after complete degradation of the BTENG. Specifically, tissue response, inflammation, or potential residue
accumulation post-degradation were not assessed. Including long-term histological or immunological analyses beyond the

degradation period would strengthen the safety validation and support the claim of full biocompatibility. 
Comment 5: 
While the study includes an AB + atropine group to control for drug-specific effects, it lacks evaluation of systemic responses
to long-term atropine administration (1 mg/kg/day for 7 weeks). Key physiological parameters—such as changes in body
weight, heart rate, behavior, or organ toxicity—are not reported, nor is there reference to atropine's pharmacokinetic or safety
profile. Including such data would improve the reliability of the mechanistic interpretation and help rule out confounding
effects from systemic atropine exposure. 
Comment 6: 
The transcriptomic analysis provides initial insights into LL-VNS–mediated gene regulation; however, the interpretation
remains superficial and lacks mechanistic validation. While key pathways such as extracellular matrix remodeling and
programmed cell death are highlighted, no follow-up experiments (e.g., Western blotting, pathway inhibition, or validation of
upstream regulators) are performed to confirm functional relevance. Including such validation would strengthen the
mechanistic conclusions drawn from the RNA-seq data. 
Comment 7: 
Although the manuscript demonstrates the structural degradation of the BTENG over time, it does not provide direct
measurements of the device’s electrical output after degradation begins. There is no quantitative assessment linking the
onset of physical breakdown to changes in output performance. To confirm functional reliability, especially during the
transition phase, it is recommended to include output measurements (e.g., voltage/current) beyond the point at which
structural degradation is first observed. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The study by Guo et al. describes the development of a novel triboelectric nanogenerator for LL-VNS with the characteristics
of biodegradation and self-powerness through utilization of body movement for charging. The authors establish a stimulation
intensity threshold for VNS, describe the 
anatomy and development of the biodegradable triboelectric nanogenerator
(BTENG), the steps of its degradation and assess its biocompatibility. Next, they assess the device’s efficacy in preventing
HF development, and reversing both established remodeling and established HF in a mouse model of AB. The authors went
on to assess the differential gene expression in treated subjects, confirming a shift toward healthy controls. Finally, to
investigate the role of VNS-related acetylcholine release in cardioprotection, they administered atropine, and observed loss
of beneficial effects of VNS in this setting. The study introduces a potentially applicable and translatable new equipment,
however there are concerns regarding the internal validity and generizability of their observations. 
Comments: 
1. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of this LL-VNS equipment in improving cardiac function, however, they do not
provide comparison with an established equipment, such as an approved implantable VNS or a tVNS device. As such, their
results are not comparable to the “gold standard” approved practices. 
2. The authors state: “No significant intergroup differences in cardiac function were observed in week 1.” Except for LVEF
and LVFS, no other measure is provided to confirm the phenotype at baseline, including invasive or doppler
echocardiographic estimation of pressures confirming aortic constriction. 
3. The authors do not report HR or BP measurements for any experiment, allowing for 
questioning of potential bias effects on
results. 
4. How was effective vagal nerve capture confirmed after implantation and during the course of treatment? 
5. The authors do not mention whether sham stimulation was performed in the experiment for establishment of optimal
stimulation parameters (first Results section). 
6. For the RNSseq experiment, how many samples were analyzed for each group? At which timepoint were they treated? 1-,
2-, or 4- weeks post-AB? 
7. The authors describe, among other statements, “Moreover, we set up a current source nerve stimulator with low current
and high resistance to ensure 154 the accuracy of the VNS intensity”. This circuit requires more details to allow
reproducibility. 
8. Evidence of degradation process is described in text and only supported by a schematic and no further
images/measurements. (Figure 3g-h). 
9. No blood testing is reported during degradation to check for levels of degradable substances. Except for specific tissue
histology, no other measure of well-being is reported (i.e. behavioral monitoring, mortality, other morbidity etc). Please revise
accordingly. 
10. The authors state (page 8, lines 300-301): “The tissue slices from the skin showed no significant inflammatory response,
and numerous ring-shaped structures were observed.” What is the nature of these ring-shaped structures? Were these ring-
shaped structures further evaluated? 
11. What time of day was the stimulation performed? Was the timing consistent? How many days per week? 
12. For the late intervention experiment about reversal of established HF, what was the duration of the stimulation? 
13. For the RNAseq results, was a confirmatory analysis performed using a second technique (i.e. qPCR or flow cytometry to
validate the findings of the main differentially expressed genes? 
14. The authors conclude that both 10 and 20 min LL-VNS have “equivalent molecular responses” however this observation
is based on clustering patterns; rigorous statistical analysis accompanied by plots is needed to support this claim. 
15. No specific genes are provided for pathways investigated in the differential gene expression analysis that is depicted in
figures 7 and S9, S10. Please provide a list of specific genes in the supplement.

17. The authors should clarify in the introduction that their hypothesis is centered around pressure load-induced HF, and not

that becomes maladaptive, leading to ventricular dilation, 92 hypertrophy, and increased fibrosis”, etc)

We have now evaluated systemic safety parameters throughout the 7-week atropine
