Reviewer Comment 1.2 —  The authors have mentioned the use of raw cryo-EM particle images 
into AlphaFold to directly guide atomic model prediction in the abstract section. While in the 
results section they have mentioned the use of reconstructed density maps from the di! erent sets 
of cryo-EM images. It is necessary to elaborate in details about the use of cryo-EM micrographs
and reconstructed density maps in specific modules/ components of the network architecture. In the
discussion section, it is mentioned CoCoFold bypasses the density reconstruction step, but in section
2.1, it is mentioned “ Predicted structures are aligned to the cryo-EM density reconstructed from 
the input particles” . Please , make this more clearer.
Reply: Thank you for your question. Since the global coordinate system of the AlphaFold-predicted
structure (which is often arbitrary) di! ers from that of the experimental particle poses (defined during
reconstruction), it is necessary to align the initial prediction to the experimental data before performing
fine-tuning.
In summary, the CoCoFold consists of the following two steps (Fig. R1):
1. I nit ializat ion: We first applied existing cryo-EM software to process the particle images and 
obtain a coarse map. Meanwhile, we used AlphaFold to generate a predicted structure. The pre- 
dicted structure was then aligned to the coarse map through an a” ne transformation, establishing 
a consistent coordinate frame.
2. Fine-t uning: Based on the obtained a” ne transformation, we constructed an end-to-end dif- 
ferentiable architecture to fine-tune AlphaFold such that its output better fits the experimental 
particle images after training. It is worth noting that this a” ne transformation is fixed during
fine-tuning, and therefore no map information is required in this step.
We have revised the “ The design of CoCoFold” subsection and the Discussion section to clearly 
distinguish between the use of the map for initial coordinate alignment and the use of particles for

Reviewer Comment 1.3 —  The network is built upon OpenFold, so there is no novelty in 
architectural design. However, authors should state the hyperparameters required to fine tune this 
model for the reproducibility of the work.
Reply: Thank you for your valuablecomment. Built upon the OpenFold framework, the primary goal of 
CoCoFold is to provide a stable fine-tuning module for AlphaFold-Multimer using raw cryo-EM particle 
images. Specifically, the key innovation lies in the Auxiliary Attention Mechanism introduced within the 
Structure Module (see Methods). Without this mechanism, directly fine-tuning AlphaFold with particle 
images causes the model collapse (predicting compact, ball-like structures), as shown in the second row 
of Table R2.
The hyperparameters are set as follows. We use ADAM optimizer to train the neural network. The
learning rates for Gaussian mixtures’ amplitudes, widths, and structure module are 0.01, 0.005, 0.0001, 
respectively. Training batchsize is 32 and gradient accumulation is used to achieve this. Training epoch 
is set to 10. The particles are randomly sampled during training. Reconstruction loss and penalty loss 
has the same weight. All of these details have been incorporated into the Methods section in the revised 
manuscript.

A ddit ional experiment s. We have incorporated 7 additional datasets to further evaluate the

For each dataset, we evaluate CoCoFold under four conditions (two scarce-particle scenarios and two

Reviewer Comment 2.3 —  The input data requirements should be clarified. It should be explicitly 
stated whether the particle orientations and CTF parameters are assumed to be known from a
previous 3D reconstruction and how these are used in the fine-tuning loop. Also, the authors could
comment on why accessing directly to the 2D images would be beneficial as compared to comparing 
the atomic model to the 3D map.
Reply: Thank you for your suggestion.
Fixed poses and CT F. In the current version, we assume that the poses and CTF parameters are 
known, which can be obtained from a standard cryo-EM processing pipeline such as CryoSPARC. These 
parameters remain fixed during the fine-tuning step and are used to synthesize particle images (each
predicted atomic structure is projected into 2D at the known pose and convolved with the corresponding
CTF) for computing the loss against the raw experimental particles. The resulting loss is then used to 
compute gradients for updating the network parameters.
M ap versus part icles. The key reason for using raw particle images rather than a 3D map is
the irreversible loss of high-frequency detail during 3D reconstruction. 3D reconstruction is inherently 
an averaging process that acts as a low-pass filter. In scenarios with limited data (scarce particles)
or missing views, the resulting 3D map often su! ers from resolution degradation and anisotropic blur-
ring, e! ectively “ smearing out” high-resolution details. To empirically validate this, we conducted a 
comparative experiment using the MSP-1 (PDB 6ZBH) dataset with a limited subset of 1.1k particles,
which yields a low-resolution reconstruction. We compared two fine-tuning strategies: (1) CoCoFold
(Ours): Fine-tuning against raw 2D particle images. (2) Map-based Baseline: Fine-tuning against the 
projections of the 3D reconstruction map. As Fig. R7 shown, fine-tuning against raw 2D particle images 
(TM-score: 0.97, RMSD: 1.82 ûA) is better than using the projections of the 3D reconstruction map 
(TM-score: 0.86, RMSD: 4.91 ûA) when meeting the scarce-particle condition (1.1k particles). We have 
added a subsection “ Comparison between direct 2D image supervision and 3D map supervision” in the 
Methods section to include above results.
Reviewer Comment 2.4 —  It might be useful to discuss whether CoCoFold could also work 
with 2D class averages (after CTF correction) as input, since this could further reduce noise and 
computational load.
Reply: Thanks for your suggestion. While 2D class averages indeed o! er higher signal-to-noise ratios 
(SNR), we believe they are unsuitable for the specific high-resolution fine-tuning objectives of CoCoFold 
for two primary reasons: (1) Loss of High-Frequency Information: Class averaging suppresses high- 
frequency information and introduces blurring artifacts, which removes precisely the structural signals 
needed for atomic-level refinement. Raw particles, although noisier, preserve the full experimental

information and enable stable optimization. (2) Ambiguity in Pose Constraints: 2D class averages do 
not contain a well-defined projection orientation. Each average represents a mixture of particles with 
di! erent poses, and the resulting image doesnot correspond to any single known view. Without a known
pose, the projection operator in CoCoFold cannot be evaluated, and gradients cannot be assigned to
atom coordinates. Therefore, utilizing raw particle images remains the optimal strategy to maintain the 
full spectrum of experimental information required for fine-tuning AlphaFold to high accuracy.

Figure R3: Design of CoCoFold. a, CoCoFold consists of three parts: a fixed part, 
a fine-tuning part, and a training part. In terms of neural network architecture, the fixed 
part and fine-tuning part are largely consistent with those of AF-m. Regarding parameter 
weights, the fixed part maintains the same parameter weights as AF-m, whereas the fine- 
tuning part utilizes the parameter weights of AF-m as the initial parameters for fine-tuning. 
The gradient descent employed for fine-tuning the fine-tuning part is backpropagated from 
the training part, which constitutes a Gaussian mixture Molmap module. This module gen- 
eratesa density map of thepredicted structureusing a Gaussian MixtureModel (GMM), e.g. 
MolMap. Projections are then computed under specified poses and CTFs and subsequently 
compared with raw particle images. The resulting FRC loss is backpropagated through the 
Gaussian mixture Molmap module, while the weights within this module undergo training 
simultaneously. b, The fine-tuning part operates as follows: The Evoformer generates fixed 
pair representations and MSA representations, adhering to the original information flow of 
AF-m. A parallel refinement branch incorporates a lightweight attention mechanism and 
linear transformation to adapt MSA representations based on image-derived constraints. 
These updated features are integrated with the IPA module and backbone frames module 
to produce refined structures. Here, f and fimage denote features derived from the original 
information flow and the image-constrained information flow, respectively. c, The training 
component operates as follows: First, the predicted atomic model is aligned to the experi-
mental coordinate system using a fixed a! ne transformation. This transformation is derived
by aligning the model to the initial map reconstructed from particle images. Notably, the 
map serves solely to provide the a! ne transformation matrix and does not participate in 
gradient updates. Subsequently, a simulated density based on GMM is generated, which is 
then utilized to produce 2D projections for iterative fine-tuning.
