Review Report 
In the manuscript titled ″Probing orbital currents through inverse orbital Hall and Rashba effects″, authors present a
comprehensive experimental study investigating inverse orbital Hall effect (IOHE) and inverse orbital Rashba effect (IORE)
in YIG/Pt/NM heterostructures. The designed multilayer system enables efficient orbital-current injection at the Pt/NM
interface, where a spin current generated via spin-pumping or the spin Seebeck effect is converted into an orbital current
through the strong spin–orbit coupling of Pt. 
Overall, this work presents an effective approach for orbital-current generation and for separating spin, orbital, bulk and
surface effects. The findings are highly relevant for the emerging field of orbitronic devices. I therefore recommend the
manuscript for publication after the following revisions.

Q1. The manuscript studies a diverse set of materials (metals and a semiconductor), but the 
scientific motivation for this specific selection is is not clearly justified. The authors should more 
clearly articulate the scientific reasoning behind choosing these particular materials (oxidized Cu, 
Ti, Ge, Au) and how they collectively advance the understanding of orbital physics across different

R1.  We agree with the reviewer that providin g a clearer scientific justification  for our material 
selection strengthens the manuscript. Our choice of CuOₓ, Ti, Ge, and Au was strategic, designed 
to build a systematic and comparative framework for understanding orbital physics across distinct 
material classes and conversion mechanisms. 
CuOx was chosen because it exhibits strong p–d hybridization between Cu and O, capable of 
generating the orbital Rashba effect (ORE) interfacially, as theoretically predicted by Go et al. 
[Phys. Rev. B 103, L121113 (2021)]. Ti and Ge were selected as prototypical systems for a bulk-
dominant orbital mechanism (Inverse Orbital Hall Effect, IOHE). Crucially, their theoretical orbital 
Hall angles have opposite signs (positive for Ti, negative for Ge), as theoretically predicted [Baek, 
Phys. Rev. B 104, 245204 (2021); Go et al., Phys. Rev. B 109, 174435 (2024)]. This combination 
provides a powerful integrated experiment: observing the signal enhancement with Ti and signal 
suppression/cancellation with Ge offers unambiguous evidence of orbital transport that cannot be 
explained by spin -based effects.  Au has been included as a reference noble metal with a 
theoretically small but negative orbital Hall angle. This allows us to test the robustness and 
predictive power of our quantitative diffusive model across a wider range of materials and to 
confirm the sign-dependent behavior observed in Ge. 
In essence, this selection is not arbitrary; it is a designed idea that enables us to:  (i) Unravel 
interfacial (IORE) from bulk (IOHE) orbital -to-charge conversion.  (ii) Use the positive/negative 
orbital Hall angle separation (Ti/Ge) as a definitive experimental signature.  (iii) Extract and 
compare quantitative orbital transport parameters (diffusion lengths, conversion efficiencies) 
across metals and a semiconductor.  
As suggested, we have incorporated a clear explanation of this rationale at the end of the Methods 
section and have reinforced it in the Conclusion.

Q4. The authors extract orbital diffusion lengths of metallic and semiconductor materials using a 
diffusive model. The orbital diffusion length extracted for Ti ( λTi = 3.5 nm) is over an order of 
magnitude shorter than a recently reported value of ~60 nm (Nature 619, 52 –56 (2023)). The 
authors should provide a detailed discussion on the potential origi ns of this significant 
discrepancy. 
 
R4. We thank the reviewer for raising this important question. The apparent discrepancy between 
our extracted orbital diffusion length in Ti ( λₒ ≈ 3.5 nm) and the ≈60 nm reported in Nature 619, 
52–56 (2023) is indeed significant. However, we believe these values probe distinct physical 
processes, rather than conflicting with each other. Recent literature has emphasized that reported 
orbital diffusion lengths span a wide range, even within the same material, as highlighted by 
Aguilar-Pujol et al. (10.1016/j.newton.2025.100290) and Idrodo  et al. 
(10.48550/arXiv.2403.09269). This spread reflects the sensitivity of λₒ to both extrinsic and 
intrinsic factors. Extrinsic influences include structural morphology, disorder, and the specific 
technique used to generate and detect orbital currents. Intrinsic factors include spin –orbit 
interaction strength, the electronic structure, and the material magnetic character of the material. 
In Ti, for example, λₒ values reported so far vary from ~2 nm (Hanle magnetoresistance) to ~61 
nm (MOKE), consistent with this broad dispersion. These discrepancies can be explained by the 
fact that different techniques a ctivate different OAM relaxation channels . Our method probes 
orbital angular momentum injected by orbital pumping  from a spin –orbital intertwined current, 
whereas in Nature 619, 52 –56 (2023) the authors probed the OHE by light propagating into Ti, 
which exhibits a complicated thickness -dependence of the MOKE signals, as recognized by the 
authors themselves. The two regimes need not share the same dominant relaxation pathway. 
Therefore, the shorter λₒ measured here does not contradict the longer value reported elsewhere; 
instead, it indicates that orbital transport in Ti involves multiple relaxation and conversion 
processes, each characterized by its own length scale. Recent th eoretical work supports this 
multichannel relaxation picture and predicts different characteristic lengths depending on the 
excitation mechanism and detection process [arXiv:2502.11040]. A sentence discussing this issue 
has been added in the revised version of the manuscript.

R5. We thank the reviewer for their positive feedback  on our results and for raising these 
appropriated questions, which allow us to clarify the mechanism of orbital transport in Ge. 
 
The orbital Hall effect in Ge originates from the intrinsic orbital texture of its p -orbital dominated 
valence bands (J. Appl. Phys. 120, 195701 (2016)). Unlike the spin Hall effect, which often relies 
on strong SOC from heavy atoms, the OHE in Ge is an intrinsic property of its band structure. 
The specific p-orbital character (px, py, pz) of a Bloch state depends on its crystal momentum, k. 
This momentum-dependent orbital composition (orbital texture) means that electrons moving in 
different directions carry distinct orbital angular momenta.  Due to this orbital texture, el ectrons 
with opposite momenta ( +k and -k ) are associated with different orbital states. This imbalance 
leads to a net flow of orbital angular momentum that is transverse to the charge current, which is 
the essence of the intrinsic OHE, as elegantly described in the theoretical work by Baek and Lee 
[Phys. Rev. B 104, 245204 (2021)]. In short, the orbital current  is a direct consequence of the 
symmetry of the Ge crystal  and its p-orbital character, not a secondary effect induced by strong 
SOC. 
  
Regarding increasing the efficiency of orbital -charge interconversion, our results provide 
important insights for future studies. According to the physics proposed by Baek and Lee, the 
magnitude of the intrinsic OHE can be modulated by adjusting the effect ive orbital curvature of 
the valence band. In this sense, some promising alternatives include: (i) Applying biaxial strain or 
creating Si ₁₋ₓGeₓ alloys can directly modify the orbital curvature of the valence bands. Tuning 
these parameters could enhance the intrinsic OHE response by optimizing the Berry phase 
landscape that governs it. (ii) As the OHE signal is highly sensitive to the p osition of the Fermi 
level within the valence band , the strongest response comes from regions of high orbital 
curvature. We propose using electrostatic gating or precise light p-type doping to position E_F at 
these "sweet spots," thereby maximizing the generated orbital current.  (iii) The efficiency of 
converting an orbital current into a measurable charge signal (via the inverse orbital Hall effect) 
critically depends on the interface where this conversion occurs. Future work should focus on 
engineering the interface between Ge and a d etector metal (e.g., by introducing ultra -thin oxide 
layers or using different metal contacts) to minimize orbital scattering and maximize angular 
momentum transfer. 
A short paragraph has been included in the revised version of the manuscript.

R3. We appreciate the pointed raised by the reviewer and clarify that the assumption of zero 
orbital injection from YIG is well supported by the electronic structure and symmetry  of this 
materials. YIG is an electrical insulator where the magnetic moments reside on Fe³ ⁺ ions 
embedded in highly symmetric oxygen octahedral and tetrahedral sites. This specific combination 
suppresses the two main sources of strong SOC: itinerant electrons and low -symmetry crystal

metallic injectors such as Ni will require explicit treatment of both spin and orbital pumping, but in
