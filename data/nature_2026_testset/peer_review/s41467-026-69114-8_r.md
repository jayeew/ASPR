Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
A hallmark of successful clinical management for invasive fungal disease is the availability of effective antifungal therapy,
and glucan synthase inhibitors represent one of the most important drug classes. The echinocandins, approved for clinical
use nearly 25 years ago, remain first-line therapy for a broad range of candidiasis and candidemia. Although they are limited
to intravenous administration, they have recently been joined by the first-generation orally bioavailable enfumafungin
derivatives, which also inhibit glucan synthase. Genetic studies of resistance suggest that the enfumafungin drug binds to
the glucan synthase complex at a distinct yet partially overlapping site compared with the echinocandins. 
Extensive knowledge has accumulated regarding the genetics, resistance mechanisms, and biochemical and kinetic
properties of glucan synthase, encoded by the FKS genes. However, until recently, little was known about the enzyme’s
structural organization or the molecular basis of drug–target interactions underlying inhibition—information critical for
optimizing antifungal therapy. Recent cryo-EM studies from this group and others have begun to illuminate these structural
features, especially concerning resistance-associated amino acid substitutions. 
Yet, our understanding of how
echinocandins and related enfumafungin drugs achieve target inhibition remains largely inferential, based primarily on
genetic analyses of resistance. 
This new study by a leading glucan synthase structural biology group provides much-needed insight into the structural basis
of drug–target interactions responsible for enzyme inhibition. 
The authors resolved five cryo-EM structures of Fks1 and Fks2
in the apo state, in complex with the triterpenoid enfumafungin drug, as well as in an open state induced 
by echinocandin
drug caspofungin. Structural analysis along with strategic mutagenesis revealed the putative enfumafungin binding site, and
the likely mechanism for clinically important drug-resistant mutations. Consistent with inferences from numerous genetic
studies in the literature and some new mutant analysis, it is proposed that the enfumafungin attaches to transmembrane
helix TM5 of the glucan synthase, reorganizes its nearby lipid environment, and stabilizes the enzyme in a unique basal
state with intact active site. New insights about the location of the glucan channel, which appears to lie between TMDs 1 and
2 are exciting. 
The elucidation of a structure from cryoEM studies with the enfumafungin bound is a key advancement of this work.
Furthermore, the observation of an elongated extra density around the TM5 of Fks1 and Fks2 in the transmembrane region,
which aligned well with the EFU molecule, was a major finding. 
Overall, this is an important contribution that strongly advances the field, and supports more advanced computationally
assisted drug design to develop next generation drugs that 
optimize enzyme inhibition across a range of high-threat fungi and have the potential to overcome FKS hot-spot resistance
known to cause refractory drug responses in patients.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this work, You and co-workers provide cryoEM structures of β-1,3-glucan synthases Fks1 and Fks2 in the apo state, the
triterpenoid drug enfumafungin-bound state, and an open state induced by echinocandin incubation. It is worth mentioning
that structures of Fsk1 were previously reported (Li et al. Cryo-EM structure of the β-1,3-glucan synthase FKS1-Rho1
complex. Nat Commun. 16:2054 (2025); Zhao et al. Structure of a fungal 1,3-β-glucan synthase. Sci Adv. 9:eadh7820
(2023); Hu et al. Structural and mechanistic insights into fungal β-1,3-glucan synthase FKS1. Nature 616(7955):190-198
(2023). In combination with single-point mutagenesis, activity measurements, growth complementation and antifungal
susceptibility assays, the authors provide new and important insights on the molecular basis of catalytic cycle, activation by
Rho1, and inhibition by triterpenoid and echinocandins of β-1,3-glucan synthases. The article is well written and the
interpretation of the experimental data support the proposed model. Therefore, I support publication of the manuscript in
Nature Communications. 
Some suggestions for improvement are listed below:

by Rho1”. Please, elaborate the main similarities/differences and how Rho1 regulates the enzymes.

identity/nomenclature.
