Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript provides a significant advancement in the scalable fabrication of perovskite solar cells by offering a new
perspective on a critical challenge: the persistent performance gap between laboratory-scale spin-coating and industrially
viable blade-coating methods. The authors compellingly identify the prolonged solvent-precursor interaction time (τint), a
previously overlooked kinetic parameter, as a primary factor limiting the quality of blade-coated films. Their core finding
reveals that an extended τint stabilizes a detrimental solvent adduct intermediate phase, which in turn increases residual
solvent content and degrades the crystallinity of the final film. To address this limitation, the authors introduce an elegant and
effective "dynamic coordination competition" strategy, which successfully engineers a more favorable crystallization
pathway for high-quality perovskite films within the blade-coating process. The success of this method is demonstrated by
the outstanding power conversion efficiencies achieved: 26.5% for small-area devices (0.0665 cm2) and an impressive
22.0% for large-scale sub-modules (722.4 cm2). This work holds significant implications for the scalable manufacturing and
commercialization of perovskite solar cells, offering valuable guidance for overcoming key industrial hurdles. Therefore, I
recommend it for publication after the authors have addressed the following issues. 
1. While the authors have convincingly demonstrated the role of the 2OH additive in the precursor solution and during the
film formation process, its eventual fate within the final, annealed film remains unclear. An analysis of its residual
concentration and spatial distribution would be highly valuable. 
2. The manuscript proposes the coordination competition strategy as a "universal kinetic knob" for controlling crystallization
in scalable perovskite fabrication. However, the supporting evidence is currently limited to a single, high-performance
perovskite composition (Cs0.02FA0.98PbI3) prepared with a specific NMP-based solvent system. The applicability of this
method to other widely used formulations, such as mixed-halide perovskites or the prevalent DMF/DMSO solvent systems,
has not yet been demonstrated. Therefore, while the strategy is shown to be highly effective for the reported system, further
investigation across a broader range of precursor chemistries and processing conditions is necessary to fully substantiate
the claim of universality. 
3. The central thesis of manuscript posits that the observed improvements are driven by a "coordination competition"
mechanism, wherein the hydroxyl groups of the 2OH additive outcompete the NMP solvent for Pb2+. However, as a
quaternary ammonium salt, the 2OH additive may also introduce other significant physicochemical effects within the
precursor solution. These could include alterations to the solution's viscosity and surface tension, which are known to
influence nucleation and crystal growth dynamics. Could the authors elaborate on how they were able to decouple these
potential synergistic contributions from the primary coordination competition mechanism? 
4. The data systematically presented in Figure 3 convincingly demonstrate that the number of hydroxyl (-OH) groups is a
dominant factor governing the additive's effectiveness. The results consistently show a stronger coordination effect and
improved crystallization control as the number of -OH groups increases from zero to two. This clear trend raises a critical
question: is the beneficial effect of this strategy directly proportional to the number of hydroxyl groups? 
5. The manuscript posits that the PbI2-NMP solvent adduct intermediate phase is detrimental to the final film quality, a
conclusion supported by observations of increased residual solvent and reduced crystallinity in blade-coated films where
this phase is prominent. This finding appears to stand in contrast to a significant body of literature suggesting that the
formation of an intermediate phase can be beneficial, for instance, by lowering the nucleation energy barrier for the
perovskite phase (e.g., Science, 2021, 372, 1327-1332). Could the authors, therefore, elaborate on their perspective
regarding this apparent dichotomy? Specifically, what factors, in their view, determine whether a given intermediate phase is
advantageous or disadvantageous for achieving high-quality crystallization?

6. The manuscript presents compelling operational stability data, demonstrating the enhanced durability of the 2OH-treated
devices under continuous one-sun illumination while tracking the maximum power point at approximately 50 °C. While these
results are convincing, it is suggested that the authors supplement the operational data with results from other key stress
tests, such as intrinsic thermal stability. Secondly, the External Quantum Efficiency (EQE) spectra for the champion devices
are not presented. Providing the EQE spectrum is crucial for independently verifying the accuracy of the short-circuit current
density (JSC) values reported in the J-V tests.

(Remarks to the Author) 
In the work "Suppressing Solvent Adducts via Coordination Competition Enables Scalable Perovskite Photovoltaics" the
authors describe how the introduction of bis(2-hydroxyethyl) dimethylammonium chloride to a double cation perovksite
solution with the solvents 2-Me and NMP can impede an unvaforable PbI2:NMP solvent phase and thus stablize the
precursor solution to yield superior crystallization and phase purity of the resulting perovskite films. The evidence for the
interaction between NMP and 2OH groups appears convincing due to a variety of measurements and DFT calculations. The
reported device performances are impressive, the scaling losses are state-of-the-art and the large area devices shown have
the active area correctly specified. 
Nevertheless, I am afraid, I cannot recommend the work for publication in its current state to meet the high standards of the
journal. There are two main arguments for this: 
1. Two of the main hypotheses of the work are not properly tested (or not correctly posed). 
The authors write: 
"Here, we identify the solvent-precursor interaction time (τint) in the wet film as the missing link. During blade coating, the
slow solvent evaporation rate prolongs τint, stabilizing the lead halide•N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) adduct, locking in
solvent residue, and ultimately compromising film crystallinity. To overcome this bottleneck, we devise a dynamic
coordination competition strategy, employing the hydroxyl-terminated molecular that ooutcompetes NMP for Pb2+
coordination." 
Yet, the time parameter (τint) is no-where determined nor properly compared to a reference that would justify the authors
claim of having identified τint as the principal cause of upscaling difficulties with perovskite solar cells. Another issue with
this claim is that "missing link" insinuates that the interaction time would be the main issue in general of up-scaling of
perovskite solution processes, while it might only be applicable to the specific precursor solution and drying method the
authors employ. 
Therefore, I would like to give the authors two choices to implement a revision of these shortcomings. 
Choice a): Drop or replace the hypothesis. 
What I perceive when I see the authors data and provided evidence is not the determination of an interaction time parameter,
but rather "intermediate phase engineering for blade-coated PSCs". In fact, there are several reports that did similar
analyses, sometimes revealing beneficial impact of certain intermediate phases, sometimes detrimental. Examples are 
https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/aenm.202500536

evidence of prior works on the topic accurately in the introduction, and then build on the state-of-the-art here to showcase
how their work for their particular precursor extents the state-of-the-art. I would, in this case, like to motivate the authors to
redesign their introduction and manuscript to meet the new claims.

(Remarks to the Author) 
This article makes a significant contribution to perovskite photovoltaics by identifying τint as a critical parameter and
demonstrating that coordination competition enables high-performance linear printed devices. The authors identify a
previously overlooked kinetic parameter governing film quality in blade-coating processes and propose a coordination
competition strategy using bis(2-hydroxyethyl) dimethylammonium chloride (2OH) to address this challenge. The material
characterization is sound, especially FTIR and crystallography studies , though statistical rigor for large-area devices can
add value to a certain extent. The work's practical impact and mechanistic insights make it suitable for high-impact
publication following the recommended revisions. The efficiency of 26.5% is rather impressive with blade-coated devices,
but an independent certification is beneficial to demonstrate the state-of-the-art performance of the blade-coated device,
especially considering the impact of the journal here. I have a few major queries from the scientific front before
recommending the manuscript for publication in Nature Communications. 
Comments for authors: 
1. The authors made a few typos, namely “hydroxyl-terminated molecular that ooutcompetes NMP”, which should be
“hydroxyl-terminated molecule that outcompetes”. 
Additionally, it would be beneficial to introduce the molecule in this
introductory paragraph, explicitly stating its chemical name at the very least. 
2. Do PACl and MACl impact the crystallization rate by controlling the intermediate adducts? Could the authors clarify the
role of such high mol% additives alongside 2OH molecule? What happens in the absence of PACl or MACl but in the
presence of 2OH? Do you expect them to behave similar to 0OH molecule used in the study? 
3. The authors should demonstrate the difference in concentration 2OH group (PbI2-2OH-NMP) 
and its impact on C=O
stretching vibration peaks by showing the peak-shift evolution with different concentrations of 2OH. The concentration of
2OH in relation to other compounds used for FTIR measurements should also be mention in detail. 
4. Why does 0OH molecules increase NMP retention in LC-MS analysis in Fig. S11. Does PACl and MACl have a similar
effect? 
5. Can 2OH completely replace NMP completely? Can the authors shed some insights into this process? 
6. The authors should mention concentrations of 0OH, 1OH and 2OH used for statistics in Fig S13. 
7. The authors should display the statistics obtained for large-area devices with and without control. This is important to
understand the true potential of the additive. 
8. Finally, please make a comparison table comparing the performance of particular bandgap blade-coated devices with the
literature, so the reader could get a sense of state-of-the-art devices.

Reply: We appreciate the reviewer’s insightful question regarding the universality of our strategy. 
Our approach targets the fundamental kinetics of meniscus -guided coating: the 2OH molecule 
modulates the stability of the intermediate phase by competitively inhibiting the formation of 
overly robust lead halide -solvent adducts. This interaction accelerates solvent removal and 
establishes a superior crystallization template. Since this mechanism relies on basic coordination 
chemistry, we hypothesized it would remain effective across various perovskite formulations. To 
validate this, we applied the optimized protocol to two distinct narrow -bandgap compositions: a 
triple-cation formulation MA 0.05Cs0.02FA0.93PbI3 and a mixed -halide system 
(Cs0.02FA0.98PbI3)0.98(MAPbBr3)0.02. The statistical distributions in Fig. R2 and Fig. R3  reveal 
consistent performance enhancements across all photovoltaic metrics for both compositions, 
mirroring our initial findings. We then tested the robustness of this strategy against alternative 
solvent environments. Replacing the standard host with a 2 -pyrrolidone (PDI) coordinating 
solvent system yielded comparable improvements, as detailed in Fig. R4 . These parallel gains 
confirm that the 2OH strategy is a generalized solution for intermediate phase engineering in 
scalable fabrication, functioning independently of specific cationic ratios or solvent choices.

Preparation of perovskite precursors and films
For the preparation of perovskite films with a composition MA 0.05Cs0.02FA0.93PbI3, 1.65 M 
perovskite precursor solution was prepared by dissolving MAI, FAPbI 3 and CsPbI 3 powder in 
mixed solvent of 2 -Me and NMP at volume ratio 10:1. Additional 10 mol% PACl and 15 mol% 
MACl, 2 mg/mL LP and 0.2 mg/mL ODADI were added to the precursor solution as additives. 
Then the filtered perovskite precursor was blade -coated onto the substrates with a gap of 120 μm 
at a coating speed of 15 mm/s under dry air quenching. The blade -coating processes were

4. Why does 0OH molecules increase NMP retention in LC-MS analysis in Fig. S11. Does PACl 
and MACl have a similar effect? 
Reply: The anomalous retention of NMP induced by 0OH stems from its chemical inertness 
relative to the coordination environment. Lacking the active hydroxyl moiety, 0OH is incapable of 
competitively displacing NMP ligands similar to 2OH. Instead, it likely stabilizes the PbI 2-NMP 
solvate structure through steric confinement or electrostatic interactions, effectively obstructing 
solvent volatilization channels. This results in the observed counterintuitive increase in residual 
solvent. This behavior stands in stark contrast to that of PACl and MACl. These chlorides function 
as the essential driver for high-quality α-FAPbI3 formation. As detailed in Point 2, they operate by 
forming RA···H-Cl-PbI2 intermediates that lower the thermodynamic barrier for the δ→α phase 
transition. Crucially, their mechanism involves the deprotonation and subsequent volatilization, a 
process that promotes nucleation and crystal growth. Consequently, while 0OH exacerbates 
solvent trapping, PACl and MACl actively engineer the crystallization kinetics to construct 
high-quality perovskite films, representing fundamentally opposing mechanisms regarding film 
evolution.

High-quality film formation of blade-coating relies on a precise kinetic equilibrium between rapid 
nucleation, driven by the evaporation of the volatile host (2 -ME), and retarded crystal growth

meniscus-guided blade coating and regulate the crystallization rate. As a solid additive, 2OH lacks 
the vapor pressure dynamics requisite for regulating wet -film drying rates. Consequently, 
removing NMP eliminates this solvent -phase buffer, precipitating uncontrolled, rapid nucleation 
and inferior grain morphology.
Crucially, NMP governs the precursor colloidal network and rheological parameters required for 
the blade-coating process window. Its strong coordination modulates viscosity and surface tension 
to accommodate meniscus -guided deposition, ensuring macroscopic homogeneity. Substituting 
this liquid regulator with high-concentration solid 2OH destabilizes the ink rheology, shifting fluid 
properties outside the optimal processing regime and inevitably compromising both film 
uniformity and crystalline quality.
Therefore, NMP functions as an essential dual -purpose regulator, defining precursor rheology 
while balancing nucleation and growth kinetics through its volatile coordination. 2OH, by contrast, 
operates strictly as a solid-state modulator to refine phase purity through coordination competition. 
Attempting to substitute the coordination solvent with this additive incurs severe thermodynamic 
penalties, yielding impurity phases and a total loss of kinetic control that precludes 
high-performance device fabrication.

Reviewer #2: 
Thank you a lot for the implementation of my suggestions. I see all of the points sufficiently 
addressed. Last minor changes: I would recommend to transfer figure R13 to the supplementary 
and reference to it in the manuscript to have the thickness measurements in. Further, I would like 
to motivate minor changes of the manuscript that clarify the terms “control” and “intermediate 
phase” as the authors have correctly explained in the answers. It can be as short as “In the control 
group (blade-coated devices without 2OH additive) ...” “In Intermediate Films (after antisolvent 
activation, before annealing) ...” Alternatively, they could include these references in materials 
and methods as Control group A,B,C or Intermediate Films and reference them properly.  
Reply: We are sincerely grateful for the reviewer constructive feedback and the final minor 
suggestions. We have added the relevant figure to the supplementary information and refined the 
definitions and discussions in the revised manuscript as follows: 
The Original sentence “Quantitative LC–MS analysis reveals that blade-coated films retain 
substantially higher NMP concentrations than their spin-coated counterparts processed from the 
identical precursor (Supplementary Fig. 2a-b)” has been revised as “This quantification relies on a 
linear calibration establishing the correlation between integrated signal intensity and NMP 
concentration (Supplementary Fig. 2a). Cross-sectional SEM imaging of the annealed perovskite 
films reveals thicknesses of ~592 nm and ~703 nm for spin-coating and blade-coating films, 
respectively (Supplementary Fig. 2b). Utilization of a standardized extraction protocol ensures 
that these thickness discrepancies do not bias the LC-MS solvent residue analysis (see the LC-MS 
in Methods and Supplementary Fig. 2c).”

Experimental Groups Definition: In this study, the intermediate phase films/ wet films are defined 
as the films immediately after antisolvent/gas -quenching activation but prior to the thermal 
annealing. The Control group refers to perovskite devices/films fabricated via the blade -coating 
process without any additives.
