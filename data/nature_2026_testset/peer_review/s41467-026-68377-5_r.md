Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript entitled “Versican expression from lung fibroblasts is an autologous fibrotic brake”, Kanellopoulou et al.
propose that versican cell-autonomously suppresses fibroblast activation in pulmonary fibrosis, thereby working as a brake
for excessive fibrosis. The authors showed that the lack of one allele of Vcan exacerbated pulmonary fibrosis. The authors
suggest that the Vcan decrease resulted in a podosome increase and cell-autonomously enhanced profibrotic phenotype of
fibroblasts. Although the data for exacerbated fibrosis in Vcan +/- mice are convincingly shown with multiple methods,
including histology, protein measurement, and 3D-imaging, the weakness of this manuscript lies in the molecular
mechanism of how Vcan deficiency activates fibroblasts. The authors proposed that Vcan deficiency promoted podosome
formation. However, the data on podosomes are images of small random dots on fibroblasts that were passaged 3 times on
plastic dishes, which are known to dramatically change their phenotype. This podosome hypothesis should be further
supported by in vivo data and an intervention that blocks podosome formation. Similarly, the proposed mechanism through
Tnc and Tlr4 should be demonstrated by in vivo experiments to establish the role of Vcan in pulmonary fibrosis. 
Specific comments 
1. If the authors used publicly available datasets, the original studies describing the datasets should be cited. 
2. It is hard to tell where fibroblast foci are in Fig. 1e; therefore, it is difficult to tell if Vcan expression is enriched in fibrotic
areas. 
3. As the scRNA-seq data show, Vcan is abundantly expressed in the control lungs too. However, if the proposed
mechanism of autonomous fibroblast activation is true, shouldn’t we see increased collagen expression in the normal lung
too? Why does Vcan deficiency only affect fibrotic lungs? 
4. The accumulation of collagen usually lasts until around day 28 after bleomycin treatment (PMID: 23520313). It is
surprising that the authors observed the rapid reduction of fibrotic genes in fibroblasts isolated on day 21 in Fig. 2a-c. 
5. Images of Fig. 2d include only a few cells. F-actin staining does not look different between the saline and bleomycin
groups. Images should be quantified to support the authors’ claims. Culturing fibroblasts on a stiff surface drastically
changes the fibroblast phenotype, making them all look like activated myofibroblasts. The authors cultured fibroblasts for 3
passages. Fibroblasts should be analyzed freshly or with fewer passages to avoid the effects of culturing. 
6. Fig. 3b, c. The legend says one-way ANOVA, but it is unclear which test was used to calculate the p-values. Other figures
also lack this information. 
7. What does it mean by “wt cells” in this sentence? 
“High TNC expression was noted in IPF fibroblasts, almost missing from the corresponding wt cells (Fig. S8c),” 
8. Podosome numbers should be quantified by staining for more specific markers in Fig. 4m, n. In addition, it is unclear what
n means here. Is it cells or mice? 
9. The authors showed that Vcan deficiency cell-autonomously activates fibroblasts and enhances collagen expression.
Therefore, aECM prepared by Vcan +/- cells is expected to have more collagens. How did the authors normalize these
fibrotic proteins and specifically investigate the function of Vcan protein? 
10. The method description is inadequate. For example, how did the authors prepare aECM? How were the fibroblasts
isolated? What are the culture conditions? How did the authors perform the proteomics analysis? The image quantification
method should include the information about how many regions/cells per sample were used, what the replicates (biological
or technical) are, and how the author avoided bias in selecting the region of interest. 
Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
IPF is characterised by the accumulation and activation of lung fibroblasts and the resulting excessive deposition of
extracellular matrix (ECM) components in the interstitium, the promotion of podosome formation and ECM invasion.
Proteoglycans (PGs) are a significant component of the interstitial ECM, and regulate several ECM-controlled signalling
pathways. 
In this study, authors explored a possible role for Versican (VCAN), a multifunctional PG, in pulmonary fibrosis and IPF
pathogenesis. This paper presents an excellent research study that fully demonstrates a systematic and comprehensive
research paradigm for investigating the biological functions related to WCAN. This includes in-depth retrieval, analysis, and
mining of relevant public transcriptomic and proteomic databases, as well as the establishment of animal models and the
application of mass spectrometry-based proteomic techniques, among other approaches. The key findings obtained in this
study include: VCAN is highly expressed in the IPF lung, and predominantly expressed from macrophages and fibroblasts in
the IPF lung, and show its biomarker characteristics. Vcan is highly expressed in BLM-induced pulmonary fibrosis. Fibrosis
stimulates Vcan expression in lung fibroblasts, and TGF stimulates VCAN expression in primary lung fibroblasts.The
decreased Vcan expression in Vcan+/- mice upregulates Col1a1, HA and Tnc expression and Tnc post-BLM, and promotes
podosome formation and enhances ECM invasion. Vcan+/- haploinsufficiency exacerbates BLM-induced pulmonary
fibrosis. Vcan expression from fibroblasts serves as an autologous fibrotic brake, controlling the underlying ECM

composition, structure and mechanotransduction, and suppressing fibroblast invasion and pulmonary fibrosis. This study
reveals that Vcan is a double-edged sword.

Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
I am supportive of this manuscript but still struggle with the authors interpretation of versican as a fibrotic brake. I don’t think
this interpretation is correct and think the title and some parts of the conclusions in the text need to be revised. This does not
undermine the quality or importance of the work presented, but would improve the clarity and presentation of the work to
readers. 
For example, the data can be fairly interpreted that altering versican expression alters a part of the fibrotic cascade and that
results in a change in fibrosis. This would be true for other ECM molecules and not something special for versican. For
example, if another fibrosis component such as fibronectin was reduced it is likely to also change the fibrotic cascade that
could be interpreted as a brake. In other words, seeing more fibrosis in the presence of reduced versican does not
necessarily mean its function is a brake, rather it would be fairer to say that the fibrotic reaction has been altered. 
To test versicans ability to act as a fibrotic brake would require a different set of experiments: 
expanding the work to other
fibrotic components, overexpression or addition of a recombinant versican within the model to test whether this limited
fibrosis, and then further mechanistic studies to unpick the signaling mechanism. I suggest a text revision to title and text
where versican is described as a brake. The brake hypothesis could still be discussed within the discussion as a possible
mechanism to be explored in the future. 
Minor comment: Supplemental Figure 6b. the data shows versican reduction associates with an increase in T-cell infiltration
which is not highlighted in the main text, but is something that has been reported in cancer immunity and therefore it would
strengthen the manuscript to mention this link (versican and T-cell infiltration in cancer PMIDs: 38517140, 40361362).

Reviewer #1: 
 
I am supportive of this manuscript but still struggle with the authors interpretation of versican as a 
fibrotic brake. I don’t think this interpretation is correct and think the title and some parts of the 
conclusions in the text need to be revised. This does not undermine the quality or importance of the 
work presented, but would improve the clarity and presentation of the work to readers. 
 
For example, the data can be fairly interpreted that altering versican expression alters a part of the 
fibrotic cascade and that results in a change in fibrosis. This would be true for other ECM molecules 
and not something special for versican. For example, if another fibrosis component such as fibronectin 
was reduced it is likely to also change the fibrotic cascade that could be interpreted as a brake. In 
other words, seeing more fibrosis in the presence of reduced versican does not necessarily mean its 
function is a brake, rather it would be fairer to say that the fibrotic reaction has been altered. 
 
To test versicans ability to act as a fibrotic brake would require a different set of experiments: 
expanding the work to other fibrotic components, overexpression or addition of a recombinant versican 
within the model to test whether this limited fibrosis, and then further mechanistic studies to unpick the 
signaling mechanism. I suggest a text revision to title and text where versican is described as a brake. 
The brake hypothesis could still be discussed within the discussion as a possible mechanism to be 
explored in the future.
