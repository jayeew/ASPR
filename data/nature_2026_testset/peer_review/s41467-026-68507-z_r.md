Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript integrates multi-sulfur isotopes, palladium, and trace elements from deposit-related sulfides and host rocks
to propose mantle processes controlling PGE-Ni-Cu and Au mineralization. However, several major concerns regarding the
interpretation need to be addressed before publication. These include verifying the aspects used to describe a deposit,
clarifying whether AI-assisted translation was employed for language issues, and establishing broader relevance to Archean
craton geology and ore exploration. I hope these suggestions help improve their work, and I look forward to the revised
manuscript. 
1. The authors simultaneously study Au and PGE-Ni-Cu without clarifying similarities or differences in their tectonic settings
and mechanism. Although identical mineralization timing and sources are claimed, controlling factors causing differences
between Au and PGE mineralization remain unexplained. If both are considered mantle-derived source, is this mantle
consensus or controversial? The conclusions are overly generalized and should be specified, for instance: what constitutes
the unified mineralization model? How do mantle processes control metal enrichment and release? In addition, while
claiming "ore enrichment," only total resource are provided without ore grades, failing to demonstrate enrichment. 
2. The introduction states that metals derive from the mantle but fails to specify which metals or the geodynamic conditions
controlling enrichment (e.g., pressure, temperature, and volatile variations). Previous studies on PGE and Au using different
methods (e.g., petrological/geochemical research cited by authors) lack synthesized findings; structural/metamorphic studies
suggest mineralization differences far exceed commonalities. How these differences and commonalities manifest requires
concrete explanations. 
3. Sections describing the Gonneville intrusion and related deposits belong in Geological Setting or Deposit Geology not the
introduction. The introduction should focus on unresolved problems and research progress regarding Gonneville PGE and
Kalgoorlie/Kurnalpi Terranes Au deposits. 
4. Although S-isotope results rule out sediments as sources for Au and S, the local sedimentary δ³⁴S ranges are unreported.
While endorsing Caruso et al.’s model of lower-crust/upper-mantle Au and S sources, the authors fail to clarify limitations of
prior studies or their own innovations. 
5. Gonneville-Julimar’s geological context lacks clarity on lithology, geochronology, and the intrusion’s tectonic setting.
Geological background and deposit geology are poorly reported, with missing deposit descriptions (e.g., ore distribution,
PGE occurrence). Comparisons with orogenic Au deposits lack background on Au deposit geology and tectonics. Post-
emplacement metamorphism lacks temporal constraints and their linkage to magmatism and mineralization. What is the
metamorphic grade? 
6. The Results section for S-isotope, PGE geochemical, and trace element data includes comprehensive methodological
and sample details. To enhance readability and align with standard practice, these technical descriptions could be relocated
to the Methods section. Claims that Gonneville intrusion’s trace elements resemble global boninite signatures and
orthomagmatic PGE deposits lack specific evidence (which elements or ratios?). Clarify whether trace element data
represent whole-rock or mineral analyses. 
7. The text mentions melts from the upper mantle without specifying whether these are asthenospheric or lithospheric in
origin, nor providing evidence for this distinction. In addition, mineralization fluids attributed to crustal devolatilization lack
clarification on whether the crust is continental or oceanic. Why not sediments? The terminology describing metal-
transporting media varies between 'fluids' and 'fluids and melts,' creating ambiguity regarding the metasomatism. 
8. The discussion proposes crustal recycling process metasomatizing lithospheric mantle. Does crustal recycling operate at
crustal scales or involve crust entering the mantle? By what mechanism? The claim that recycled crust comprises plume-
derived mafics and sediments lacks evidence. If sediments are included, the manuscript should demonstrate their
dehydration or metasomatism effects elsewhere. How are contributions from continental or oceanic crust versus sediments
distinguished? Provide further evidence.

9. Au and PGE deposits occur in different terranes but share mantle-derived metals; the nature of mantle property differences
underlying this distribution requires clarification. Do isotopic signatures differ? Figure 3 shows small δ³⁴S/Δ³³S variations in
PGE-related sulfides/host rocks, precluding crustal contamination, whereas orogenic Au’s larger variations permit crustal
sources or mantle-crust assimilation. Differentiate sulfide types in Figure 3: ore-related vs. intrusive vs. granitoid. 
10. The authors propose that enriched mantle partial melting generates metal-rich melt-fluids mineralizing at different crustal
depths. At what depths do PGE versus Au mineralize? Why do they separate into distinct terranes? How do shared metal
sources guide exploration? Specify concrete indicators. 
11. Sections suggesting then dismissing crustal contamination for Bi-Te enrichment should be merged. S-isotopes only
preclude crustal assimilation for PGE deposits, not for Au deposits. Spatiotemporal links between Au deposits and
sanukitoids/lamprophyres lack chronological, isotopic, or spatial evidence in the current figures and maps; these
relationships should be substantiated with additional data. 
12. Crustal sulfur mixing modifies the mantle sulfur reservoir is understandable, but how does it homogenize sedimentary
Δ³³S? At what depth? Through what process? Does sedimentary S enter first, followed by crust? 
13. Plume-induced drip tectonics or subduction environments driving crustal recycling lack supporting evidence. How are
these environments proven? Do they coexist? Do they yield identical mineralization? How do metasomatism timing/location
guide exploration? How does temporal data assist prospecting? 
14. Figures 1–2 lack geographic coordinates and clear definitions of terrane boundaries/ages. Sanukitoids are described as
mantle-derived but mapped as granitoids; however, not all granitoids are mantle-sourced, as some may derive from mafic
crust (Hartnady et al., 2022, EPSL) or tholeiitic sources (Nagel et al., 2012, Geology). I recommend mapping sanukitoid
distributions to clarify their spatial relationships. Mafic intrusions linked to PGE lack spatiotemporal characteristics on maps.
Figure 3 has inconsistent legend/plot symbols. Figure 4’s PGM types by area is undefined. What deposit types are shown?
Why do they mineralize at different depths? Controlling factors? Mineralization depths? Figure 4 misspells granatoids and
omits Bi-Te enrichment/crustal recycling elements. 
15. The manuscript repeatedly emphasizes that metal sources, timing, and spatial distribution serve as key proxies for
PGE/Au exploration targeting, yet fails to specify concrete exploration criteria. 
16. Significant AI-editing artifacts exist: Au research progress is listed bullet-style (Lines 59–82); summaries are redundant;
regional geology dominates the introduction while scientific questions are underdeveloped; discussion shows AI-style
phrasing (Lines 198–206, 208–211); and sequencing is incoherent ("first" without "second" in Lines 232/237).

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Review of ‘Unveiling the Metallogenic Continuum of an Archean Craton’ 
Authors: Demmer et al. 
The study by Demmer and colleagues sets out to link magmatic and orogenic ore forming processes in an Archean craton
setting (Yilgarn, Western Australia). The argument is made that PGE and Au mineralisation, traditionally associated with
mafic magmatism and relatively late-stage regional-scale hydrothermal circulation, respectively, have three important
features in common. These features are (a) the timing of formation, within a ~20 Ma period, (b) the association of both types
of mineralisation with Bi-Te enrichment, and (c) the similar sulfur isotope characteristics (particularly Δ33S) of both ore types.
The authors argue that PGE and Au mineralisation share an enriched or metasomatized mantle source, which according to
them is the main ingredient for the ore deposits and which, if correct, challenges the traditional approach of studying these
types of ore deposit separately, with potential implications for exploration targeting. 
There are aspects of this study that I liked. One is the effort to bridge the divide between mineralising systems that are
normally studied by very different types of geologist (e.g., igneous petrologists/geochemists versus structural
geologists/metamorphic petrologists), which I think is novel. Another is the big picture thinking, with respect to ore deposit
formation and regional scale tectonomagmatic processes. Another again is the integration of multiple geochemical tools,
including sulfur isotopes, lithophile and chalcophile trace elements, and electron microscopy. However, I did also feel like
there were gaps or jumps in logic throughout the manuscript. There are also several places where I am not sure the authors
are representing their data as accurately as I would hope. I have several more substantial comments, detailed below, and
then minor issues tied to line number that follow. My overall impression is that the manuscript needs a fair bit of work/revision
to make it tighter and more self-contained, as well as to bring it to a publishable standard. I hope these comments help with
that effort. 
1. One of my main impressions after reading this article is that the new data collected is all from the mafic-ultramafic
intrusions + PGE mineralisation. The links to the gold mineralisation are being made mainly through what is known from the
literature, and I think that this part of the story needs to be stronger. The period of gold mineralisation is given as ~20 Ma and
the age of the G-J PGE deposit is constrained to within 8 Ma within that period. But 20 Ma is quite a long time period for arc

style orogenesis, and the layered intrusion is small enough that it was likely emplaced and crystallised in <1 Ma, can this
temporal link be made any more robust? Perhaps with more discussion of the timing of the various lamprophyre and
granitoid intrusions? My feeling otherwise is that well of course one might expect both orogenic gold mineralisation and
earlier mafic (mid-?) crustal magmatism to form PGE deposits in an Archean craton/greenstone belt setting like this. 
2. I am not convinced by the Bi-Te argument, especially for the PGE mineralisation. I don’t know as much about Au
mineralisation. Certainly for the PGE, I don’t think that a mineralogy dominated by Bi-Te necessarily or automatically tells
you much about crustal contamination or mantle source composition. As pointed out by the authors, sulfide melt will naturally
evolve past iss to the point that these ligands become important players and it is very difficult to reconstruct the mass
balance accurately, to the point that we know what was part of the original magmatic budget and what was sourced
externally. It is interesting that the PGE mineralogy seems to be dominated by Bi-Te, but if they really only examined PGM
>2 µm in size, then my opinion is that they have only scratched the surface of the PGE budget and the vast majority of the
PGM in the rock remain unidentified. The question then becomes whether the several biggest PGM better reflect the
chalcophile element budget of the rock better than the bulk of those which are too small to be seen. Some recognition of this
issue in the manuscript would be helpful. Can the whole rock PGE data be used with the mineralogical observations to
construct some kind of mass balance, where it can be said that the visible PGM can or cannot account for the whole rock
abundances of the elements (at least Pt and Pd)? Going back to the links to gold, could some sort of survey of published
mineralogical information be carried out to make this part more robust? I am thinking of histograms to illustrate the claim that
the gold mineralogy is dominated(?) by tellurides. Incidentally, that observation made me wonder why there was a deficiency
in Bi in the gold deposits….? 
3. Linking to the point above, I was not fully convinced that the sulfide mineralogy/microstructure of the G-J intrusion is
magmatic. If sulfide was remobilised at a late stage during orogenesis, then it might have been affected by the hydrothermal
systems responsible for gold mineralisation. How certain are the authors that there has not been local remobilisation of the
magmatic sulfide, either as melt or in fluid (ie dissolution and reprecipitation)? This could also remobilise TABS elements.
[eg see Andrew Tomkins papers, such as 2007 in Mineralium Deposita]. The lithological distributions shown in Fig 2c could
be used to argue against significant remobilisation, perhaps, since they occur along lithological boundaries and have
reasonably consistent strike. However, I felt some petrographic images showing magmatic sulfide textures would have been
really helpful, and any other lines of evidence the authors can think of to make their case here. For the former, I am thinking
of the typical semi-massive ore textures where sulfide is interstitial to cumulate frameworks composed of silicates like olivine
or orthopyroxene. Other evidence might be to use the sorts of approaches by the S-J Barnes lab (Mansur et al. 2021,
Mineralium Deposita) to discriminate between magmatic and hydrothermal sulfide compositions. In any case, if the rocks
have been to amphibolite grade metamorphism, then the authors need to justify why they are sure the sulfides retain
magmatic characteristics. Sulfide ores are well known to be highly mobile in these sorts of settings (ie greenstone belts), at
metamorphic grades even lower than this (greenschist). 
4. I found the references to ‘homogenisation’ of the mantle to be a bit vague, when it came to discussing the sulfur isotopes
(e.g., Lines 77-78; Line 178), and was not sure what the importance of this was to the overall model proposed. What exactly
is this process? Why does the mantle need to be homogenised with respect to sulfur isotopes if melts that are being
extracted from it will presumably do just this?

(Remarks to the Author) 
This is my second reading of this manuscript. The revised version is much improved. I appreciated the competent and
comprehensive responses to my comments on the first version, as well as the responses to the other reviewers. The detailed
geological context and sample information provided in the supplement have made the manuscript much stronger
scientifically, and I think the revisions to the text have made it a better read now too. I have a couple more fairly minor
comments for the authors to consider as follows: 
1. I appreciate the detailed work documenting the PGM with grainsize variations and cutoffs, now provided in the
supplement in an effort to be as rigorous as possible. The figures are helpful in this regard too. I maintain that 2 microns is
still on the large end of the size spectrum for PGM populations in all ore deposits in my experience and I would like to see a
caveat statement in the supplement somewhere along the lines that PGM <2 microns could exist and have not been
documented. I think the statement that '....the ≥ 2 µm2 is representative of the full range of PGM-types present across the
intrusions...' should be toned down a little. It might also help to be more clear throughout this section of the supplement that
the focus seems to be on Pd-PGMs, and not other flavours of PGM. Can it be clarified whether Supp Figure 14 includes Pt
PGM as well as Pd PGM? Are there others? If so, is that appropriate? Another quibble here is that Pt PGM are referred to in
the main manuscript text (PGM host 98% of Pt), but the focus seems to be on Pd PGM in the supplement (like in Supp Figure
16). Can a distinction be made to show how the different PGM contribute to the overall budget more clearly. Overall, I
recognise the authors have done a good job with this issue – I just think they need to provide a little more detail in some
places and be a little more circumspect in others, and I don’t think this will detract from the impact of the work in general. 
2. Throughout the supplement, it is repeatedly stated that sulfides and metamorphic silicates are in equilibrium with each
other. Photographs are shown, but it is not clear exactly what microstructural evidence for textural equilibrium the authors are
invoking from the photos. Is it grain boundary relationships, dihedral angles etc? Are there references that can be cited? This
is an important interpretation to substantiate because it underpins some of the much bigger conclusions of the study.

 You are correct: this manuscript is the first release of academic quality data for
