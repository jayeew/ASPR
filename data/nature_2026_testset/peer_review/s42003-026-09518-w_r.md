Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript investigates the role of MAT2A in osteosarcoma (OS) progression, focusing on its influence on aerobic
glycolysis through transcriptional activation of PARN via serum response factor (SRF). The study demonstrates that MAT2A
is upregulated in OS tissues and cells, and that its overexpression enhances proliferation, migration, and tumorigenic
potential by promoting glycolysis. Mechanistic assays reveal that MAT2A facilitates SRF binding to the PARN promoter,
which in turn regulates the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway. These findings are supported by a combination of bioinformatic
analyses, in vitro functional assays, and in vivo xenograft models. 
The overall impression is that the study is well-structured and methodologically sound, offering valuable insight into the
metabolic regulation of OS. The authors convincingly demonstrate the oncogenic role of MAT2A in OS and its regulatory
network involving SRF and PARN. The data are comprehensive and well-integrated across molecular, cellular, and animal
model systems. Importantly, the identification of MAT2A as a potential therapeutic target is timely and relevant, considering
the limited treatment options for advanced OS. 
However, while the mechanistic framework is compelling, there are areas where the manuscript could be strengthened. 
First, although the study establishes a link between MAT2A and PARN via SRF, the molecular details of how MAT2A
influences SRF function remain vague. It is not clear whether MAT2A affects SRF expression, nuclear localization, or its
post-translational modification. The authors show that MAT2A knockdown reduces SRF levels in the nucleus, but the
mechanism behind this should be further explored or at least discussed in more detail. 
Second, the role of the PI3K/AKT pathway is somewhat underdeveloped. While the data suggest that this pathway mediates
the glycolytic and proliferative effects of MAT2A/PARN, direct evidence, such as rescue experiments with constitutively
active AKT, would provide stronger mechanistic support. Additionally, the authors could clarify whether the observed
metabolic shifts are indeed a result of transcriptional regulation alone or involve post-transcriptional mechanisms as well. 
Third, while the in vivo data are persuasive, the study would benefit from an expanded analysis of clinical correlations. The
sample size of patient tissues, while acceptable, could be more thoroughly discussed in terms of heterogeneity, treatment
background, and survival data to contextualize the findings within a clinical framework. 
Finally, there are occasional ambiguities in language and some grammatical inconsistencies that could be improved for
clarity and readability. A careful proofreading or editing by a native English speaker would enhance the manuscript’s
presentation. 
In summary, this is a significant contribution to the field of osteosarcoma research with a solid foundation of experimental
evidence. Addressing the mechanistic gaps regarding SRF regulation, strengthening the PI3K/AKT pathway connection,
and expanding clinical relevance would further increase the impact and translational potential of the work. 
Given the scientific quality of the study, its comprehensive experimental design, and the novelty of the MAT2A–SRF–PARN
axis in osteosarcoma, the manuscript is certainly within the scope of a this journal. However, while the core findings are
compelling and well-supported, there are some mechanistic and clarity issues that warrant additional attention before

- The mechanism by which MAT2A modulates SRF activity (expression, localization, or modification) remains

(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors have provided a thorough and convincing response to the concerns raised in the original review. After
examining both the rebuttal and the revised manuscript, it is clear that the revisions substantively strengthen the work and
directly address the issues previously noted. 
Regarding the mechanism through which MAT2A influences SRF, the authors have now presented detailed experimental
evidence demonstrating that MAT2A enhances SRF SUMOylation, thereby stabilizing the protein and promoting its nuclear
accumulation. This clarification fills an important mechanistic gap in the prior version. The inclusion of data showing that a
catalytically inactive MAT2A mutant retains this regulatory capability further reinforces the conclusion that MAT2A engages
in noncanonical, SAM-independent interactions with SRF. This addition significantly improves the mechanistic
completeness of the model. 
The authors have also substantially strengthened the evidence linking MAT2A/PARN activity to the PI3K/AKT pathway. By
incorporating both PARN rescue experiments and restoration of glycolytic phenotypes using constitutively active AKT, the
revised manuscript now convincingly establishes the downstream signaling cascade. These experiments, together with
complementary metabolic assays, clearly demonstrate that AKT activation is functionally required for the glycolytic and
proliferative effects driven by the MAT2A–SRF–PARN axis. 
With respect to clinical correlations, the authors now provide a clearer description of the cohort used for their tissue
microarray analysis, including information on sample heterogeneity and treatment background. Their acknowledgment of the
absence of survival data, as well as a clearer discussion of the clinical limitations of the current dataset, gives appropriate
transparency. The expanded statistical correlation analyses enhance the manuscript’s clinical relevance without overstating
the findings. 
Language and clarity have also improved; the manuscript reads more smoothly and appears to have undergone appropriate

The article illustrateda new mechanism of the MAT2A -SRF-PARN axis in 
osteosarcoma. MAT2A promotes the transcription of PARN in osteosarcoma by 
facilitating the direct binding of SRF to the PARN promoter, thereby promoting the 
process of aerobic glycolysis and driving the progression of osteosarcoma.It provides a

Comments: 
1、Does MAT2A interact directly with PARN? 
Response: Thank you for this important question. In the revised manuscript, we 
provide additional experimental evidence addressing this point. Using co-
immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) assays in both MNNG/HOS and U -2OS cells, we found 
that PARN does not directly interact with MAT2A, nor does it interact with SRF. These 
results indicate that MAT2A does not regulate PARN through physical association but 
rather through SRF -mediated transcriptional activation. The corresponding data have 
been added to the revised manuscript (Figure 5A). 
2、It is suggested that the key differentially expressed genes be marked in the volcano 
map of Figure 1F. 
Response: We appreciate the reviewer ’s suggestion. In the revised manuscript, we 
have redrawn the volcano plot and explicitly labeled the key differentially expressed 
genes (DEGs) to improve clarity and interpretability. The updated figure has been 
incorporated into the revised version. 
3、Please explain exactly at which site the mutated PARN (PARN-MUT) occurs? 
Response: Thank you for this important question. The PARN-MUT construct used in 
our luciferase assays contains a targeted mutation within the SRF-binding motif of the 
PARN promoter. Based on bioinformatic prediction and preliminary experimental 
validation, the native SRF -binding sequence in the PARN promoter is:  5'-
GCCCCGCCCCCGCCGCCGCGCA-3'; To disrupt SRF recognition, this motif was 
replaced with the following mutated sequence: 5'-TAAGATGGAAGTAGTGATATAC-
3'. This substitution abolishes the SRF consensus binding site, resulting in a promoter 
that no longer responds to SRF or MAT2A. We have added a clarification of the exact 
mutation site in the revised manuscript.

4、All the pictures in the article lack rulers. 
Response: Thank you for pointing this out. We have now added scale bars to all 
microscopic images throughout the manuscript to improve clarity and ensure 
consistency across the figures. 
5、In the xenograft tumor model, are there any differences in body weight among mice? 
Response: We appreciate the reviewer’s comment. In the revised manuscript, we have 
included body weight measurements for all in vivo experiments, including those shown 
in Figures 2E, 8C, and 9L. No signi ficant differences in body weight were observed 
among the groups, indicating that the treatments did not cause overt systemic toxicity. 
6、The migration of cells is limited to the cellular level and is not demonstrated in the 
animal model. 
Response: We thank the reviewer for this insightful comment. In the present study, our 
primary focus was to elucidate the MAT2A–SRF–PARN axis and its role in driving 
osteosarcoma progression, with cell migration assessed only as one representative 
malignant phenotype in vitro. We did not extend our investigation to in vivo metastasis 
models in this work. We acknowledge that determining whether MAT2A contributes to 
osteosarcoma metastasis is an important question, and we plan to address this in future 
studies using appropriate metastatic models. 
7、It is recommended to conduct relevant experiments in combination with MAT2A 
inhibitors, such as FIDAS-5. 
Response: Thank you for this valuable suggestion. Following the reviewer ’ s 
recommendation, we have incorporated experiments using the MAT2A inhibitor 
FIDAS-5 in both in vitro and in vivo settings (now presented in Figure 9). FIDAS -5 
treatment significantly reduced glycolytic activity, suppressed OS cell proliferation and 
migration, and decreased AKT pathway activation, closely phenocopying the effects 
observed with MAT2A knockdown. In the in vivo xenograft model, FIDAS-5 markedly 
inhibited tumor growth without causing significant body weight loss, indicating good 
tolerability. These results further support MAT2A as a promising t herapeutic target in 
osteosarcoma. 
8、The specific mechanism of action between MAT2A and SRF remains unclear. Is the 
interaction between the two related to the enzyme activity of MAT2A?

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 11 -->

Response: We appreciate the reviewer’s insightful question regarding the mechanism 
underlying the interaction between MAT2A and SRF. In the revised manuscript, we 
provide additional evidence to clarify this regulatory relationship. 
1. We further investigated whether MAT2A affects SRF through post -translational 
modification. Our new data demonstrate that MAT2A enhances SRF SUMOylation, 
which increases SRF stability and promotes its nuclear accumulation (Figure 5). These 
findings provide mechanistic support for how MAT2A strengthens SRF -mediated 
transcription of PARN. 
2. To evaluate whether the enzymatic activity of MAT2A is required for SRF regulation, 
we constructed a catalytic -inactive MAT2A mutant (MAT2A -D180A). As shown in 
Figure S2, MAT2A-D180A displayed comparable effects to the wild -type protein on 
SRF nuclear localization and PARN expression, indicating that MAT2A modulates SRF 
in an enzyme-activity-independent manner. 
Together, these results further refine the mechanistic understanding of the MAT2A –
SRF–PARN axis. 
9、The title of Table 2 does not require punctuation marks. 
Response: Thank you for pointing this out. We have revised the title of Table 2 by 
removing unnecessary punctuation marks to ensure consistency with the journal ’s 
formatting requirements.
