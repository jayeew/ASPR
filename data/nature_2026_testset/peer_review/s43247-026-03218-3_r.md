REVIEWER COMMENTS: 
Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
This manuscript presents a systematic and forward-looking planning and design proposal centered on the concept of a
"floating city." It covers a wide range of dimensions including urban structure, energy systems, citizen welfare, and
environmental sustainability. The overall structure is clear, the illustrations are informative, and the manuscript demonstrates
a commendable balance between innovation and practicality. However, to further enhance the academic rigor and impact of
the paper, the following aspects are recommended for improvement: 
（
1
）
The manuscript currently presents a broad set of goals, encompassing nearly all dimensions of the floating city
concept. This breadth, while comprehensive, may dilute the clarity of the core research focus. It is recommended that the
authors clearly articulate the main innovation or central research question in the Introduction, to help readers identify the
primary contribution of the study. 
（
2
）
While the manuscript cites a substantial number of references, these are primarily focused on engineering and energy-
related aspects. Citations from urban studies, sociology, climate policy, and other interdisciplinary domains are relatively
lacking. Incorporating such literature would strengthen the manuscript’s relevance and depth from a broader academic
perspective. 
（
3
）
The current description of methods focuses heavily on parameter calculations, but lacks an integrated overview of the
research design and logical framework. It is suggested that the Methods section include a research framework diagram or
workflow chart, outlining the process from conceptual development to design elaboration and quantitative evaluation. In
addition, the authors should specify which indicators were used to assess sustainability and feasibility, and how these were
categorized and applied. 
（
4
）
Although the appendix provides a rich set of diagrams and visual materials, the main text lacks sufficient integration
and explanation of these figures. It is recommended that the Results section include more direct references to the
supplementary illustrations, with interpretations of key design elements such as green space layout and the multi-layered
mobility system. 
（
5
）
The manuscript emphasizes lifestyle and social welfare but pays limited attention to socioeconomic factors such as
construction and operational costs, policy mechanisms, and governance models. It is suggested that the authors provide a
preliminary cost estimate or discussion on both initial construction and long-term maintenance. Additionally, brief
discussions on policy acceptance, legal frameworks, and community engagement would be beneficial. Consideration of
existing social modeling tools or real-world case studies could also provide valuable support. 
（
6
）
The manuscript introduces three conceptual pillars—resilience, sustainability, and urban welfare—and links them
respectively to water, nature, and man. However, the rationale behind these associations remains unclear. The authors are
encouraged to elaborate on how these three dimensions relate to one another, and why each is specifically associated with

its corresponding element. 
Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author): 
The topic is interesting but the proposed model (at least the way it is presented) lacks details and there is also a lack of
reliability and validity assessments. 
- The introduction should better frame this research within the broader context of Utopian urban planning models and
concepts. You should explain that there have been utopian ideas proposed (and most of them archived) in the past two
centuries. By careful engagement with the relevant literature you should explain why another utopian idea is needed and
how likely is this one going to be different and go beyond just being utopian. 
- The Introduction should also provide more tangible numbers. For instance, what share of coastal areas are going to be
affected by climate change impacts , etc. To what percentage will this idea be applicable? Etc. 
- The Introduction should end with clear research questions 
- To examine resilience, welfare, and sustainability, it is essential to provide different scenarios and more tangible and easy
to communicate models. For instance, when discussing resilience, it is essential to clarify resilience to what? Resilience
under what specific scenario? Resilience of what specific aspect of the city? What specific resilience characteristics? Etc. A
lot is written on urban resilience and its assessment in the literature. Better engagement with such literature would be helpful
in developing a more comprehensive model.

REVIEWERS' COMMENTS: 
Reviewer #3 (Remarks to the Author): 
I would like to commend the authors for their resilience in responding with grace to some of the requests made by the
reviewers. Before I begin my substantive review, I'd like to point out that because there is not a lot of research in this area,
there is often a desire for any new research in this area to cover the (limited) field and do quite a bit of heavy-lifting. I don't
often think it is necessary - and if I had been one of the two initial reviewers, I would've suggested that this be published with
minor revisions. For example, some of the detail on sea level rise and its impact may not have needed to be quite so
granular - but I think in rising to the challenge of the reviewers, the authors have produced a piece of work that (I hope) will
be referenced repeatedly and over the years by both academics and the industry. To that end, I would like to congratulate
the authors for pulling together a wonderful piece of work. 
In response to the section that contextualises floating city proposals as part of a rich history of work in the field, may I point
the authors to the new edited collection 'Large Floating Solutions' by Springer, particularly chapters 1, 15 (Designing for
Buoyant Urbanism), 17 (Urban Form Design Guidelines for Floating Cities), 19 (Why Floating Cities Can Help Combat
Climate Change). This might help with terminology - particularly in separating out 'satellite floating cities' (often in
international waters) and floating cities that are extensions of the (often coastal) urban environment. 
I didn't understand the phrase: 'we have identified resilience, urban welfare, and sustainability as the main directories of the
floating city urban planning' - that objectives paragraph may need a bit more work as it is currently a bit confusing. This
paragraph would be improved if the authors repeated and enlarged the portion of their abstract where they state that this
paper 'proposes an innovative floating city vision based on....' a novel approach where.... [insert elaboration here]. This is the
missing link that needs to tie the introduction to the next part of the article. I think it's obliquely mentioned in the paragraph
above in 'Furthermore, the new concept proposed represents a step forward...' - but this describes the intended effect rather
that introduces that a concept is being proposed. 
In the second '2 Results' - the authors jump straight into a description of the floating city. Would it not be better to first
describe the ideal location, the site, the water depth? 
I am not an engineer, but I was curious to see that the breakwater proposed had hard 90 degree corners. My understanding
is that this would not be an ideal structural form in the ocean. 
I see that the authors have enlarged the design criterion of 'resilience' to encompass non physical aspects (ie. resilience in
the social sense). I suggest the authors accept that there is a separate concept of social resilience but note that this is
addressed in section 3.2 as this concept of urban welfare does overlap with the concept of social resilience. 
Finally, I think the recommended analysis for future floating city concept investigation needs to take into account research
that has been put forward in this space. May I please direct the authors back to the Springer edited collection 'Large Floating
Solutions' that I've pointed to above.

Reply 7: 
The rationale behind the association between the three conceptual pillars (directories) of the work 
and the three elements of water, man, and nature has been clarified in the revised version of the 
manuscript, according to the reviewer’s suggestion. 
 We associate resilience with water because water represents at the same time the main stressor 
for the floating city and its most important resource, as discussed in section 3.1. According to
