New Comments 
The study is ambitious and the atlas will be valuable, but several data analysis and interpretation issues need to be
addressed. 
Major issues: 
1. The discovery cohort merges your in-house data with five published studies, and validation merges 12 further studies with
207 new cases. Counts are corrected using ComBat seq; microarray platforms are harmonized separately. While PCA plots
and consensus CDFs are shown, ComBat seq on heterogeneous clinical datasets can remove biological signal or imprint
residual study effects. Please add the following: 
(a) Leave one study out subtype reproducibility: train the subtype templates without one study and test on the held-out study
to demonstrate platform 
and center robustness. 
(b) Association of batch with subtype: per cohort, quantify whether subtype calls correlate with study or primary site after
adjustment. 
(c) re run the discovery clustering with an alternative approach such as Harmony or MNN Correct and report subtype
concordance (ARI). 
2. The CDF and delta area curves are shown, and survival separation is used to support k = 4. Please also report: (a) Within
tumor type stability (e.g., clustering only lung derived BrMs) to ensure k is not driven by primary cancer mix.(b) Prediction
strength or PAC metrics for cluster stability. (c) Permutation tests where sample labels are shuffled to verify that observed
silhouette widths exceed null expectations. 
3. The NTP validation is appropriate across platforms, and a pathway-based elastic net classifier achieves 84% accuracy on
a 30% test split. However, random splits can leak center-specific signals into both splits. Please add leave-one-study-out
cross-validation across studies and report per study performance and confusion matrices. Also report calibration and class
balance metrics. 
4. Multivariable Cox models adjust for primary site, age, sex, and cohort. Given strong subtype–primary site associations,
please show within primary survival analyses (at least lung, breast, melanoma) and include treatment covariates where
available. The clinical message should not rest on unadjusted K–M alone. 
5. WGCNA is run on 138 tumor samples that include 20 GBMs. Including GBMs in network construction may induce
modules that capture primary vs metastasis signal rather than BrM heterogeneity, yet module–survival associations are then
evaluated in BrMs. Please rebuild WGCNA on BrM proteomes only and re test module–survival links, or explicitly show that
modules are stable if GBMs are excluded. Also provide standard proteomics QC (peptide/protein IDs, coefficient of variation
across reference standards, missingness per sample) and clarify the impact of DreamAI imputation on network topology. 
6. The higher RNA–protein concordance in CMS4 may be confounded by tumor purity and cell mixing, since CMS1 has the
lowest ABSOLUTE purity. Please re estimate correlation after adjusting for purity (partial Spearman) and, where possible,
using cell type corrected expression (e.g., integrating deconvolution estimates). Report whether the CMS4 > CMS1
difference persists. 
7. ActivePathways is a reasonable choice, but please state the multiple testing procedure and FDR thresholds used for
combined RNA–protein results and provide full pathway tables in Supplementary Data for transparency. 
8. Extended Data Fig. 4b suggests BrMs have the highest TMB among all cancer types compared to TCGA primaries.
Because pipelines differ, cross-project TMB rankings are not robust unless harmonized. Please either re compute TMB
using a uniform pipeline for both datasets or remove the “highest among all cancers” statement. 
9. APOBEC-associated Signature 13 enrichment in CMS4 and smoking Signature 4 are plausible given primary sites, but
signatures are sensitive to exome coverage and cancer type composition. Please show signature exposures within each
major primary and include bootstrap confidence intervals for exposures. Clarify whether the COSMIC v3.2 reference was
used and whether SigProfiler assignment replicates the result. 
10. Associations between 22q11 deletions and higher CTL infiltration are interesting but could be affected by purity
(apparent deletions in low-purity immune-rich tumors). Please (a) Recall or re-score CNAs with purity ploidy aware methods
and confirm the 22q11 finding using purity adjusted copy number.(b) Re-test CNA–immune associations using linear models

that include purity and primary site.(c) For CNV–expression “cis” effects, apply multiple testing correction and report FDRs
rather than p < 0.01 alone. 
11. The text suggests CMS1 resembles earlier metastatic stages while CMS3/4 resemble later adapted states. There is no
longitudinal clonal analysis to support timing. Please soften or remove the temporal inference or add explicit caveats. 
12. For snRNAseq, please provide per-sample QC metrics (UMIs, genes per nucleus, % mitochondrial, doublet rate), donor
effects after integration, and the number of nuclei per subtype. Clarify whether doublets were removed and how ambient
RNA was handled; add a table of marker genes used for each cluster. 
13. The positive EMT–CTL correlation appears specific to BrMs across bulk RNA/protein, DSP, and spatial analyses.
Because EMT scores can capture stromal content, please verify that the association holds within malignant cell
compartments in snRNA seq and that it persists after controlling for fibroblast/endothelial abundance in bulk deconvolution
models. Also quantify the effect size and confidence intervals, not only p-values. 
14. In the Visium analysis, “T neighbored tumor” is defined using a tumor fraction threshold of 0.37 and adjacency to T cell-
enriched spots. Please provide sensitivity analyses for these thresholds and show that key findings (e.g., EMT gene
enrichment in T neighbored regions) are stable. 
15. CMS2 high signature predicts better survival in advanced melanoma and NSCLC ICB cohorts, but those cohorts are not
restricted to patients with BrMs. Please clearly state this limitation and, if possible, analyze ICB treated patients with known
brain metastases or provide a sensitivity analysis showing that results are not driven by extracranial disease. 
16. For targeted metabolomics, OPLS DA and VIP > 1 are reported, but cross-validation metrics (R2Y, Q2), QC sample
performance, and batch drift control are not shown. Please include these, and describe internal standards and normalization.
The paired lung primary vs BrM analysis should report paired statistics, effect sizes, and FDRs. 
17. For OncoTreat/VIPER predictions and PDO validation, the gamitrinib analysis uses OncoTreat with Bonferroni p-adj
thresholds and validates in 23 BrM PDOs, yet only exemplar cases are shown; aggregated performance is needed. Please
provide: (a) The confusion matrix of predicted sensitive vs measured IC50 based sensitive, with AUC, sensitivity, and
specificity. (b) Replicate counts per PDO and inter plate variability.(c) Clarify a methods inconsistency: the PDO drug screen
section mentions palbociclib, while the Results and plots emphasize abemaciclib. Reconcile this discrepancy and present
results for all CDK4/6 inhibitors tested. 
18. The re analysis of the Cosgrove cohort suggests better survival after radiotherapy in CMS1 (p = 0.042), but radiation
modality is unknown and no adjustment for other covariates is possible. Please treat this as hypothesis-generating and
temper wording accordingly. 
Minor issues: 
1. “CMS” is widely used for colorectal cancer subtypes; consider a different acronym to avoid confusion in the literature. 
2. Ensure that every figure uses the same subtype color scheme and the same survival terminology. In Fig. 2 and Extended
Figs, label whether analyses include GBM or BrM only.

(Remarks to the Author) 
This revision addresses the major methodological and interpretive concerns raised in the previous round. The data resource
is valuable, the subtyping framework is now well supported by multiple lines of evidence, and the proteogenomic analyses
are presented with appropriate rigor and caution. Remaining issues are minor and largely editorial. From my perspective, the
manuscript is now suitable for publication in Nature Communications after minor revisions along the lines noted below. 
A few small points you might consider in a final polish: 
1. In the Data availability section, explicitly state that the iProX dataset will be fully public upon publication, as noted above.

legends; I still see occasional CMS labels lingering in some legacy text in the older version, though the latest compiled
version seems mostly consistent.

available for most patients (and thus not included) will help manage expectations.
