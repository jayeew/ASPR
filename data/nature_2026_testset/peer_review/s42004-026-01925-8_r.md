Response:
We sincerely thank the reviewer for their thoughtful summary of our work and for recognising the novel aspects
and interest of the topic.
We clarify that this work focuses on whether crystallisation occurred or not under different conditions, not on
the dynamics including timing of crystallisation onset. The in-situ visualisation with phase-shifting interferometry
(PSI) is used to determine the concentration and temperature distribution, which are needed to quantify the
supersaturation ratio S at which crystallisation occurs. Although PSI is time-resolved, the crystal growth kinetics
is not the focus of this study . PSI has a low magnification and low resolution, and therefore is not a suitable
tool to assess the onset of crystallisation.
In this study , four types of experiments were performed: three with aspatially uniform temperature (i.e. isother-
mal) distribution and one experiment with a non-uniform temperature distribution. Here, “isothermal” refers to
spatial variations, not temporal.
In the experiments with a spatially isothermal temperature distribution, the following was observed.
1. No visible crystallisation was observed after holding at SIT,15 ◦C for 2 hours (Fig. 3a).
2. Crystallisation occurred after being held at SIT at 14.5 ◦C for less than 15 minutes (Fig. 3b–e). This is referred
to as cooling crystallisation.
3. When a low-concentration solution is slowly injected onto top of the original solution, crystallisation occurred
at the diffusion interface with SDF < SIT. Thus, crystallisation occurred at lower S under diffusive mass flux,
referred to as isothermal diffusion crystallisation .
This was presented in the original manuscript:
Line 221, original manuscript
The experimental results show that crystallisation under isothermal diffusion, which occurs in the
metastable zone, is not at the location where S is highest in the cell but at the location where there is
a high mass flux.
In the experiment with a designed non-uniform temperature distribution in which the upper boundary is heated
while the lower boundary cooled, the following was observed.
1. Crystallisation occurred under thermophobic thermodiffusion. However, at the cold boundary , STD, cold <
SIT, i.e. crystallisation occurred at lower S under thermodiffusive mass flux, referred to as thermodiffusive
crystallisation.

R2C3 Concentration gradient ( ∆C) is commonly used, but what is its definition? In the caption of Figure 3 it

Response:
Thank you for this query .
We would like to clarify that ∆C is the concentration difference, not the concentration gradient. It is true that ∆C
is defined as the concentration difference between the local concentration and the mean concentration in the cell.
The reason we present our concentration distribution within the cell as ∆C(x, y) in the figures is that PSI is an
optical method based on refractive index difference and can only measure the concentration difference between
two points, not the absolute concentration of an isolated point. Therefore, the mean concentration is chosen as a
reference and we report the spatial concentration difference measured by PSI (e.g. Fig. 3).
Revision:
The reason for reporting ∆C instead of absolute C within the cell is added:
Line 355, revised manuscript
However, PSI can only determine the difference in concentration or temperature between two points by
measuring the change in refractive index; it cannot provide the absolute concentration or temperature of an
isolated point without a reference value. Therefore, all temperature distributions within the crystallisation
cell are reported relative to the boundary temperature, and all concentration distributions are reported
relative to the mean concentration.
