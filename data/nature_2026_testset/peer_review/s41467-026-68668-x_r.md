Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The study investigates the relationship between carbon stocks of forests and the species richness of different taxonomic
groups at a regional scale across Europe. The main focus of the paper is on how different carbon stock types – aboveground
carbon, standing deadwood and lying deadwood – influence taxonomic groups in different ways which makes. The
multifaceted approach of this study makes it a very interesting and relevant paper. Additionally, the scale of the study is very
impressive with sites all over Europe which makes the results quite robust and relevant to a broad audience. However, I
have got concerns about the current structure of the introduction which is unnecessarily long and needs to be streamlined.
Additionally, the methods should be expanded on a bit, especially given the data comprises a compilation of many different
datasets. Finally, I believe the data analysis has been oversimplified which takes away the study’s full potential impact. If all
of these points can be addressed than I believe it would make a great contribution to Nature Communications. Please find
my major and minor comments below. 
Major comments 
1) Introduction: 
a. As it reads, the introduction is long (currently at 18 paragraphs which are all very variable in length) and hard to follow with
a lot of information that is not necessarily crucial to the main message of the paper. I think some work should be put into
streamlining this a bit. For instance, lines 91 to 135 describe the policy context for this paper although it is not explicitly what
the paper is looking at. Lines 91 to 135 should be significantly reduced in size in one or two coherent paragraphs.
Additionally, lines 167 to 187 again goes into the policy side of things and should be reduced. 
b. Rather than going into broad forest management issues, there should also be more effort in trying to link how different
pools of carbon are managed differently across forest types. As the core findings of this study show that species richness
relates differently to various carbon pool types, it would be better to have some context as to how that may relate with
different ways that these pools are managed. For instance, a reduction in live aboveground pools through harvesting does
not necessarily lead to a reduction in lying deadwood pools (see Pfeifer et al., (2015)). 
2) Methods 
a. The method needs more information in some places. For instance, in line 442, what was the sample method used (cover,
line transect, opportunistic…)? For line 444, how were the number of trees chosen? 
b. There are no details about the silvicultural system, forest category and protocol used although these are used in the
analysis later. 
3) Analysis 
a. Why was only species richness used to investigate their relationship with carbon stocks? Other measures such as
Shannon Diversity or the presence of threatened/vulnerable species or other ways of looking at biodiversity could be as
valuable to investigate. 
b. I think the authors should add an analysis (i.e. correlation) to show the relationship between different carbon pools. Are
deadwood pools generally related to aboveground pools? And how would this influence the results of the analyses and
what are the policy consequences of that? 
c. In the BRT models, silvicultural system, forest category and protocol are incorporated inside the models yet the results
from these are reported nowhere. These need to be reported somewhere. The model outputs and analysis seem to be

oversimplified in some cases. 
d. I think it would be interesting to look at how the relationship between carbon stocks and biodiversity changes with
silvicultural type. The authors make a mention of this in the discussion lines 314-318 and leave it as an open question
although it is probably something that could be investigated with this data. 
e. It would also be valuable to add climatic conditions within their model, as the coordinates of each site are known, it would
not be hard to compile these for each site. This would allow to address more open questions in the discussion such as these
about the relationship between beetles and lying deadwood (lines 352-353).

Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
What are the noteworthy results? 
Authors have analysed a new multi-taxa biodiversity dataset, and compared observations of taxa biodiversity with the
estimated tree carbon pools (live and dead) in the sampled sites/plots. The results of broad scale BD-C links are valuable
and the topic is policy relevant. The manuscript further discusses the policy links, but there the discussion seems immature. 
Will the work be of significance to the field and related fields? 
- The dataset is large, and it presents much-needed information about how diversities of different taxa are associated with C
pools, so this is significant. 
How does it compare to the established literature? If the work is not original, please provide relevant references.

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 3 -->

- Large-scale BD studies are rare, and few have considered multiple taxa at the same time. This is also the strength of the
paper. 
- The work is clearly original. 
- Policy discussion does not stand out, and I am not sure if it offers novelty presently. 
Does the work support the conclusions and claims, or is additional evidence needed? 
- Work has been made to harmonise diversity data, and it appears adequate. 
- I was missing figures offering a glimpse of actual (or scaled) data, particularly to see the spread of responses to evaluate
how strong the relationships between C and diversity are. Perhaps something to consider for the next version of the
manuscript? 
Are there any flaws in the data analysis, interpretation and conclusions? Is the methodology sound? Does the work meet the
expected standards in your field? Do these prohibit publication or require revision? 
- Data analysis is straightforward and fairly simple. The applied machine learning method is effective, and authors did not
seem to overinterpret its results. 
- Authors do not explain how they ended up in the selection of variables for BRT (table 2). The selection might be obvious
but why did not they include e.g. climate variables, which might affect the broad-scale relationships? Did they test them? Did
they include tree species richness among vascular plants or only ground vegetation? 
- Explanations for protocol and silvicultural system and forest type are currently missing, so their importance is hard to
evaluate, although they are not the focus of the manuscript 
- The description of the C calculation flow in the main section and supplementary are fairly unclear. Figure 6 and caption
could be improved easily. There also seems to be an error in Eq.6, possibly a typo. 
- Certain statements regarding to policies should be revised. See minor comments for details 
- All needed changes are doable, so the manuscript could become publishable after a major revision. 
Is there enough detail provided in the methods for the work to be reproduced? 
- Yes, at least almost. 
Suggesting that authors interpret and evaluate the goodness of models in discussion and
supplementary.
