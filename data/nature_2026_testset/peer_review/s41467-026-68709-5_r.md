Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Review comments on NCOMMS-25-43746 
Overall: The paper by Leyva et al. reported the detection of sugars in the samples returned from the asteroid (162173)
Ryugu, as well as that in Orgueil meteorites. The authors utilized an ultrasonic-assisted extraction and a stereoselective
analytical method using GCxGC-TOF-MS. As a result, they reported the detection of D and L-arabinose and D and L-xylose
from the extract from Ryugu. From the Orgueil extract, the detection of not only arabinose and xylose, but also other
aldopentose sugars such as ribose, lyxose, and ribulose was reported. The present results were the second detection of
abiotic aldopentose sugars in extraterrestrial samples after Furukawa et al. (2019), who confirmed the presence of
indigenous sugars based on their 13C enrichments. On the other hand, enantioselective analyses of sugars were performed
for the first time in the present study, which can, in principle, definitely distinguish between abiotic and biotic sugars. 
The detection of biologically relevant molecules in extraterrestrial materials is of special interest from the viewpoint of
prebiotic evolution and the origin of life on Earth and elsewhere in the solar system. Moreover, the detection of sugars, which
may be minor in extraterrestrial materials, from tiny portions of Ryugu samples is challenging. So, the report on the
successful detection of sugars in such a tiny, valuable sample deserves publication in Nature Communications. 
However, there is a critical problem in the present manuscript: a blank control was really insufficient in the present study. For
example, although significant sugar contaminants were detected in the procedural blank (PB) experiment, the authors did
not evaluate their contribution to the detected sugars in Ryugu and Orgueil. This is based on their opinion that the recovery
of sugars is poor when some mineral matrices are used. If no sugars were detected in the PB without such minerals, the
sugars detected in the sample are reasonably acceptable as indigenous to the samples. However, as long as significant
contaminations are present in the PB and no evaluation of their contributions is performed, the reported quantification values
and the enantiomeric parameters cannot be accepted as indigenous to Ryugu and Orgueil. 
I guess pristine Ryugu samples are no longer available, so the reanalysis of Ryugu samples would be difficult, unfortunately.
However, according to the authors, they still have enough Orgueil meteorite samples. So, I strongly recommend authors to
evaluate the potential contamination very carefully under the same analytical conditions with the meteorite samples and to
perform extraction and analysis of sugars in Orgueil meteorites again. As for the Ryugu results, based on the new analyses
of PB and Orgueil, the presence of sugars should be evaluated in more detail, although any reliable quantification would not
be possible. I hope the following comments are helpful in improving the current manuscript. 
-L8: I cannot understand the meaning of “unequal distribution”. This should be rephrased appropriately. 
-L10, “robust formation of sugars in laboratory simulations of interstellar ice chemistry”: I do not think that the robust formation
of sugars in interstellar ices has been established in earlier laboratory experiments. For example, de Marcellus et al. (2015)
and Meinert et al. (2016) performed laboratory experiments at liquid-nitrogen temperature, which may be less relevant to
processes in the coldest regions in the interstellar medium (ISM), whose typical temperature is as low as 10 K. Nuevo et al.
(2018) and Zhang et al. (2024) used unrealistic ices in the ISM for the reactants (e.g., methanol and CO mixed ices, water
and methanol mixed ices, without other components). I do not doubt the formation of sugars in their experiments, but I do not
fully agree with the “robust formation of sugars” in interstellar ice chemistry.

Major comments: 
This research investigated sugars in the sample of the carbonaceous asteroid Ryugu. The sample is extremely valuable,
and the chiral separation of sugars represents a significant novel development. Thus, this research is suitable for
consideration in Nature Communications. However, I have several concerns with this analysis, primarily related to the
potential contamination of biological and unclear sources of sugars, which may be derived from a standard chemical
mixture. Suppose this contamination occurred outside of the sample solution, such as in the GC-MS injector. In that case, a
repeat analysis of the sample with a clean GC-MS system will provide a convincing result. However, if this contamination
occurred in the sample solution, I have no idea how to show the presence of extraterrestrial sugars in the Ryugu with this
sample. Thus, the manuscript, at least in this form, is not sufficient to show that the Ryugu sample contains abiotic sugars. 
1. The central issue is the contamination of both D-sugars and L-sugars in the sample of Orgueil and potentially Ryugu. In

this type of analysis, the procedural blank that undergoes the same conditions as the sample is crucial for demonstrating
contamination. The procedural blank of Orgueil shows contamination of D-ribose, L-ribose, D-lyxose, and L-lyxose. Since L-
ribose and L-lyxose are not biological contaminants, this suggests that cross-contamination of the standard or sample
occurred with the procedural blank. I guess the contamination was from the standard because the significantly higher
abundance of ribose and lyxose than arabinose and xylose is not reasonable as abiotic sugars, and the relative abundance
between sugars is different from that of the Orgueil. Given the presence of cross-contamination, the chromatogram of the
Orgueil sample may be consistent with the sugar composition of the standard + biological contaminants. The author should
explain considering this possibility, both in Ryugu and Orgueil. To discuss this possibility, the authors should show the
chromatogram of the standard mixture. 
2. The amounts of D-xylose and L-arabinose in the procedural blank for the Ryugu sample should be listed in the Table. The
abundance of D-xylose in Ryugu maybe comparable to that detected in the procedural blank. They should be compared. 
3. L268-269 and table 1: The authors describe that “Among these, ribose, lyxose, and ribulose showed racemic distributions,
consistent with an extraterrestrial origin.” However, near-racemic ribose and lyxose are also detected in the procedural
blank, indicating that the detected ribose and lyxose are possible contaminants from the standard. If we suppose that the

greater amounts? As the author shows, the recovery of ribose is only 4%, indicating that ribose is present at a concentration
more than 300 times that of arabinose. This is not reasonable. The compositions of sugars in the procedural blank implies
that the same source of contamination occurred more significantly to Orgueil. 
4. The data on ribulose is missing. This should be shown. 
5. The chromatograms and abundance of ribose and lyxose in Ryugu should be shown. 
6. The authors underlined the novelty and advantage of this extraction repeatedly. However, the ultrasonic-assisted room
temperature extraction has been conducted in previous studies with water and 2% HCl (Cooper et al. 2001 and Furukawa et
al., 2019). The clear difference in this study is extraction by methanol. However, extraction of sugars with methanol is not
effective, owing to their low solubility compared to water and HCl. For example, Alessandro et al., (Alessandro C. Galvão,
Maicon R. Zangalli, Pedro Felipe Arce, Weber S. Robazza, Solubility of fructose and sucrose in mixtures of methanol and
water: Experiment and application of equations of state and activity coefficient model, Journal of Food Engineering, 369,
2024,) shows that the solubility of fructose in methanol is less than 10% in water. The loss of the extraction is evaluated in
this method using the addition of sugars to Orgueil. Thus, this is not an essential problem, but clearly this extraction is not the
advantage but disadvantage from previous studies. 
7. The table 2 shows the abundance of amino acids and their blank-subtracted values. The values of “UNH(a)” represent
sample + contaminants and the value with “UNH(b)” are real extraterrestrial amino acids. Many of the values in “b” is n.v.,
meaning that they are smaller than the blank. This means that the procedural blank does not work as procedural blank.

2019). Did the authors trim Orgueil to remove contamination, or did the authors use an inner part of the original fragment of
Orgueil? The data of Orgueil analysis in this manuscript shows contamination of biological sugars and amino acids. If they
analyzed with exposed surface, this could be the primary reason of biological contamination.

(Remarks to the Author) 
Below you will find my review of manuscript NCOMMS-25-43746 entitled “Abiotic sugars in 9162173) Ryugu and the

Overall this manuscript is very interesting. The results presented are important to the astrobiology and planetary science
communities, and timely given the ongoing studies and publications related to the analyses of samples from asteroids
Ryugu and Bennu. There are, however, a few things about that manuscript that bother me, and I would like the authors to
consider my comments below before I can recommend this manuscript for publication in Nature Communications. 
General comments: 
The thing that bothers me the most about this manuscript is the strange imbalance between the study of sugars and that of
amino acids. On the one hand, the title of manuscript only refers to the detection of sugars in Ryugu and Orgueil samples,
but on the other hand, half of the paper is dedicated to the detection of amino acids. Indeed, the Results, Discussion, and
Methods section all significantly describe the protocol, detection, and data interpretation of amino acids in those samples at
almost the same level as for sugars. In particular, Tables 2 and 3 are huge and only related to amino acids. But somehow
the title doesn’t mention anything about amino acids at all. 
I do acknowledge and appreciate the fact that the authors included detailed protocol and results obtained for the detection of
amino acids in both Ryugu and Orgueil samples, and I understand that this information helps (1) compare the results
presented in this manuscript with previous studies, and (2) assess the origin and level of contamination of the samples for
both amino acids and sugars, i.e., extraterrestrial vs. terrestrial contamination. 
However, this makes the actual objective of this manuscript confusing, to say the least. Is the manuscript about the detection
of sugars (as the title suggests) or about the detection of both sugars and amino acids? Was this manuscript originally two
separate manuscripts that eventually ended up stitched together, because some information from the study of amino acids
could help support the extraterrestrial origin of sugars? 
In addition, there are two statements in the manuscript that are not supported by any of the figures nor the tables: 
Claim 1: The manuscript clearly mentions several times the (first reported) detection of ribulose in both Ryugu and Orgueil
samples. However, there are no figures to support this claim anywhere. At the very least, this claim should be supported by a
figure with GCxGC-TOFMS data (chromatograms + mass spectra) showing the detection of ribulose and a comparison with
the procedural blank and the reference standard, similarly to other detected sugars. As is, the authors are asking the readers
to accept the first detection of ribulose in extraterrestrial samples on a “trust me when we tell you” basis. 
Claim 2: Similarly, there are no supporting figures for the detections of the amino acids mentioned in the manuscript. I
understand that the current version of the manuscript wants to focus on the detection of sugars (see my other general
comments above), but at the same time the authors make many claims regarding the detection of several amino acids, use
those data to determine enantiomeric excesses and to show that the amino acid data support an extraterrestrial origin for
both the sugars and amino acids detected here. Therefore, supporting the claims of detection of amino acids is critical to the
message this manuscript wants to convey and, like the detection of ribulose, these detections can’t be based only on good
faith. 
Finally, I am a bit concerned about the levels of contamination in the procedural blanks (PBs). Indeed, Figures 2, 3, and 5
show high levels of contamination for the compounds (both sugars and amino acids) that the authors are searching for in the
samples. For example, the intensity of the peak for L-Ara in the PB shown in Figure 2 is over 7 times higher than the intensity
of the peak for L-Ara in the Ryugu sample. Similarly, the intensity of the peak for D-Xyl in the PB shown in Figure 2 is
comparable to the intensity of the peak for D-Xyl in the Ryugu sample. I understand that other elements of the analysis still
point towards an extraterrestrial origin of these compounds, but this is quite concerning, and the authors should provide a
more solid explanation of why these abundant contaminants are still consistent with an extraterrestrial origin for these
sugars. On a similar topic, why were the values reported in Tables 1, 2, and 3 for the abundances of sugars and amino acids
in both Ryugu and Orgueil not corrected for PBs? How can the abundances and enantiomeric excesses found for the
detected compounds be compared with previous studies if the values used for these determinations are not corrected? 
In summary, some decisions need to be made by the authors regarding the revisions to this manuscript. I see 2 possible
options: 
Option 1: The authors could almost entirely remove the text and parts of the figures regarding the study of amino acids, and
publish those very interesting results in a separate paper. In this case, they could easily add the GCxGC-TOFMS data
missing to support the detection of ribulose, expand the discussion regarding the contaminants seen in the PBs, and decide
what to do with the uncorrected values reported in Table 1. 
Option 2: The authors could officially make this manuscript a study of the detection of both sugars and amino acids in Ryugu
and Orgueil. To this purpose, they would need to add figures showing GCxGC-TOFMS data for amino acids to support their
detection claims, expand the discussion regarding the contaminants seen in the PBs, and decide what to do with the
uncorrected values reported in Tables 1, 2, and 3. This option may however result in a significant increase of the length of
the manuscript.
