Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors proposed an approach of inducing non-polar nanocluster confinement through the design of a high-entropy
(Bi0.2Na0.2Ba0.2Sr0.2Ca0.2)(Ti1-xSnx)O3 lead-free relaxor ferroelectrics for energy storage application. The strategy looks
good but is not timely. Because the quality of the work does not match with the requirement for a reputed journal like Nat.
Comm. since a lot of work is happening in this area and mostly, the scientific community is giving importance to thin film-
based capacitors. Also, the energy storage performance parameter values are far lower than values reported for RFE based
thin films and simple binary oxide based ultra-thin film capacitors. 
Therefore, I am sorry to say that work cannot be
considered for publication in this journal.

Dear Editor 
Thank you very much for giving us an opportunity to revise our manuscript entitled “Non-Polar 
Nanocluster Confinement Engineering Realizes Exceptional Capacitive Energy Storage in Pb-
Free High-Entropy Relaxors”. We sincerely thank the reviewers for their time and for providing 
comments on our manuscript. We have carefully considered the points raised and provide a point-
by-point response below. We hope that our clarifications and the significant revisions made to the 
manuscript will adequately address the reviewer's concerns. 
Reviewer 1's Comments 
Comment 1: The authors proposed an approach of inducing non-polar nanocluster confinement 
through the design of a high-entropy (Bi 0.2Na0.2Ba0.2Sr0.2Ca0.2)(Ti1-xSnx)O3 lead-free relaxor 
ferroelectrics for energy storage application. The strategy looks good but is not timely. Because 
the quality of the work does not match with the requirement for a reputed journal like Nat. Comm. 
since a lot of work is happening in this area and mostly, the scientific community is giving 
importance to thin film-based capacitors.  
Reply: We thank the reviewer for acknowledging that our strategy “looks good.” Regarding 
timeliness and quality, we respectfully offer a different perspective based on the current research 
landscape and the specific contribution of our work.
 Timeliness of the Strategy:  The field of lead-free energy storage is indeed rapidly 
evolving. A key contemporary challenge is no longer merely pushing the absolute value 
of energy density ( Wrec) in idealized thin-film systems, but rather designing dielectric 
materials that achieve a superior co-optimization of Wrec and efficiency (η) in scalable, 
practical forms. Our work addresses this precise challenge by introducing a novel “non-

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 5 -->

polar nanocluster confinement” strategy, which is a timely and active research direction 
focused on manipulating nanoscale polarization dynamics to overcome intrinsic material 
limitations. 
 Complementarity of Multilayer ceramic capacitors (MLCCs) and Thin Films:  We 
agree that thin-film capacitors achieve outstanding performance in laboratory settings. 
However, the scientific community invests significant effort in bulk ceramics and MLCCs 
for a crucial reason: they represent the practically viable pathway for macroscale power 
electronics due to their superior robustness, scalability, large volume, and cost-
effectiveness. Thin films and MLCCs are largely complementary technologies serving 
different application spaces. Our work advances the fundamental science and performance 
ceiling of the bulk ceramic platform, which remains indispensable. 
 Quality and Novelty:  The quality of our work is demonstrated through a combined 
approach of phase-field simulation, multiscale structural analysis, and experimental 
validation, which provides a comprehensive and mechanistic understanding of the 
structure-property relationship. This level of fundamental insight into a new material 
design strategy meets the high standards for novelty and scientific rigor expected in 
leading journals. 
Comment 2: Also, the energy storage performance parameter values are far lower than values 
reported for RFE based thin films and simple binary oxide based ultra-thin film capacitors. 
Therefore, I am sorry to say that work cannot be considered for publication in this journal. 
Reply: We agree that the absolute Wrec values of thin films are higher, a direct consequence of 
their nanometer-scale thickness enabling extremely high breakdown fields (>500 kV mm -1). 
However, a direct comparison is not equitable due to the fundamental differences in 
dimensionality, fabrication, and application, as noted above.
The more relevant and fair comparison is within the category of lead-free MLCCs, which is the 
focus of our study. Within this context, our reported performance is among the very best, 
particularly in its combination of high Wrec and ultra-high η:
 Our work achieves Wrec ~ 18.5 J cm-3 with an exceptionally high η ~ 92.4%.
 This co-optimization is rare and critically important. Many systems show high Wrec at the 
expense of η (often falling to 80-85%), which leads to significant energy loss and heat

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 6 -->

generation in practical devices.
 To contextualize this, we have included a new table (Table S1, Supplementary Information) 
and a Wrec vs. η plot in the revised manuscript, comparing our results with other state-of-
the-art lead-free bulk ceramics, MLCCs and thin films. This visualization clearly shows 
that our material occupies a leading position in the high-efficiency, high-energy-density 
region for MLCCs.
Therefore, we believe the performance of our material is not "far lower" in its relevant domain 
but is, in fact, highly competitive and represents a significant step forward in solving the Wrec-η
trade-off.

Summary
In summary, we respectfully argue that our work is both timely and of high quality, as it 
introduces a novel microstructure design strategy to a core problem in the field. It delivers top-
tier performance within the technologically crucial domain of bulk ceramic and MLCCs, 
supported by deep physical insight. We have significantly revised the manuscript to better 
contextualize our work and temper claims, and we hope it is now deemed suitable for publication.
We are grateful for the reviewer's feedback, which has helped us improve the presentation of 
our work.
Reviewer 2's Comments
The manuscript of Xie et al., reports a study on lead-free relaxor ferroelectric capacitors based on 
a high-entropy BNBSCT1-xSx system. The authors propose a “non-polar nanocluster confinement” 
strategy to address the trade-off between recoverable energy density (Wrec) and energy efficiency 
(η), supported by phase-field simulations and multiscale structural analysis. Although concept of

polar clusters as pinning centers is potentially useful for designing high-performance dielectrics 
of this kind. While the topic is certainly relevant to the field of sustainable energy storage and the 
approach appears interesting, the manuscript in its current form falls short in several important 
aspects that hinder its scientific impact and clarity. I provide my comments below:
Specific Comments:
1) Insufficient Positioning with Respect to State-of-the-Art: The introduction and discussion do 
not adequately situate the present work within the rapidly evolving state-of-the-art of lead-free 
relaxor ferroelectrics for capacitive energy storage.

higher values of Wrec = 19.0 J•cm-3, although a bit smaller η = 90%. This work should be cited

2. Non-Zero Current after Ferroelectric Switching at High Fields (>20 kV/mm): We 
appreciate the Reviewer's sharp observation regarding the behavior of the current-
density curves in Fig. 4S. The fact that the current does not return to zero after the 
sharp polarization switching peak is a common and expected phenomenon in relaxor 
ferroelectric ceramics, especially under high applied electric fields. We have added a 
discussion on this point in the Supplementary Information accompanying Fig. 4S. 
The primary reasons for this are:
o Increased Leakage Current: At high electric fields (approaching the 
material's breakdown strength), the contribution of ohmic leakage current 
becomes significantly more pronounced. This leakage current, which is

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 17 -->

approximately proportional to the applied field, forms a conductive 
"background" upon which the displacement current (from polarization 
switching) is superimposed. Therefore, even after the polarization switching 
is complete, the measured current does not drop to zero but to a finite value 
corresponding to this leakage component (Mater. Res. Bull. 2022, 147, 111617).
o Dielectric Relaxation in Relaxor Ferroelectrics: As a canonical relaxor 
ferroelectric, our BNBSCT 1-xSx material contains a high density of polar 
nanoregions (PNRs). The dynamics of these PNRs under a high, rapidly 
changing electric field involve a broad distribution of relaxation times (J. Adv. 
Dielectr. 2011, 01, 107-118; Acta Mater. 2023, 255, 119071). This results in a 
long-tailed, frequency-dependent dielectric response, which manifests as a 
persistent current after the main switching peak. This is distinct from the 
sharp, square-like current response of a normal ferroelectric.
In summary, the non-zero current baseline at high fields is attributed to the combined 
effects of increased leakage current and the intrinsic dielectric relaxation behavior of the 
relaxor state. This behavior is consistent with other high-performance relaxor ferroelectric 
systems reported in the literature and is directly linked to the energy loss (and thus the 
efficiency, η) of the material. We thank the Reviewer for prompting us to include this 
important physical explanation.

proportional to dP/dE, which in turn reflects the differential dielectric constant ( εd) of the
