Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
I have read the revised manuscript “Quantum Hall Effect at 2 mT” by A.S. Mayorov et al. The authors have addressed many
of my concerns in the revised manuscript. While my concern in the original review that many of the results are only
understood qualitatively still stands, I think the experimental results are important and interesting and the revised manuscript
is suitable for Nature Communications, though I have some minor comments that should be addressed before publication: 
1) The paragraph beginning “Here, we can speculate on the physical origin of the absence of QHE plateaus below 2 mT…”
is confusing. The simplest explanation is that the observation of plateaus is limited to the regime mu_q*B > 1, and the
estimation of mu_q is accurate. I suggest rephrasing the paragraph, for example: “It is possible that our estimation of mu_q is
a lower bound, and other factors limit the observation of QHE plateaus below 2 mT….” or similar. 
2) I am not convinced that quantisation of the electron number on each edge between contacts plays any role in the
observation of QHE plateaus at low magnetic field. It isn’t clear why electron number should be quantised or can’t be less
than one on an edge – the contacts to the edges are metallic and don’t confine the electrons. This part of the discussion
could be removed, or, if kept in the paper, it should be better motivated with additional references or theoretical support. 
3) The sentence “At room temperature, the mobility of all the DLG devices is close to the phonon scattering limit of about 2 ×
10^5 cm2V−1s−1 (Fig. S1).(31)” is unclear and ambiguous. It is not clear what “the phonon scattering limit” refers to, and the
reference to Ref. 31 implies it is the intrinsic limit set by acoustic phonon scattering. That would be incorrect: the analysis in
the supplement would include the intrinsic acoustic phonon scattering resistivity of graphene within the rho_s term, since the
acoustic phonon resistivity in graphene is independent of carrier density. (The authors could compare the magnitude of
rho_s to the acoustic phonon scattering contribution found in Ref. 31, approximately 30 Ohms.) The mobility that the authors
extract from Fig. S1 is instead the field-effect mobility (the linear-in-n contribution to conductivity) and results from extrinsic
phonon scattering, i.e. due to the surface phonon polaritons of the hBN. This limit was first estimated in Phys. Rev. B 81,
195442 (2010) for graphene on hBN, and the authors could compare to the magnitude calculated there, or to other
experiments. However, screening likely affects this result as well so it isn’t clear that agreement is expected!
 
 
Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors have satisfactorily addressed my comments. I recommend publication.
