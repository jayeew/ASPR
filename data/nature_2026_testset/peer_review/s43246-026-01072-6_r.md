Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
This study presents a detailed and comprehensive investigation of the magnetic nodal line bands in hcp cobalt film by
combining spin- and angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (Spin-ARPES) and density functional theory (DFT)
calculations. The experimentally measured band structures are in good agreement with theoretical predictions. The
discussion on the band topologies is also well supported by theoretical calculations and experimental evidence.
Furthermore, this magnetic nodal line structure crosses the Fermi level, indicating its potential for applications in spintronics.
I would like to recommend this paper for publication after the following concerns are adequately addressed, and also,
corresponding revisions are made in the manuscript. 
Remark 1: The paper investigated the nodal lines in both the kz=0 and kz=π/c planes, making the precise photon energy
corresponding to kz crucial. How do the authors determine the photon energies corresponding to kz=0 and kz=π/c? It would
be better if the authors provide detailed photon energy scans data set, which can determine the specific planes for 70eV
(kz=0) and 98eV (kz=π/c)? Please also provide the value of the inner potential (v0) used in this process, which might differ
from the value obtained from bulk single crystalline cobalt. 
Remark 2: The paper briefly mentioned that the trace of the Wilson loop was calculated to substantiate the nodal line
conclusion. However, considering the complexity of the band structures near the Fermi level, it will be helpful if the authors
specify which particular bands were included in the Wilson loop trace calculation to establish this topological characteristic? 
Remark 3: The authors mentioned that the remnant magnetization is slightly canted, as also manifested by the small spin
polarization in the out-of-plane direction, even though the external magnetic pulse was applied along the in-plane direction.
Is such canted magnetization resulted from any intrinsic mechanism, or due to the alignment error between sample surface
and magnetic coil? I will be informative if the author can provide the magnetization curve (M-H) data as an important
characterization of the sample. 
Remark 4: In Fig. 2 of the Supplementary Material, are the labels for the space group names and the corresponding Fermi
surfaces possibly swapped? Please confirm this issue again. 
Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author):

Reviewer #4 (Remarks to the Author): 
The manuscript entitled "Manifold of magnetic nodal lines in an elemental ferromagnet" presents the study of the electronic
structure of hcp cobalt using density functional theory (DFT) calculations and angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy
(ARPES) with spin analysis. The work reanalyzes in detail the band structure of this well-known ferromagnet, in light of the
topological properties suggested by the analogy with nodal line magnetic semimetals. The theoretical treatment is divided
into two sections that discuss the presence of nodal features based on symmetry arguments for the two possible magnetic
configurations and that determine the position and dispersion of nodal rings/lines by DFT. The spectroscopic analysis is
devoted to the identification of the predicted topological features on the high-symmetry planes of a remanently magnetized
thick Co(0001) film grown on W(110). The authors were able to interpret the complex experimental band patterns as due to
the coexistence of electronic structures associated with in-plane and out-of-plane magnetization. Besides the actual
correspondence between experiment and theory, which is sometimes fully satisfactory (nodal rings on the kz=0 plane) and
sometimes only qualitative (nodal lines on the kz=Pi/c plane), the attempt to reinterpret the electronic structure of a simple
and well-known material according to the topological classification represents a highly valuable innovative approach. Above
all, this approach allows the authors to separate the analysis of the electronic structure from the problems associated with
the growth and characterization of multi-element materials. Similar studies on other simple ferromagnets could expand the
ability to characterize the typical structures of magnetic nodal lines semimetals. 
That said, I believe there is room for improvement in some areas before accepting this manuscript for publication. The most
relevant are the first three in the list below and concern the data reported in Figure 4. 
- The correspondence between the measured and calculated Fermi surfaces on the kz=Pi/c plane is not evident, given that
the number and shape of Fermi contours in the two cases are different. The authors could use the labels LL and UL in Fig.
4b to facilitate comparison and de-emphasize the similarity between theory and experiment in the text. 
- It is unclear why the properties of the Weyl nodal line along the AL direction are discussed using the ARPES data in Fig.
4c, collected at a photon energy of 70 eV which probes the kz = 0 plane. 
- The relationship between the bulk and surface states highlighted in Fig. 4 is only hinted at by analogy with nonmagnetic
systems described in the literature. What is the correct way to conceptualize this relationship for magnetic systems? 
Minor considerations: 
- The use of "Gamma-containing plane" (kz = 0) and "A-containing plane" (kz = Pi/c), instead of "Gamma plane" and "A
plane", should be considered for greater clarity. 
- In the section "Spin-resolved photoemission of nodal rings in kz=0 plane," there are several errors in the labeling of the
panels of Figure 3. 
- In the conclusion, I would remove the word "Although," since cobalt remains a prototypical ferromagnet. The results
reported in the present manuscript do not negate it.

The trace of the Wilson loop for a given nodal line was calculated using all bands below the node, including the band that forms the lower part of the considered crossing.   We have expanded the Methods to incorporate this clarification.    Remark 3: The authors mentioned that the remnant magnetization is slightly canted, as also manifested by the small spin polarization in the out-of-plane direction, even though the external magnetic pulse was applied along the in-plane direction. Is such canted magnetization resulted from any intrinsic mechanism, or due to the alignment error between sample surface and magnetic coil? I will be informative if the author can provide the magnetization curve (M-H) data as an important characterization of the sample.  We thank the referee for pointing this out. The ARPES and spin-resolved ARPES measurements were performed in remanence following the application of an external magnetic field pulse. This means that, during our measurements, no external magnetic field is applied and we measure a remanent magnetic state which is an intrinsic, ground state of the system. Such a remanent magnetization direction is a stable, equilibrium state which results from the competition between shape and magnetocrystalline anisotropies, resulting in a total in-plane magnetic anisotropy with a small canting of the remanent magnetization in the out-of-plane direction, as seen in our spin-resolved spectra. Note that the magnetic properties of hcp Co films grown on W(110) are well known and have been experimentally investigated extensively in the past (see for instance, H. Fritzsche et al., Phys. Rev. B 51, 15933 (1995) and A. Kleibert et al., Phys. Rev. B 72, 144404 (2005), which we now added as Refs. 49 and 50 in our revised manuscript). As a result of the competition between these anisotropies, the hysteresis loops along the in-plane direction are not perfectly squared. More in detail, from a macroscopic perspective, following the application of an external magnetic field pulse, the magnetization will align at first along the direction of the field during the duration of the pulse, and then start to precess typically on picosecond time scales, following a spiral trajectory until the total magnetic energy is minimized, with the remanent magnetization reaching an equilibrium state along the direction of the total in-plane anisotropy. This process is usually well described by the Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert (LLG) equation, and the exact mathematical derivation of this intrinsic mechanism can be found in e.g., A. Hubert & R. Schaefer, Magnetic domains (Springer, Berlin, 1998), A. Ahanaroni Introduction to theory of ferromagnetism (Oxford Science publications, 1996). For these reasons, the measured remanent magnetic state is the result of an intrinsic mechanism which in equilibrium conditions is weakly sensitive to small misalignments between the direction of the applied magnetic field pulse and the direction of the in-plane magnetic anisotropy. We are extremely grateful to the Reviewer for highlighting this error. We expect a similar situation for surface states despite the fact that correlations at the surface are theoretically predicted to be stronger with increasing binding energy. For these reasons, in our calculations, we focused on the analysis of the main features which are the nodal lines, and found that performing LDA+U calculations is sufficient to fully characterize the nodal lines as well as the symmetries that protect the crossings, leading to good qualitative agreement between experiment and calculations. While beyond the scope of the present work, this would be valuable more generally, as with decreasing 3d band filling, the effect of non-local correlations becomes stronger whereas correlation effects tend to be weaker. The Reviewer is correct that there is not an obvious one-to-one correspondence between the bands in Fig. 4a and 4b. Supplemental Figure 3: Relationship between photon energy and kz for hcp Cobalt. (a) ‘Universal curve' of electron mean free path as a function of electron kinetic energy, adapted from Ref. 3 of the SI. Green areas indicate the approximate range covered by the photon energies used in this work. (b) Lorentzian functions demonstrating the uncertainty in kz of photoemission intensity. The curve corresponding to a mean free path, λ, of 5 angstroms most applicable for this work, is highlighted in green. Vertical dashed lines show the size of one hcp cobalt Brillouin zone along kz (black) and the FWHM of the λ=5 angstrom Lorentzian (green). (c) Relationship between the nominal kz plane, kz0, and the photon energy for three different kx (ky=0). An inner potential, V0, of 14.8 eV and a c-axis lattice constant of 0.407 nm were used.   1) While 98 eV was selected to observe the A point (kx,ky =0, kz=pi/c), band mapping with a constant photon energy extracts a curved contour through kx,ky,kz space. In other words, when probing momentum slices away from kx=ky=0, the effective kz plane also changes. For example, the Fermi surface maps shown in Fig. 4a is well centred at the respective Γ and A points for kx=ky=0, but at higher in-plane momentum values, the effective kz plane drifts, and indeed the bands in the high momentum regime begin to resemble the expectation for the opposite plane. This kz drift is illustrated in Sup. Fig. 3(c). 2) In addition to this, there is significant uncertainty in kz due to the finite mean free path in photoemission. For 70 or 98eV photons, the electron mean free path is approximately 5 angstroms (Sup Fig. 3(a)). This real-space c-axis localisation results in a delocalisation in kz. In practice, this means that, while a nominal kz plane can be selected using a free-electron final state approximation, the measured signal integrates over a significant range of kz. This is illustrated in Sup. What is the correct way to conceptualize this relationship for magnetic systems?  This is a good question. We imply in our manuscript that the observed surface states are related to the nodal crossings, and that they are therefore analogous to the drumhead states in non-magnetic nodal line systems. While we feel this is the likely scenario, we do not have explicit confirmation and therefore stop short of making that claim.  If the surface states are related to the nodal line, then to zero order one can expect the same type of band dispersions as drumhead states in non-magnetic nodal line semimetals, but exchange split alongside the nodal lines themselves. While drumhead states are spin-degenerate in the absence of spin-orbit coupling, the same type of states in magnetic systems will be spin polarised due to the exchange splitting. We note that drumhead surface states in ferromagnetic nodal line systems have been reported previously (e.g. Belopolski et al., Science 365 (6459) 1278-1281 (2019)).   While we only characterise nodal lines and the surface states present alongside them in the spin minority manifold of hcp cobalt, if the surface states are drumhead states, then they should also be present in the spin majority manifold at higher binding energies alongside the deeper-lying nodal line.
