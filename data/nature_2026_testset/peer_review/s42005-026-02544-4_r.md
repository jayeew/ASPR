Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors systematically studied the Fermi surface structure of TaTe4 and provided evidence for the reconstruction of the
Fermi surface. They also observed anisotropic linear magnetoresistance. Their high-quality data provide not only an
important supplement to the previous ARPES studies of the electronic structure of TaTe4 but also new information about its
transport properties. I recommend the publication of the manuscript in Communication Physics after the authors address the
following issues: 
1. The authors claimed they revealed a quasi-cylindrical Fermi pocket that was unobserved previously. This might not be
exactly correct. Previous DFT and ARPES studies have revealed this Fermi pocket. But ARPES studies failed to reveal its
three-dimensional structure. 
2. The DFT calculation of the Fermi surface in Fig. 1(f) seems to be different from the previous result, as a small cylindrical
Fermi surface around a/m is missing. I suggest that the authors comment on this difference.

Thank you for the insights on the anisotropy of the magnetoresistance in our samples. In this regard, we would 
like to break down the response in two parts, according to suggestions (a) and (b): 
a) While it is true that the geometric shapes of the Fermi surface pockets of TaTe4 are highly anisotropic, and 
that the mechanism discussed in Physical Review B 102.11 (2020): 115145) might seem applicable to TaTe4 due 
to this, there are a few considerations to take into account. First, SiP2 samples of the aforementioned PRB paper 
display  linear magnetoresistance only at magnetic field orientations close to a specific crystallographic axis, 
starting at very low fields. This trend rapidly shifts towards a nearly quadratic dependence as the magnetic field 
approaches another crystallographic direction. This means that SiP2 low field linear magnetoresistance is highly 
anisotropic, which points toward a different mechanism for the field regime below about 8T in our samples. On 
the other hand, for the high field linear regime, the linear magnetoresistance does show a strongly anisotropic 
character, for which we thoroughly developed arguments related to a magnetic breakdown mechanism in the 
manuscript, as elaborated in the answer to point b, in the following paragraph. 
 
b) The mechanism discussed in the PNAS paper (116.23 (2019): 11201-11206) is indeed a possible mechanism 
for TaTe4 samples, as we discussed in detail in the original version of the manuscript. This mechanism is valid 
for field regimes for which magnetic breakdown effects can be possible (the higher the field the more probable),

which are a combination of two sections of the FS, with an exponential amplitude as presented in Fig. 4(b)(iii),

breakdown effect with the presence of linear magnetoresistance at high fields in page 10, section of Linear MR, 
discussing on how hot spots in the Fermi surface, or sharp corners, have been closely linked to the emergence of 
linear magnetoresistance in previous reports as a consequence of scattering of charge carriers at these locations 
in reciprocal space.
