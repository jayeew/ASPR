Given the lower density of magnesium compared to aluminum and iron, the liquid metal readily 
floats, which in a conventional single -chamber cell results in its exposure to the anode gas 
environment and consequent oxidation. To address this, a dual-chamber configuration was therefore 
employed in the multi-anode experiments, incorporating isolated cathode and anode chambers. This 
physical barrier successfully avoided contact. Liquid magnesium was then safely extracted from the 
cathode chamber using a vacuum transfer ladle, resulting in notable improvements in product purity 
and current efficiency. Furthermore, the gas produced at the anode was purified, enabling argon 
recovery and thereby improving the experimental protocol's economic and environmental attributes.

Given the stochastic nature of electrolyte evaporation and subsequent deposition on the 
tungsten wire surface, this study applied a boron nitride (BN) coating to the tungsten wire 
surface uniformly three times prior to experimentation to mitigate oxidation of the tungsten 
wire by chlorine gas (the anode product). The BN-coated tip of the tungsten wire was lightly 
polished with 3000 -mesh sandpaper to ensure good electrical contact.  After conducting 
electrolysis experiments under varying current conditions, the average tungsten concentration

was 0.001 wt.% with  BN-coated tungsten filaments, compared to 0.004 wt.% with  uncoated

However, post -electrolysis inspection of the tungsten anode collector revealed partial 
degradation of the BN coating. Two primary mechanisms account for this damage: 
(a) Mechanical failure: Under high-temperature experimental conditions, the BN coating 
failed to form a robust interface with the tungsten wire. During sample extraction, mechanical

(b) Chemical consumption: Thermodynamic calculations (Fig. 4B) indicate that BN can

measurable corrosion protection, its overall effectiveness is compromised by the combined 
effects of elevated temperature and a highly oxidative environment.  Future studies will focus 
on exploring additional protective coatings for tungsten wires to further enhance corrosion 
resistance.

The optimized DFT models for each species and the energy values of reactants/products in the 
argon-plasma anode reaction are provided in Tables S1 and S2, respectively. The results, 
presented in Fig. 8, show that the electrochemical reaction ΔG for 2Cl- - 2e- = Cl2 is positive in the 
absence of Ar+, indicating that the oxidation of Cl- to Cl2 is non-spontaneous and requires an external 
driving force. In contrast, when argon plasma is introduced as the anode, Ar + actively engages in 
the reaction by interacting with Cl- ions in the electrolyte. This involvement facilitates the oxidation 
of Cl- and enables the spontaneous generation of Cl 2. The resulting reaction (2Ar+ + 2Cl- = 2Ar + 
Cl2) exhibits a negative ΔG, demonstrating that the oxidation of Cl- to Cl2 proceeds spontaneously 
in the presence of Ar+.

Question #7. Were chlorine atoms not detected in the AES measurement? 
Response #7: Thank you for raising this important point regarding our AES analysis. We have 
verified that no chlorine atomic spectral lines were detected in the AES test, a finding consistent 
with both the NIST spectral database and existing experimental data.  The spectral data 
presented above were collected  within two minutes upon reaching stable experimental 
operation. As this dataset already satisfied the basic requirements of the study, and continuous 
spectral monitoring during the prolonged electrolysis process would have imposed a substantial 
operational burden, no further spectral measurements were taken thereafter. Theoretically,
