Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript entitled "Atomically Tweaking Spin-Crossover Cooperativity to Augment Molecular Memory Density" by Liu
and coworkers presents a combined STS and DFT study of molecular chains composed of Ni atoms linked with
dehydrogenated tetrahydroxybenzene units. The authors demonstrate collective spin-state switching in these chains,
extending their previous work (Refs. 39, 40, 42). In this study, they convincingly show that introducing Fe atoms or
dehydrogenated diamino-benzoquinone-diimine (DABQDI) linkers effectively disrupt the cooperativity along the chains,
dividing them into smaller, individually switchable subunits. The data are of high quality, and the theoretical calculations

strongly support the interpretation of the observed phenomena. Overall, the manuscript presents significant findings that will
be of interest to the broad readership of Nature Communications. I therefore recommend publication after minor revisions.
Below are my specific comments and suggestions that should be addressed prior to acceptance: 
i) 
Figure 1b): The lower right dashed rectangle and the chosen color scheme for the DABQDI linker are misleading. I
suggest using green ellipses to indicate the chain disruption caused by modified linkers (as in Figures 5, 6) and extending
the dashed rectangle to include two metal atoms and three linkers for consistency and clarity. 
ii) page 8, top: The sentence "Doping with Fe and N-ligands introduces FeO4 and NiO2N4 coordination centers" appears to
contain a typographical error. Did the authors intend to write NiO2N2 or NiN4? 
iii) 
Figure 3: For the high-spin (HS) Ni_O centers, a peak below zero bias is observed, which is absent in the low-spin (LS)
Ni_O species. The authors describe the falling edge of this peak as a superposition of two Fano resonances. In the
Supplementary Information, they attribute these spectral features to a molecular spin screened by two independent Kondo
channels. I am aware that this feature has been reported in previous work (e.g. Figure 2 in Ref. 39) over a reduced voltage
range. However, given that the spectra are now shown over a broader energy window, it would be very informative to know
whether comparable features appear in the DFT density of states for the LS and HS Ni complexes that correspond to the
observed peak.

Author reply: 
We appreciate the Reviewer’s insightful comment.  
To date, for the pure NiO chains, the maximum number of NiO centers within a single chain that 
undergo a collective spin transition trigger by one local STM-tip voltage pulse is 28 (corresponding 
to a chain length of ~21 nm). The corresponding STM images before and after the manipulation are 
shown in Figure 4a  in the main text (also shown below as Fig . R1). It’s found that the manipulated 
chain length depends on the pulse conditions (voltage and tunneling current) . In experiments, the 
higher the pulse voltage or tunneling current, the longer the chain that can be manipulated. Owing to 
the space limitation of the manuscript and the scope of this work on regulation of SCO cooperativity, 
these experimental details are not included here. However, our next manuscript in preparation will 
quantitatively explore the dependence of the manipulated chain length on the manipulation conditions. 
 
Fig. R1 STM images of a Ni O chain before (top panel) and after (middle panel) application of an 
STM-tip voltage pulse to the Ni atom highlighted by the black arrow (V = 10 mV in constant-height 
mode). Twenty-eight Ni atoms with their spin states switched are labeled. 
For the doped chains, the voltage -pulse-induced collective spin transitions are confined within 
the NiO segments separated by the Fe O or NiN centers. In other words, the manipulated chain length 
is dictated by the length of the Ni O segment, which makes the Ni O segments quite promising for 
reliable memory bits. Taking the NiO-FeO chains as an example, we have successfully achieved spin 
manipulations in the NiO segments containing up to N = 12 NiO centers; The NiO segments with N > 
12 are rarely observed in our experiments. The length of these Ni O segments can be calculated by 
0.76*N (nm), where 0.76 nm corresponds to the NiO-NiO distance in the chain.

4. What is the repetition rate of tweaking SCO cooperativity demonstrated in Fig 4. … 
Author reply: 
It’s demonstrated in our previous work (ACS Nano 2020, 14, 11283) that the intrinsic switching 
rate of the SCO chain structure depends on the manipulation conditions (bias voltage and tunneling 
current). In general, higher bias voltage s and tunneling current s lead to faster SCO switching. For 
example, the switching rate is on the order of 10 0 s-1 at V = 90 mV and I ~ 100 pA, and increases to 
102 s-1 at V = 150 mV and I ~ 230 pA. However, in practical experiments, the spin transition of a NiO 
chain or segment is triggered and verified by applying an STM-tip voltage pulse followed by STM 
imaging. The time required for these operations is typically 101~102 s, corresponding to a rate of 10-
2~10-1 s-1, which is much slower than the intrinsic switching rate of the Ni O chain or segment . 
Therefore, the STM  operations become the rate -determining steps. In short, the intrinsic switching 
rate of the SCO chain structure depends on the manipulation conditions, whereas the overall 
manipulation rate is limited by the STM operations.

Experimentally, we first determined the nearest neighboring NiO-NiO distance in the Ni O chain 
by measuring the total length (L) of a pure NiO chain containing N NiO centers (typically N ≥ 10), 
which gave an average NiO-NiO distance of L/N. Repeating such measurement for multiple times, the

weighted average of all data  yielded a Ni O-NiO separation of 7.6 ± 0.2 Å . This result is further 
confirmed by comparing the chain structures with the Au(111) substrate lattice (as highlighted by the 
white grids in Fig. R6), revealing that the chains extend along the equivalent <123̅> directions of the 
substrate, as indicated by the black arrows in Fig. R6. Moreover, the coordinated metal centers in the 
chains (as marked by the grey dots in Fig. R 6) sit on equivalent substrate sites, demonstrating the 
commensurability between the chain periodicity and the substrate lattice. Therefore, the Ni O-NiO 
distance can be experimentally derived from the substrate lattice constant along the <123̅> directions, 
namely, 7.6 Å. 
 
Fig. R6 STM image showing the NiO chains and the Au(111) substrate lattice (V = 50 mV and I = 70 
pA) simultaneously. The substrate lattice is highlighted by white grids. Coordinated metal centers are 
illustrated by grey dots. Equivalent < 11̅0> and < 123̅> directions are marked by white and black 
arrows, respectively. 
Subsequently, the NiO-FeO and NiO-NiN distances in the doped Ni O chains were measured and 
compared with the Ni O-NiO separations acquired from the same STM images. Statistically these 
measured values did not show significant differences. Moreover, it’s also found that the dopants did 
not change the chain orientations, i.e., the doped NiO chains still extend along the equivalent <123̅> 
directions. Therefore, it’s concluded that “These doped NiO chains manifest a constant metal-metal 
separation of D = 7.6 ± 0.2 Å, being identical to that in the pure Ni O chains and commensurate with 
the Au(111) lattice along the chain orientations”. 
The negligible change in metal-metal separations between the undoped and doped chains can be 
attributed to the efficient chain -substrate interaction, as evidenced by the  chain-substrate 
commensurability. In other words, the Au(111) substrate lattice acts as a template that dictates the 
chain growth orientations and constrains the location of the metal centers to the specific equivalent 
substrate sites, which results in a constant metal-metal distance. The energy penalty arising from the 
compressed or stretched coordination bonds introduced by the hetero -metal/ligand dopants is 
compensated by the chain-substrate interaction. Accordingly, we have now added a sentence to the 
main text as below: 
Paragraph 1, Page 9: “The same metal-metal separation in both pure and doped NiO chains can 
be attributed to the efficient chain -substrate interaction that compensates for  the energy penalty 
arising from the compressed or stretched coordination bonds introduced by the hetero -metal/ligand 
dopants.”
