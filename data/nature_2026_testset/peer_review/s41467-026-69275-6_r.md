Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Xu et al. demonstrate that the topological antiferromagnetic Mn3Sn exhibits long-range spin transport up to 60 nm-thick via
current-induced SOT-switching and DL-SOT efficiency analyses. They also proposed a theoretical model based on spin
diffusion theory that incorporates the spin canting effect at the interface to reproduce the thickness dependence of the SOT
switching ratio. Antiferromagnetic spintronics is a hot topic in the spintronics community, and these analyses are valuable for
advancing this field. However, successful SOT switching in thicker Mn3Sn films (up to 100 nm) has already been reported in
the Al2O3(1-102)/Mn3Sn(11-20)/Pt stack, which is the same as this manuscript, and a similar tendency of SOT switching
ratio on the thickness has also been observed. [APL Mater. 11, 071116 (2023)] Moreover, there are several concerns
regarding their analysis. Therefore, several concerns and comments need to be addressed before it can be considered for
publication. 
(1) The authors claim that the Mn3Sn film is single crystal; however, the presence of higher-order XRD peaks is not direct
evidence for a single crystal. Furthermore, several other peaks, in addition to those of Al2O3 (1-102), (2-204), and (3-306),
are clearly observed in Fig. 1(d). The authors should identify all observed peaks and label them in the figure. 
(2) The authors claim that tensile stress at the Al2O3 interface induces a topological spin texture in Mn3Sn. Do the authors
have any XRD or TEM data to support tensile stress? 
(3) Can the authors exclude the possibility that the observed thickness dependence of spin transport arises from changes in
the crystallinity or magnetic properties of Mn3Sn with changing film thickness? I would recommend that the authors include
supplementary data, such as electrical conductivity, saturation magnetization, and anomalous Hall resistivity, for Mn3Sn
films with different thicknesses, and discuss their possible contribution in the manuscript. 
(4) The dimensions of the Hall bar device should be provided so that readers can estimate the current density. 
(5) Can the authors exclude the heating effect on the switching experiments? [Sci. Adv. 8, eabo5930 (2022).] 
(6) Could you add the details of the measurement sequence or reference for the “SOT tunable AHE measurements”
mentioned on line 215 in the main manuscript? Does this measurement mean the regular SOT switching? If so, please add
information on the external magnetic field and the applied current used in these measurements. In addition, if this
measurement corresponds to the SOT switching, the authors need to explain the difference between the amplitude of the
anomalous Hall resistance observed in the SOT switching shown in Fig. 2(c) and that in Fig. 3(e). 
(7) Is the SOT attenuation length depicted in Fig. 4 (a) truly calculated by using the equation described on line 229 in the
main manuscript? According to line 288, lambda_0 is 0.70 nm, and this value should correspond to the maximum value of
the SOT attenuation length because the value of beta times (theta_c)^2 is positive. In contrast, the maximum value in Fig.
4(a) is ~38 nm, indicating a significant deviation from the expected quantity. 
(8) On line 297, it is stated that “beta = 0.30 - 0.40 is fixed by matching the experimental peak at t ~ 40 nm,” whereas a value
of 0.47 is used in the main manuscript. Moreover, this statement indicates that beta is a free parameter to fit the experimental
data, which contradicts the claim that the model is parameter-free.

(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript presents an impressive study on the spin–orbit torque (SOT) driven switching of canted magnetic octupoles
in Mn
₃
Sn/Pt heterostructures. The authors convincingly demonstrate that perpendicular spin currents can reversibly
manipulate the magnetic order in antiferromagnetic Mn
₃
Sn films up to 60 nm thick — a remarkable distance far exceeding
conventional ferromagnetic limits. The observation of enhanced switching efficiency peaking at ~40 nm and its correlation
with twin topological spin structures provides strong evidence for an intrinsic long-range spin transport mechanism. 
The work is original, timely, and of broad significance to the spintronics community. It advances our understanding of spin
current propagation in non-collinear antiferromagnets and introduces a conceptually important link between octupole order,
spin–orbit torque, and topological spin textures. The combination of experimental verification and theoretical analysis is
thorough and compelling, and the results will inspire further exploration of topological spin transport phenomena. 
Overall, the manuscript is well written, the data are convincing, and the conclusions are well supported. I recommend
publication in Nature Communications after minor revisions, primarily to: 
Comments for the Authors 
1.Consistency of octupole dynamics at large thickness 
In the thicker regime (e.g., around 60 nm), are the switching dynamics and octupole motion consistent with those in thinner
samples? It would be useful to discuss whether the magnetic octupole dynamics remain coherent across the entire thickness
or if partial domain decoupling occurs at larger scales. 
2.Provide fitting parameters of atomic simulation 
Please include the quantitative fitting parameters used to extract the spin diffusion length and switching efficiency in the
main text or Supplementary Information. This will improve reproducibility and allow readers to better evaluate the proposed
thickness dependence. 
3.Generality across other non-collinear antiferromagnets 
The observed phenomenon is likely to be more universal. Please briefly discuss whether similar long-range SOT effects
could be expected in other non-collinear antiferromagnets such as Mn
₃
Ge or Mn
₃
Pt. This comparison would help generalize
the physical picture and emphasize the broader impact of your findings. 
4.Figure 1d revision and Figure 4 consistency 
In Fig. 1d, the blue dashed line partially overlaps the measured peak position, obscuring the experimental feature. Please
adjust the plotting to clearly distinguish the data peak from the guide line. The overall style and presentation of Fig. 4 differ
substantially from the other figures (color scheme, labeling, and scaling). It would be better to harmonize its format to
maintain visual and stylistic consistency throughout the paper. 
5.Several groups have previously reported electrical or SOT-driven manipulation of magnetic order in Mn
₃
Sn thin films,
typically finding reversible behavior up to thicknesses. The present observation of switching up to 60 nm (with a peak
efficiency near ≈40 nm) thus extends the accessible thickness range. We attribute these differences primarily to (i) the out-of-
plane 30° canting of the octupole moment realized in our epitaxial films, (ii) the use of an amorphous Pt overlayer that
provides strong interfacial spin–orbit coupling and efficient perpendicular spin-current injection, and (iii) twin topological spin
canting that supports long-range coherence. In contrast, prior reports employed which may limit spin coherence or reduce
interface transparency. You need to clarify whether the long-range switching reported here is intrinsic to Mn
₃
Sn or contingent
on sample-engineering choices.

Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
 
Xu et al. demonstrate that the topological antiferromagnetic Mn3Sn exhibits long-range spin 
transport up to 60 nm-thick via current-induced SOT-switching and DL-SOT efficiency 
analyses. They also proposed a theoretical model based on spin diffusion theory that 
incorporates the spin canting effect at the interface to reproduce the thickness dependence 
of the SOT switching ratio. Antiferromagnetic spintronics is a hot topic in the spintronics 
community, and these analyses are valuable for advancing this field. However, successful SOT 
switching in thicker Mn3Sn films (up to 100 nm) has already been reported in the Al2O3(1-
102)/Mn3Sn(11-20)/Pt stack, which is the same as this manuscript, and a similar tendency of 
SOT switching ratio on the thickness has also been observed. [APL Mater. 11, 071116 (2023)] 
Moreover, there are several concerns regarding their analysis. Therefore, several concerns 
and comments need to be addressed before it can be considered for publication. 
 
We thank the reviewer for the constructive comments and for pointing out the 
related work on current-induced SOT switching in thick Mn 3Sn films. We agree that 
successful SOT switching in Mn 3Sn layers with thicknesses up to ~100 nm has been 
previously reported. However, our work goes significantly beyond demonstrating SOT 
switching in thick films and addresses a fundamentally different physical mechanism. 
1, The key novelty of our work lies in revealing and quantitatively analyzing 
long-range spin transport mediated by magnetic octupole order in the topological 
antiferromagnet Mn3Sn. Previous studies primarily reported phenomenological SOT 
switching behaviors, whereas our work focuses on uncovering the microscopic origin 
of spin transport and torque generation. By systematically analyzing the thickness 
dependence of the damping-like SOT efficiency and switching ratio, we demonstrate 
that spin angular momentum can propagate over unusually long distances (~60 nm) 
through octupole-related spin channels, rather than conventional spin currents alone. 
2, To address concerns regarding the validity of our analysis, we have 
supplemented the experimental results with first-principles calculations and 
crystallographic characterizations . These results provide direct evidence for a 
topological interfacial spin texture and magnetic octupole moments at the Mn 3Sn /Pt 
interface. The ab initio calculations reveal finite octupolar spin polarization and 
interfacial spin canting consistent with the non-collinear antiferromagnetic symmetry 
of Mn3Sn, establishing a solid microscopic foundation for our theoretical model. 
3, We propose a  spin diffusion model that explicitly incorporates interfacial

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 5 -->

spin canting associated with magnetic octupole order , which is absent in previous 
reports. Importantly, the thickness dependence of the SOT switching ratio is reproduced 
quantitatively by our simulations without invoking phenomenological fitting 
parameters. The excellent agreement among experimental measurements, spin-
diffusion simulations, and first-principles calculations strongly supports the physical 
picture of octupole-mediated spin transport. 
4, Our work provides a  clear and unified physical mechanism for ultralong-
range SOT control in antiferromagnets , identifying magnetic octupoles as active 
carriers governing spin dynamics. This insight goes beyond earlier demonstrations of 
SOT switching and represents a conceptual advance for antiferromagnetic spintronics 
and multipolar spin transport. 
We hope this clarification addresses the reviewer’s concerns and highlights the 
distinct scope, originality, and significance of our work.

(3) Can the authors exclude the possibility that the observed thickness dependence of spin

transport arises from changes in the crystallinity or magnetic properties of Mn3Sn with

We appreciate the reviewer’s important suggestion. All Mn₃Sn films in this study 
were fabricated under identical growth and annealing conditions, ensuring highly 
consistent crystallinity and magnetic properties across different thicknesses. As shown 
in Fig. a, the (11-20) diffraction peaks of the 10 nm and 40 nm films are similar,

saturation magnetization, we believe that the observed thickness dependence of spin 
transport cannot be attributed to structural or magnetic variations. Nevertheless, 
following the reviewer’s recommendation, we have added the corresponding transport 
quantities in the Supplementary Information and included a brief discussion in the 
revised manuscript.
