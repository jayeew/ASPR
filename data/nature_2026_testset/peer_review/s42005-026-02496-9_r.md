Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In the manuscript entitled "Non-Hermitian skin effect without point-gap topology", the authors studied the interplay between
Anderson localization and non-Hermitian skin effect (NHSE) based on a quasicrystal system with nonreciprocal hopping.
They revealed that Anderson localization can cause the disappearance of point gaps under periodic boundary conditions
(PBC), while the non-Hermitian skin effect still persists under open boundary conditions (OBC). T In addition, I find some
other results quite interesting: in this 2D system, the degree of localization between different directions can transform into
each other, and the boundary conditions in the x-direction affect the NHSE in the y-direction. However, I also think the
authors' description of this part of the content is rather confusing. If the authors can present it clearly, I will recommend the
paper for publication. The specific comments are as follows: 
1. According to the authors' description, "When ty < e^(-|g|), eigenstates are localized in the y dimension while extended in
the x dimension, whereas they have opposite localization properties when ty > e^(-|g|)". Does this mean that bulk states are
localized only in one direction, and at the two sides of the PT-like-symmetry broken-unbroken boundary, the direction of
localization can switch (from y to x)? I consider this property quite interesting. However, based on the relevant descriptions in
the supplementary materials, it seems that only some bulk states exhibit this property. Thus, there appears to be a certain
degree of misunderstanding in the above description. The authors are requested to clarify this contradictory point and
characterize the variation of the localization degree of all bulk states in both the x and y dimensions with the relevant
parameters. 
2. In the paragraph starting at line 235, the authors state, "Then, the localization and energies of bulk states remain the same
when we turn the PBC into OBC in the y dimension (y-PBC into y-OBC)". Does this mean that the NHSE in the y-direction
only emerges when both directions are set to OBC, i.e., under y-OBC/x-PBC, bulk states are localized in the y-direction but
at different positions within the bulk? This is also very interesting. However, in the next paragraph describing the results
under y-OBC/x-PBC, under the introduced imaginary gauge transformation, it seems that all states appear again at the
boundary of the y-direction. Such contradictory descriptions confuse me. If the NHSE in the y-direction indeed only emerges
when both directions are set to OBC, could the authors add some clear numerical results, including calculating the
dynamical results under y-OBC/x-PBC, to compare whether the final states are extended in the y-direction compared to the
current results? 
3. In the manuscript, the authors repeatedly describe the changes in degeneracy under different boundary conditions. While
this is mathematically intuitive, what interesting physical consequences does this property entail? Is this property reflected in
dynamics? 
Furthermore, I would like to discuss the issue of the title with the authors: "Non-Hermitian skin effect without point-gap
topology". This title seems to pose a significant challenge to the non-Bloch energy band theory. However, the latter was
established in crystalline systems, while this work is conducted in a quasicrystal system. Those who hold such expectations
may feel a certain degree of disappointment when reading the article. Therefore, I am inclined to suggest adding information
about "incommensurate magnetic field" or "quasicrystal" to the title. I think this would not significantly reduce the appeal of
the article and would make it more accurate.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
First of all, this is a piece of work with considerable research value, addressing a scientific problem of significant interest. On
the other hand, the presentation of the manuscript suffers from serious shortcomings: overall, it is obscure and difficult to
follow and understand. Despite multiple attempts to carefully reread the manuscript paragraph by paragraph in order to
clarify the logic, I still found myself frequently stuck at key points, unable to accurately grasp the core meaning the authors
intended to convey. If the journal’s target readership is the general community within the field, the current version clearly
does not meet that standard. 
Constrained by the limited clarity of the exposition, I can only, based on my personal understanding, offer the following
analysis of the physical phenomena presented by the authors: 
1. On the case of full open boundary conditions (full OBC): 
This is the most accessible part of the manuscript. By introducing a similarity transformation, the authors successfully map
their constructed model onto a Hermitian system. According to the fundamental properties of Hermitian matrices, the energy
spectrum of such a system must be entirely real. At the same time, the mapping directly guarantees the presence of the non-
Hermitian skin effect (NHSE). The logic here is clear, and the conclusion straightforward to verify. 
2. On whether the topological origin of the NHSE is the point gap: 
To answer this central question, the most direct approach should be to carefully examine the key operation of the “similarity
transformation”—specifically, to analyze how the system’s topological properties and spectral characteristics evolve as the
boundary condition transitions from open (OBC) to periodic (PBC). Physically, this corresponds to a quasi-1D system (OBC
in the x-direction, PBC in the y-direction). Unfortunately, the authors’ discussion of this crucial transition is extremely brief,
and lacks the necessary detailed calculations and data support, making it difficult for readers to be convinced. 
From the supplementary material, one can tentatively observe that for any given hopping parameter $t_y$, the spectrum
appears to possess a nonzero spectral number. But does this phenomenon hold across all parameter regimes? If so, there
should be a clear correspondence between the NHSE and point-gap topology. 
3. On the spectral transition under full periodic boundary conditions (full PBC): 
Under full PBC, the system exhibits a striking spectral transition: prior to the MT transition, the spectrum changes from
complex (x-OBC, y-PBC) to real (full PBC). What is the physical mechanism behind this transition? How can this process be
theoretically explained? Furthermore, is this transition related to the system’s quasi-periodicity? These questions likewise
require further clarification from the authors. 
In addition to the above, two further issues deserve attention: 
1. When the parameter $\beta$ takes commensurate values, how do the system’s spectral characteristics, topological
properties, and the NHSE change? 
2.Are the various physical phenomena discussed in the manuscript (such as the real-complex spectral transition, NHSE,
and MT transition) intrinsically related to Anderson localization? If so, what mechanism underlies this connection? 
In summary, if the authors can provide detailed arguments and supplementary explanations on the above points in a revised
version, making the logical development more complete and the presentation more transparent, I would be very happy to
support publication of the paper. However, in its current form, due to the opacity of the exposition and the absence of crucial
details, I cannot recommend it for publication.

Comment #1.4
In the manuscript, the authors repeatedly describe the changes in degeneracy under diﬀerent boundary conditions.
While this is mathematically intuitive, what interesting physical consequences does this property entail? Is this property
reﬂected in dynamics?
Our reply:
The reason that we described the changes in degeneracy is that we have to understand why the eigenstates under
all OBCs show NHSE when ty < e  jgj, even though the spectrum is real without point-gap topology and eigenstates
are Anderson localized in the y dimension when under all PBCs. These two aspects seem to be contradictory, based
on the understanding of the non-Bloch band theory. Degeneracy indeed plays a central role in between. On the other
hand, the dynamics of the wave packet is governed by the Hamiltonian, which is a combination of spectrum and
eigenstates, not by spectrum or eigenstates solely. The wave packet dynamics is the same under various boundary
conditions, as long as the wave packet does not touch the boundaries. Thus, when ty < e  jgj the wave packet expands
only in the x dimension under various boundary conditions, before hitting the boundaries. Therefore, the degeneracy
itself is not directly reﬂected in the bulk dynamics.

Prior to the MT transition ( ty < e  jgj), the spectrum indeed changes from complex ( x-OBC, y-PBC) to real (full

symmetry breaking is diﬀerent for edge and bulk states, and it is not directly related to quasiperiodicity. In action,

Comment #3.6
2.Are the various physical phenomena discussed in the manuscript (such as the real-complex spectral transition,
NHSE, and MT transition) intrinsically related to Anderson localization? If so, what mechanism underlies this
connection?
Our reply:
All these physical phenomena are intrinsically related to Anderson localization. They do not exist in corresponding
periodic systems with commensurate β. With an incommensurate β, the system is quasiperiodic. The quasiperiodicity
acts as a quasirandom disorder, and induces directional Anderson localization of states. Under full PBCs, eigenstates
are Anderson localized in the y dimension while extended in the x dimension when ty < e  jgj. Whereas when
ty > e  jgj they are Anderson localized in the x dimension while extended in the y dimension. On the other hand,
the Hamiltonian of the system has MT symmetry. When ty < e  jgj, eigenstates, which are Anderson localized in the
y dimension while extended in the x dimension, are also eigenstates of the MT operator. Thus, the MT symmetry
is unbroken. Whereas when ty > e  jgj, eigenstates are Anderson localized in the x dimension while extended in
the y dimension. The localization direction changes and eigenstates are no longer eigenstates of the MT operator.
The MT symmetry is broken and the MT transition happens. The same as in the PT symmetry breaking, the
real-complex spectral transition is a manifestation of the symmetry breaking. Furthermore, under full OBCs, in the
normal NHSE, two plane waves with opposite momenta superpose to form a standing wave, which becomes a skin
state due to nonreciprocity. However, in our quasiperiodic system, states are directionally Anderson localized under
full PBCs. The open boundary in the x dimension forces these y-dimension-localized states in the degenerate subspace
to hybridize into extended states, which further turn into skin states by nonreciprocity. All the above-mentioned are
scattered throughout the manuscript, and we have added many details to clearly explain the mechanism.
