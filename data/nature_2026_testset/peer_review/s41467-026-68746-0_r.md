Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript reports on the experimental demonstration of Grover’s search algorithm implemented in a single trapped-ion
qudit, specifically within the d=5 and d=8 subspaces of a $^137$Ba$^+$ ion. The authors employ multi-tone control to
realize high-fidelity single-qudit operations and report algorithmic success probabilities and squared statistical overlap
(“classical fidelity”) that compare favorably with previous qubit-based demonstrations. Overall, the paper is clearly written,
the results are novel and technically sound, and the work represents an important step towards exploring the algorithmic
potential of high-dimensional quantum systems. 
That said, I believe several issues should be clarified before the manuscript can be considered for publication. 
Major comment 
The implementation does not involve any entangling gates. While this significantly simplifies the experimental sequence
and allows for competitive performance at small dimensions, it also introduces a fundamental limitation for scalability:
doubling the database size requires doubling the qudit dimension d, rather than simply adding an extra qubit to the register.
This makes it unclear to what extent the single-qudit approach can be scaled to larger problem sizes. The authors cite works
on theoretical proposals for multi-qudit Grover algorithms and experimental progress on qudit entangling gates, but it
remains ambiguous whether and how such approaches could be integrated into the present setup. I recommend that the
authors explicitly discuss the prospects and limitations of extending their architecture beyond a single qudit. This would
strengthen the broader impact of the paper. 
Minor comments 
1) The construction $|\bra{k}\sigma_x^k\ket{k+1}|$ in Eq. (5) seems to be redundant, since it is equal to unity by definition. It
might be clearer to omit this factor. 
2) In the Supplementary Material, the “Randomized Benchmarking” section mentions the use of single-qubit Clifford gates for
the d=5 and d=8 implementations. Does this imply that only the two lowest levels were used in RB? If not, further clarification
would be useful, as the present phrasing may confuse readers. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript describes an experimental demonstration of control over a trapped-ion qudit with up to eight computational
states, used to implement Grover's algorithm with an efficient mapping to qudit levels. Using multi-tone control, the authors
apply oscillating RF magnetic fields to coherently drive transitions within the 5D5/2 manifold of a single Ba137 ion. 
The addition of multiple energy levels and the associated increase in Hilbert space using native atomic states is an
intriguing route to expanding quantum computing capabilities without necessarily scaling up device size or control
infrastructure. This demonstration clearly illustrates the potential utility of the qudit paradigm: while the experiment itself is
relatively simple, it convincingly shows how higher-dimensional systems can enable hardware-efficient algorithm

implementations. These results will be of interest to the quantum information community and beyond, and the manuscript
has the potential to engage the broad readership of Nature Communications. Before the manuscript is recommended for
publication, we suggest that the authors consider and address the following points: 
1. The authors state that performance is limited by magnetic-field coherence. Could they comment on the dominant source of
magnetic noise in their system? For example, is it primarily from trap fields, or from ambient laboratory field fluctuations? A
brief discussion of whether standard passive shielding or active stabilization techniques could improve the coherence time
would be helpful, as this will inform readers about how far this limitation can be pushed in future experiments. 
2. It would be helpful to include a clearer description of the full SPAM sequence, either in the Methods or Supplementary
Information. This should include the specific 1762-nm transitions used for preparation and readout, how exactly inaccessible
states are reached via intermediate RF pi-pulses, and the measured SPAM fidelity. Clarifying more how “null” detection trials
are handled would also be useful. 
3. Although the current work focuses on a single qudit, readers would benefit from a brief discussion of how this approach
might scale to multi-qudit processors. For instance, will control be realized via local RF electrodes, global fields with
frequency selectivity, or optical Raman coupling? Given the large required bandwidth for d=8 how would neighboring qudits
be isolated to avoid cross-driving if on-chip RF electrodes are used? Even a high-level description of one possible future
architecture (or its suitability to an existing architecture) would help place this work in context for the broader community. 
4. The experiment currently demonstrates control of up to eight levels out of the 24 available in the manifold. It would be
valuable to discuss the prospects and trade-offs for utilizing even more levels in future experiments. For example, how would
increasing d affect pulse complexity, coherence, and susceptibility to off-resonant driving? 
5. More information on the calibration stages would be helpful in the supplementary or methods section. It would also be
interesting to note whether these calibrations are performed only once during gate design or need to be repeated
periodically due to drifts. 
6. At approximately ~7 G, the hyperfine and Zeeman manifolds in the D orbital are partially mixed. Please provide, in the
Supplementary Information, a table of the chosen eigenstates with their energies and compositions, along with the closest
spectator levels and detunings relevant for off-resonant coupling. This would make it easier for interested readers to
understand potential sources of leakage and error. 
7. The note “We note that error correction is not employed in these implementations and higher fidelity should be possible” is
a little vague. We suggest that the authors either provide a specific reference or example of the code they have in mind for
such a qudit system, or simply remove this statement. 
8. There is a typo in the beginning of the Discussion that appears to swap the measured coherence times for d=5 and d=8;
please align with Fig. S3. Another one is in the word Hadamard in Fig. 2. We encourage the authors to carefully review the
manuscript for similar minor errors and inconsistencies. 
9. Can the authors provide more information regarding Fig. S2? In particular, it would be helpful to include the explicit
expression of the drive (currently described only implicitly in words), clarify whether the plots are based on theory or
experiment, and explain in detail what the four different subplots in panel (a) represent, including the specific parameters
being varied. 
10. In Fig. 1b and 1c, it would be useful to explicitly note that the two panels depict the same underlying spectrum, with
different sets of allowed transitions highlighted for d=5 and d=8. 
11. The discussion of Fig. 2 should provide more detail on how the optimal circuit decompositions are chosen, and all
relevant parameters and symbols in the figure (e.g., the displacement operators) should be defined in the caption. 
In conclusion, this is a timely and accessible manuscript that will interest a broad segment of the quantum information
community. We will consider recommending publication in Nature Communications after the authors address the points
raised above.

Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
We thank the authors for their responses to our concerns. Overall, they have addressed most of the questions raised in the
initial review, and the manuscript in its current form is now suitable for publication in Nature Communications. 
That said, the manuscript might benefit from a more nuanced discussion of how their methods extend to use of multiple
Zeeman qudits given the large bandwidth per qubit required for such operation (e.g., would the magnetic field addressing
vary quickly in space?), as well as expanding the discussion on the ability to use larger qubit dimension in their setup if more
complex control is available.
