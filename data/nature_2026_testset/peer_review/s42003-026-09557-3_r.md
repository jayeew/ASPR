Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This study reports, for the first time, a high-quality telomere-to-telomere (T2T) genome assembly of the Chinese indigenous
pig breed, Neijiang pig. Based on this assembly, several in-depth biological analyses were conducted, including centromere
characterization, comparative genomics, population genetic diversity, genome-wide association studies (GWAS) on
reproductive traits, and investigations into the genetic basis of head morphology. 
The manuscript demonstrates a certain degree of novelty and scientific significance. The T2T genome assembly provides
valuable reference data for research in pig genomics. As a local breed with significant historical value and rich genetic
diversity, the T2T genome of the Neijiang pig offers a precious resource for studies in pig genomics, domestication and
breeding, as well as breed conservation. The authors utilized advanced sequencing technologies and computational
methods to achieve an impressive genome assembly quality. The subsequent biological analyses also yield valuable
insights into the genetic characteristics and domestication history of pigs. 
Although I believe the manuscript presents substantial content and may interest the readership of Communications Biology,
there is still room for improvement before publication. In particular, enhancements are needed in data presentation, the depth
of result interpretation, the breadth of discussion, and certain methodological details. I recommend minor revision to further
refine the scientific narrative and presentation.

Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This study presents a high-quality, nearly gap-free NJP-T2T genome assembly for the Neijiang pig, integrating PacBio HiFi
reads, Nanopore ultra-long reads, and Hi-C sequencing data. The authors conducted comprehensive genome quality
assessment and annotation, followed by population genetic analyses, including genetic structure, inbreeding, GWAS, and
selection signatures, leveraging the assembled genome. This work establishes a foundational genomic resource for
Neijiang pig research and offers valuable insights for future swine genetic improvement. However, several issues require
further examination and revision to ensure robustness. Specific concerns are detailed below. 
1.In the selection analysis section, you primarily investigated selection signals between Shizitou and Erfangtou individuals.
However, the phenotypic description is inconsistent, with some instances referring to “head morphology” and others to
“craniofacial morphology”, which represent distinct traits. Please ensure the terminology is unified throughout the manuscript
for clarity and precision. 
2.The font sizes in all figures are currently too small to be clearly legible, and some figures lack proper x- or y-axis labels.
Please optimize all figures by increasing font sizes and ensuring complete labeling to improve readability and interpretation. 
3.In line 380, it is stated that "The final genome size was 2606.97 Mb." It is recommended to present the genome size in GB
for clarity. Additionally, the discussion section could address why the genome size of NJP-T2T is smaller than that of
T2T_Mpig1.0, T2T_RCpig1.0, and JH-T2T. 
4.In the NJP-T2T genome annotation section, have any new genomic features been identified, such as novel genes that are
missing in the Sscrofa11.1 reference genome? 
5.The NJP-T2T assembly was extended by 215.22 Mb in autosomes and the X chromosome compared to Sscrofa11.1."
What specific information does this additional 215.22 Mb provide, and what are the sequence characteristics? Are there
repetitive sequences? 
6.In Table 1, it is recommended to include a comparison with the existing T2T pig genomes. 
7.The low-coverage sequencing data from 115 individuals has an imputation accuracy of 0.85, which is relatively low. You
should specify the breed and sample size of your imputation panel in the manuscript. 
8.In the variant detection section, what advantages does the NJP-T2T genome have over Sscrofa11.1, and does it allow for
an increased number of detected variants? 
9.Overall, the T2T assembly work is excellent, but I have concerns regarding the population genetics analysis. Is it
appropriate to conduct GWAS with only 115 individuals? The statistical power seems insufficient, which could lead to false
positives. Would it be more effective to analyze extreme groups instead? Have the significant loci for reproductive traits been
identified in GWAS from other public pig populations, or are they linked to known reproductive trait loci? Additionally, do they
overlap with QTL regions for reproductive traits in pigs? 
10.The same issue applies to the selection analysis section; with only 5 SZT and 3 EFT samples, the sample size is too
small. Population stratification or genetic drift may be misinterpreted as selection signals, and there is a lack of appropriate
experimental validation for the selected genes.

5. The NJP-T2T assembly was extended by 215.22 Mb in autosomes and the 
X chromosome compared to Sscrofa11.1." What specific information does this 
additional 215.22 Mb provide, and what are the sequence characteristics? Are

A: Our initial estimation of approximately 215.22 Mb of additional sequence was 
derived by subtracting the length of Sscrofa11.1 from that of NJP-T2T; however, 
this approach has limited precision. To more ac curately address this, we 
redefined the additional regions based on previously unresolved regions 
(PURs), determining a refined total length of 181.35 Mb. These sequences are 
primarily located near centromeres and other structurally complex 
chromosomal regions, which had previously been difficult to assemble due to 
high repetitiveness and complexity. This study represents the first complete

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 19 -->

resolution of these regions. Sequence characterization revealed that 
approximately 43.21% (78.37 Mb) of the PURs consi st of centromeric satellite 
sequences, 14.21% (25.77 Mb) are large segmental duplications (SDs), 13.26%

duplications, and the remaining 25.81% (46.81 Mb) are unclassified sequences.

highly repetitive regions, while a substantial portion remains unannotated, 
suggesting the presence of novel structural sequences or underexplored repeat 
elements. Overall, this 181.35 Mb of previously unresolved sequence not only 
fills genomic gaps but also provides critical resources for studying pig 
chromosome structure and functional elements, particularl y centromeric
