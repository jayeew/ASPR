Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this study, the authors developed a novel dual-biomarker response platform based on nucleic acid logic gates targeting
miR-21 and lncRNA MALAT1. This platform leverages rolling circle amplification technology and incorporates two critical
components: a primer switch and a padlock probe switch—thereby ensuring high diagnostic accuracy. This method enables
precise discrimination among various tumor cell types according to the expression levels of miR-21 and MALAT1, with the
dual-RNA response assay achieving an AUC value of 0.92, which underscores its potential for clinical application.
Nevertheless, several concerns remain that must be addressed. Therefore, it is recommended that the manuscript undergo
major revisions prior to publication in Communications Chemistry. 
Several concerns are outlined below: 
1.The detection performance of low-abundance samples needs to be further validated: The standard curve covers 0.1 - 10³
nM, but the target in clinical samples may be lower than 0.1 nM. The current data cannot confirm the platform's detection
capability for extremely low-abundance targets. It is recommended to supplement low-concentration gradient experiments to
determine the detection limit and ensure it meets the requirements for early diagnosis. 
2.The study exclusively validated lung cancer cell lines and lysed lung cancer specimens, without encompassing non-
invasive samples such as blood and body fluids (e.g., circulating RNA), which are pivotal for early non-invasive diagnostics.
It is recommended to augment the dataset with diverse clinical samples, including serum and pleural effusion, to address
this critical need. 
3.The clinical performance has not been compared against established clinical gold standards. While the evaluation
included a comparison with conventional single-target nucleic acid testing, it did not include validation against reference
methods such as histopathological analysis or approved molecular diagnostic kits, which are considered standard
benchmarks in clinical practice. It is recommended to supplement the efficacy data through comparative studies with these
gold standards, including key performance metrics such as sensitivity, specificity, and positive predictive value. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript presents a dual-biomarker nucleic acid logic gate platform that integrates responses to both microRNA-21
(miR-21) and lncRNA MALAT1, enabling improved diagnostic sensitivity and specificity for cancer detection. The approach,
combining primer-switch and padlock probe elements within a rolling circle amplification (RCA) framework, is innovative and
relevant to the field of molecular diagnostics and biosensing. Overall, the work is scientifically interesting and potentially
impactful, as it advances the application of multi-input logic systems in nucleic acid-based detection. However, before the
manuscript can be considered for publication, several important issues must be addressed to strengthen the data
presentation, improve clarity, and better support the conclusions. For this reason, I recommend the major revision of the
paper. Addressing the points below will substantially improve the quality and credibility of the work. My specific comments
are detailed below: 
1) Validation of dual recognition mechanism (Figure 1a): The authors should expand the data shown in Figure 1a to more
comprehensively demonstrate the dual-binding mechanism. Specifically, additional PAGE combinations should be
included, such as: 
• Padlock + MALAT1 
• Hairpin probe (HP) + miR-21

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 2 -->

• Padlock + miR-21 
• HP + MALAT1 
These controls are essential to fully validate the specificity and cooperative action of the two nucleic acid inputs. 
2) References for the TMB chromogenic assay (Section 3.4): The statement “In the UV-Vis analysis, the TMB chromogenic
assay evaluated the HRP-mimetic activity of the RCA products…” should be supported by appropriate references. Since this
is a widely used detection technique, citing 2–3 representative studies will reinforce the methodological rigor. 
3) Description of mismatch and control targets (Section 3.5): The sequences of the mismatch or control targets should be
clearly described in both the text and figure. In addition, the rationale for their design should be provided (e.g., whether they
correspond to similar mRNA sequences that could realistically be found in biological samples). This clarification is important
for assessing assay specificity. 
4) Clarification of axis labeling in Figure 3d: The appearance of a “negative infinite” value on the X-axis is confusing. If this
arises from the logarithmic representation of a zero-concentration ratio, the authors should correct this or reformat the data to
avoid implying an unrealistic scenario. The current scale may mislead readers regarding physiological conditions. 
5) Clinical translation and applicability: The Discussion or Conclusions should include remarks on the potential for clinical
translation. Specifically, can this dual-biomarker RCA platform be adapted for point-of-care applications using miniaturized
electrochemical or optical devices? A brief discussion on practicality, challenges, and future directions would greatly
strengthen the manuscript’s impact. 
6) Figure 3 readability and axis clarity: Panel (d) in Figure 3 is not easy to interpret. I recommend adding a secondary X-axis
showing the molar concentrations corresponding to the logarithmic ratios. This will help readers understand the clinically
relevant range covered by the sensing platform. 
7) Error calculation and data reproducibility: The manuscript should specify how error bars were determined. Do they
represent standard deviations? If so, how many independent experiments, electrodes, or sensing replicates were used to
estimate these values? Clarifying this will improve transparency and reproducibility. 
8) Comparison between optical and electrochemical readouts: Since the platform can support both optical and
electrochemical detection, the authors should provide calibration curves for each modality and discuss any differences in
sensitivity or detection limits. This would highlight the platform’s versatility and analytical robustness.

I would like to express my sincere appreciation for the authors' thorough revision of the manuscript and for the hard work
they put into conducting the additional experiments. The quality of the work is evident, and the manuscript has improved
significantly. While I am satisfied with the responses to most of the previous comments, one final point regarding the
statistical presentation remains unclear, and this needs to be clarified to ensure the method is robust. 
Specifically, the statement that 'error bars indicate standard deviations based on three independent measurements' suggests
that the data may rely on instrumental replicates (e.g. three scans on a single electrode) rather than true experimental
replicates. To demonstrate the reproducibility required for publication in this journal, it is important to show that the assay
performs consistently across different fabrication batches. 
Therefore, I kindly request that the authors demonstrate that the reported error accounts for inter-electrode and inter-well
variability (using at least three distinct electrodes and wells). I would like to emphasise that it is not necessary to repeat the
entire dataset. I am only requesting that this reproducibility be demonstrated for the most significant experiments, such as the
main calibration curves or the primary detection assay. If the authors can provide this confirmation for the key data points, I
will be happy to recommend immediate acceptance of the work.
