General Assessment 
This study presents a novel pH-responsive ratiometric photoacoustic sensor (PPS) for real-time monitoring of tumour
microenvironment (TME) acidification in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). The authors integrate bioinformatics, molecular
imaging, and therapeutic intervention to demonstrate the role of ATP6V0C in TME acidification and its implications for
tumour progression and metastasis. The work is well-structured, methodologically rigorous, and clinically relevant. However,
certain aspects require clarification and additional validation to strengthen the claims. 
Strengths 
1. Innovative Imaging Tool: 
The development of PPS for pH-sensitive photoacoustic imaging is a significant technical advance, enabling non-invasive,
high-resolution monitoring of TME acidification. 
The dual-wavelength ratio-metric approach enhances specificity and minimizes background interference with potential to
influence HCC diagnosis and targeted therapy. 
2. Mechanistic Insights: 
Strong bioinformatics and clinical validation linking ATP6V0C overexpression to HCC progression and poor prognosis. 
Molecular docking and functional assays convincingly demonstrate the interaction between esomeprazole (ESOM) and
ATP6V0C, supporting the therapeutic rationale. 
3. Multimodal Validation: 
The study employs multiple models (subcutaneous, orthotopic, metastatic) and human specimens to validate PPS utility. 
Correlative analyses (hypoxia, angiogenesis) strengthen the biological relevance of TME acidification. 
4. Therapeutic Implications: 
The combination of PPS-mediated photothermal therapy (PTT) and ESOM provides a synergistic anti-tumour effect,
supported by RNA-seq and single-cell transcriptomics.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The study by Zeng et al. reports the development of a real-time imaging protocol to monitor acidity in time in the context of
hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). In particular, the authors identified ATP6V0C as an important factor for acidification and
levels of ATP6V0C correlated with acidification and poor prognosis. Therefore they designed a sensor for tumor detection
and targeted HCC therapy. 
The manuscript is well written, the results convincing and the conclusions are supported by the numerous data shown in the
manuscript figures and in the supplemental material. 
Major point: 
Discussion is quite short and it seems more a summary of the data presented in the results section. Indeed, data are not
discussed critically comparing them with literature data (in the discussion there is not even a single citation). Moreover, even
suggestions about the use of these sensors together with other therapeutical means remain general and superficial without
details. 
I believe that the authors should discuss their data throughly, comparing their sensors with others and underlining the
advantages of their sensors together with their limitations. In addition, they should discuss throughy their applications in
combination with defined therapeutical means. 
Minor points 
Figure 1 is not cited in the text. I believe that it is a sort of graphical abstract of the entire manuscript but it is named figure 1,
and should be cited in the text. Moreover, the figure legend of this figure should be more informative giving details on the
proposed ways to use the sensor developed, in particular because it is the first figure that one encounters even before
reading results. 
Reviewer #4 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The article presents a highly innovative approach to detecting and monitoring the acidity of the tumor microenvironment
(TME) during tumor progression and metastasis using a pH-responsive ratiometric photoacoustic sensor (PPS). The study
combines cutting-edge photoacoustic imaging technology with the molecular targeting of ATP6V0C, a subunit of V-ATPase,

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 3 -->

which plays a crucial role in TME acidification. The results show that the sensor can effectively visualize spatiotemporal
changes in tumor acidity, providing real-time monitoring of TME dynamics in vivo. Additionally, the article explores the
potential therapeutic implications of combining the PPS with proton pump inhibitors, offering a novel approach to cancer
therapy. Overall, the paper is well-written, presenting significant advancements in both diagnostic and therapeutic strategies
in cancer research. The experimental design is robust, and the use of in vitro, in vivo, and clinical samples strengthens the
findings. However, there are a few areas where clarification and further explanation are needed. 
1. i) Clarification on the Sensitivity Range of PPS: The manuscript indicates that PPS is sensitive to pH changes within the
physiological range (pH 8-6). Could you provide more detailed information about the sensor's performance outside this
range, particularly in more extreme acidic conditions that may occur in advanced tumors? 
ii) Specificity and Sensitivity of PPS. The study shows that PPS exhibits significant photoacoustic signal enhancement
under acidic conditions. However, in complex in vivo environments, how do the authors rule out interference from other
factors (e.g., inflammation or local ischemia) that may also alter pH? Are there experimental data supporting PPS's
specificity in non-tumor acidic conditions? 
2. PPS Application in Different Cancer Types: While the study focuses on hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), it would be
valuable to discuss the potential applicability of PPS in other types of cancers with different TME characteristics. Are there
any limitations in the current approach for other cancer types, such as those with lower acidity or different metabolic profiles?
3. In Vivo Imaging Depth: The study demonstrates the ability to detect PPS signals at depths of up to 15 mm in tissues.
However, this depth is quite limited in clinical settings, particularly for larger tumors. how does imaging quality and
quantitative accuracy perform in even deeper tissues (e.g., visceral tumors)? Are there supporting data? 
4. Mechanism of Synergistic Photothermal Therapy. The study suggests that combining PPS with the proton pump inhibitor
(PPI) esomeprazole (ESOM) synergistically suppresses tumor growth, but the underlying molecular mechanism is not
thoroughly explored. Are there data showing whether ESOM directly enhances PPS's photothermal effect by inhibiting
ATP6V0C, or do the two act independently? 
5. Reproducibility and Generalizability. The study employs multiple tumor models (e.g., subcutaneous, orthotopic, and
metastatic), but are there performance differences in PPS across these models? Do the authors plan to validate PPS's
generalizability in other cancer types? Additionally, some experiments have small sample sizes (e.g., n=3)—could this affect
statistical power?
