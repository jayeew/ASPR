Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Review of “A role for condensin-mediator interaction in mitotic chromosomal organization” by Iwasaki et al. 
Condensin-mediated genomic contacts organize eukaryotic genomes into 3D chromosomal structures or domains, which
are crucial for accurate mitotic chromosome segregation. However, the specific identities and functions of the condensin
loading mechanisms involved in forming these domains and their boundaries are still unclear. In this manuscript, the authors
reveal a new interaction between the Cnd1 condensin and Pmc4 mediator subunits in S. pombe. Through a combination of
genetic mutation analysis, Hi-C interaction data, and DNA-FISH techniques, they demonstrate that this condensin-mediator
interaction is essential for recruiting condensin to highly transcribed and mitotically active genes at the boundaries of
condensin domains. Additionally, mediator-driven transcription facilitates the formation of mitotic chromatin domain
boundaries through phase separation. 
Critique: Overall, the study is well-designed, featuring convincingly controlled experiments and high-quality results. A key
finding is the identification of a separation-of-function mutation, cnd1-K658E, which specifically disrupts its interaction with
the mediator subunit. This impairment leads to diminished condensin-mediated chromatin domains and defects in
chromosome segregation, underscoring the functional role of chromosomal architecture during mitosis. Although the results
generally support the conclusions, there are some inconsistencies between the Hi-C and DNA-FISH analyses (see below).
Addressing these discrepancies and incorporating a discussion on them would greatly enhance the study's impact and its
suitability for publication in Nature Communications. 
Specific comments 
1) Previous studies indicate that RNA pol II antagonizes condensin activity (PMID: 38446663) and that the mediator subunit
nut2 (med10 in S. pombe) is a suppressor of the condensin cut3 mutant (PMID: 24362309). It would be important for the
authors discuss these findings in relation to their results and cite the papers to provide a comprehensive background.
Additionally, since these previous studies show that chromosome segregation defects in the condensin mutants were
rescued in mediator and Pol II subunit mutants, could the authors perform Hi-C experiment in cells depleted for Pmc4 and
Cnd1 together to see if the organizational defects of Pmc4 are also rescued? 
2) Using Hi-C analysis, the authors conclude that Pmc4 depletion enhances genomic contacts between two adjacent
condensin domains (Fig. 3f-j). However, the DNA-FISH results in Fig. 4c do not support this conclusion, as they show that
the distances between the two condensin domains are significantly extended in Pmc4-depleted cells. Could the authors
clarify this discrepancy and design a relevant DNA-FISH experiment to support their Hi-C data? 
Related comment: In Extended Data Fig. 4, the authors designed two sets of FISH probes to visualize paired genomic loci,
either within the same domain or spanning different domains. A more informative experiment would be to measure intra-
domain and inter-domain distances relative to a single probe. For example, the authors could use probes A, B, and C, where
A and B are intra-domain, and C is in an adjacent domain, with both A and C equidistant from B. 
3) Using a set of phase separation-related experiments, the authors conclude that mediator-related condensates assist in the
formation of condensin-mediated chromatin domains. However, the current results seem incoherent and potentially
exaggerated. The Hi-C contact maps with 1,6-hexanediol (HD) treatment (Fig. 6c-f) look very different from those in Pmc4-

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 2 -->

depleted cells (Fig. 3f-j). Furthermore, distances between the two condensin domains were significantly extended in Pmc4-
depleted cells (Fig. 4c) but shortened in HD-treated cells (Fig. 6g). Could the authors comment on and clarify these
discrepancies? It would also be interesting to see what happens to Pmc4 bodies in the cnd1 mutant. 
4) The authors conclude that mediator-driven transcription promotes boundary formation of mitotic chromatin domains.
However, the Hi-C contact maps of Pmc4-depleted cells are significantly weaker than those observed in Cnd1-depleted
cells, even though both were synchronous cultures. Could the authors discuss this further? 
Minor comments 
1) It is interesting to note the increase in the cross-like pattern in Cnd1-depleted cells, suggesting a high frequency of
interactions between centromere-proximal regions in the absence of condensin. Do the authors know if condensin depletion
affects heterochromatin at the pericentromeres? 
2) Could the authors provide a Western blot or microscopy analysis to support the degradation of the tagged proteins in the
degron experiments? 
3) Supporting the current data in Fig. 5 with Cnd1-Pk ChIP enrichments in Pmc4-depleted cells could be crucial, as their
direct interaction is a significant theme of the paper. 
4) The discussion section could be improved by citing relevant papers to provide a comprehensive background. 
5) The text in the results section for Fig. 4 and Extended Fig. 4 is confusing and incoherent in its current form. It requires
modification for clarity.

(Remarks to the Author) 
The condensin complex plays a vital role in the 3D chromosome organization and chromosomal segregation during mitosis.
The authors identified the interaction between the Cnd1 condensin and the mediator subunit Pmc4 in fission yeast,
Schizosaccharomyces pombe. They further identified the cnd1-K658E point mutation which disrupts the interaction, leading
to diminished condensin-mediated chromatin domains during mitosis and chromosomal segregation defects. They also
demonstrated that the condensing-mediator interaction is important for recruiting condensin to highly transcribed genes and
mitotically activated genes. Finally, the study suggests that phase separation may be involved in the formation of mitotic
chromosomal architecture. The manuscript is well organized, and the results are interesting. The experiments are generally
well designed and executed. However, there are some concerns as listed below: 
Major points: 
1. In the section “Protein interaction between the Cnd1 condensin and Pmc4 mediator subunits”, the authors found that the
condensin subunit Cnd1 interacts with Pmc4. But in the next section “Co-localization of condensin and mediator at gene
regions”, they used Cut14 instead of Cnd1. Why the authors changed a different condensin subunit for this experiment is not
clear. 
2. In Fig 6b, the immunofluorescence (IF) microscopy images showed that the condensin subunit Cnd2 does not significantly
overlap with Pmc4, which seems contradictory with the results of Fig. 1d. Also, for Fig 6b, why the Cnd1 or Cut14 subunit
was not used for the IF? 
3. In Fig. 1a, the authors test the interaction of partial and full-length Pmc4 with Cnd1. Why did the authors test 10-239 aa?
Please explain it. 
Also, please add a positive control and negative control for the Y2H assay in Fig. 1a&2a. In addition, the authors didn’t
provide the loading control result in Fig. 2a. 
4. To study the Cnd1-K658Emutant effect in vivo, the authors depleted endogenous Cnd1 by AID and then expressed the
mutant version by the pREP81 plasmid. However, the expression level of proteins by pREP81 plasmid, which uses a weak
nmt1 promoter, could be very different from the native ones. 
Another drawback with the nmt promoter is that induction of
protein expression is rather slow and it takes several generations to achieve full activation. Also, the native proteins may not
be totally removed by the AID system. Since the authors showed that Cnd1-K658Emutant is viable, using the mutant at the
native site with its own promoter will be much better.

5. The phase separation of Pmc4 is not convincing. For in vitro phase separation experiments, FRAP assays are needed to
further demonstrate that Pmc4 has phase separation property. Also, can the in vitro Pmc4 droplets move and fuse together?
In addition, did deletion of the disordered domain disrupt the droplet formation? 
For in vivo phase separation experiments, 1,6-hexanediol has been shown to have many side effects; FRAP assays are

the distribution pattern of Pmc4 in vivo? 
Do the in vivo Pmc4 spherical spots move and fuse together? 
6. The authors did many ChIP-seq. This reviewer suggest doing some ChIP-qPCR to verify the ChIP-seq results. 
7. The authors showed that the cnd1-K658E mutation is sufficient to cause chromosomal segregation defects. How is the

2. Page 12, What is the meaning of IF? Please clarify it. 
3. Page 8: “we depleted endogenous Cnd1 expression by inhibiting its transcription...”. AID system depletes endogenous
protein by protein degradation, not “by inhibiting its transcription”.

(Remarks to the Author) 
Comments to authors (NCOMMS-24-44284-T) 
During each cell cycle of all eukaryotes, interphase chromosome is highly condensed by a pentameric condensin complex
composed of 2 SMC ATPases, a kleisin and 2 HEAT-repeat proteins to survive sister-chromatid separation and faithfully
transmit genetic information to daughter cells. The failure to form structured mitotic chromosomes by condensin subunit
mutation causes profound segregation defects like cut+ phenotype in fission yeast. At early mitosis of fission yeast, the
cytoplasmic condensin is transported to nucleus and loaded to heterochromatin like centromere and rDNA and chromosome
arms but the precise mechanism which specifies condensin loading sites is poorly understood. In this manuscript, Iwasaki et
al. focuses on how transcription-associated mediator complex contributes to condensin loading and formation of highly
ordered mitotic chromosomal structure. Using Y2H screening method, they identified mediator Pmc4, which interacts with
HEAT repeat protein Cnd1. By the ChIP-seq analyses, they showed that condensin subunit and Pmc4 localize to RNA PolII-
transcribed genes and RNA PolIII-transcribed genes. They also screened and identified cnd1-K658E mutant which disrupts
Cnd1-Pmc4 interaction while maintaining Cnd1-Cnd2 interaction. Using the Hi-C analyses and FISH microscopy, they
showed that Cnd1-Pmc4 interaction is important for the regulation of genomic contacts in condensin domains. By comparing
ChIP-seq during mitosis and nascent RNA-seq, they showed that condensin localization is highly correlated with gene
expression. Finally, they showed that Pmc4 is phase separated and they also suggest that phase separation is involved in
formation of mitotic chromatin domains. These results show broad interest considering the high conservation of these
condensin complex and mediators in other eukaryotes including human. 
Comments: 
1. Even Cnd1 is the binding protein of Pmc4, Cnd1 ChIP-seq is never tested in pmc4 depletion mutant. Instead, ChIP-seq of
Cut14 (Fig. 1d) and Cnd2 (Fig. 5a) were tested in pmc4 depletion mutant. Authors should show the interaction of Pmc4 with
other members of condensin like Cut14 or Cnd2 prior to Cut14 and Cnd2 ChIP-seq in pmc4 depletion mutant even if it is
highly predicted because of tight condensin complex formation. 
2. In Fig. 2a-d, can they conclude that phenotype of cnd1-K658E can be caused by abolishment of Cnd1-Pmc4 interaction
only? This can be structural change which can affect condensin function and pmc4 interaction without affecting Cnd1-Cnd2
interaction. Why authors didn’t mutate endogenous cnd1 or cnd2 genes by site-directed mutagenesis (Fig. 2 & 5)? Gene

expression using plasmid can have higher expression level than endogenous protein and the phenotype can be
exaggerated. Alternatively, the expression of plasmid based expressed Cnd1 or Cnd2 proteins should be compared to
endogenous Cnd1 or Cnd2 proteins. 
3. The authors should show septation index to show the progression of mitosis after cdc25-22 arrest and release for Hi-C or
ChIP-seq. The progression can be different in different mutant like condensin and the weak signal of condensin binding can
be seen by picking up off-site cell cycle. 
4. What is the meaning of condensin binding to highly transcribed region? They should provide reasonable speculation
about the function of Condensin on highly transcribed region for mitotic chromosomal structure formation. The pattern of
condensin binding to chromatin during mitosis and interphase is very similar (Fig. 1 and 5) even the intensity during
interphase is weaker. Highly transcribed genes are still active during mitosis or bookmarked during mitosis? 
5. 1,6-hexandiol (HD) can disrupt hydrophobic protein-protein and protein-RNA interactions and can have diverse effects in
cells including phase separation. Authors should not conclude that the defect of mitotic chromosome structure by HD
treatment is caused by only phase separation effect. It will be more specific if they test chromosomal structure change by
mutating IDR of Pmc4. 
6. Untag strain is needed for ChIP-seq analyses (Fig. 1d & Fig. 5a-b). It is commonly noticed that even untag strain has
strong signal in highly transcribed genic regions when ChIP is carried out especially with antibody against tag. To exclude
this possibility, untag strain should be analyzed together. 
7. To conclude conservation of mediation-condensin for chromosomal organization in human U2OS cells (Extended Data
Fig. 6f, g), they should also try to deplete Med4 to see chromosomal segregation defects in human cells.
