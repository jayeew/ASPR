Reviewers' comments:
 
Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
The paper presents a study on the significantly improved ductility in a high-strength dual-phase 19Al-20Fe-20Co-41Ni high-
entropy alloy via weakening the Kurdjumov-Sachs (K-S) orientation relationship (OR) between the L12 and B2 phases
through moderate plastic deformation and recrystallization. The detailed study shows that the threefold increase in ductility
compared to the as-cast alloy is attributed to a B2→body-centered tetragonal (BCT) transformation, promoted by weakening
the interface OR. In situ synchrotron tensile testing is also employed to confirm the progressive phase transformation. The
work is interesting and meaningful, highlighting the critical role of OR in governing phase transformation and proving
important insights into the development of ductile-strong alloys. The paper can be accepted for publication after addressing
several minor concerns as commented on below. 
In Fig. 3c, the data can be plotted into diagrams instead of a “table”, so that it can fit better to the main figure. 
Regarding the ordered structures in the initial alloy prior to deformation, are there possibilities for the coexistence of
disordered FCC and ordered L12 phases in the claimed L12 regions, as well as disordered BCC and ordered B2 phases in
the claimed B2 regions? The current TEM and SXRD results may hard to exclude such possibilities. Also, how about the
evolution of the ordering features during deformation? Would be nice to clarify more on this point. 
Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author): 
This is a very comprehensive work on the role of interfaces in dual phase alloys and strategies to modify interfaces to
improve the mechanical properties. A remarkable three-fold increase in ductility while maintaining yield strength has been
documented in near-eutectic 19Al-20Fe-20Co-41Ni alloy. This alloys a mixture of L12 and B2 phases showing the classic
Kurdjumov-Sachs (K-S) OR between the L12 and B2 phases. The authors have a novel approach of weakening the K-S
orientation relation through plastic deformation and recrystallization. The plastic deformation produces B2-phase to body-
centered tetragonal (BCT) phase transformation that produces substantial improvements in mechanical properties. A
particularly appealing aspect of this study is the use on in-situ synchrotron x-ray diffraction study to demonstrate B2-BCT
transformation with increasing strain confirming the central hypothesis of this work. This paper will be of significant interest to
a broad audience of communications materials, and I recommend this manuscript for publication. 
Reviewer #3 (Remarks to the Author): 
The manuscript presents an interesting study on a near eutectic dual phase HEA and proposes that thermomechanical
processing weakens the L12/B2 orientation relationship, enabling a stress induced B2 to BCT transformation that markedly
improves ductility while maintaining high strength. The topic is timely and relevant, and the combination of mechanical
testing with synchrotron XRD and EBSD provides useful insight into the deformation behavior of this alloy system. While the
overall idea is appealing, several aspects of the microstructural evidence, particularly the statistical robustness of the EBSD
data, the interpretation of orientation relationships, and the explanation for the contrasting behavior between the cast and
processed conditions, require clarification or additional support. Addressing these points will help strengthen the central
conclusions and improve confidence in the proposed mechanism. Below are my specific comments: 
1. Please provide larger-area IPF and phase maps for both the NE-cast and NE-T conditions. The EBSD/phase map for the
cast condition in Fig. 1c appears to contain relatively few grains and may not be representative of the bulk microstructure.
Showing large-area IPF + phase maps (or multiple maps from different regions) will increase confidence that the reported
texture and phase statistics are representative. 
2. The manuscript focuses on weakening of the Kurdjumov–Sachs (K–S) orientation relationship, but FCC/BCC interfaces
can also adopt Nishiyama–Wassermann (N–W) variants. The authors should discuss the possibility of N–W ORs and
explicitly show larger-area IPFs and pole figures (with statistics) to support the claim of K–S weakening. Based on the small
map area in Fig. 1, the evidence for OR “weakening” is not yet statistically robust. 
3. The grain statistics for the as-cast material (Fig. 1) appear limited. This may explain why the cast sample in Fig. 2e–f looks
heavily textured. Please include larger-area IPF/phase maps (especially for the cast material) or show multiple maps so
readers can assess whether the presented maps are representative. 
4. Lines 194–196 state: “The back stress contributes to the strengthening of the L12 phase while the forward stress facilitates
local plastic deformation in B2 phase, eventually promoting crack nucleation in the B2 phase.” This sentence is confusing as
written. If forward stress actually facilitates plasticity in B2, that would tend to relieve stress concentrations rather than
directly produce cracks. If instead plasticity in B2 is constrained and forward stress concentrates, crack nucleation is
expected. Please rewrite. 
5. Supplementary Fig. 1 shows cracks nucleating within coarse B2 grains in the cast samples. According to Fig. 2a, the
stress required to trigger the B2→BCT transformation in the NE-T condition appears lower than the fracture stress of the NE-

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 3 -->

cast material. Since the reported phase compositions are similar, please clarify why the B2 phase in the cast alloy does not
transform before fracture. If the transformation is driven primarily by the applied stress in NE-T, why does the cast
microstructure fail by cracking instead of transforming? Moreover, if the orientation relationship in the cast alloy restricts B2
deformation, one would expect crack initiation at the L12/B2 interfaces rather than within the B2 grains. Please discuss what
microstructural factors (OR, constraint, grain morphology, defect density, etc.) distinguish the two cases. 
6. The statement that the OR “weakens after recrystallization” needs clarification. Recrystallization can produce new grain
nucleation and orientation randomization, but it can also produce coherent ORs. Please comment on the mechanism if
possible. 
7. The authors report EPMA/WDS bulk phase compositions in Supplementary Table 4 and state that the compositions are
near nominal. To rule out composition-driven transformation (for example local Al enrichment that could lower stability), local
chemical mapping across transformed and untransformed regions would be helpful. TEM-EDS line scans/mapping or small-
volume techniques (APT or high-resolution EPMA across interfaces) would allow discrimination between an OR-driven
pathway and a composition-driven pathway. 
8. A more detailed microscopic description of nucleation sites for the B2→BCT transformation would strengthen the
mechanism. Do transformations nucleate at triple junctions, at coarse B2/L12 interfaces, or at pre-existing
defects/dislocations? High-magnification micrographs or TEM (SAED/HR-STEM) showing the local lattice and interface
structure at transformed regions would make the proposed mechanism more convincing. 
9. Typo: line 389 references “Fig. 6e”. Please check this and correct the figure call if necessary.

the solidification process (https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abf6986).  
To verify the crystal structures experimentally, we annealed the samples at different 
temperatures. As shown in Fig. R1  a–i, the near -eutectic (NE) alloy exhibits distinct 
precipitation behaviors after annealing at different temperatures. At 700 ℃, only very fine

completely disappear after annealing at 1200  ℃. To further confirm those precipitates, 
sample annealed at  1000 ℃ was selected to conduct TEM analysis. In Fig. R1 g, 
precipitations are present both along grain boundaries and within the matrix . The 
corresponding nanobeam diffraction pattern (Fig. R1 h) indicates that the precipitates 
possess a disordered FCC structure. In contrast, clear superlattice diffraction spots are 
observed in Fig. R1i, as highlighted by the red circle. These spots originate from the matrix 
and confirm the presence of an ordered B2 structure. 
These results demonstrate that ordered and disordered phases can coexist at lower 
annealing temperatures below 1100 °C, and that the degree of ordering is strongly 
dependent on temperature and composition. Therefore, an annealing temperature of 
1200 °C was selected to ensure that the matrix consists exclusively of the ordered B2 and 
L1₂ phases. All deformation tests in this study were conducted at room temperature. After 
deformation, the interface between the B2 and BCT phases was examined, revealing no  
compositional variation and only a gradual change in lattice parameter, shown in our 
related publication (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matlet.2024.136549).  
We have incorporated this clarification into the manuscript and added Fig. R1 as 
Supplementary Fig. 8.

Reply: We thank the reviewer for raising this important question, which indeed addresses 
the central mechanism discussed in this work: why certain B2 grains undergo the 
B2→BCT transformation, whereas others fracture without transforming. 
Although the phase compositions of the  NE-cast and NE -T samples are the same, 
their deformation responses differ. In the NE-T sample, statistical analysis shows that the 
B2 and BCT phases exhibit comparable grain morphologies and similar Schmid factor 
distributions, indicating that neither gra in shape nor resolved shear stress alone governs

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 14 -->

the occurrence of the transformation.  Instead, a combined analysis of the Schmid tensor 
𝑺𝑠, the strain tensor 𝜺, and the strain contribution 𝑬𝜺 reveals that the transformation is 
strongly dependent on strain compatibility between neighboring phases.  
Specifically, the B2→BCT transformation can be activated only when the adjacent 
L1₂ phase undergoes elongation along crystallographically matching directions, thereby 
providing sufficient strain accommodatio n for the B2 lattice to distort along the [100] 
direction. In this case, the applied stress is effectively converted into a transformation -
driving strain, rather than being concentrated as local stress. 
In contrast, in the NE -cast sample, the orientation r elationship between the B2 and 
L1₂ phases does not favor such cooperative deformation. As a result, the L1₂ phase cannot 
provide adequate strain accommodation for the B2 phase, and the applied stress cannot be 
efficiently transformed into a lattice distort ion driving the B2 →BCT transformation. 
Instead, stress accumulates within the coarse B2 grains, leading to crack initiation inside 
the B2 phase rather than at the L1₂/B2 interfaces. 
Therefore, the difference in transformation behavior between the NE -cast and NE-T 
samples is not governed by the applied stress magnitude alone, but by the combined effects 
of orientation relationship, strain compatibility, and local mechanical constraint. These 
microstructural factors collectively determine whether the B2 phas e undergoes a stress -
