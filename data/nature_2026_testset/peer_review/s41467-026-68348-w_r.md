Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The article entitled “Sequence-Encoded Layered Heteroleptic Metalla-[2]catenanes for Programmable Supramolecular
Function” is well written and well-justified. This study introduces a novel class of supramolecular architecture known as
heteroleptic metalla-[2]catenanes (MCATs), designed to investigate how the spatial arrangement of functional motifs
influences macroscopic behavior. Unlike traditional radial structures such as metallocages and metallacycles, MCATs
feature a layered and stereoscopic configuration, enabling more complex molecular information encoding. Using N-
heterocyclic carbene (NHC) ligands combined with silver (Ag(I)) nodes, MCATs were constructed with two or three types of
ligands, forming specific sequences through direct assembly or supramolecular transformation. Their structures were
confirmed via single-crystal X-ray diffraction and high-resolution mass spectrometry, while theoretical calculations
(semiempirical and DFT) demonstrated that the obtained sequences are thermodynamically more stable than alternative
isomers. Three heteroleptic MCATs were synthesized: MCATCAAC, MCATCBBC, and MCATCABC, each with different
ligand combinations. These were formed through both direct assembly and supramolecular fusion of homoleptic precursors.
π···π interactions between ligands played a key role in forming interlocked structures, and a unique intramolecular self-
ordering behavior was observed. Spectroscopic studies (NMR, DOSY, ESI-MS) confirmed the formation of well-defined and
stable assemblies in solutions, even under varying concentrations and temperatures. Additionally, MCATs were shown to
rapidly transform into one another via supramolecular fusion, indicating that heteroleptic structures are thermodynamically
favored products. Functionally, the near-infrared (NIR) photothermal conversion efficiency of MCATs was evaluated.
Heteroleptic structures exhibited significantly higher efficiency than homoleptic ones. For instance, MCATCAAC showed an
efficiency of 79.6%, outperforming MCATCBBC (39.7%) and MCATCABC (46.5%). This is attributed to the arrangement of
π-conjugated motifs, which enhance electron transport and energy conversion. Theoretical calculations supported
experimental findings, showing that observed sequences (CAAC, CBBC, and CABC) have lower binding energies,
indicating greater stability. IGMH analysis confirmed the presence of dispersion interactions between π-conjugated planes,
essential for forming interlocked structures. 
Comments on the article: 
a) In the introduction, when citing different examples of metallocages and metallacycles with NHC ligands, I recommend the
following citations for referencing them: Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 2022, 61, e202208189 and Acc. Chem. Res. 2020, 53,
1401−1413 
b) In the ESI, in the synthesis of compounds 1 and 4, add the solvents or elution solvent to the chromatographic column. 
c) In the synthesis of compounds 3, 6, and 8, the solvent trichloromethane could be replaced by another solvent in their
preparation. 
d) In the ESI on line 91, the numbers in the drawing do not match the NMR data, as signal 18 does not exist in the

e) In the dosy experiments, it is necessary to know the concentration at which the experiment is performed in order to
calculate the size. Please indicate the concentrations for each dosy experiment in each ESI figure. 
f) The separation of the three compounds MRBB/MCATABAA/MCATBAAA has been tested using a chromatographic
column. 
The paper is novel and provides a breakthrough in supramolecular chemistry and stereoscopic layered arrangements
provide a versatile platform for exploring how spatial molecular coding guides emergent functions.

(Remarks to the Author) 
As we know, π-π stacking aromatic compounds plays an important role to create interesting optoelectronic properties by
intermolecular interactions between these π-molecules. However, to achieve structurally well-defined spatial arrangement of
functional motifs remains a great challenge. Recently, some groups have reported to obtain this kind of π-stacks through
host-guest assembly. And more, these pure supramolecular π-stacks are not enough stable and easy to dissociate as
materials especially for actual application due to the weak intermolecular interactions. The authors report to apply
tetranuclear metalla-[2]catenane (MCAT) as a versatile platform to achieve layered arrangement of two or three kinds of
various functional motifs. More interestingly, using this strategy, several two or three-component four-layer π-stacks are
synthesized in one step with high yield. Although the [2]catenane also belong to supramolecules, the interlocked
cyclophanes can provide additional stability. Furthermore, they probe sequence–function relationships in layered
supramolecular architectures. Overall, I think this manuscript presents an innovative study on the development of structurally
well-defined spatial arrangement of various functional motifs. I recommend publication after minor revision. 
1. As for H2B, only the simple binuclear MRBB is formed. Is it possible that the intermolecular interaction between
anthracene moieties is much weaker than other two fused polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons? 
2. As the authors stated, the labile nature of the Ag–CNHC bond help to achieve fusion reaction between MCATs. What
