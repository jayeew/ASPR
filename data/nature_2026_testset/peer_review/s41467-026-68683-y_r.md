(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript innovatively proposed a new non-compressible hemorrhage hemostasis strategy based on ultrafast self-
gelling self-expanding powder. The hemostatic powder integrates rapid physical crosslinking with spontaneous gas
foaming, exhibits a fast gelation rate, high expansion ratio, outstanding antibacterial activity, and strong tissue adhesion,
facilitating its application in the management of non-compressible hemorrhage. The ultrafast self-gelling self-expanding
powder can achieve non-compressible hemorrhage hemostasis through the multiple synergistic effects of expansion
plugging, self-gelling adhesion and activating the coagulation factors. This is an innovative and meaningful work in the field
of non-compressible hemorrhage hemostasis. However, there still existed some issues in this article as following. Therefore,
I recommend it to accept in Nature Communications after minor revision. 
1. How to prepare the hemostatic powder? The representative photographs of key steps in the preparation of the hemostatic
powder should be added to Figure 2.

Powder for Non-compressible hemorrhage Hemostasis in Pigs,” a hemostatic powder with self-gelling , self-expanding, and

1. The current introduction provides a somewhat lengthy description of the background and limitations of existing materials

2. The conclusion section is currently rather weak and reads merely as a summary of results. To strengthen the manuscript,

3. Dopamine (DA)-modified MMT is crucial for adhesion, but what is the grafting rate of DA onto MMT? How does the zeta
potential of MMT change after grafting? It is suggested to provide related chemical characterizations. 
4. Does the pressure generated by material expansion within a sealed cavity pose a risk of compressive injury to adjacent
critical structures such as nerves? It is recommended to discuss or provide related data. 
5. The text mentions that hydrogels have “poor sterilization compatibility.” What sterilization method is used for this powder?
(Gamma irradiation? Ethylene oxide?) It is necessary to specify and demonstrate that this sterilization method does not
damage the material's chemical structure and functionality. 
6. The paper highlights the advantages of powder formulations in terms of storage stability and portability. It is suggested to
provide stability data to support this core advantage. 
7. The authors claim to achieve “ultra-fast” gelation within 2 seconds. However, this key parameter is supported only by
qualitative video evidence (Supplementary Video S1) and lacks quantitative rheological data—particularly sweep
experiments demonstrating the evolution of G' and G'' over time and the precise gelation point. 
Some minor comments are listed below: 
1. In Figure 2, the legend for panel 2h states that the data is "mean ± SD", but there are no error bars in the figure. Please
add error bars. 
2. Regarding Figure 7e, the figure currently lacks significant markers for intergroup comparisons (such as asterisks). Given
that the text description mentions a strong significant effect (p < 0.001), these markers must be added to the figure for an
intuitive interpretation of the statistical results.

4. Does the pressure generated by material expansion within a sealed cavity pose a risk 
of compressive injury to adjacent critical structures such as nerves? It is recommended 
to discuss or provide related data. 
Response: Thanks for your constructive question. As you mentioned, compressive 
injury to adjacent critical structures is an important issue that should be considered in 
the de sign of expandable hemostatic materials. Therefore, we further studied the 
expansion behavior of self -expanding self -gelling hemostatic powder in the bionic 
wound cavity through simulation. The results showed that the expanding hydrogel 
exerts a maximum extrusion pressure of 35  kPa on the surrounding tissues, which is 
much lower than the breaking strength of skin (~10 MPa) 6, blood vessels (~2 MPa) 7 
and muscles (~100 kPa)8, , and also lower than the in-situ stress of surrounding tissues
