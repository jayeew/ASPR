Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript presents a comprehensive and well-executed study identifying a novel enoyl-CoA hydratase/isomerase,
DspII, that functions in concert with the previously characterized DspI in Pseudomonas aeruginosa. The authors
demonstrate that these two proteins form a functional complex essential for the biosynthesis of cis-2-decenoic acid (CDA), a
quorum-sensing signal molecule that promotes the transition from chronic to acute infection. Notably, the study reveals a
dual functionality for the DspII/DspI : in addition to their enzymatic role in CDA synthesis, they also exerts a regulatory effect
on the expression of type III secretion system (T3SS) genes. The authors propose this effect is exerted by DNA binding and
by antagonizing GacA-mediated transcription of the small RNA RsmZ. 
The findings are highly significant and novel, particularly in uncovering an unexpected transcriptional regulatory role for
quorum-sensing synthases. The manuscript provides novel mechanistic insights into the virulence switch in P. aeruginosa
and broadens the functional landscape of DSF-family quorum sensing enzymes. The experiments are logically structured,
rigorous, and well controlled. The manuscript is clearly written, and the data are solid. 
General/major comments: 
- The PA0744 gene (encoding DspII) is longer (1104 bp) than dspI (819 bp). Does DspII contain additional domains that
might explain its non-enzymatic DNA-binding function? Domain prediction analysis (e.g., InterPro, Pfam) would strengthen
this point. 
- The separation between CDA-mediated biofilm dispersion and CDA-independent T3SS activation is clearly shown.
However, the mechanism by which DspII–DspI antagonizes GacA activity is not fully explored. A catalytic mutant could help
test whether DNA binding/regulatory activity is independent of enzymatic activity. 
- EMSAs with the reconstituted DspII–DspI complex may show increased DNA affinity or revel binding to rsmY promoter. 
- While cdrA is a valid reporter of intracellular c-di-GMP, LC-MS/MS quantification could validate these results and exclude
secondary effects.
