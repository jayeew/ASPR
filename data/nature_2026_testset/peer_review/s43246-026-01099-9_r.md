Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
The manuscript introduces Perovskite-R1, a large language model (LLM) that is domain-adapted and fine-tuned on a
specialized corpus of 1,232 perovskite-related papers and a 33,269-compound “drug-like” library. The model is designed to
propose precursor additives and experimental protocols for perovskite solar cells (PSCs). 
The development of domain-specialized LLMs for materials discovery is a timely and critical area of research, aligning with
existing efforts in materials-aware LLMs and large-scale AI-driven predictions, such as CrystaLLM and GNoME. This active
landscape necessitates demonstrating capabilities that extend beyond mere text assistance. 
The authors’ construction of a domain-specific LLM for PSC precursor-additive design and experimental planning directly
addresses a significant bottleneck in the field: navigating rapidly expanding and complex literature. The attempt to connect
LLM recommendations to laboratory validation is highly valuable and successfully differentiates this work from purely
computational LLM studies. 
Overall, the work is promising, but it currently requires modification of the content, an overhaul of the evaluation
methodology, and increased transparency regarding the training data and evaluation process. I recommend a major
revision. 
Here are some revisions: 
1) The manuscript's structure must be aligned with the conventions of a materials science journal like Nature
Communications Materials rather than a computer science conference (e.g., ICLR or ICML). The authors should restructure
the paper to include standard sections such as "Introduction", "Results and Discussion", and "Conclusions". 
2) The claim of a “closed-loop discovery” process is inaccurate in the manuscript, as the described workflow demonstrates
only a single-pass cycle. The current process encompasses dataset construction, supervised fine-tuning of the LLM,
prompt/context engineering, and culminates in the laboratory validation of the LLM-based recommendations. The authors
should either demonstrate a second, successful iteration of the process or modify the claim to a more accurate description. 
3) The provision of a training-set example is appreciated, revealing two primary data formats: structured explanations of
scientific papers and identification of perovskite precursors. Since the training dataset was generated using the OpenAI o1
model, its quality and reliability are critical concerns. The authors should: 
a. provide a comprehensive and quantitative introduction to the entire training dataset, including statistics on size, source
distribution (papers vs. drug-like library), and the relative proportion of different question–answer pair types; 
b. provide an evaluation of the quality and factual accuracy of the AI-generated dataset. 
4) In the 5623rd entry of the training-set example, the generated conversation is about several candidates of Boc-protected
N-compounds for perovskite synthesis. In the generated chain-of-thought (CoT) and answer, it analyzes all the compounds
and chooses one as a potential candidate. It is rare to see a Boc-protected N-compound for perovskite synthesis, since the
Boc group usually limits the activity of the N-compound. The authors shall give an explanation of this entry and also need to
clarify: 
a. Was this specific entry used for fine-tuning Perovskite-R1? 
b. Was this entry used for evaluating the performance of different LLMs? If so, the authors must provide the exact replies
generated by each language model for this particular query. 
5) The manuscript describes a custom-trained LLM that is central to the research and currently only accessible by the
authors. For research involving the training and application of an LLM to assist experiments, community evaluation and
examination are essential. As a reference, recent works (i.e., DeepSeek-R1 in Nature) have released the original weights of
the LLM for evaluation. It is recommended that the authors open-source the weights of Perovskite-R1 for community
evaluation and application. 
6) In the benchmark performance, the authors should: 
a. 
clarify whether the benchmark was used during fine-tuning or overlaps with training data, and provide split and leak-check
details; 
b. release the original outputs and scores from each LLM during evaluation; 
c. include results from the following state-of-the-art LLMs under matched prompts and settings: Gemini 2.5 Pro (released
alongside Gemini-2.5-Flash-Thinking), GLM-4.5-Air (a recent strong open model), Phi-4 (Microsoft’s scientific LLM), and
Llama-3.3 (Meta). Report prompts, context length, temperature/top-p, and whether CoT is enabled for all models.

Perovskite-R1: A Domain-Specialized LLM for Intelligent Discovery of Precursor Additives and Experimental Design 
In principle, I am positive. The work is timely, has an interesting approach, shows promising results, and could potentially be

However, there are a few limitations and omissions I think need to be addressed before the paper can be published. Those
are mostly not about the quality of the research itself, but omissions in what is presented and made available and. Those
concerns should be fairly easy to fix. 
1. 
The synthesis and characterization procedures of the solar cells are not disclosed. 
For the paper to be publishable, the authors need to give a detailed description for how the perovskite cells were produced
and characterized. As it is now, we do not even know the cell architecture. 
2. 
The perovskite data is not disclosed. The paper shows a few IV curves for selected devices. However, we do not get the
data in any useful format. We do not know how many cells were made. We do not know the distribution in the performance.
Please present statistical evaluations and disclose the raw measurement data. 
3. 
When it comes to the selection of the four additives (two by the AI system and two by humans). I want a description of the
human thought process for how those were selected. Were they selected at random or was there any thought behind it?
What was the reasoning? 
What was the pool of molecules to select from? Was it the same as for the AI system? What
criterions did the AI system use to select two? 
4. 
State if any of the additives have been used before. If they have, please give references. If they have not, please state that. 
5. 
Concerning data availability. When I clicked the link, I didn't find the full training data set. If that is available in some way, I
did not find it. I would like it to be available. 
6. 
Model availability. 
One of the biggest concerns. Where do I find the trained model, and how to use it myself? For this work to be really useful,
the reader would need access to the trained model so they can use it. 
7. 
For code availability. What would be really useful is if something like a Jupyter notebook were provided that demonstrates
how to utilize the resources and how to reproduce the results in the paper. That would also give an entry point for readers to
start experimenting themselves. Provide that (and fix the other issues) and this would really make the paper much more
valuable. 
8. 
The authors talk about closed loop research. I do not think they really live up to that label as the experiments were done
manually.
