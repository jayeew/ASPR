Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
The authors demonstrated high-yield production of a fusion protein consisting of mini-spidroin linked to IdeS enzyme. The
enzyme maintained its catalytic activity even when the fusion protein had been fabricated into hydrogel or spun
fibres,whether wet or dry. The author claimed that this is the first successful example of wet-spun spider silk fibres using a
recombinant fusion protein mixed into the spinning dope. The work is very interesting, and the manuscript is written well.
However, it could be improved by addressing these points. 
1. [Line128] The authors mentioned that using the ratio of 40:1 was better than 20:1 during cell disruption, as it helped
solubilize the protein. Please add an explanation of why using more buffer improves solubility. Also, the way the ratio was
written is different in the results section and in the methods section. For example, the authors used 40:1 in the results
section, but they used 1:40 in the methods section. 
2. [Line 134] The authors showed that the binding of His tag to the column was suboptimal, resulting in about 13.2 mg/g
protein after purification. Could it be possible that the IdeS masked the accessibility of the His tag? It may help if the authors
compare IMAC-based purification of the A3I-A-IdeS to A3I-A. 
3. In this work, most proteins are in the insoluble fraction when the ratio of 20:1 was used (Supplementary Fig. 4), and it
seemed that the recombinant protein did not bind very well to the resins during purification. However, in reference no. 32, the
same group reported that “The target proteins bound efficiently to the columns”, and most protein produced was in the
soluble fraction. Please discuss this difference. 
4. [Section 2.3] The authors mentioned that the gel time inversely correlated with the protein concentration. This is quite
unexpected to me. Most hydrogels from biomolecules gel faster when there is a higher protein concentration. Please
discuss. Also, the gel-time experiment shown in Fig. 4 is difficult to understand. It would be easier to understand if the
authors show tilting tubes with solution/gel at different time points. 
5. [Line 235] The authors should discuss more about the enzyme activity detected in the 1st washing, which could be due to
some soluble protein that was not tightly incorporated into the gel that could leach out during washing. 
6. How much IdeS is present in the hydrogel and spun fibres? I believe that hydrogel has more IdeS than the fibres do
because all A3I-A-IdeS self-assembled to form the gel, whereas about 1% or 10% of the fibres had A3I-A-IdeS. I suggest the
authors calculate the amount/size of gel or fibres required for a certain therapeutic application based on the effective dose
reported for IdeS. In addition, it would be beneficial for the readers if the authors suggested ways in which the materials are
going to be applied in clinics. 
7. [Supplementary Fig.4] Please label the lanes after SN in the SDS-PAGE. 
8. The reviewer recommends some more references that should help guide the readers through the production of
recombinant silk-therapeutics. [1] Biomimetic peptide conjugates as emerging strategies for controlled release from protein-
based materials. (2025) Drug Delivery, 32(1), [2] Spider silks: recombinant synthesis, assembly, spinning, and engineering
of synthetic proteins. Microb Cell Fact 3, 14 (2004), and [3] Optimizing protein delivery rate from silk fibroin hydrogel using
silk fibroin-mimetic peptides conjugation. Sci Rep 14, 4428 (2024). 
9. The authors should provide standard deviations in Table 1 to reflect batch-to-batch variation.

Reviewer #3 (Remarks to the Author): 
Pessatti et al. present an innovative fusion of the IgG-cleaving enzyme (IdeS) with a recombinant mini-spidroin, (A3I)
₃
-(A)
₁₄
,
with the goal of producing enzymatically active hydrogels and fibers. While conceptually strong, the manuscript lacks
sufficient structural and functional validation in its current form. 
The CD data presented primarily reflect the α-helical character of the spidroin domain and provide no independent evidence
that the β-rich IdeS retains its native tertiary structure. Moreover, no structural information is presented for the assembled
hydrogels or fibers—no FTIR, solid-state NMR, XRD, or related analysis is provided to confirm β-sheet formation in the
repetitive spidroin region, retention of α-helical structure in the terminal domains, or enzyme integrity after spinning. The
“fiber activity” assays are qualitative and lack rate normalization, enzyme loading data, or quantitative wash analyses.
Consequently, the central claims of structural preservation and retained catalytic activity in the fiber state are not
convincingly demonstrated. Incorporating solid-state structural analysis, quantitative enzyme assays, and appropriate
controls would be necessary to support the concluding statement that the work “demonstrates the integration of a full-length
IgG-cleaving enzyme into robust spider silk-inspired materials that retain activity in both hydrogel and fiber formats.” 
Major Comments 
1. Structural characterization is insufficient 
a. The CD data do not isolate the IdeS contribution and therefore cannot confirm enzyme folding. The calculated CD
spectrum from the AlphaFold model of the full fusion protein is compared to experiment, but there is no discussion of the
model’s confidence metrics. What are the per-domain confidence values, and are the IdeS and spidroin regions modeled
reliably? 
b. The authors should also calculate the CD spectrum for the spidroin-only construct and compare it to the fusion protein.
The spectra likely overlap substantially, which would indicate that the CD signal provides minimal information about the
enzyme’s structural state. IdeS contains roughly twice as much β-structure as α-structure, yet the experimental spectra show
predominantly α-helical features which are likely dominated by the spidroin terminal domains. 
c. No FTIR, solid-state NMR, XRD, or related analysis is provided to verify β-sheet formation in the spidroin repetitive region
or the enzyme’s structural integrity within the solid fibers. Even if the fusion protein content is low (1–10 wt%), some
structural characterization of the spidroin fraction in fiber form is expected. 
d. It is implied that pure A3I–A–IdeS does not spin successfully. If so, please clarify whether 10 wt% is the highest level
compatible with fiber formation and whether higher loadings compromise spinnability.

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 4 -->

2. Functional enzymatic activity in the fiber and hydrogel formats appears only qualitative 
(Note: this reviewer is not an expert in enzymology, and a specialist in enzyme kinetics should also evaluate the
manuscript.) 
a. Enzyme activity in fibers and gels is demonstrated only through SDS–PAGE band shifts, without quantitative kinetic
analysis or normalization to enzyme loading. 
b. Enzyme content and leaching are not quantified. 
c. Negative controls (spidroin-only fibers) and soluble IdeS controls at equivalent molarity are missing. 
3. Interpretive overreach 
The conclusions claim that IdeS remains folded and active within the solid-state silk matrix, but the current data do not
substantiate this mechanistic statement. These interpretations should be toned-down unless supported by additional
quantitative or structural evidence. 
Overall Recommendation 
Major Revision — The manuscript presents an interesting and potentially impactful concept, but the structural and
mechanistic evidence is currently insufficient for publication in Communications Materials. Substantial additional validation
including direct structural characterization of the fibers and quantitative enzyme activity measurements are required to
support the authors’ central claims.

1. [Line128] The authors mentioned that using the ratio of 40:1 was better than 20:1 
during cell disruption, as it helped solubilize the protein. Please add an explanation 
of why using more buffer improves solubility. 
 
Reply:  We thank the reviewer for raising this question. This behaviour is indeed 
difficult to explain mechanistically, but we speculate that liquid–liquid phase 
separation (LLPS) of the mini-spidroins may be involved. From previous work, we 
know that mini-spidroins can undergo phase separation during expression in the 
cytoplasm (Mater Today Bio, 2022 - https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mtbio.2022.100492), 
and that different protein variants have very different LLPS propensities (Comm 
Chem, 2024 - https://doi.org/10.1038/s42004-024-01357-2). By adding more buffer 
during lysis, the salts and/or crowding agents that promote LLPS are diluted, 
potentially reducing the driving forces for phase separation. The aim is to shift the 
equilibrium towards the soluble dimer state to avoid pelleting proteins with cell debris 
during centrifugation, but the conditions may, of course, vary greatly depending on 
the protein. A sentence explaining this has been added to the results section 2.1 
(main text, page 4), where we state that: “We speculate that the increased recovery 
of soluble proteins is due to the release of protein from condensates formed at high 
protein concentrations in the cytoplasm”. 
 
Also, the way the ratio was written is different in the results section and in the 
methods section. For example, the authors used 40:1 in the results section, but they 
used 1:40 in the methods section. 
 
Reply:  We apologize for this mistake and have corrected the inconsistencies in the 
buffer-to-wet cell pellet ratios in the manuscript and the SI.

suboptimal, resulting in about 13.2 mg/g protein after purification. Could it be 
possible that the IdeS masked the accessibility of the His tag? It may help if the 
authors compare IMAC-based purification of the A3I-A-IdeS to A3I-A.

Reviewer #3 (Remarks to the Author): 
Pessatti et al. present an innovative fusion of the IgG-cleaving enzyme (IdeS) with a 
recombinant mini-spidroin, (A3I)₃-(A)₁₄, with the goal of producing enzymatically 
active hydrogels and fibers. While conceptually strong, the manuscript lacks 
sufficient structural and functional validation in its current form. 
 
The CD data presented primarily reflect the α-helical character of the spidroin 
domain and provide no independent evidence that the β-rich IdeS retains its native 
tertiary structure. Moreover, no structural information is presented for the assembled 
hydrogels or fibers—no FTIR, solid-state NMR, XRD, or related analysis is provided

structure in the terminal domains, or enzyme integrity after spinning. The “fiber 
activity” assays are qualitative and lack rate normalization, enzyme loading data, or 
quantitative wash analyses. Consequently, the central claims of structural 
preservation and retained catalytic activity in the fiber state are not convincingly 
demonstrated. Incorporating solid-state structural analysis, quantitative enzyme 
assays, and appropriate controls would be necessary to support the concluding 
statement that the work “demonstrates the integration of a full-length IgG-cleaving 
enzyme into robust spider silk-inspired materials that retain activity in both hydrogel 
and fiber formats.”
