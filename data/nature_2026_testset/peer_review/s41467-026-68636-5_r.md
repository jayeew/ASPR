Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The noteworthy results in this report are summarized as follows.1) The crosstalk between ERS and mitochondrial metabolic
regulate macrophage phenotypic transformation. 2) By utilizing camouflaged nanorobots, alleviating macrophage ERS was
effective in restoring Ca2+ homeostasis in mitochondria and inhibiting cGAS–STING to regulate macrophage phenotypic
transformation and reshape the immune microenvironment. 3) Animal model results provide the potential of camouflaged
nanorobots as a grounded approach for SCI treatment. This study may make a significant contribution to offering a new
strategy for challenging SCI. Overall, this is very nice work. 
Major concerns 
1. Scheme 1 illustrates the process of nanoparticles entering cells, but I find it difficult to understand why both neurons and
astrocytes are present in the cytoplasm. These elements seem to be quite chaotic. At the same time, please pay attention to
the standard format for the abbreviation of calcium ions. 
2. Figure 1 lacks detailed data on metabolomics; please provide more information. 
3. Please provide more characterization results for the NMs in Figure 2, with at least the identification of membrane proteins
being essential. 
4. The author mentions that NM-modified nanoparticles are more easily taken up by macrophages but lacks relevant results.
Please use flow cytometry or fluorescence staining experiments to verify this. 
5. RAW264.7 macrophages are biased toward the M1 phenotype. The work would be strengthened by using bone marrow
derived macrophages or a different macrophage cell line to repeat the main results. 
6. The CCK-8 experiment is used to verify cell proliferation rather than cell viability, and it is necessary to supplement with
live/dead staining to further confirm the cytotoxicity of 100 μg/mL BP@D/N. 
7. Fluorescent images of Figure 5B are not clear enough. Images need to be shown to demonstrate an example of dsDNA in
the cytoplasm that is being quantified. 
Minor concerns 
1. Using Western Blot to detect the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines is not accurate. 
2. The scale for some images is missing. 
3. The background of the Western Blot seems a bit overexposure in Figure 3d. 
4. Please ensure that the text on each image is clearly visible. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, the authors explored the regulatory roles of endoplasmic reticulum stress in the phenotypic transformation
progression of macrophages. Utilizing data from these results, they reported a dual-targeted camouflaged nanorobots
system which is used to regulate endoplasmic reticulum stress–mitochondrial metabolic crosstalk. Thess nanorobots could
reverse macrophages’ inflammatory phenotype, demonstrating their ability to treat spinal cord injury. Here are some
questions for the authors’ consideration.

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 2 -->

(1)This study confirmed the crosstalk between endoplasmic reticulum stress and mitochondrial metabolism in macrophages,
which is very interesting. The authors supported the changes in mitochondrial metabolic pathways through metabolomics
and the protein expression of key mitochondrial metabolic enzymes; however, analyzing cellular energy metabolism is
actually the most direct approach. I suggest that the authors include data related to oxygen consumption rate by Seahorse. 
(2)Did you detect the cellular uptake of nanorobots? 
(3)In this study, RAW264.7 cells were used. Did nanorobots also affect primary macrophages? I suggest checking some
main effects of nanorobots (eg. anti-inflammation) in bone marrow-derived macrophages (BMMs). This will provide more
reliable results. 
(4)Some images have a low resolution (eg. Fig 7a-d, Fig S3, Fig S5), making the results difficult to discern. Please replace
these images with higher resolution images. 
(5)The author has provided some diagrams, but these images and text are not easily legible (Fig 6a and 8a). Please enlarge
the figures and text for easier viewing. 
(6)In Fig 6, LPS seems to increase the expression levels of CD206 in macrophages, which suggests that it can induce M2
polarization of macrophages? 
(7)Can MK-28 reflect the phenomena of coordinate the crosstalk between the endoplasmic reticulum and mitochondria
through ERO1α? 
(8)Define all abbreviations at first use, which is particularly true in the Introduction. 
(9)Please carefully check for grammatical errors. For example, “causing Ca2+ overload in the mitochondria”. 
(10)It is necessary to include the value of scale bars for reference in all images. 
Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This study focuses on nerve regeneration after spinal cord injury (SCI) and designs a dual-targeting disguised nanorobot
(BP@D/N) aimed at improving the SCI microenvironment by regulating macrophage (Mφ) organelle communication. The
key findings and processes of the research are as follows: Mechanism exploration: It reveals that abnormal crosstalk
between endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress and mitochondrial metabolism is crucial for macrophage phenotype
transformation, with Ero1α-mediated Ca²
⁺
 shuttling being an important mechanism locking macrophages in an inflammatory
phenotype. This process exacerbates inflammation by activating the cGAS–STING–NFκB pathway, hindering neural repair.
Nanorobot design: Using BPQDs as the core, coated with hollow mesoporous polydopamine (PDA) to form BP@D, then
further coated with NM to construct BP@D/N. Effect investigation: BP@D/N can inhibit Ero1α expression, block abnormal
Ca²
⁺
 transfer between ER and mitochondria, alleviate mitochondrial Ca²
⁺
 overload, reduce mtDNA release, thereby
suppressing cGAS–STING pathway activation and promoting macrophage polarization towards an anti-inflammatory
phenotype. Simultaneously, it promotes neural stem cell (NSC) differentiation and axon growth via paracrine effects and
inhibits glial scar formation. Animal model: In an SD rat SCI model, BP@D/N significantly reduced ROS levels at the injury
site, improved motor function, decreased spinal cord cavity area, promoted neuron regeneration, and demonstrated good
biocompatibility. This is a very interesting study; however, there are several concerns that reduce the manuscript’s rigor.
Before formal publication, the authors should address the following issues: 
Major comments: 
1. Regarding phosphoprotein detection, the authors used different comparison methods in Figures 3d–h, 5d–f, and e–f,
which I do not find appropriate. Phosphorylation detection should be reflected by the relative ratio of phosphorylated protein
to total protein to determine whether signalling pathways are activated. 
2. The authors should verify changes in ER calcium levels within macrophages and the maintenance of ER homeostasis
before and after inducing ER stress. 
3. The authors found increased ER calcium concentration in macrophages under inflammatory conditions and that the
IP3R1–GRP75–VDAC1 pathway increases mitochondrial calcium. I am particularly interested in whether other calcium
channels open in macrophages under ER stress, and if so, whether the nanorobots can affect these other calcium transport
pathways. 
4. To my knowledge, MK-28 is mainly a selective PERK agonist. It is recommended that the authors further assess the
nanorobot’s effects after modulating ERO1a expression levels. 
5. Previous studies have confirmed that mitochondrial dynamics are closely related to mitochondrial function. Do
mitochondrial dynamics change under ER stress? Can nanorobots influence mitochondrial dynamics? 
6. Can the nanorobot directly affect changes induced by ER stress inducers such as Ionomycin? 
Minor comments: 
1. In Figures 1g and h, the authors should adhere to gene nomenclature conventions rather than using protein naming
styles. 
2. The authors summarise each section’s content, which is unnecessary; excessive summarising reduces readability. It is
recommended to streamline these parts. 
3. The discussion section lacks depth and should be expanded. 
4. Some figures lack annotations (subscripts), such as Figures 3e–h, making them difficult to interpret. 
5. There are some grammatical issues in the text, for example, “disappearance of ER were noted.” Also, some punctuation is
incorrect; please carefully proofread and correct these. 
6. Some images lack scale bars, which increases difficulty in interpretation. Please add them.
