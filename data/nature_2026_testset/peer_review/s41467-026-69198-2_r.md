Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Dear Editor: 
A manuscript entitled “Self-Assembled Molecules for Perovskite Surface Modification” reported enhanced efficiency and
stability by SAM modification on the perovskite top surface in n-i-p PSCs. In contrast to buried SAM interfaces in p-i-n PSCs,
the authors innovatively designed SAM molecules that can be strongly associated with HTL and systematically analyzed the
beneficial role of SAM for top interfaces in n-i-p PSCs. In addition, the authors investigated the effect of solvent on SAM
orientation and the importance of SAM interfacial dipoles, which is crucial for research in the field. These SAM not only
contribute to the superior performance of n-i-p devices but also apply equally well to p-i-n devices. This manuscript’s
findings could provide a guide for designing novel high-performance SAM; thus, I recommend acceptance of this manuscript
after the following minor comments are properly considered or addressed. 
1. Does the schematic in Figure 1g imply that carriers pass through the gap between the SAM? 
2. The Pb peaks of PSK/SFX-P1 and PSK/SFX-P2 seem to exhibit different behavior in XPS characterization. The authors
should discuss the reasons for this. 
3. Theoretical calculations show that the binding energy of SFX-P2 is much higher than that of SFX-P1. However, the defect
state density and performance show that the SFX-P1-based device is better. Can the authors analyze the reasons for this?
Or what is the dominant factor for the performance improvement? 
4. The difference in peak strength between the GIWAXS 2D pattern and the 1D profile line diagram for perovskite is barely
discernible. I think the authors can present the data more clearly in table form. 
5. Unnecessary text is present in Figure S12. 
6. Could the SFX-P1 modification layer improve reproducibility in large-area fabrications? 
7. The authors mentioned that SFX-SAM can enhance the performance of p-i-n PSCs through dipole interaction. However,
no data on the energy levels of the SAM/perovskite interface in p-i-n PSCs have been provided in the manuscript. 
8. What is the purpose of using PEACl in the p-i-n PSCs? Is PEACl also used in NiOx-only devices? I think evaluating the
role of the SAM layer in the p-i-n device should not include an additional PEACl layer. 
9. Please provide the IPCE data in the p-i-n device to evaluate the effect of SAM on light absorption in the range of 300 nm
to 450 nm. 
10. The manuscript mentioned the solvent effect on SAM orientation. I suggest that the authors appeal to researchers in the
conclusion to focus on this issue to better optimize SAMs and thus advance the development of PSCs.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The paper by Zhai et al. introduced new SAMs as interlayers primarily for nip-type perovskite cells. 2 SAMs, namely SFX-P1
and SFX-P2, improve the performance of nip-type cells from about 23% to 26%. The working mechanism is attributed to
various factors: perovskite surface defect passivation, improved energy alignment, enhanced hole extraction, and partially
enhanced built-in voltage. The stability improves as well, although not quite to the state-of-the-art values achieved for
perovskite cells. The manuscript is solid, contains a lot of data, and the results are valuable. However, unfortunately, the
interpretation of some results falls into various traps that are present in the literature, especially related to the passivation.
Therefore, I would suggest a major revision of the manuscript before a renewed review. 
Firstly, with regard to the device performance, I think a certification should be provided for an NC-level publication. The
EQE-integrated current seems to be somewhat off as well. 
With regard to the passivation of the perovskite surface through the SAMs, I am not quite convinced about that. On the one
hand, this is due to the reduction of the PL emission (S45), which is not consistent with defect passivation. The shifts in the
Pb4f core levels do not prove defect passivation either. 
Also, other measurements provide no further evidence for passivation, such as SCLC and the intensity-dependence of the
Voc. Please note that it has been shown several times (https://dx.doi.org/10.1021/acsenergylett.0c02599,
https://doi.org/10.1021/acsenergylett.1c01449), that SCLC on such thin films cannot be used to determine the trap density.
There are at least 5 factors that make the trap density estimation unreliable. 
Regarding the ideality factor, a value closer to 1 rather than 2 indicates by no means a radiative recombination-dominated
process. It's clear from the PL that non-radiative recombination dominates by far. Also, it does not mean that radiative
recombination becomes more dominant if the values go to 1. In fact, it can be the opposite, that a value close to 1 indicates
severe interfacial or surface recombination (e.g. DOI: 10.1039/c7ee02415k, DOI: 10.1021/acsenergylett.6b00719). The
intensity range used is also very small. Please try to improve the argument here, or remove it since it does not add much
value in my opinion. 
Judging from the strong dipole effects and better energy alignment, I think the energy alignment will very likely be able to
explain the gain in Voc and partially FF, while better charge extraction is also likely due to the dipole effect. 
In general, I would strongly suggest including drift-diffusion simulations to back up the interpretation of these results. 
Similar to above, with regard to the reduction of the PL emission in the presence of the transport layer, this cannot be taken
as proof of better charge extraction (although it might be related to extraction). At the end, given that the PL is the ratio of
radiative to total recombination, it means that the non-radiative recombination increases in the presence of the transport
layer with the SAMs (Figure S61), limiting the QFLS and Voc-potential. It's hard to explain a better device performance with
such a reduction of the radiative efficiency in the presence of the SAM and transport layer. Maybe this has something to do
with lateral diffusion if an extremely small laser spot was used for the measurement (I am not sure; details such as intensity
and laser spot should be provided in the caption). I would strongly suggest avoiding correlating quenching in PL with
improved charge extraction. Perhaps, the TRPL decay and TAS are better ways to analyse the enhanced charge extraction,
if their influence can be clearly disentangled from interfacial recombination. 
Another point with regard to TRPL is the averaging of the 2 decay times to obtain an average lifetime. Considering that the
different decay components include charge transfer and interfacial recombination, it does not make sense to me to specify
an average lifetime. Please try to disentangle extraction and recombination and specify the characteristic time constants for
both processes. Furthermore, the laser fluence should be specified to correctly interpret the results. 
As for the Vbi, its determination from the Mott-Schottky analysis is also not straightforward (DOI: 10.1021/acsaem.8b01119).
There might be some correlation between the apparent Vbi and the real Vbi, but often, the extrapolation just follows the Voc.
I would suggest adding at least a comment that the interpretation is still up for debate for thin film perovskite, or providing DD
simulations for further justification. 
Overall, I would suggest identifying the dominant mechanism governing the improvement, rather than proposing a large
range of mechanisms, potentially putting more emphasis on the improved energy alignment, removing questionable
interpretation and corresponding data (considering that the manuscript certainly has sufficient data), and corroborating the
interpretation with DD simulations.

(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript entitled: "Self-assembled molecules for perovskite surface modifications' presents an original approach to

using self-assembled molecules as selective contacts in perovskite solar cells". However, the results pertain only to a single
type of molecular structure, despite the authors' aim to extend their findings to a broader perspective, with which I disagree
entirely. The hype in the title, which I believe aims to generalise the findings, would be more appropriate for an extensive
review rather than a communication. Moreover, in the abstract, the authors assume that the observed large dipole on their
particular molecules is the sole reason for the improvement in their device performance. They claim that the underlying
modification mechanisms and molecular design principles for the SAMS action remain unclear, yet it is evident that a myriad
of manuscripts already explain the different actions observed for SAMS on perovskite solar cells depending on their
molecular structure. Several reviews compile these various actions, and the authors have neglected to cite them in this
manuscript. 
Regarding the science in the manuscript, I believe that the authors have assembled a comprehensive collection of
characterisation techniques to support their hypothesis, which includes both experimental and computational work. Indeed,
the results bolster their specific hypothesis. Moreover, the achieved efficiency in n-i-p devices is remarkable, as well as the
stability. However, to the best of my knowledge, there is nothing new that justifies an urgent communication instead of a
more structured manuscript. Neither the approach nor the hypothesis is novel, and the efficiency and stability of perovskite
solar cells are well documented in the bibliography. 
Last but not least, the references used by the authors fall short in providing perspective for Sams-based solar cells. They
have cited important work on dipole moments, perovskite composition, and others, but have missed or neglected important
references on SAMS from pioneering groups and significant work that describes the use of SAMS as dipoles ( for instance,
Abate et al.).

removing ambiguous measurements and tried to simulate their devices using SCAPS. However, I think the simulations need
to be considerably improved. 
First, regarding the statement: "Small valence band cliff (Figure S64a and Table S10). Such a small cliff can facilitate hole
extraction by providing a gentle driving force." Such a cliff (energy misalignment) should not provide more driving force for
extraction in perovskite cells but lowers the selectivity, increases interface recombination, and reduces the extraction
efficiency (e.g. ref.10.1039/c9ee02020a). 
More importantly, however, in the following, the authors then explain that: "Introducing a buffer layer (SFX-P1) between the
perovskite and SFX-DM further optimizes the interface. As shown in Figure S64b and Table S10, the VBO at the
perovskite/SFX-P1 interface remains -0.34 eV, while a slight positive VBO of +0.02 eV appears at the SFX-P1/SFX-DM
junction. This small valence-band spike increases the built-in potential (Vbi) across the interface, suppresses hole back-
injection, and reduces interfacial recombination, while maintaining efficient hole extraction (spikes > 0.3 eV would instead
hinder transport).” I quickly checked this claim, and it appears to me that the main effect in the simulation is that the SFX-P1
interlayer is highly doped (NA=1e21 cm3, which is much higher than the SPX-DM, 1e18 cm3). Indeed, such a highly doped
interlayer can improve the voltage drop over the perovskite (rather than the total Vbi) and thereby suppress interface
recombination (10.1002/solr.202100219). However, it seems to be totally unrelated to the main effects claimed in the paper.
In Figure 4a the authors discuss the improved energy level alignment through the 
SFX-P1 molecule. Therefore, they could
simply simulate a better-aligned transport layer as an approximation. 
Minor points: 
In addition, I note that Table S7 includes several errors, e.g., in the first line of the thicknesses. 
Regarding the explanation of non-linearity in EQE. Indeed, the EQE is often lower, but I am not sure if this explanation
makes sense, since a lower light intensity should not lead to a lower EQE. Anyway, the authors have now provided
certification, and the values are close.

We appreciate the reviewer’s insightful comment. As correctly noted, Jangwon 
Seo et al. (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41560-018-0200-6) reported that the Tg of DM and
