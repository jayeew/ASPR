Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript reports combined experimental and theoretical investigations of Ti atoms adsorbed on 2 and 3 layers of
MgO on Ag(100). From STM images the Ti atoms present two different adsorptions sites (atop of O atom and bridge site
between two O atoms). Electron spin resonance and scanning tunneling spectroscopy measurements revealed a spin S = ½
for Ti atoms adsorbed on 2 layers of MgO and on bridge site of the 3 layers of MgO while a spin S = 1 is observed for the
atop O site of the 3 layers of MgO. The adsorption site can be manipulated with atomic precision by the STM tip.
Experiments and calculations indicate that the measured spin values are compatible with pristine adsorbed Ti atoms and do
not require hydrogenation of the Ti atoms as previously suggested. 
The experimental results clearly validate the spin values of the different systems combining two different spectroscopic
methods (STS and ESR). The manipulation of the spin by atomic manipulation is well evidenced from the experimental
observations. The novel interpretation of pristine rather than hydrogenated Ti atoms is compatible with the combined
experimental and calculated results. 
Investigations of spin properties of single atoms on thicker decoupling layer are highly interesting due to the possible
increased spin lifetimes and coherence times, which are desirable for the realization of quantum coherent spin structures. 
Most important are the different spin values depending on the adsorption site of the Ti atoms on the 3 layers of MgO and their
reversible switching using atomic manipulation. These observations may paved a way to the fabrication of desired
architectures of spin assemblies with tunable spin values. 
Finally the new interpretation involving pristine rather than hydrogenated Ti atoms is also important for future (and maybe
past) experiments. Accurate description of the electronic structure of the single atoms is mandatory to the interpretation of
more complex systems. 
The manuscript is clearly written and the results are presented and discussed in relation to previous works which are
referenced appropriately. 
I think that the manuscript deserves a publication in Nature Communications but needs to be improved taking in
consideration the following remarks.

Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript of Phark et al. reports on a combined experimental-theoretical study of Spin-State Engineering of Single
Titanium Adsorbates on Ultrathin Magnesium Oxide. The topic is highly relevant for an atomic scale engineering of spin
qubits for the realization of various quantum platforms on material surfaces, and a very nice example of Ti ions on MgO/Ag
substrate are reported. The conducted research is of a very high quality, and the STM/STS and STM-ESR experimental
results of a controlled creation of different spin states of Ti atoms are well supported and explained by DFT calculations.
Two experimental atom manipulation techniques for long- and short-range repositioning are also reported in detail. The
manuscript is well written, though some clarifications/improvements will be needed. The reported new knowledge on the
dependence of the spin states depending on the local adsorption site and oxide film thickness will be highly relevant for the
design of future artificial spin systems with atomic precision. I recommend the manuscript for publication in Nature
Communications after a minor revision considering the following points: 
1. A considerable part of the theoretical results addresses the effect of TiH_x adsorbates in the search for agreement with the
experimental findings, but this aspect of the role of hydrogen is not clearly motivated in the Introduction and throughout the
manuscript. From the current literature it is evident that it is an important problem, but the manuscript should better motivate
this aspect, in my view. It is also somewhat unfortunate to introduce the description and discussion of the hydrogen-related
results with the 1st sentence in III. Theory with "Such a reversible change of spin states is unlikely to be the result of
selective adsorption or desorption of atomic hydrogen (H)." An explicit indication of why it is still studied in much detail in the
following part would be a crucial improvement. 
2. Considering the significant numerical differences in the obtained results by using Quantum Espresso (QE) and VASP
softwares, which are reported and discussed in detail in the Supplementary Information (SI, Table VII), it is not completely
clear how and why the results of Table I were chosen. Moreover, there is an inconsistency between Tables I and VII, please
correct them. It is also reported that all numerical parameters for QE and VASP calculations are identical (except the
differences in the implementations in the two codes), but the reported k-sampling is still different: 3x3x1 for VASP and 4x4x1
for QE, please reformulate your statements accordingly. Overall, the discussion about the compatibility of the QE and VASP
results is confusing in its present state. What do we learn from this? 
3. In Section III.A it is claimed that "... single Ti atom in a positively charged Ti+ configuration, resulting from charge transfer
to the Ag substrate ...". However, there is no direct evidence provided for the negative charge transferred to the Ag substrate.
This should be provided by analyzing the difference of Bader charges before and after adsorbing Ti on the MgO/Ag (\Delta Q
= Q_{Ti+MgOAg} - Q_{Ti} - Q_{MgOAg} and its change on the Ag substrate). The reduced electron work function with
increasing MgO thickness is nicely demonstrated experimentally. Instead of referring to [54], was this not demonstrated by
the performed DFT calculations? Please report the calculated work functions. 
4. To enrich the context of the manuscript considering the charge and spin states of individual atoms on surfaces I
recommend the inclusion of the following references into the Introduction: 
- https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.127.236801 , where a nice experimental example of the effect of the local adsorption
geometry on the spin states of Fe atoms are presented on a Moiré structure platform. 
- https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6528/aaf6dc , where the theoretical limits of SPM subatomic resolution in relation to the
charge/spin states and Hubbard U parameter of a Co atom on an oxide surface are investigated. 
5. Although in Section VII.A in the SI it is stated that "In the following, we present DFT total energy calculations of TiHx ...",
there are no actual total energies reported for the gas phase TiHx in section VII.B (SI). 
6. Fig. S2 is not referred to in the SI text and there is no description of it except its caption, is it intentional? 
7. Some references in the manuscript to the Supplementary Information (SI) are too general, and should be made explicit to
the particular sections in the SI. 
8. Please correct typos throughout the SI, for example "The trend with MgO thickness is also nicely recover" (recovered),
"results points" (results point), "Bader surface" (not Bader volume?), "The d orbitals result in highly localized Wannier
functions (quadratic spread \Omega^2\approx 1 \AA^2), whilst the s-orbital is more diffuse (\Omega^2\approx 3 \AA)"
(Angstrom or squared-Angstrom? Should be consistent).

(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript covers the results in an orderly fashion and it presents evidence explaining the intriguing nature of the spin-
states of Ti adatoms on MgO ultrathin films. The literature on the system of Ti adatoms deposited on 2 monolayers (ML) of
MgO ultrathin films grown on Ag(100) has assumed that Ti adatoms become hydrogenated in ultrahigh vacuum immediately
after deposition, thus becoming TiH molecules. In the early publication, “Engineering the Eigenstates of Coupled Spin-½
Atoms on a Surface” by Kai Yang et al. in 2017, this hypothesis was supported by the following rationale, theoretical
predictions and experimental observations: 
- Tunneling spectroscopy revealed S=½ characteristics on Ti adatoms deposited on 2ML MgO, namely a zero-bias Kondo
resonance. 
- In disagreement, DFT calculations predicted a S=1 for Ti adatoms on 2ML MgO. 
- It is well known that hydrogen is a predominant component of the residual gas in ultrahigh vacuum. Moreover, a high
affinity of Ti for hydrogen has been experimentally observed in the system of Ti adatoms on hexagonal boron nitride. 
- In agreement, DFT calculations predicted a S=½ for hydrogenated Ti adatoms on 2ML MgO. 
Whether the Ti adatoms on 2ML MgO were really getting immediately hydrogenated in ultrahigh vacuum has never been put
into question in the past 8 years. This manuscript submitted by Soo-hyon Phark unveils this mystery while playing for the first
time with the parameter of thickness (or amount of layers) of the MgO ultrathin film.

The manuscript covers the results in an orderly fashion and it presents evidence explaining the

>> Sharp jumps of differential conductance signals in STS spectra have been attributed to the  
opening/closing of inelastic tunneling channels, mostly involving either phonon [Stipe et al. Science  
280,  1732-1735  (1998)]  or  spin  [Heinrich  et  al.  306,  466-469  (2004)]  excitations.  This  type  of 
experimental technique has been standardized in STM with “inelastic tunneling spectroscopy (IETS).  
Spin-IETS using a STM is thoroughly reviewed in a S. Loth’s paper [New J. Phys. 12, 125021 (2010)], 
where one finds 2 representative cases. One is IETS on a spin of no (negligible) magnetic anisotropy  
energy (MAE), e.g., Ti on 2ML-MgO/Ag (S = 1/2) [Yang et al. Phys. Rev. Lett. 122, 227203 (2019)]  
and  Mn  on  CuN  (S  =  5/2)  [Loth  et  al.  Nat.  Phys.  6,  340-344  (2010)].  This  case  allows  IETS 
accompanying only spin excitations between the states, split by Zeeman energy under a magnetic  
field. The resultant STS spectrum shows across the zero-bias a square-shaped gap-like feature,  
originating from the Zeeman energy difference between the two states relevant to the spin excitation.  
The other is IETS on a spin of sizable magnetic anisotropy energy (i.e., E MAE >> EZeeman, only available 
for a spin of S > 1/2), e.g., Fe on 2ML-MgO/Ag (S = 2) or Fe on CuN (S = 2), which easily splits the  
energies of different |m S| by > 1 meV. This case allows IETS corresponding to spin excitations  
between the states, split by MAE, at a rather higher energy, and can be distinguished from that of the  
Zeeman energy splitting at a nominal magnetic field (e.g., a few Tesla) which is < 1 meV, e.g.,  ~0.1  
meV for S = 1/2 at 1 Tesla. One well-known example is the feature at |V b| = 14 meV in the spectrum  
of Fe/MgO [Baumann et al. Phys. Rev. Lett. 115, 237202 (2015)], as also shown in the reply to the  
comment 4 of the referee #1. We believe that a sharp jump at |Vb| = 18 meV in our spectrum of TiO(3) 
also stems from a similar origin.
