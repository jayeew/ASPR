REVIEWER COMMENTS: 
Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
Review of “Impacts of Weathering during Glacial Retreat on North Atlantic Nd Isotopic Records” by Salinas-Reyes et al.
(submitted to Communications Earth & Environment) 
This manuscript investigates weathering dynamics of Nd in a deglaciating landscape in southwestern Greenland. The
authors document systematic shifts in dissolved and particulate Nd isotope compositions with exposure time, suggesting a
transition from incongruent to congruent weathering farther from the ice sheet. The study represents an important
contribution to understanding continental weathering signals in Nd isotope records and could help resolve uncertainties
regarding North Atlantic authigenic Nd records. However, before publication, the authors should address the following key
issues: 
1. Mineralogy and Petrology 
The authors attribute incongruent weathering to detrital mineralogy, but direct mineralogical evidence is lacking. For
instance: 
• Allanite abundance: Does it actually decrease with distance from the ice sheet? 
• Grain-size fractionation: Is mineral composition correlated with particle size in a way that supports their conclusions? 
Additionally, the petrological context is unclear: 
• Bedrock composition: Is the study area dominated by granites, or are there sedimentary rocks or igneous intrusions?
Provenance heterogeneity could complicate interpretations—are the observed trends truly time-dependent weathering
signals, or could they reflect spatial variations in source rocks? 
One puzzling observation is the increase in sand fraction with distance from the ice sheet, which seems counterintuitive
given that prolonged weathering typically reduces grain size. The authors should discuss possible explanations. 
2. North Atlantic Marine Records 
While I agree with the authors’ emphasis on the role of detrital input in shaping North Atlantic Nd records, their discussion
oversimplifies several nuances: 
• Provenance heterogeneity: The Canadian Shield is only one of several major sediment sources (e.g., Iceland, Europe).
How do these competing inputs influence regional seawater εNd? 
• Temporal variability: The highly non-radiogenic εNd during Heinrich events and deglaciation clearly reflects Canadian
Shield detritus, but does this also apply to the Holocene and LGM? It is not clear if these records are truly in equilibrium with
sediments, and if so, which sources dominate? 
• Spatial constraints: Non-radiogenic εNd spikes are typically confined to the IRD belt (30–60°N), yet the Bermuda Rise
cores lie outside this zone. Is the Shield’s influence a localized seafloor diagenetic effect, or does it affect basin-wide
seawater evolution? 
Fig1: I suggest indicate the study region in the map. 
Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author): 
Review report to the manuscript entitled “Impacts of Weathering during Glacial Retreat on North Atlantic Nd Isotopic
Records” submitted to Communications Earth & Environment by Salinas-Reyes et al. 
The authors address a interesting topic that has been raised in several studies without definitive answer: where does the
transient pulse of less radiogenic Nd come from, which is observed in Northwest Atlantic deglacial deep sea records, and
how is it caused? While the theory that they originate from reactive material delivered by retreating ice sheets has been
postulated repeatedly since at least Howe et al. 2016, direct mechanistic evidence has been missing and the issue has
resurfaced again and again and caused complications in the interpretations of authigenic deep ocean Nd isotope records. 
I thus think this study tackles an interesting and relevant topic and helps to definitively answer this question. Furthermore, it
offers insights that may lead to the exploitation of such observations for improved ice sheet and ocean circulation
reconstructions. The fact that the observations used for their study come from a deglaciating landscape from Greenland – a
type of landscape emerging even more in the future – increases the study’s relevance and elegance. 
I found reading the manuscript generally easy, albeit I suggest some important rephrasing which I think would improve the
readability further. I have no reason to doubt the high quality of the data and I generally agree with the data presentation,
interpretation and the suggested implications. I have few comments and suggestions, and think this is a high quality piece of
work that requires little revision before potential publication. 
I have two main suggestions on how to make the study more relevant: 
1) The “early Holocene εNd anomaly” from NW Atlantic deep ocean authigenic εNd records has been investigated in more
detail and disentangled into a local signal and a deep water-transported signal by Pöppelmeier et al. (2019) (also based on
Pöppelmeier et al. 2018 and many others). I am thus wondering whether elaborating on this could enhance the study? For
example, assuming a similar circulation and Nd regime during the early Holocene as today, the authors could probably give

a crude estimate of the amount of Nd added to (lower) NADW to decrease its εNd by the 1.4 units mentioned in Pöppelmeier

we showed in Pöppelmeier et al. (2019) (Fig. 7) is intriguing and seems relevant enough that it could at the very least be

2) It is mentioned that the same processes that lead to the emission of less radiogenic Nd likely also led to radiogenic Pb in

would explain the radiogenic Pb isotope excursions observed in NW Atlantic sediments would strengthen the main point of
the study and further increase its significance because of the better generalisation. 
Similarly, we investigated the REE patterns associated with these unradiogenic Nd isotope signatures in Blaser et al.
(2020). I am wondering whether elaborating more about the REE patterns could also lead to more details in the mechanisms
of the dispersion of this pulse of early weathering during the deglaciation? I don’t think the authors should massively
elaborate as this would inflate the text, but some elaboration, possibly with the help of supplementary figures, could be
helpful. 
Apart from these two suggestions, another main point is linguistic in nature and might be partially personal feeling, so the
authors (who I think are native speakers as opposed to myself) should carefully weigh whether they follow my
recommendations here. I repeatedly stumbled over phrases which I think are not accurate or cumbersome when we discuss
Nd isotopes. This may be a personal issue, but I would like to ask the authors to consider my linguistic thoughts elaborated
further in the detailed comments below. Amending these issues would make the manuscript significantly more easy to read,
at least for me.

Reviewer #3 (Remarks to the Author): 
In their manuscript “Impacts of Weathering during Glacial Retreat on North Atlantic Nd Isotope Records” submitted to
Communications Earth & Environment, Salinas-Reyes and co-authors present dissolved and particulate Nd isotopes from a
transect of 6 sites within a deglaciating landscape in Svalbard representing a range of exposure ages from ice to coast (~
160 km). They demonstrate a trend towards more radiogenic values with increasing distance from the ice edge (older
exposure) for both dissolved and particulate material and show that the offset between solid and dissolved is greatest
nearest the ice. From this, the authors conclude incongruent weathering has an important role in the more recently exposed
watersheds and with time the shift towards more congruent dissolution, at least in terms of εNd, results in a more consistent
signature between the two phases. This has potentially major implications on the use of εNd as a paleoceanographic tracer,
as the signature of both seawater and the sediment could hypothetically be changed solely by a shift in weathering regime. 
Overall, this is a novel dataset investigating a how changes in weathering, in this case associated with proximity to the ice
margin, impacts the εNd of both the dissolved and solid phases coming off the continent with implications to
paleoreconstructions in terms of being able to change εNd with no change in water mass movements. The main story, the
dissolved and the detrital εNd with distance from ice edge is well thought out and thoroughly developed. I have no critiques
of this part, and am excited to see this data add to the conversation in the literature. However, equal care does not seem to
be given to the authigenic part of the story. I do believe the authors are likely correct in proposing “that North Atlantic
authigenic εNd values result from seawater-sediment interactions, as influenced by the reactivity and supply of comminuted
Precambrian detritus,” and there is precedent for this conclusion from Bermuda Rise and further north in the Atlantic (i.e.
Abbott et al. 2022 which is oddly not cited within given the relevance). While I think this idea is well founded, the
presentation of support for these ideas in the manuscript in its current form I find problematic and thus my recommendation is
that the manuscript only be accepted after moderate revisions. 
A big part of my concern over the authigenic story here comes from the presentation in Figure 1. The seemingly cherry-
picked at worst, and incomplete at best, authigenic data in Figure 1 is misleading. “Authigenic” is used to indicate any
number of protocols to get at “sea water” signatures from sediment leaches, to fish teeth to forams – if they all showed the
same thing in this region, the ambiguity may be ok, but especially at Bermuda Rise the choice of authigenic phase makes a
big difference and by choosing to only show the forams that mimic the detrital record is incomplete to say the least. The
“authigenic” data shown appear to be the foram record from Roberts (more on the citation a bit later), but both the authigenic
sediment leaches of Roberts et al. 2010 and Abbott et al. 2022 from the same site look quite different from the forams.
Meanwhile the data presented for Orphan Knoll (OK) appears to be mainly sediment leaches (Filippova reference) and the
Baffin Island Shelf (BIS) similarly appears to include many sediment leaches with potentially a few forams mixed in
(Filippova reference). The references for figure 1 also get a bit sloppy, the detrital data shown is, by the looks, a mix of data
from Böhm et al. (cited clearly) and other references within that paper. The cited Roberts 2012 paper is not listed on any
major site (Science Direct etc) but the data appears to be from her Roberts et al. 2010 paper? 
-Roberts NL, Piotrowski AM, McManus JF, and Keigwin LD (2010) Synchronous Deglacial Overturning and Water Mass
Source Changes, Science, 327, 75-78. 
-Abbott AN, Löhr SC, Payne A, Kumar H, and Du J (2022) Widespread lithogenic control of marine authigenic neodymium
isotopes records? Implications for paleoceanographic reconstructions, Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta, 319, 318-336. 
I suggest the authors be very careful, and very transparent, in their presentation of this data in the revised manuscript.
Potentially, the difference between behavior of the various authigenic phases can lend further support to their argument but
as is, the data is misrepresenting what is known about authigenic phases in the North Atlantic since the LGM. Further, in its
current presentation I don’t think they demonstrate that the authigenic record is a reliable tracer of weathering processes as
they claim in the conclusions – but if the issues around the transparency of authigenic data choices are resolved, this may
also become supported. 
Overall, again I think the authors conducted a really interesting study that is novel and worthy of publication following
moderate revisions to address data ambiguity and some referencing gaps.

REVIEWERS' COMMENTS: 
Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
The manuscript has been improved by the revision. But some points raised is the previous review were not fully addressed
and I hope the authors could take this opportunity to clarify. 
1. I appreciate that the authors added discussion on how variations in the grain size were partially driven by fluvial transport
in response to my previous question. But this raises the issue that to what degree the observed eNd offset between
dissolved and bed load truly reflect chemical weathering and chemical equilibrium, instead of removal of the reactive phases
by transport. The ensuing paleo discussion were founded on the principle that it is chemical weathering that drives the
observed offset. If physical transport dominates then the story would be different. 
2. As noted in previous comments, the authigenic records can only show if sedimentary authigenic phases and detrital
sediments can reach chemical equilibrium, they cannot tell if seawater can reach equilibrium with the detrital phase, given
authigenic phases, even foram, likely do not directly record seawater eNd in the North Atlantic. Although I agree that
seawater eNd will proceed to chemical equilibrium with the reactive detrital phase during weak circulation, it would be too
strong a conclusion to argue that true equilibrium can be achieved based on existing data. What happens on the seafloor is
analogous to what happens in the watershed, but it would be difficult to prove that the relevant timescales are the same
considering that marine weathering and continental weathering are so different. 
L122: This paragraph repeated discussions in the preceding paragraph. These two paragraphs should be simplified to
remove redundancy. 
L144: This paragraph lacks proper reference to literature. These processes have been investigated before. That sediment-
seawater interactions controls the eNd of NADW is traced back to Jeandel and colleagues’ classic work on modern NADW
(e.g., Lacan and Jeandel, 2005) and has been affirmed in studies of authigenic eNd records of NADW, which should be
acknowledge, as is the idea that exposure time and reactive phases control sediment-seawater interactions (Wilson et al.,
2013; Abbott et al., 2015; Blaser et al., 2019; Du et al., 2020; Jaume-Seguí et al., 2021, to name a few).

Reviewer #1  
This manuscript investigates weathering dynamics of Nd in a deglaciating landscape in 
southwestern Greenland. The authors document systematic shifts in dissolved and particulate 
Nd isotope compositions with exposure time, suggesting a transition from incongruent to 
congruent weathering farther from the ice sheet. The study represents an important 
contribution to understanding continental weathering signals in Nd isotope records and could 
help resolve uncertainties regarding North Atlantic authigenic Nd records. However, before 
publication, the authors should address the following key issues: 
We appreciate Reviewer 1’s interest in our work and they raise interesting questions about the 
identification of which minerals are preferentially removed by weathering across the Greenland 
transect. We have a second manuscript in preparation that delves into this question using a data 
set that includes Sr, Nd, and Pb isotopes and concentrations and the REE patterns for mineral 
separates, as well as the mineralogical composition of sand and silt grain size fractions from our 
Greenland sediment samples. Those data and the arguments they support are too extensive to 
include in this paper and although they support our inference here, they would detract from our 
primary message about how Nd isotopes vary with weathering across the landscape and how 
those variations can contribute to our understanding of changes in authigenic Nd isotopes in the 
North Atlantic. However, we have published the data in the Arctic Data Center (ADC) repository, 
and provide the citation to that dataset (Salinas-Reyes et al., 2025). To retain focus on the 
primary message of the current manuscript, we have presented enough of these data in 
supplementary figures to support our claims, while guiding readers to the full data set.

evidence is lacking. For instance:

(coastal) based on data that are available in the published ADC database (Salinas-Reyes et al.,

• Grain-size fractionation: Is mineral composition correlated with particle size in a way that 
supports their conclusions?

• Provenance heterogeneity: The Canadian Shield is only one of several major sediment sources

Lines 180-186 (217-225), 214-217 (257-263). We describe relative contributions of different 
sources at different times. Slow circulation (high residence time) and limited sediment inputs 
during the LGM, following a major event of shield-derived sediment input (H2), would favor the 
approach to chemical equilibrium between the seawater and sediment. Throughout the Holocene, 
the decreasing influx of shield-derived sediments as they become trapped in fjords and the

definitive answer: where does the transient pulse of less radiogenic Nd come from, which is 
observed in Northwest Atlantic deglacial deep sea records, and how is it caused? While the 
theory that they originate from reactive material delivered by retreating ice sheets has been 
postulated repeatedly since at least Howe et al. 2016, direct mechanistic evidence has been 
missing and the issue has resurfaced again and again and caused complications in the 
interpretations of authigenic deep ocean Nd isotope records.

this question. Furthermore, it offers insights that may lead to the exploitation of such 
observations for improved ice sheet and ocean circulation reconstructions. The fact that the

1. I appreciate that the authors added discussion on how variations in the grain size were partially

what degree the observed eNd offset between dissolved and bed load truly reflect chemical 
weathering and chemical equilibrium, instead of removal of the reactive phases by transport. The 
ensuing paleo discussion were founded on the principle that it is chemical weathering that drives 
the observed offset. If physical transport dominates then the story would be different. 
We think the offset between dissolved and bedload ɛNd in ice-proximal watersheds clearly 
reflects preferential chemical weathering of phases with low ɛNd during early exposure. The 
negative excursions in the authigenic record following documented pulses of sediment into the 
North Atlantic are consistent with this weathering reaction. However, we recognize that the 
decreasing dissolved-bedload ɛNd offset results from a combination of chemical weathering and 
export as suspended load of reactive phases. Unfortunately, our data are insufficient to 
deconvolve the contributions from each of these processes to the depletion of reactive phases 
across the transect. However, we consider that our observations of Nd isotopes across the 
transect still illustrate reactions and processes that occur in the North Atlantic and shape the 
authigenic ɛNd record, as described in the discussion. In addition, we have modified the end of 
the section “ɛNd variations across the transect” (Lines 92-96) to clarify that it is unlikely these 
processes (e.g., dissolution of soluble phases and winnowing of fine-grained sediment) reach 
equilibrium as pointed out by the reviewer, they will change toward equilibrium with the new 
environmental conditions caused by the loss of the ice sheets. We included citations to previous 
literature where this idea of disequilibrium and shift toward equilibrium in deglaciated 
landscapes is discussed.  
2. As noted in previous comments, the authigenic records can only show if sedimentary 
authigenic phases and detrital sediments can reach chemical equilibrium, they cannot tell if 
seawater can reach equilibrium with the detrital phase, given authigenic phases, even foram, 
likely do not directly record seawater eNd in the North Atlantic. Although I agree that seawater 
eNd will proceed to chemical equilibrium with the reactive detrital phase during weak 
circulation, it would be too strong a conclusion to argue that true equilibrium can be achieved 
based on existing data. What happens on the seafloor is analogous to what happens in the 
watershed, but it would be difficult to prove that the relevant timescales are the same considering 
that marine weathering and continental weathering are so different.

Reviewer #3 
In their revised manuscript “Impacts of Weathering during Glacial Retreat on North Atlantic Nd 
Isotope Records” submitted to Communications Earth & Environment, Salinas-Reyes and co-
authors present dissolved and particulate Nd isotopes from a transect of 6 sites within a 
deglaciating landscape in Svalbard representing a range of exposure ages from ice to coast (~ 
160 km). They demonstrate a trend towards more radiogenic values with increasing distance 
from the ice edge (older exposure) for both dissolved and particulate material and show that the 
offset between solid and dissolved is greatest nearest the ice. From this, the authors conclude 
incongruent weathering has an important role in the more recently exposed watersheds and with 
time the shift towards more congruent dissolution, at least in terms of εNd, results in a more 
consistent signature between the two phases. This has potentially major implications on the use 
of εNd as a paleoceanographic tracer, as the signature of both seawater and the sediment could 
hypothetically be changed solely by a shift in weathering regime. The authors did an excellent 
job at addressing reviewer and concerns and now are much more transparent in their presentation 
of authigenic data and ties to their new transect in the revised manuscript. I think the authors 
conducted a really interesting study that is novel and worthy of publication. 
We appreciate the reviewer’s feedback and thank them for their valuable contributions to 
improving the manuscript.
