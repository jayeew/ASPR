REVIEWER COMMENTS: 
Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
Review of ‘Rewiring climate modeling with machine learning emulators’ 
I really wanted to like this paper. The message the paper puts forward, on the importance of surrogate models, or emulators,
and their use in simulating future climates and particularly climate impacts is important and dear to my heart. But in its
current form I think it is badly flawed, and I cannot recommend it for publication. The flaw comes from the authors concept of
what a surrogate model is. They are very dismissive of Gaussian process emulators (GPs), putting them in the same
category as polynomials and linear regression surrogates. They seem to think that the only statistical/ML models that can be
used as emulators are neural nets, although they don’t explicitly state this. They claim that GPs do not scale well (true but
there are ways around it – see for example Baker, E., Harper, A. B., Williamson, D. & Challenor, P. Emulation of high-
resolution land surface models using sparse Gaussian processes with application to JULES. Geosci Model Dev 15, 1913–
1929 (2022).) and that they are ‘linear’ which they are not. There is no mention of deep GPs which extend range of functions
GPs can fit, allowing for non-stationarity and step changes, see Ming, D., Williamson, D. and Guillas, S. (2022) Deep
Gaussian Process Emulation using Stochastic Imputation. Technometrics, 65, 150–161. Williamson and co-workers have
been using them very successfully as surrogates for models of forests in the UK but I cannot find any references. Neural nets
(NNs) certainly have a place in surrogate modelling, but have many disadvantages compared to GPs. They need vastly
more data, are difficult to understand and explain and do not readily produce an estimate of the additional uncertainty arising
from the emulation. A number of the papers they cite approvingly such as Edwards use GPs rather than NNs which given the
tone of the rest of the paper is rather odd. To be suitable for publication the paper needs to be much more even handed
between the different types of emulators. 
The other major issue I have with the paper is its discussion of experimental design. Building an emulator is dependent on
good design. We need to cover the space of possible inputs with the minimum number of model runs, as they are expensive,
but we do want the emulator to be valid everywhere. There is a large literature on this. The current CMIP designs are quite
inadequate for the task of building reliable emulators. The authors talk about co-design but with little discussion of what this
means. In my opinion it will mean the much bigger changes in the convention ‘MiP’ designs than in the ‘emulator designs.
There are just too few scenarios (even with the unrealistic ones mentioned in the papers) and the gaps are too big to fill!
When I have discussed filling such gaps I have always been told that I cannot generate new emission pathways as these
need socio-economic pathways to back them up. And for similar reasons you cannot integrate across possible future
pathways. I don’t expect the authors to solve these issues, but I think that a discussion paper such as this should at least
raise them for debate. 
As it currently stands, I don’t think this paper is suitable for publication. But I do think it is fixable. If they address the above
points I think it could be a significant contribution to the climate impact literature.

Major comments: 
-The authors mention traditional statistical emulators and their inability to scale to higher dimensions after which they focus
on a specific subset of emulators used to simulate processes from physical models (for example ACE2 and BGC-UNet).
Especially when considering arguments of scenario co-development and the actual use of emulators in previous IPCC
reports this is not representative of the foundational use-case of emulators: scalable methods to rapidly assess different
climate scenarios within bounds of interpolation. I believe this remains a fundamental miscommunication link between
physical- and impact- oriented emulator developers. I recommend that the author re-visit this phrasing to consider these two
aspects and diverging use-cases of emulators instead of purely focussing on physical emulators as replacement to model
components/parameterisation schemes which are yet to be demonstrated in informing climate risks. 
-Taking the first point further, particularly in the case of scenario co-development while higher emission scenarios would be
interesting, it would be worth considering and mentioning overshoot scenarios. There are still important physical questions
of climate hysteresis and whether DL can learn this.

concrete. While the author provides good examples as to where the field is headed in this, it would strengthen the argument
to identify critical requirements and intricacies needed in climate model/emulator benchmarking. Should these emulators be
compared to General Circulation Models? How can extrapolation guarantees and generalisability to out-of-sample be
appropriately tested, do we need to have community-defined iconic events? How will we reconcile aspects of representing
extremes and conserving natural laws across the various use-cases of emulators within the field of climate, or should this be
decentralized? 
Minor comments: 
-Second paragraph of intro, “In this Perspective” de-capitalise perspective 
-Second paragraph of intro, saying do it a million and billion times faster could be made a bit less hyperbolic (perhaps this is
personal choice)

This manuscript proposes to build on existing developments in climate emulators and machine 
learning to enhance several aspects of climate modelling. The developments over the past 
years suggest that there is indeed a potentia l, that would accelerate the exploration of 
scenarios, uncertainties and sensitivities. The manuscript describes three key priorities  to 
facilitate this innovation. 
I think that this work is an important and timely contribution, worthy of Communications Earth 
& Environment . Yet, I did notice some issues that would need to be addressed before 
publication. I detail them below, but here is a summary: 
1. The need for pursuing modelling efforts in Earth System Models (ESMs) is not mentioned at 
all. Yet, even if  emulators replace components, even with a co-design of simula tors and 
emulators, emulators still need to be train ed on data , usually from simulators.  Modelling 
efforts, e.g. on the representation of processes, must  still occur  to ensure a continuous 
development of both simulators and emulators. I think that it should be mentioned. 
2. There is a lack of clarity on which emulators are defined, despite Box 1. I see 4 categories of 
climate emulators, (1) simple climate models/reduced -complexity Earth system models, 
(2) spatial climate emulators, (3) emulators of modules of the Earth system and (4) dynamic 
downscaling). Weather generators are not exactly about climate as mentioned in Box 1. This 
box does mention (1) quickly, doesn’t mention (4), and seems to merge (2) and (3) . If so, it 
should be more explicit. 
3. Throughout the text, ML -based emulators are presented as superior to traditional  climate 
emulators in terms of  accuracy, computational speed and flexibility . (Lütjens et al., 2025)  
shows that simple pattern scaling ma y outperform, the manuscript mentions this work but 
concludes that the issue is fixed simply with the number of realizations. However, this is not 
the conclusion presented in (Lütjens et al., 2025) , which I quote: “Our work serves as a 
cautionary tale, highlighting the possibility of overlooking simpler techniques that may 
achieve comparable or superior performance.” Furthermore, this manuscript reduces these 
traditional approaches to linear pattern scaling, while the re are many , many  other 
approaches, including several that include  non-linear effects . Finally, this manuscript  
presents many capacities as enabled  by ML-based emulators, while they were already 
explored with other emulators beforehand. Overall, I recommend giving a more balanced 
perspective on climate emulators here. I fully acknowledge the immense potential of ML -
based emulators, but other emulators remain useful.  
 
Major points 1:

ESMs were replaced with targeted emulators, we would need to ensure that this progress still  
happens. In my opinion, the goal  (1) would need to acknowledge that we would still need ESMs
