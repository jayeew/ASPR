Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
I have read the response letter and the revised manuscript carefully. I think the main concern has been addressed and the
current version can be published on Nature Communications. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
I appreciate the authors' detailed response to my comments. Most of my comments are now addressed. However, I suggest
that the authors avoid overemphasizing the simplicity of the device and if possible, provide a discussion on how to further
explore the potential of the high refractive index of the TMDCs used to improve the device design freedom to enhance the
randomness and diversity of the spectral transmittance curves. 
The acquisition of as many uncorrelated encoded response curves as possible remains a timeless pursuit in the field of
computational spectroscopy, and this demand becomes even more stringent when extending towards broadband operation.
The authors' current approach of tuning material thickness and leveraging reflections at the SU-8/InGaAs photodetector
interface is interesting. However, it must be noted that the degrees of freedom offered by this single-dimensional tuning
remain limited. It is difficult to convince readers that the current work can foresee richer response curves, making it
challenging to meet the requirements of broadband spectroscopy. Consequently, the authors should at least provide a
discussion on how to systematically introduce higher device design degrees of freedom to further diversify the spectral
transmittance curves. Furthermore, a brief exploration of potential fabrication pathways and challenges for the possibly
required, more complex structures would be highly beneficial. This is crucial for ultimately achieving high-performance
broadband spectroscopy. Given the intrinsic advantage of the high refractive index of TMDC materials, such an outlook
based on structural innovation would be highly compelling.

Authors' Response: 
We appreciate the reviewer's positive evaluations of our manuscript.  In response to 
reviewer's constructive suggestions, we have made the following modifications. First, we have 
deleted the description of device's simplicity. Second, we have provided an analysis on how to 
further explore the potential for enhancing the randomness and diversity of the spectral 
transmittance curves. Third, a brief exploration of potential fabrication pathways and 
challenges about enhancing the randomness and diversity is provided. The detailed description 
has been incorporated into the revised manuscript, as indicated in the highlighted text below.  
To further enhance the randomness and diversity of spectral responsivity in future 
implementations, one could substitute the  SU-8 polymer with a material possessing greater 
intrinsic dispersion, or employ nanopatterned TMDCs with superior dispersion characteristics. 
However, the latter approach  is critically dependent on high -precision nanofabrication  with 
exacting process tolerances , presenting a major obstacle to the monolithic integration  of 
spectral encoders with photodetectors. Therefore, acquisition of spectral encoder with spectral 
diversity, high light throughput, and broad bandwidth remains a timeless pursuit.
