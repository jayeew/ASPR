Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
I thank the authors for their efforts in addressing my questions and revising the manuscript. They have responded thoroughly
by incorporating updated results and adding relevant explanations, primarily in the Discussion section. The new data are
more robust than in the original submission, and the expanded discussion provides clearer context and more nuanced
interpretations. Overall, I am satisfied with how my concerns have been addressed. 
However, one issue remains regarding the Ramachandran outliers. Although the authors mention in their response that this
has been revised, the final model table and the PDB validation report still contain the outlier numbers. I double-checked two
models—the consensus structure and substate 5—and both still show 3–5 outliers, which was not addressed in the rebuttal.
This point appears to have been overlooked. 
Additionally, one of my earlier questions regarding the five substates remains unanswered. Do the authors believe that these
five substates represent all major conformational intermediates? Could larger cryo-EM datasets reveal additional states that
are currently missing? 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
AMPA receptors made of GluA4 subunit are calcium permeable and expressed in PV positive interneurons and cerebellum,
where they express distinct gating characteristics and synaptic plasticity. The gating mechanism of this important AMPA
receptor subtype is incompletely understood. Hale et al. investigates this problem by reporting the open gate cryo-EM
structures of GluA4 flip isoform with TARPgamme2 auxiliary subunit. The construct design follows their previously
established GluA2-TARPgamma2 fusion. The activated open gate conformations were captured by applying CTZ and
glutamate. The complex was extracted from the cells using DDM/total brain lipid and then exchanged to GDN to improve
stability for cryo-EM analysis, an optimal condition that is different from other AMPAR subunits. 
The work has been revised thoroughly in response to previous reviews. The cryo-EM maps and models are improved. The
new data is provided on the single channel physiology in reconstituted lipid bilayer of the GluA4-TARPgamma2, showing
four conductance states with or without physiological concentration of Ca2+. They demonstrate Ca2+ reduces the
conductance of all four sub conductance states, and at the same time alter the occupancy among those states. They
suggest, based on high occupancy of the open channels in O3 and O4, that the cryo-EM structures captured must be in
those sub conductance states. 
The structures of the NTDs are carefully analyzed. The NTDs form dimer-of-dimer assembly but exhibit extensive structural
heterogeneity in the way the NTD layer is positioned relative to the rest of the complex. Despite these variety, they appear to
have little impact on the organization of the LBDs and the TMD. 
The structural characterization of the M3 gate kink is clearly described. 
One of the significant findings is that the kink conformation relates to the way four activated LBDs are spatially arranged. The

We are excited that the reviewers agree that our manuscript has been improved during the revision process. 
We address their points below.  
 
 
Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
 
I thank the authors for their efforts in addressing my questions and revising the manuscript. They have responded 
thoroughly by incorporating updated results and adding relevant explanations, primarily in the Discussion section. 
The new data are more robust than in the original submission, and the expanded discussion provides clearer context 
and more nuanced interpretations. Overall, I am satisfied with how my concerns have been addressed. 
However, one issue remains regarding the Ramachandran outliers. Although the authors mention in their response 
that this has been revised, the final model table and the PDB validation report still contain the outlier numbers. I 
double-checked two models—the consensus structure and substate 5—and both still show 3–5 outliers, which was 
not addressed in the rebuttal. This point appears to have been overlooked. 
Additionally, one of my earlier questions regarding the five substates remains unanswered. Do the authors believe 
that these five substates represent all major conformational intermediates? Could larger cryo-EM datasets reveal 
additional states that are currently missing? 
 
Response: Thank you for the comments, and for the enthusiasm toward our revision. 
 
Regarding the model outliers, these outliers reflect the quality of our cryo-EM data and the fact that 
experimental modeling is not perfect. These outliers are minimal compared to similar studies (e.g., 
Gangwar...Sobolevsky NComms 2024; Kang…Furukawa Nature 2025; Mondal…Twomey Nature 2025; 
Wang…Twomey Nature 2025), and having perfect geometry in any cryo-EM based study would not accurately 
reflect local resolution differences in cryo-EM data nor experimental variation (e.g., Richardson…Richardson 
2018 PMID: 29533239).  
 
In our experimental conditions, these substates represent the major conformational intermediates. We agree 
that collecting additional data to populate rarer states would enable reconstruction. However, it is impossible 
to project the dataset size that this would require. We edited the text accordingly to make these points more 
clear.
