Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript by Peng et al. presents the near-atomic resolution cryo-EM structure of Brochothrix thermosphacta
siphophage NF5, complemented by cryo-ET snapshots of infection. The structural dataset is impressive: the authors have
resolved the head, neck, tail, and baseplate, built atomic models for 11 proteins, and complemented their near atomic
cryoEM structure with cryoET tomograms of phages attached to host cells. This work significantly expands structural
coverage of Gram-positive infecting siphophages, which remain underrepresented compared to the well-studied Gram-
negative systems such as λ, T5, and chi. 
The technical achievement involved in solving such a large and complex assembly, and the cryo-ET data are a valuable
complement that help place the purified structures into a cellular context is impressive. However, I found the paper to be
primarily descriptive, with much interpretation done in light of structural similarities to other phages and pointing out some
differences, but offering limited new mechanistic insight. The work is certainly publishable in principle, but the authors need
to better articulate what new insights, mechanistically or otherwise, result from this work. 
Major Concerns 
Novelty / Mechanistic Insight 
Most interpretations (e.g., role of gp17 as lysin, gp19 as a fused BppU/RBP, Tal-mediated triggering of DNA release) are
inferred from homology or analogy to other siphophages. 
There is little direct mechanistic advance beyond descriptive comparison. The paper would benefit from the authors
explicitly explainingwhat is new here — whether in evolutionary terms (e.g., unique domain fusions, Gram-positive specific
adaptations), infection biology, or architectural principles. 
Use of the Term “in situ” 
The manuscript describes a “near-atomic in situ structure.” This is misleading. The near-atomic reconstructions are from
purified virions (single-particle cryo-EM), while the in situ data (cryo-ET of infected cells) are much lower resolution. This
should be clarified in the text. 
Interpretation of Cryo-ET 
Figure 5 shows interesting tomograms, but the resolution is limited and no subtomogram averaging is performed. The claim
of capturing distinct conformational states of infection should be tempered or better supported with quantification. 
Minor Concerns 
The comparative analysis of baseplate proteins is useful, but in places the language may overstate the evolutionary
implications. Stronger support from sequence-based phylogeny would make these points more convincing.

Some figures (especially tomography panels) are difficult to interpret; improved annotation would help readers unfamiliar
with cryo-ET. 
The text is dense with structural detail, which may overwhelm non-specialists; a simplified schematic or table summarizing
key similarities/differences across phages could help. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Peng et al present novel cryo-em structures of siphophage NF5, which infects a gram-positive bacterium B.t. implicated in
meat spoilage. The host is related to Listeria, which play a major role in food poisoning. The work combines SPA of purified
phage particles with in-situ cryo-ET of infected bacterial cells. 
This work adds to a growing number of cryo-EM structures of siphophages, in particular ones infecting gram-positive
bacteria. As such it is relevant and stands out. 
Despite a few grammatical errors, the manuscript is written well. Minimal background is provided, and the most relevant
literature is cited. The description of the observed structures in the results section is adequate. 
I would expect this paper to advance the knowledge about siphophages infecting gram-positive bacteria. 
However, there are some unjustified or insufficiently supported claims that should be rectified: 
1. The authors claim at multiple points throughout the text, including in the abstract, that they present “an in situ near-atomic
resolution structure”. This is incorrect – only the in-vitro purified single particles averaged page structures are at “near-
atomic” resolution. But they are not in-situ. The in-situ tomograms are far from near-atomic resolution. This claim must be
removed. 
2. While the cryo-ET contributes one of the most interesting aspects – the infection process – it only received a short
paragraph in the results section. Nevertheless this is mentioned as a highlight (e.g. line 72, 85, etc). Was there only a single
tomogram? It would be desirable to expand this section and describe these observations in greater detail. The conclusions
summarized in the proposed model of infection rely almost entirely on this one tomogram, while details of the described re-
arranged tail-fibers and the claimed putative channel for DNA translocation are not clearly visible in the presented figure 5.
Have the authors tried segmentation of these tomograms? The claimed structural rearrangements should be shown at a
greater level of detail to support these conclusions. Otherwise, the model in 5E remains very speculative.

phage host recognition, and in NF5, they are connected to the baseplate through the 
DTP. By comparing the DTP structures of siphophages infecting Gram -negative and 
Gram-positive bacteria, we propose that the st ructural differences in bacterial cell 
walls uncover the divergence of key structural proteins between these two groups of 
siphophages. This finding reveals the host specific adaptations of structural proteins 
in Gram -positive infecting siphophages, which remain less well understood.  These 
explanations have been presented in the discussion sections (lines 412-416).

siphophages have been well characterized through structural analyses of phage –
receptor structures and cryo -ET (Sci Adv. 2023;9(12):eade9674 ; Nat Commun.
