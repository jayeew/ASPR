Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
Review for COMMSENV-25-1969 
Zhang et al. present a detailed investigation of early Cambrian K-bentonites from four sections in Yangtze and Baoshao
blocks. They conclude a super-eruption event that can transfer abundant phosphorus to the ocean and contribute to the
extinction of first stage of Cambrian explosion. While I am not a specialist in the field of geochronology, the interpretations
regarding the source and the depositional time of K-bentonites appear generally sound and convincing. However, evidence
for the relationship between volcano eruption and biological extinction is weak (see major comments below).Given that this
constitutes the primary novelty of the study, the authors should significantly strengthen this aspect before the manuscript can
be considered for publication. 
Major comments: 
(1)The authors should clarify the definition, scale (global or regional), timing and different opinions about the geological
events they mention, such as ‘Strangelove ocean’ (Line 14), ‘the earliest Phanerozoic oceanic crisis’ (Line 17), ‘two pulses
of the Cambrian explosion’ (Line 30), ‘the earliest Phanerozoic extinction’ (Line 42). For example, the Sinsk event, 5 Ma
younger than 520 Ma, was proposed to be the first major extinction (Myrow et al., 2024 Sci. Adv.). How is it related with the
‘Strangelove ocean’? 
(2)As shown in Fig.1 and Fig.5, it seems that the location of K-bentonites between the two phases of Cambrian explosion is
only shown in the Meishucun section, while there is no corresponding biological change in the other sections. Please add
more details about the timeline of the biological evolution, as well as the evolution of redox conditions, in both the main text
and figures. Moreover, how is this eruption event recorded in the Yangtze Gorges Area that preserves abundant fossils?
Even there is no study section from this area, it is important to include a comprehensive discussion for a CEE paper. 
(3)The main connection between the eruption and biological extinction, suggested by the authors, is abundant release of
nutrients (e.g., P). It is nice they try to calculate the P flux, but only absolute value (15.5 Tmol) is not very useful. 1000 years
of modern P input can also reach this value. The authors should either present P flux in unit of mol/y, or compare the total P
flux with the values from other well-studied volcanic event, phytoplankton bloom event, anoxia event or extinction event.
These comparisons can also be noted in Fig. 11. In addition, is there any other proxy (e.g., biomarker? TOC?) supporting

Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author): 
The manuscript has undergone another round of revisions, which have addressed certain issues. However, the core
scientific problem has not been accurately identified or resolved—namely, the contradiction between the massive release of
phosphorus and the 'Strangelove ocean' period, considered to be nearly devoid of life. Significant content and structural
adjustments to the manuscript are still required. 
1.Lines 72–73 of the manuscript already state that the 'Strangelove ocean' is considered nearly devoid of life, reflecting an
extremely low biological presence during that sedimentary period. Phosphorus is widely recognized as a nutrient element
that promotes biological prosperity, as referenced in articles such as "Volcanic fertilization of Late Triassic lacustrine algal
blooms." The manuscript posits, based on calculations, that "the eruptions have caused a rapid increase in delivery of
phosphorus (2.59 × 10^14 mol) into the contemporaneous ocean." This massive input of phosphorus, instead of stimulating
a biological bloom, aligns with a period described as devoid of life, creating a significant contradiction. Furthermore, since
the calculation of phosphorus release is based on weathering effects, it is necessary to further clarify the differences
between airborne volcanic ash deposits and waterborne volcanic ash deposits. Relevant references include "Distinctive
volcanic ash–rich lacustrine shale deposition related to chemical weathering intensity during the Late Triassic: Evidence
from lithium contents and isotopes." Overall, to resolve the contradiction between phosphorus release and scarce biological
activity, it is recommended to either remove or substantially downplay the content related to phosphorus release. 
2.In addition to carrying life-nourishing elements such as P and Fe, volcanic activity also transports substantial amounts of
toxic elements detrimental to biological development. It is recommended that the authors explore and analyze this
perspective, investigating its coupling relationship with the 'Strangelove ocean' from the standpoint of environmental
change. Relevant articles for reference include "Preservation of organic matter in shale linked to bacterial sulfate reduction
(BSR) and volcanic activity under marine and lacustrine depositional environments" and "Influence of volcanic activities on
redox chemistry changes linked to the enhancement of the ancient Sinian source rocks in the Yangtze craton."

Reply: Thanks for the comments from the Reviewer . Below is our clarification of the 
events we mentioned, along with explanations of how we revised them in the updated

(1) ‘Strangelove ocean’: The ‘Strangelove ocean’ recorded in the early Cambrian strata 
in South China was first proposed by Hsu et al. (1985) based on a negative inorganic 
carbon isotope excursion they discovered in the basal Shiyantou and Shuijingtuo 
formations at Meishucun and in the Three Gorges area, respectively. U-Pb dating of the 
volcanic zircons in the K-bentonites in this study shows that the age of this stratigraphic 
horizon is ca. 520 Ma that can constraint the timing of the ‘Strangelove ocean’. Despite 
the early Cambrian ‘Strangelove ocean’ in South China is potentially a global event, no 
contemporaneous counterparts have been determined elsewhere yet due to the 
uncertainty of the international stratigraphic correlation. Therefore, we revised the 
definition of ‘Strangelove ocean’ in the Abstract of the updated manuscript as follows 
(Lines 14-15): “The ‘Strangelove ocean’ documented in the early Cambrian strata 
(ca. 520 million years ago) in South China , has been thought to be driven by an 
extraterrestrial impact, but this viewpoint has been do ubted for the emerging 
geochemical evidences.” Besides, the express related to the ‘Strangelove ocean’ in the 
Introduction of our manuscript has been revised as following (Lines 72-81): “During 
the deposition of this stratigraphic unit, a negative inorganic carbon isotope 
excursion (Hsu et al., 1985; Zhou et al., 1997; Zhu et al., 2007; Li et al., 2013) and

deposited (Wille et al., 2008; Li et al., 2017; Wang et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024; Figs.

in South China, this ocean was considered nearly devoid of life and termed the 
‘Strangelove ocean’ by Hsu et al. (1985).” We also revised the sentence regarding the 
‘Strangelove ocean’ in section 5.6 as following (Lines 6 48-651): “The ‘Strangelove

been thought to be a heavy crisis of the earliest Phanerozoic marine environment, and
