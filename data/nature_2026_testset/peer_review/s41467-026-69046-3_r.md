Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors claim that “they identify a new mechanism of plastic deformation localization that dynamically promotes the
homogenization of plasticity in face-centered cubic solid solution-strengthened metallic alloys. This phenomenon is
attributed to a new mechanism referred to as dynamic plastic deformation delocalization, which opens a new design space
for enhancing the mechanical performance of metallic materials. However, this phenomenon is very similar with the reported
results in previous multi-principal element alloys. It has been confirmed that the deformation mechanisms originate from
chemical compositional heterogeneities at the atomic level and resulting wide variations in generalized stacking fault energy
and associated dislocation activities. (such as 
Acta Materialia, 2022, 225: 117571; Progress in Materials Science, 2024,
143: 101252; Nature Communications, 2023, 14: 7469). In addition, the author did not conduct a in-depth analysis of the
underlying physical mechanisms of the phenomenon in the manuscript. If published in NC now, the reviewer are worried
about the innovation and analysis depth of the current manuscript. It is suggested that the author conduct a thorough
analysis of the manuscript based on published research about the chemical inhomogeneity of multi-principal component
alloys. Some comments are as follows: 
1.The sample structure lacks more specific characterization: (XRD), EDS mapping. 
2.whether the initial structure of the sample is the same (FCC disordered solid solution). For example, there may be short-
range ordering in VCoNi (Nature, 2021,712: 592). 
3.The stress-strain curve is one of the important means to evaluate the performance of high entropy alloys. It is
recommended that the author should add the result. 
4.TEM samples were prepared using FIB, but the potential impact of ion beam damage was not mentioned. The high-density
stacking faults and twinning observed in Fig. 3 may be partially attributed to the preparation process rather than the actual
deformation mechanism. FIB often introduces dislocation loops or non-intrinsic stacking faults. This may lead to the
evidence of "twin slip competition" in Fig. 3 being exaggerated (such as abnormally high stacking fault density). 
5.Due to the inherent chemical heterogeneity in multi-principal component alloys, SFE already exists within a distribution
range, leading to simultaneous activation of different deformation mechanisms. However,an average value is used as a
function of temperature in the manuscript is clearly inappropriate. It is recommended that the authors statistically analyze the
evolution of the SFE frequency with the temperature to obtain the dominant range of SFE for optimal performance. (such as
Nature Communications, 2023, 14, 5705; Acta Materialia, 2022, 225, 117571) 
6.It is suggested that the authors conduct in situ TEM tensile experiments or molecular dynamics simulations to further
investigate the dynamic formation process and interaction mechanism of nanotwinning and stacking faults/full dislocations. 
7.There are only the low magnification images in the high plastic localization regions. The atomic scale STEM images
should be added. 
8.For the low SFE alloys, is it precisely because of the unique local chemical inhomogeneity (Compositional
undulations/wave, short-range ordering, Localized chemical order, etc. Nature, 2019, 224,574; Mater. Horiz., 2021, 8, 948;
Nature, 2024, 625, 701; Nature Materials, 2023, 22, 950) have promoted the cross slip of defects. The author should conduct
the in-depth discussion.

We want to respectfully mention that fatigue ratio based on the ultimate tensile strength (UTS) is
not an appropriate metric when analyzing behavior in the very high cycle fatigue (VHCF) regime.
In this regime, fatigue life is governed primarily by crack initiation and loading within the elastic
domain. The deformation mechanisms active during the early stages of local plasticity and crack
initiation occur locally at low strain levels and are therefore distinct from those operating at
high deformation levels. For example, in CrCoNi, extensive twinning and the associated dynamic
Hall–Petch effect develop only at large plastic strains, whereas these mechanisms are not observed
during VHCF testing [1]. Consequently, using UTS as a reference for the fatigue ratio is not justified.
The historical correlation between fatigue strength and UTS was established decades ago for
fatigue lives around 107 cycles and was based on a limited dataset consisting primarily of ferrous
materials.
In contrast, we adopt the more physically meaningful definition of fatigue ratio proposed by
Ashby[2], which relates fatigue strength to the yield strength (YS). This approach is conceptually
sound since VHCF testing is performed within the macroscopic elastic regime, and fatigue
strength is governed by the onset of local plasticity during the first loading cycles, processes that

The SFE values for VCoNi and FeNi36 decrease with increasing temperature (opposite to the trend
shown by the other two alloys) due to the phase stability of hcp relative to fcc [11] with d-electron
density effect [12]. The element Co tends to make alloys hcp-stable at low temperatures. The
stability of fcc increases with increasing temperature. The elements Cr and Mn behave similarly,
supported by high d-electron density around Fermi level at low temperature. Since electron
density is high around the Fermi level, they easily react upon deformation (local transition, stacking
fault), which suggests a lower SFE at low temperature. As temperature increases, the electrons
are more distributed and relatively insensitive reacting on stacking fault, hence SFE increases. In
contrast, V and Fe make alloys fcc-stable (relative to Cr, Co and Mn) and have opposite effects. We
have added a brief explanation under the section "A dynamic delocalization mechanism: the role
of stacking fault energy" in the revised manuscript as follows:
VCoNi [13] and FeNi36 both exhibit increasing stacking fault energy with decreasing temperature,
whereas CrMnFeCoNi and CrCoNi show the opposite trend [14]. This is due to preference of hcp
stability by Co, Cr, and Mn elements such that high d-electron density around Fermi level easily

Finally, some of the investigated alloys, such as CrCoNi, VCoNi, and CrMnFeCoNi, may exhibit
short-range ordering (SRO) [29, 21], which could influence the previously discussed mechanisms.
SRO can promote partial-dislocation–mediated plasticity processes, including stacking fault for-
mation, twinning, and HCP phase nucleation, leading to the generation of deformation debris
that refines the microstructure while introducing additional obstacles to dislocation motion
[30, 31, 32, 33, 34]. Chemical fluctuation such as SRO was also observed to promote or inhibit
cross-slip [35, 34, 36, 37, 38]. These processes may therefore also contribute in promoting or
inhibiting the phenomenon of dynamic plastic delocalization. However, SRO is not a necessary
condition for such behavior, as plastic delocalization is also observed in the binary FeNi36 alloy,
which exhibits weak SRO and mechanical properties largely unaffected by it [27]. Moreover,
SRO in CrCoNi and CrMnFeCoNi has been observed to have no noticeable effect on macroscopic
properties and therefore may not be an influential factor on the identified mechanisms [23, 26].
