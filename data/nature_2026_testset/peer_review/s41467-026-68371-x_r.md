Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, Wang X. et al reported a predictive role of YTHDF3 in paclitaxel-based NACT by upregulating INCENP
and CDCA8. It have been well-reported that either YTHDF3 or METTL3 are associated with poor prognosis of many
cancers. Authors firstly reported the m6A-modification dependent role of YTHDF3 to promote the translation of INCENP and
CDCA8, which was not surprising at all. 
1, The mechanism of INCENP and CDCA8 to confer paclitaxel non-response should be the major novelty of this study,
which was completely missing. 
2, In 4e/f, knock-down of INCENP and CDCA8 inhibited cell growth in the absence of paclitaxel, indicating that the
upregulation of INCENP and CDCA8 may render poor prognosis irrespective of paclitaxel treatment. 
3, In 4K, knock-down of YTHDF3 inhibited cell growth significantly in the absence of paclitaxel and the growth-inhibition of
YTHDF3 KD cells seems to be significantly more sensitive to paclitaxel with the difference quite moderate. 
4, Authors should make clear that it is paclitaxel-based NACT but nit NACT in general and point out INCENP and CDCA8 in
the title. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Wang et al. are evaluating the role of YTHDF3 in neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NACT) for esophageal squamous cell
carcinoma (ESCC). The authors identify YTHDF3 as a critical mediator of poor response to paclitaxel-based NACT, link
YTHDF3's regulation of INCENP and CDCA8 mRNA translation in an m6A-dependent manner to enhanced cell division,
thus offering a mechanistic explanation for its role in NACT resistance. This study is exploring an important topic. The
manuscript reads well, with a clear and logical flow throughout. The data is compelling for the most part, and extensive
analysis has been performed to substantiate the role of YTHDF3 in NACT resistance in esophageal squamous cell
carcinoma (ESCC). To improve this manuscript, the authors should address the following points. 
Major comments: 
1. The authors used a heatmap to claim that 9 m6A regulators were upregulated in non-responders (Fig. 1b). However, I
noticed that the sixth responder (from the left) also exhibited a more pronounced upregulation of these 9 m6A regulators.
Conversely, the sixth and ninth non-responders showed decreased expression of m6A regulators, including YTHDF3, which
the authors highlighted. The authors should address these observations in the manuscript to clarify the interpretation of this
data. Additionally, the selection of YTHDF3 as a candidate is not strongly justified based on the heatmap. Furthermore, the
authors mention METTL3 in later sections; therefore, its expression should also be shown in this heatmap. The authors
should also clarify what the "p" denotes beside the heatmap in the figure legend. 
2. The authors used a heatmap and GSEA results to claim that INCENP and CDCA8 are regulated by YTHDF3 and
subsequently identified these two genes as downstream targets of YTHDF3. However, there are other candidates listed, and
the authors should clarify the criteria used to select these two genes. 
3. The authors used the same scale bar for Fig. 4a and Fig. 4c, implying that the figures should display cells with the same

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
YTHDF3 predicts neoadjuvant chemotherapy response and outcomes in Esophageal Squamous Cell Carcinoma by
Xiangyu Wang, have studied the role of YTH domain-containing family protein 3 (YTHDF3) in predicting poor responses to
neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NACT) in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) patients. YTHDF3 enhances cell
mitosis by regulating the translation of specific mRNAs, leading to diminished effectiveness of paclitaxel-based NACT. High
levels of YTHDF3 correlate with worse patient outcomes. The study suggests that Targeting YTHDF3 not only improves
responses to chemotherapy but also offers a potential therapeutic strategy to enhance treatment efficacy and survival rates
in cancer patients. 
YTHDF3 is crucial for predicting outcomes in breast cancer and hepatocellular carcinoma. Previous studies have
established a YTHDF3-based model for overall survival prediction. While research on esophageal cancer has been limited,
the mechanisms underlying YTHDF3 overexpression are now understood. The authors' study of YTHDF3 upregulation in
esophageal cancer, alongside the use of paclitaxel as a neoadjuvant chemotherapy agent and its associated sensitivity,
could provide a significant contribution worthy of publication in Nature Communications. 
One of the major criticism for this manuscript is, multiple way of action of Paclitaxel has been identified, till Paclitaxel is in the
clinic. There are seminal publications by Antonio Fojo group 
and 
Fernando Cabral group more than decades ago stating
that Paclitaxel can inhibit cell division as a antimitotic agent, but can also inhibit, cell migration and angiogenesis. The

authors fully focused on the effect of cell division after YTHDF3 overexpression. Though there are recent publications on the
impact of YTDF3 on cell migration and angiogenesis, the authors overlooked that for this publication. 
There are several items that can be improved before considered for publication. To investigate the role of YTHDF3 in
response to paclitaxel, the authors have assessed whether inhibiting YTHDF3 could increase the sensitivity of ESCC cells
to paclitaxel treatment. 
The term “Adjacent” in Fig 1E or throughout the manuscript is difficult to follow. What the authors meant by adjacent? Also ,
what is the basis for the selection of “responders vs non responders.” The authors stated in the Fig 1n that elevated YTHDF3
levels were negatively correlated with tumor remission rates (Fig. 1n).Did the author found correlation coefficient? A graph
showing reverse correlation will be beneficial. Also which method the authors used to calculate correlation? There are
similar problems when the authors talk about positive and negative correlations, the numbers and significance of the
correlation 
should be provided. 
The authors have used YTDHF3 mutant. Are they dominant negative? If they are not what is the rationale of using them,
when the authors have already used shRNA and mock sh RNA for their study. The authors have calculated mitotic sister
chromatid separation and nuclear envelop breakdown. Why the authors used both the methods? Is it to rule out mitotic
slippage? Better explanation is needed. 
The authors did not address the significance of the pathways impacted by YTDF3 downregulation, beyond those related to
cell division. Given the availability of RNA sequencing data, it would be valuable to explore additional effects on other
signaling pathways, such as those involved in cell migration or epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and
mesenchymal-to-epithelial transition (MET). A more thorough examination of these pathways could enhance the novelty of
the manuscript. 
A significant limitation of the paper is the authors' focus solely on INCENP and CDCA8. While the data clearly demonstrate
effects on the cell division pathway, it's important to note that the average cell division time in humans is around 90 days,
with only about 2% of cells being mitotic at any given time. Additionally, Paclitaxel is administered at maximum tolerable
doses, leading to only a limited number of mitotic blocks in vivo within the treatment window. This context may explain why
the IHC data from patients do not show an increased propensity for mitotic blockage. I encourage the authors to discuss how
YTHDF3 downregulation may affect or sensitize other antitumorigenic pathways. 
Reviewer #4 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The work described in the manuscript seeks to identify biomarkers predictive of clinical response to neoadjuvant
chemotherapy (NACT) in patients with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) before surgery. Using transcriptomic
analyses in tumors from patients who responded to paclitaxel-based NACT vs the non-responders, they identified an
enrichment for YTHDF3 transcript in the non-responders. YTHDF3 is a cytoplasmic regulator of m6A (N6-methyladenosine).
YTHDF3 was also upregulated in cancer versus adjacent normal tissue, and correlated with higher clinical tumor stage and
tumor infiltration depth. They used many cell biology and multi-omics tools to elaborate on a potential mechanism by which
YTHDF3 induces the resistance phenotype. 
Authors show that high levels of YTHDF3 upregulate the translation of INCENP
and CDCA8 transcripts (components of the chromosomal passenger complex) by recruiting eID3A proteins to its target
mRNAs. Authors speculate that using Ebselen, a chemical inhibitor of YTHDF3, will induce defects in the mitotic spindle
and sensitize ESCC patients' response expressing high levels of YTHDF3 to paclitaxel. However, there are major concerns
for this manuscript as outlined below. 
Major concerns: 
1. Taxanes are mostly used in combination with cisplatin or carboplatin in the neoadjuvant setting for ESCC. So, the
correlations in the current study between the YTHDF3 transcript and response to NACT treatment, refer to clinical response
to the drug cocktail used and not to paclitaxel specifically. Thus, the current study by addressing mechanisms relevant to
taxanes only, do not really address the clinical problem of NACT failure in patients with ESCC 
a. Thus, in Figure 1 clinical response does not reflect paclitaxel-only response and also if the transcript is associated with
response to treatment, overall survival is not the appropriate metric. Instead they should use pathologic response prior to
surgery 
b. In Figure 1O-time to death should be displayed as opposed to death alone-also the authors do not mention if the patients
received additional treatment after surgery and how such treatment is weighing into their correlations 
2. The authors claim that YTHDF3 KD or OE modulates the sensitivity of paclitaxel. Unfortunately, there are important
concerns in the data that do not support this statement. Several important controls are missing, thereby diminishing the
experimental rigor of these results 
- Is there any correlation between the basal expression levels of the YTHDF3 with taxane sensitivity/resistance in the KYSE
cell panel?

(Fig.2A) and vs. Ebselen treated (unknown concentration) (Fig.2C) at different concentrations of paclitaxel. 
The ranges of

paclitaxel to estimate an IC50 of 92nM.

cells treated with Ebselen (unknown concentration) (Fig 2D). The authors also need to test the effect of Ebselen on the cell
