Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
A dual Janus foam with dual asymmetries in photothermal and thermal rectification is designed, which exhibit high
photothermal property contrast and excellent thermal rectification ratios (122%). The entire material system bears a striking
resemblance to the author's previously published work (Janus particles stabilized asymmetric porous composites for thermal
rectification, Nature Communications, 2025, volume 16, Article number: 5650), and also the thermal rectification factor—a
crucial metric for thermal rectification—has not been significantly enhanced compared to their previous results. Thus, the
innovation of this paper is insufficient for publication in Nature Communications. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, the authors designed dual Janus foams (DFs) with dual asymmetries in photothermal and thermal
rectification towards thermal management. This work is interesting. However, there are many issues should be addressed. 
1. The directional thermal management performance of DFs should be compared with that of existing asymmetric materials. 
2. The authors claimed that “This method is promising in practical application for its processability in a large area with the
easy preparation of emulsions and molding.” It seems not easy for the complicated process of both JPs and Janus foam.
More experimental evidence is needed. 
3. Outdoor practical application of the DFs should be performed, including performance under different weather conditions
(sunny, cloudy, rainy) and long-term stability tests, etc. 
4. The authors attribute the thermal rectification performance to the asymmetric porous structure and the introduction of rGO,
however, specific mechanism is not elaborated in detail. The discussion of distribution, orientation, and contribution of rGO
to thermal rectification is not sufficient. 
5. The mechanical properties of the DFs should be given. 
6. Can the DFs keep stable under wind and rain? This is crucial for practical applications. 
7. In the characterization section, detailed sample preparation and measurement condition should be added for repeatability.

Response 
We are grateful to receive the reviewer ’s comment. We believe the q uestion and challenge 
from the reviewer will help us to review our work with deeper and comprehensive 
consideration and make it more suitable for publication in Nature Communications. There 
are some points we want to make clearer, and we hope this will add ress the reviewer ’s 
concern. 
The dual Janus foams prepared and investigated in this work are an extended and upgraded 
material system based on our previous work. In the previous work, we developed a strategy 
to form thermal rectifying materials based on th e asymmetric porous structure and 
demonstrated its basic working mechanism. Here, we developed the basic single asymmetric 
structure with thermal rectifying conduction behavior to dual Janus structures featuring the 
asymmetric thermal conduction in additio n to asymmetric photothermal attributes. The 
material system employed both in this work and the previous work shares a similar basis,

which certifies the generality and expansibility of this strategy. Based on this generalizable 
method, we upgrade the material system to realize better performance in directional thermal 
management. 
In the field of thermal rectifying materials research, rectifying factors are still challenging to 
improve, especially on the solid macroscopic scale and in mild conditions. Rectifying factors 
of thermal transport are hard to lift compared to electrical counterparts, just like thermal 
conductivities are hard to improve compared to electrical conductivity. According to the 
literature we have reviewed, the rectifying factors of a symmetric materials on the solid 
macroscopic scale and in the mild conditions over 100 % are still challenging. And the 
enhancement from 38 % in our previous work to 122 % in this work is actually significant. 
Besides, we also investigate another parameter, the temperature difference ratio, which was 
more practical and was improved nearly 21 times from 3 % based on a single Janus structure 
(the structure constructed in our previous work) to 65 % based on a dual Janus structure (the 
structure in this work). 
Apart from the above development mentioned, the dual Janus foam prepared in this work 
exhibited more pronounced performance under sunlight on sunny days and maintained the 
basic thermal rectifying feature without sunlight due to their asymmetric thermal conduction, 
which has been verified in our previous work.  
Therefore, the resemblance between these two works is mostly attributed to the preparation 
strategy’s generalizability and expansibility. But the two works differ not only in the 
designed structure, from single asymmetry to dual asymmetries, but also in the improved 
thermal rectifying property and enhanced directional thermal management performance. We 
hope this response will make our work clearer for the reviewer. We thank the reviewer and 
the helpful challenges with our best sincere.

First, the basic thermal rectification property of composites prepared in this work is 97 
closely related to the asymmetric porous structure , which is an accumulated 98 
phenomenon during the heating process with a significant dynamic nature.  99 
The asymmetric porous structure resulted in different thermal conductivities of each 100 
layer of the Janus foam. Upon heating from one side, the side with higher thermal 101 
conductivity, the heat will be quickly transported through and accumulated in this side, 102 
which causes strong motivation to transport heat through the second part of the 103 
conduction process, the side with lower thermal conductivity. On the reverse direction, 104 
heating against the side with lower thermal conductivity, the heat will be hindered at 105 
the very beginning of the composite, and the higher thermal conductivity of the second 106 
part of heat conduction just cannot work with its full capability, resulting in an overall 107 
lower thermal conduction speed. Therefore, during the dynamic heating process of the 108 
asymmetric porous composites, the thermal rectification behavior will occur.  109 
Second, based on the discussion above, the thermal conductivity difference between the 110 
two layers will consequently affect the thermal rectification performance. Hence, rGO 111 
fillers with high thermal conductivity were chosen and introduced into one layer of the 112 
Janus foam. The introduced rGO fillers formed a smoother heat conduction path inside 113 
one layer of the composites. With more rGO added, the conduction path becomes more 114 
connected, and therefore the thermal conductivity of this layer increases, leading to 115 
improved thermal rectification performance. Therefore, the distribution of rGO inside 116 
the matrix will actually influence the continuity of the thermal conduction path.  117 
Here, we also observed that a small portion of JPs is close and adhered to rGO particles, 118 
as in Supplementary Fig. 6 (displayed below in Fig. R5 for convenience). This can be 119 
attributed to the affinity of the benzene rings of polystyrene in JPs to the hexanol carbon 120 
rings in the rGO sheets. And this can improve the dispersity of rGO inside the matrix. 121 
As to the orientation of rGO, it will influence the efficiency in forming  a thermal 122 
conductive path. However, it is more complicated to modulate and out of the range of 123

Response 241 
Thanks for the reviewer ’s question. Actually, the thermal rectifying materials could 242 
function with manual flipping to expose different faces and exhibit different properties. 243 
However, they could also work without manual flipping in some certain rationally 244 
designed systems.  245 
One scenario is the occasion that the regulated object needs to accumulate as much heat 246 
as possible from the environment, so the material could be placed with the solar 247 
absorption side outward. During the daytime, the Janus foam could accumulate heat 248 
from solar radiation , and with faster heat transport inwards in the forward heat 249 
conduction direction. During the night, the Janus foam could lag the heat dissipation 250 
outwards in the reverse heat conduction direction. As in all, the material without manual 251 
flipping can accumulate heat as much as possible from solar energy with solar 252 
absorption side outwards. In other words, the materials could also reject and dissipate 253 
heat from the object to the environment by being statically placed with the white side 254 
outward. 255 
Another potential and promising application of thermal rectifiers is harvesting energy 256 
from single fluctuating thermal resources like the ambient environment by constructing 257 
a thermal rectifying circuit as afore proposed by other researchers 9, 10. Two thermal 258 
rectifiers with the opposite orientation are placed against two separate thermal 259 
capacitors, which then clamp the heat engine (e.g., the thermoelectric generator). In this 260 
system, one thermal rectifier could absorb solar energy during daytime and quickly 261 
transport heat from the fluctuating environment and hinder heat dissipation with the 262 
reverse thermal conduction from inside to outside during nighttime. As a result, the side 263 
of the heat engine close to the thermal rectifier in this orientation will maintain a high 264

18 
 
temperature during function. On the other side of the heat engine, with the reverse -265 
placed thermal rectifier, it will maintain a low temperature during function. The well -266 
maintained temperature difference between the two sides of the heat engine is crucial 267 
for generating power continuously. Therefore, with the rationally designed system, the 268
