3) In the Results and Discussion section, author described resistance change depends 
on the bending cycle. For multilayered Ag 2Se case, presenting the resistance change 
could better demonstrate the interfacial stability comparing the pristine film. In 
addition, through S.I. Fig. 10., the slope of the resistance change depends on bending 
cycling appears to vary depending on the substrate thickness, please explain why this 
behavior is affected by the thickness even it is same material.  
Response： 
We appreciate your insightful comments. To validate the resistance stability of the 
multilayered Ag2Se films, we conducted additional bending tests. Since the top surface 
of the multilayered Ag2Se is covered by an insulating oxide layer, electrode windows 
were reserved for resistance measurements, as shown in Figure R1a. We maintained a 
bending radius of 3.75 mm, consistent with that of the intrinsic Ag 2Se film. Owing to 
the presence of the top protective layer, the overall resistance of the bent sample 
remained between 1.4 and 1.5 Ω, and the relative resistance increase was within 1% 
even after 1000 bending cycles (Figure R1b). Such excellent mechanical robustness of 
the multilayer film can be attributed not only to the stable interfacial bonding but also 
to the encapsulation effect provided by the surface anti-reflection layer, which is 
consistent with our previous report1.

multilayered Ag2Se film and bending test setup.  b Resistance and the relative resistance 
change of the multilayered Ag2Se film as a function of bending cycles.

We appreciate your comment regarding the environmental influence during the

However, to further address your concern, we conducted additional experiments

actual wearable bag. The outdoor tests were carried out in Baoding, China, on 20 
November 2025, under sunny weather conditions. The maximum and average ambient

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 13 -->

temperatures were 13.5 °C and 6.1 °C, respectively, with wind speeds ranging from 0.3 
to 2.5 m s⁻¹. The experimental setup, shown in Figure R6a, includes a solar radiometer, 
a source meter connected to a computer for recording the Uoc, a temperature logger for 
measuring the ΔT, an anemometer for monitoring wind speed, and a portable power

shown in Figure R4a and b. 
The results in Figure R6b indicate that the Uoc varies synchronously with ΔT, 
exhibiting a consistent trend whereby Uoc increases with increasing ΔT. When the wind 
speed is lower than 0.5 m s-1, both ΔT and Uoc remain relatively stable at higher values. 
As the wind speed increases, both ΔT and Uoc decrease simultaneously. Notably, higher 
wind speeds enhance convective heat loss on the photothermal side, resulting in a 
reduced ΔT and, consequently, a lower Uoc. Nevertheless, such environmental 
perturbations can be effectively mitigated in future practical applications through 
improved system-level packaging and protection strategies.
