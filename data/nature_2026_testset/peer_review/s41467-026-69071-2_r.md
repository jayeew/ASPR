Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, Zhang Y et al present a montmorillonite-based oral fermentation system designed for restoring intestinal
homeostasis. This system is fabricated by loading metabolic substrates in the lamellar structure of montmorillonite via cation
replacement and adsorbing the substrate-related bacteria through electrostatic interactions. After oral delivery, the
continuous release of loaded substrates and the formation of biofilms by carried bacteria enable long-term efficient synthesis
of beneficial metabolites in the intestine. Importantly, it demonstrates its superior 
therapeutic efficacy in murine models.
Overall, this work presents significant novelty. The data are well-organized and compelling. Thus, this work is recommended
for publication in Nature Communications. While, some minor issues need to be addressed to improve the manuscript. 
1.In figure 1B, the continuous release of carried tryptophan and lactulose and the formation of E. faecalis biofilms ensure
long-lasting efficient production of lactic acid and indole in the gut. To facilitate broad accessibility, it would be better to label
lactic acid and indole with their corresponding structural formulas in the figure. 
2.The authors describe that the cation exchange capacity of MMT enabled high loading contents of tryptophan and
lactulose. How does MMT load tryptophan and lactulose via cation exchange? An explanation is helpful for understanding
the mechanism involved in the preparation of MMT@Bac@TL. 
3.Figures 3i and j show the enhanced biofilm-forming ability of MMT@Bac compared to the Bac group. It would be beneficial
if the authors could discuss why did MMT@Bac promote biofilm formation. 
4.On page 9, the statement “We further quantified the formation of biofilms using Congo red staining after 24 and 48 hours of
incubation in SIF. In the MMT@Bac@TL group, the absorbance at 495 nm showed approximately 3- and 2-fold increments
compared to the Bac and MMT@Bac groups, respectively” is unclear. Please clarify at which time point (24 or 48 hours)
these comparisons were made. In addition, on page 11, the description of “The growth rate was 3-fold higher in the
MMT@Bac@TL group in contrast to the MMT@Bac group” might need further validation. 
5.The method for detecting ex vivo formation of biofilms by Bac, MMT@Bac, and MMT@Bac@TL on ileum sections is
interesting. A comprehensive description of the method would be beneficial for reference by other researchers. 
6.The antibiotic cocktail-induced gut dysbiosis has a more pronounced effect on the tight junction protein ZO-1 than on
occludin. Elucidating the underlying mechanisms for this difference may provide insights into the enhanced therapeutic
effects of MMT@Bac@TL. 
7.It is recommended to adjust the y-axes of figures 7c and 8c to make the changes in intestinal length more visible. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, the authors constructed an oral delivery system using montmorillonite loaded with Enterococcus faecalis
and metabolic substrates, aiming to restore intestinal homeostasis through in-situ synthesis of metabolites in the intestine.
While the research is clearly presented and the data are detailed, the manuscript lacks sufficient innovation and notable
highlights. Its core focus, constructing an oral delivery system by loading bacteria and substrates onto montmorillonite, has
already been explored in similar studies within the fields of clay carrier applications and probiotic delivery. It fails to
demonstrate breakthrough technological or mechanistic innovations, thus offering limited supplementary value to the
existing research framework. 
Secondly, there are several detailed issues to address: The description of the interaction mechanism between

montmorillonite, Enterococcus faecalis, and metabolic substrates is superficial, failing to clarify how ion exchange facilitates
adsorption. In terms of structural characterization, the state of Enterococcus faecalis on the montmorillonite surface in the
SEM images is insufficiently clear. Regarding animal experiments, there is inconsistency in group design: in the evaluation
of therapeutic efficacy against Salmonella typhimurium-induced enteritis, no control group or positive drug group was
included (with 6 mice per group). In contrast, for the study on fungal infection-related colitis, although healthy control and
positive drug groups were established, only 4 colon samples were photographed per group in Figure 8b, which may easily
lead to misunderstanding. 
Additionally, the discussion of experimental results in this paper is not sufficiently in-depth, remaining largely at the level of
result description. For instance, key issues such as why the simultaneous loading of tryptophan and lactulose leads to
significantly higher production of indole and lactic acid compared to the control group, and the mechanism by which biofilms
promote metabolite production, have not been analyzed in depth. 
In summary, I believe that this manuscript has not met the publication standards of Nature Communications. 
Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript reports the development of a montmorillonite-based oral fermentation system (MOFS) that enables
sustained in-situ biosynthesis of beneficial metabolites in the gut, thereby restoring intestinal homeostasis. The work is
original and well-conceived, with a rational materials design, comprehensive experimental validation, and promising
translational potential. The results convincingly support the conclusions, and the study aligns well with the scope of Nature
Communications. However, several experimental details require clarification, and some data presentations and language
usage need refinement. 
1. While Congo red and crystal violet staining were used to evaluate biofilm formation, additional quantitative analyses via
SEM or CLSM analysis would strengthen the findings. 
2. The authors show MOFS retention in ileum, cecum, and colon, but it remains unclear whether this distribution relates to
local pH, mucus viscosity, or motility. A brief mechanistic discussion or supporting experiment is suggested. 
3. Please include more detailed time-course measurements of tryptophan and lactulose levels in intestinal contents to
support claims of sustained release in vivo. 
4. While IL-1β, VEGF, and TGF-β were assessed, additional pro-inflammatory cytokines and epithelial integrity metrics
should be considered to strengthen the anti-inflammatory claims. 
5. Although MOFS reduces Candida albicans, the mechanistic basis is only partially explained. 
6. In PCA and LEfSe analyses, please provide sample size per group, clearly indicate the statistical methods used, and add
p-value annotations to confirm the significance of the microbiota shifts. 
7. For greater relevance, especially in antimicrobial or anti-inflammatory efficacy studies, a benchmark group using a
clinically available probioticshould be included to contextualize the advantage of MOFS. 
8. Some related references such as (Nat. Commun.,2022,13, 3432) are suggested to be cited.
