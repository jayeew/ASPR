Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This study presents a well-designed Phase Ib clinical trial that investigates the novel combination of afuresertib, a pan-AKT
inhibitor, with fulvestrant in patients with pretreated HR-positive/HER2-negative advanced breast cancer. A major strength of
this study is its focus on a clinically relevant patient population, including a notable proportion of individuals with prior
CDK4/6 inhibitor exposure and visceral metastases, thereby addressing real-world treatment challenges. The incorporation
of a robust biomarker strategy is another commendable feature, as it prospectively analyzes alterations in the
PI3K/AKT/PTEN pathway and ESR1 mutations, facilitating exploratory analyses of treatment efficacy in molecularly defined
subgroups. Additionally, the trial design includes a safety run-in phase to assess tolerability and employs a dose non-
escalation strategy grounded in prior pharmacokinetic data, demonstrating careful attention to patient safety. The observed
promising antitumor activity, coupled with a manageable safety profile, provides a compelling rationale for proceeding to
Phase III investigations. Furthermore, the discussion effectively contextualizes the results within the broader therapeutic
landscape, drawing comparisons with other AKT inhibitors, such as capivasertib, and thoughtfully exploring the biological
implications of the biomarker findings, particularly the interplay between ESR1 mutations and PI3K/AKT pathway
alterations. These insights offer valuable implications for the development of future biomarker-guided therapies. The
following are specific suggestions for revision:

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript of " Afuresertib plus 1 fulvestrant for pretreated HR-positive, HER2-negative, advanced breast cancer: a
phase Ib trial" by Drs. Pin Zhang, et al reports results from a single-arm, open-label, proof-of-concept phase Ib trial to
evaluate the antitumor activities and safety of 
afuresertib plus fulvestrant for patients with HR-positive, HER2-negative
advanced breast cancer. The primary endpoint objective response rate according to RECIST 1.1. 
A total of 31 patients were
accrued to the phase Ib part. The overall response rate is 25.8% (8/31, 90% CI 13.5-41.8%); The clinical benefit rate (CR,
PR, or SD maintained ≥ 24 weeks) reached 71.0% (22/31, 90% CI 54.8-83.9). All patients experienced at least 1 treatment-
related AEs (TRAEs), and 8 (25.8%) 
developed of grade 3 or higher TRAEs. They conclude that afuresertib plus fulvestrant was well-tolerated and had promising
antitumor activities against pretreated, advanced HR-positive, HER2-negative breast cancer, supporting further Phase III
studies. 
Overall, the manuscript was well prepared. 
The trial was well designed with reasonable justification of sample size and
appropriate statistical analysis. 
Here are some minor issues: 
1. The “Full Analysis Set (FAS) that included all patients who had received at least one 
dose of afuresertib or fulvestrant”, which is called as-treated population, not the intention-to-treat population. 
2. Line 363 “size of 20 participants,32 which had a 91% probability of detecting ≥ 3 responders”, the 32 comes from
nowhere. 
3. Please report the summary of follow up time for patients included in the analysis. 
4. The planned sample size for the Phase Ib part is 20. The 31 patients included in the analysis is much larger than the
planned size.

Answer: thank you for your important question. This phase 1b trial does not have a 
control group, limiting efficacy interpretation and generalizability. The phase 3 trial 
(AFFIRM-205) is ongoing. Though cross trial compariso n requires caution, 
afuresertib has shown promising efficacy in patients previously treated with CDK4/6 
inhibitors, with a median PFS of 8.2 months, compared to 7.2 months for capivasertib 
in CAPItello-291. Afuresertib also showed a more favorable safety p rofile. The rate 
of grade 3 or worse AEs was 29% and no treatment discontinuations due to AEs, 
compared to 41.7% for grade 3 or worse AEs and 13.0% for adverse events leading 
to discontinuation in patients receiving capivasertib plus fulvestrant. Following  
progression on CDK4/6 inhibitor therapy, treatment choices are increasingly guided 
by genomic profiling. Patients with PI3K–AKT pathway altered tumors may benefit 
from capivasertib plus fulvestrant or alpelisib plus fulvestrant.2 In patients harboring 
ESR1 mutation who received prior CDK4/6 inhibitors, vepdegestrant, an oral 
proteolysis-targeting chimera (PROTAC) ER degrader, and imlunestrant, a selective 
ER degrader, demonstrated notable efficacy. 3,4 Similar to capivasertib, afuresertib 
targets all three isoforms of AKT, downstream of PI3K, and may offer broader 
activities than alpelisib that targets only PIK3CA-mutated cancers. Afuresertib plus 
fulvestrant achieved an objective response rate (ORR) of 33.3% among patients with 
PIK3CA/AKT1/PTEN altered tumors compared to 26.6% with alpelisib for PIK3CA-
mutated tumors and 24.5% with for ALK pathway altered tumors.1,5 Grade 3 or worse 
AEs occurred in 76% of the patients treated with alpelisib plus fulvestrant, including 
grade 3 or worse hyper glycemia in 36.6%. These results suggest that afuresertib

intolerant to alpelisib.

ORR was 33.3% for patients with ESR1 mutated tumors and 42.9% for patients with
