Reviewers' comments:
 
Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
In this work, the authors developed a machine learning potential for simulating monolayer MoTe
₂
 and investigated the 2H–
1T′ phase transition. Using kinetic theory, they revealed that the process involves both diffusive and diffusionless
mechanisms, making the findings both meaningful and insightful. 
The accuracy of the machine learning potential is critical. Before application, it should be validated against density
functional theory (DFT) calculations by comparing key properties such as lattice constants, phonon dispersion,
adsorption/diffusion energies, and related benchmarks. 
Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author): 
This manuscript presents a comprehensive study of the 2H↔1T′ phase transition dynamics in two-dimensional transition
metal dichalcogenide MoTe
₂
. By combining density functional theory (DFT), machine learning interatomic potentials (MLIP),
molecular dynamics (MD), time-stamped force-bias Monte Carlo (tfMC), free-energy barrier calculations, and kinetic Monte
Carlo (KMC) simulations, the authors provide a detailed picture of the transition mechanisms, spanning from nucleation to
growth, from diffusive to diffusionless pathways, and from forward to reversible transitions under cyclic loading/unloading.
The manuscript is well organized, methodologically sound, and offers results of significance for phase engineering and
device applications of 2D TMDs. Therefore, I recommend its publication after the authors address the following issues: 
1. In the analysis of 1T′ phase growth via absorption of random vacancies, the maximum barrier is reported to be ~0.4 eV,
and it is stated that this transition can occur “readily” under ambient conditions with 4% armchair strain. Since 0.4 eV is still
considerably larger than thermal energy at room temperature (≈0.026 eV), the authors should further explain why this
process is considered facile. 
2. The study focuses exclusively on MoTe
₂
. It would strengthen the impact of the work if the authors could comment on
whether the proposed mechanisms are expected to hold for other TMDs such as MoS
₂
. A short discussion in the conclusion
would enhance the generality and broader relevance of the paper. 
3. In the KMC analysis, a fixed forward hopping barrier of 0.4 eV is adopted as a representative value. This assumption may
be overly simplified. The authors should discuss the sensitivity of the results to variations in the barrier height. 
Reviewer #3 (Remarks to the Author): 
The authors investigate 2H<->1T' phase transition in monolayer MoTe2 using first-principles calculations, molecular
dynamics based on machine-learning potentials, Monte-Carlo simulations, and analytical kinetic models. The manuscript
provides a full picture on the phase transition from 
atomic-scale migration processes to domain growth at meso-/macroscopic scale, including the roles of Te vacancy density
and external strain that are needed to drive the transition. The discovered mechanisms are plausible and consistent with
what I know from the MoTe2 (or other TMDs). The manuscript is easy to follow with clear visualizations. I think the
manuscript is suitable for publication in Communications Materials. 
I have only two minor technical comments: 
- Figure 2(b,c), it is not clear how the free energy difference is calculated. Figure 2d shows comparison for HA vs PAFI, but
which one is used for (b,c)? Also, I did not find anything in methods section(s) about HA calculations. On a related note, how
is strain defined when done at finite temperature? Is it with respect to the equilibrium lattice constants at 0K or at the given
temperature? 
- MTP seems ok, given the large structural variety, although force error of 65 meV/Å is not great. The authors provide a
comparison between DFT and MTP for structural parameters and phonon dispersions in the Supplementary Materials. Since
the authors also have DFT NEB calculations available (as they were used 
to train MTP), I would be interested to see
DFT/MTP comparison for those. Also, why SCAN gives imaginary phonon modes for 1T'? It should be stable.

1. In the analysis of 1T′ phase growth via absorption of random vacancies, the maximum barrier

Response: We thank the reviewer for these insightful technical questions that help clarify our

The free energy differences in Figure 2(b,c) were calculated using the harmonic approximation (HA)
method. We apologize for this omission in the Methods section and have now added the following
detailed explanation in Methods section of Free energy calculations (page 24):
“The free energy differences in Figure 2(b,c) were computed within the harmonic ap-
proximation (HA). For each strained configuration, the Helmholtz free energy was cal-
culated as F (T ) = Etotal + Fvib(T ), where Etotal is the total energy from the MLP,
and Fvib(T ) is the vibrational free energy contribution obtained from diagonalizing the
dynamical matrix of harmonic phonons. The free energy difference ∆ F (T ) between
phases was then determined as the difference between their respective Helmholtz free
energies at temperature T .”
We have also clarified in the Figure 2 caption that panels (b,c) specifically show HA results, while
panel (d) provides the comparative analysis between HA and PAFI methods.
Regarding strain definition , all finite-temperature strains are defined with respect to the zero-
temperature equilibrium lattice constants. We have clarified this on page 8 as follows:
“All strain values reported in this work, including those at finite temperatures, are
defined with respect to the equilibrium lattice constants obtained at 0 K. This reference
frame ensures consistent comparison across different temperatures and phases.”
We thank the reviewer for pointing out these important methodological details that needed clari-
