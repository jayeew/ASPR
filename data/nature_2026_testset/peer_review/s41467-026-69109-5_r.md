Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors synthesized a series of biaxial atropisomers by integrating two naphthyl rotors with a phenyl-based liker, and
investigated a correlation between molecular dynamics and electron through-space conjugation (TSC). It is interesting that
TSC-induced fluorescence enables direct visualization of dynamic conformer populations and kinetics during crystallization.
However, separation of syn and anti by crystallization was performed only for 11-NB-2DMe, and it remains unclear whether
direct visualization by TSC-induced fluorescence will become a common technique. Furthermore, it is unclear from the
authors' explanation how they distinguish between TSC and TBC, or on what basis they determined that it was TSC. Axial
chirality is common in the compounds they synthesized, and their elucidation of the stability of axial chirality is not novel.
Therefore, this referee does not recommend publication of the work in nature communications. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This work by Zhang, Tang, and their colleagues develops naphthyl-based biaxial atropisomers, focusing on their complex
stereoconformations and dynamic interconversion mechanisms. Structural modulation yields three LaPlente-type systems
with controlled interconversion kinetics, elucidating the correlation among molecular dynamics, electronic TSC, and
fluorescent signals. By exploiting TSC-induced fluorescence changes, a novel in-situ spectroscopic method is established,
enabling the real-time mapping of atropisomer populations and the visualization of dynamic processes such as sequential
crystallization, thereby advancing the fundamental understanding and analysis of molecular dynamics. Overall, this study
demonstrates a methodologically rigorous experimental design, complemented by robust theoretical validation that
substantiates the reported findings. The manuscript merits publication in its current form pending the following targeted
minor revisions: 
1. The introduction insufficiently addresses the advantages of fluorescence spectroscopy in its second paragraph, which
requires substantive expansion to adequately cover these merits. 
2. Solvent peak labels in all NMR spectra should be revised to specify that signals originate from residual protons in
deuterated solvents, not the deuterated compounds. 
3. For Supplementary Figs. 10-11, expanded views of the 1H and 13C NMR spectra should be provided to facilitate detailed
peak differentiation for the conformational isomers of 11-NB, as demonstrated in Supplementary Figs. 12-13. 
4. All integration in 1H NMR spectra should correspond to the expected hydrogen counts in molecular structures. When
structurally symmetric molecules exhibit half the expected integration, an explanatory note should be added to the figure
caption. 
5. The Supporting Information's "Computational Details" section should add procedures for calculating half-life values (t1/2),
as these values are extensively cited in the main text without computational substantiation. 
6. The sequential crystallization process enables real-time mapping of selective crystallization (Stage II) via ratiometric
fluorescence. Can high-purity 11-NB-2DMe-syn crystals be obtained at this stage, and if so, can their structural integrity be
further verified through complementary characterization techniques?

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript described the modulation of the molecular steric hindrance by changing the connection site of the
naphthalene unit and adding methyl groups, constructing a series of atropisomers from 22-NB, 11-NB to 11-NB-8DMe.
Ultimately, the syn and anti conformers of 11-NB-2DMe were completely separated and unambiguously characterized by
single-crystal X-ray diffraction. Furthermore, based on the differential extent of molecular motion, the stability of the through-
space conjugation (TSC) was modulated. Consequently, the different conformations of 11-NB-2DMe exhibited distinct TSC-
induced emission properties. Utilizing this characterization, the entire recrystallization process was monitored. In overall, the
molecule in this work was carefully designed, and the experimental results are highly encouraging. This manuscript is
recommended for publication in Nature Communications after addressing the following minor issues: 
1. The authors employed 2-MeTHF as the solvent for low-temperature experiments in Fig. 2a-c. Could the solvent affect the
observed phenomena? Testing alternative solvents in similar experiments would provide valuable supplementary insights. 
2. In Fig. 4b-c, the absorbance spectra show a linear correlation with concentration over the range of 1 μM to 10 μM. Could
the authors comment on whether this linear relationship holds at higher concentrations? Does the solution concentration
used during the crystallization process fall within this validated range? 
3. The separation of the syn and anti conformers of 11-NB-2DMe appears to be challenging. While Supplementary Fig. 7
illustrates the use of crystallization for this separation, could the authors provide more specific details regarding the
experimental procedure used to obtain pure samples of both 11-NB-2DMe-syn and 11-NB-2DMe-anti? 
4. The manuscript lacks description of the half-life (τ) calculation methodology. This section should be supplemented with
specific fitting procedures and equations to ensure others can accurately reproduce the data. 
5. The term "intenisty" (Line 278) is misspelled, please correct it to "intensity".

"The above thermodynamic and kinetic regulation of atropisomers reveals two essential

Comment 2: It is unclear from the authors' explanation how they d istinguish between TSC and 
TBC, or on what basis they determined that it was TSC. 
Reply: Thank you for your suggestion. Accordingly, we will elaborate on the distinction between 
TSC and TBC in great detail. 
 
Fig. R2 Representative molecules exhibiting through -bond conjugation (TBC) and through -space 
conjugation (TSC), where red and blue, respectively, denote the phase orientations of their 
molecular orbitals. 
The key difference between TBC and TSC lies in the arrange ment of molecular orbitals. TBC 
typically relies on side-by-side molecular orbitals . Consequently, when extending a molecule's 
planar structure, an increase in side -by-side molecular orbitals leads to a more significant redshift 
in the molecule's emission wavelength. In contrast, TSC is based on head-to-head molecular 
orbitals. TSC arises specifically when the molecular orbitals of aromatic rings overlap spatially 
(Fig. R2). 
The structure of the biaxial atropisomer resembles a 'fully -conjugated molecule', w hich 
understandably causes confusion in our system. Should the emission of the biaxial atropisomer be 
attributed to TBC or TSC? 
We provide a definitive answer: the emission originates from TSC . This is a crucial point in 
our work, demonstrating that TSC ca n dominate the photophysical properties of 'fully -conjugated 
molecules' instead of TBC alone. We will substantiate this conclusion through three aspects. 
 
Supplementary Fig. 39  Temperature-dependent PL spectra of three compounds in 2 -Me-THF, 
concentration (c) = 10 μM. 
First, we synthesized several model compounds (Naph, 2 -NB, 1 -NB) to compare their 
photophysical properties. As shown in Supplementary Fig. 39, Naph exhibits intrinsic emission at 
321/336 nm. The emission of 2 -NB is redshifted to 358 nm relati ve to Naph, attributable to

This spectral shift indicates that steric hindrance between the naphthalene and benzene rings in

Critically, TBC and TSC emissions exhibit distinct spectroscopic signatures: TBC typically
