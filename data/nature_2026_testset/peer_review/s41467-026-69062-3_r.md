Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript aims to demonstrate that myofibroblast-derived tenascin C (TNC) facilitates sensory nerve proliferation via
αvβ1 integrin in the mouse carotid-ligation model of intimal hyperplasia (IH). It proposes that post-injury neuronal growth
establishes pro-inflammatory neuro-immune interactions between CD11c+ macrophages and neurons, exacerbating
inflammation through neuronal CCL2 production and subsequent macrophage-driven production of pro-stenotic factors
TGFβ and IL1β. Using single-cell RNA sequencing of ligated and sham carotid arteries, the study identifies a subset of TNC-
producing myofibroblasts. Adventitial and neointimal myofibroblast activation is illustrated using lineage-tracing mice, while
potential neuronal growth after ligation is examined with whole carotid confocal immunofluorescence (IF) imaging and
lineage-tracing experiments. The study incorporates in vitro models with established cell lines and primary cultures to test
mechanistic hypotheses. 
While the manuscript presents some strengths, including novel findings and technically diverse approaches, significant
experimental and interpretative flaws must be addressed before publication. Moreover, the translational relevance of the
findings remains unclear. The study relies exclusively on a mouse model of IH, which does not convincingly mimic human
pathology. Notably, there is no evidence of excessive nerve proliferation in human stenotic lesions, and the carotid ligation
model has historically shown limited translational success. These limitations and concerns about rigor and reproducibility
undermine the study's impact. 
Strengths 
1. Novelty: The investigation of neuro-immune interactions in IH is a relatively underexplored area. The identification of TNC
as a mediator in neuro-regulation and intimal hyperplasia is intriguing. 
2. Impressive Results: Findings such as the complete inhibition of IH with resiniferatoxin (RTX) highlight the potential
mechanistic significance of neuronal contributions to vascular pathology.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this study, Tong and colleagues investigated the cellular and molecular mechanisms underlying vascular neointimal
hyperplasia in a mouse model induced by carotid artery ligation. Using single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq)
transcriptomic analyses, they identified a subpopulation of fibroblast-derived TNC+ myofibroblasts that play a critical role in
this process. Mechanistically, their results suggest that TNC promotes a neuro-immune pathway. While the study is of
general interest, several aspects require improvement before publication. 
1. The results predominantly rely on qualitative analyses (e.g., immunofluorescence data). To strengthen the findings,
quantitative analyses (such as flow cytometry) should be employed, particularly for immune profiling-related data. 
2. A notable observation is the quantitative difference between βIII-tubulin staining and Nav1.8-TdTomato staining. This
discrepancy suggests that neuronal types other than sensory/nociceptors might contribute to the observed pathophysiology.
Additional investigation into these neuronal subtypes is warranted. 
3. Figure 4a demonstrates a pronounced absence of TNC expression in sham tissues and a substantial increase in ligated
samples. However, the increase observed in Figure 4b (ELISA analysis) is less dramatic. This discrepancy may indicate a
lack of specificity or high background signal in the ELISA assay and warrants further validation. 
4. In Figures 4d-g, discrepancies in the number of animals represented in the graphs need to be addressed. Authors should
clarify whether certain animals were excluded from the analyses and, if so, provide justification. 
5. While the authors suggest that fibroblast-derived TNC may promote axonal growth, no direct evidence is presented. To
substantiate this claim, experiments evaluating the effect of recombinant TNC on axonal growth in DRG neurons should be
included. 
6. The authors refer to CD11c+ macrophages as M1-like macrophages. However, CD11c expression in monocyte-derived
cells is typically associated with monocyte-derived dendritic cells (MoDCs). This terminology should be clarified and justified
based on existing literature. 
7. The claim that sensory neuron-derived CCL2 plays a key role in vascular neointimal hyperplasia lacks sufficient
supporting evidence. Additional experiments are needed to confirm this mechanism. 
Minor. 
The abstract should be written clearly and concisely.

(Remarks to the Author) 
I acknowledge the authors’ considerable efforts to revise the manuscript and address several of the issues raised in the
initial review. The additional data have improved the overall rigor and quality of the work. Nevertheless, several major
concerns remain unresolved, which continue to limit the impact and clarity of the study. 
Major Concerns 
Human data limitations :The study continues to rely heavily on murine observations. While the inclusion of βIII-tubulin
staining of human carotid and popliteal arteries is a positive addition, these data do not substantively advance the
conclusions.All specimens analyzed are from diseased arteries, and no non-diseased controls were included. As a result,
these findings demonstrate only the well-established presence of arterial innervation without providing meaningful insight
into its relationship with inflammation or disease progression. 
Fibroblast culture conditions : The revised manuscript asserts that gelatin coating softens the substrate and prevents
fibroblast activation. This interpretation is incorrect and unsupported by evidence. Gelatin enhances cell adhesion but does
not alter substrate stiffness. For studies examining stiffness-dependent activation, commercially available systems using
well-characterized hydrogels or silicone matrices should be employed 
Imaging quality and interpretation: The whole-mount carotid artery images presented in Figure 1 remain challenging to
interpret. There is insufficient definition of the vascular wall in both whole-mount and cross-sectional images. It remains
unclear whether the reported red signal originates from the vessel wall or the periadventitial fat, limiting confidence in the
spatial interpretation of the data. 
Lack of advanced validation: The absence of spatial transcriptomic analyses or lineage tracing—despite the availability of
PdgfrαDreER-tdTomato mice—continues to weaken the manuscript. The surrogate analyses included in the revision do not
adequately address prior concerns regarding the limited specificity of immunofluorescence microscopy. 
Animal number inconsistencies: The inconsistencies in animal numbers across experimental readouts remain insufficiently
justified. The authors should provide a supplementary table detailing animal numbers, exclusions, and the rationale for
these discrepancies to clarify potential concerns about statistical robustness. 
Minor Points 
• The term “endovascular intima” should be corrected throughout. 
• The phrase “intensifying NIH” should be revised for clarity and precision.
