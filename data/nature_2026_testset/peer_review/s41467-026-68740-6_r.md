questions to enhance the manuscript's quality.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This work presents the concept of regulating the interfacial water network via Ru single-atom concentration to enhance
electrochemical hydrogenation of quinoxaline. However, to fully validate its claims and ensure its impact, substantial
revisions are required to address critical gaps in the experimental evidence, data interpretation, and material
characterization. 
1. The authors convincingly demonstrate that Ru SAs promote water dissociation to generate H*. However, a critical
paradox remains entirely unaddressed: why do these H* species preferentially participate in the kinetically more complex
multi-step hydrogenation of quinoxaline rather than simply combining to form H2, the thermodynamically favored and
kinetically facile HER pathway? The manuscript provides no insight into the factors that might suppress HER. 
2. The manuscript highlights the high FE for the target product but fails to quantitatively address the competing HER. Direct
quantification of H2 production (H2 FE) under the exact ECH reaction conditions are needed. This is essential to understand
the catalyst’s selectivity and to identify the destination of electrons when the FE for 1,2,3,4-tetrahydroquinoxaline was below
100%. 
3. The consistent omission of quinoxaline conversion rate prevents a full assessment of the catalytic efficiency and practical
utility. The conversion (%) and yield must be provided for all key performance tests alongside FE data. 
4. They claimed that the rationale for the volcano-shaped activity dependence on Ru concentration (Fig. 5e). However, the
quantification method is inadequate. EDS is a semi-quantitative technique with a typical error of >5-10%, which is
unacceptably high for accurately determining the low loadings of single atoms (0.6 at%, 1.0 at%, 1.4 at%) that are central to
the argument. 
5. The demonstration of stability in an MEA electrolyzer is a strength. However, the energy efficiency and practical
advantage of the process remain unclear. The LSV curves of the ECH || OER system (Supplementary Fig. 27) should be
directly compared with the LSV of conventional water electrolysis (HER || OER) using the same MEA setup and electrodes.
This comparison is critical to demonstrate whether the ECH process occurs at a lower cell voltage than water splitting, which
is a key indicator of its potential energy efficiency. 
6. While not always mandatory, a preliminary Techno-Economic Analysis (TEA) would significantly strengthen the impact of
the work. 
7. The DFT calculations present a single hydrogenation pathway for quinoxaline without justifying its likelihood over other
plausible sequences. 
8. Please enhance details on electrode fabrication ATR-SEIRAS and provide the details for in-situ Raman.

Comment 1. In principle, Ru single atom doping causes lattice expansion of Co3O4, and 
the diffraction peak should shift towards lower values. Why does the diffraction peak

Response: Thank you for your comments. 
When Ru atoms are doped into ns -Co3O4, their atomic radius is larger than that of Co 
ions in the lattice. This not only induces lattice expansion but also generates 
compressive stress. If the compressive stress outweighs the lattice expansion effect, 
lattice contraction will occur. Although the atomic radius of Ru is larger, compressive 
stress dominates in RuSA/ns-Co3O4 system. Therefore, compared to ns-Co3O4, the (311) 
main diffraction peak of Ru SA/ns-Co3O4 shifts toward higher angles. This anomalous 
phenomenon has been observed in similar literature ,1-3 suggesting the local structure

Comment 3. The notion of obtaining Ru single atoms through a facile chemical 
dealloying and annealing process is indeed questionable. 
Response: Thank you very much for this comment.  We believe there are four main 
reasons why Ru single atoms can be obtained through simple chemical dealloying and 
annealing processes. 
1. Precursor alloy design: In the Al-Co-Ru ternary alloy (such as Al80Co19.8Ru0.2), Al 
serves as the sacrificial phase, Co as the structural phase, and Ru as the inert doped 
phase. The alkaline solution preferentially dissolves Al, while Co is oxidized to CoOx; 
Ru, due to its chemical inertness, retains its atomic state  (Fig. R8, see supplementary 
Fig. 4 in revised supporting information).1 The subsequent annealing further promoted 
the diffusion of Ru, enabling Ru atoms to be embedded in the Co 3O4 lattice in a more 
highly dispersed form, which was also been reported in other literature.2, 3 
  
Fig. R8. Schematic illustration of the preparation of RuSA/ns-Co3O4. 
2. Local concentration limit : The extremely low Ru content (0. 5–1.0 at%) not only 
prevents Ru atoms from aggregating during the dealloying process but also forces them 
to disperse as single atoms in the oxide matrix  (Table R1, see supplementary Table 1 
in revised supporting information).4
