Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, a 2D mircro-resonator array configuration is presented, which supports simultaneous formation of
topological bandgaps at the fundamental and second harmonics (the so-called “dual-frequency” topological bandgap). It is
claimed that this geometry enables efficient second harmonic generation due to a coherent buildup in topological edge
states. 
These findings may be of interest to the research community working in the area of topological photonics. However, the
manuscript is written with a lot of technical jargon, which makes it difficult to read for a non-specialist in the field.
Furthermore, perhaps due to the specific format of a Communications Physics journal submission, a lot of important details
of the model and methodology are either completely skipped (in most of the cases), or buried in the supplementary materials.
In particular: 
1) The models in Eqs. (1) and (2) are written in terms of creation and annihilation operators of the individual photons.
However, all of the results presented in the manuscript are related to classical fields. This is very confusing, if not
misleading. In various places of the manuscript Authors refer to a_s and a_f as “fields”, rather than operators. Furthermore, in
the supplementary section S2 a_f is referred to as “the fundamental phase vector”, with no explanations provided as to what
different components of this “vector” represent. In the same section, a “vector of pump amplitudes” E_I is introduced, which is

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 2 -->

not even a part of the model in Eq. (1). Why there are many “pump amplitudes”, and what different component of E_I
correspond to? A much more detailed discussion of the setup, the models, and methods of solving these equations must be
presented. 
2) There is no explanation provided of how the band diagrams in Figs. 2(a)-(b) and the transmission spectra in Figs.2(c)-(d),
Fig. 3(a)-(b) were obtained. Furthermore, the band diagrams in Figs. 2(a)-(b) seem to be of a 1D system (at least, there is
only single direction of the wavevector k_x on the horizontal axis). But what is this 1D system, and how is it related to the 2D
system discussed here? 
3) It is stated that the field profiles presented in Fig. 2(e)-(f), and in similar plots in later parts of the manuscript, have been
obtained by the “transfer matrix method”. Given the complexity of the geometry (2D vs 1D), it would be useful to explain in
more detail how this transfer matrix method was setup in this case. 
4) Again, it is not clear how the solution in Eq. (3) is related to the Hamiltonian in Eq. (2)? Why and how is it possible to find
separately the field distributions of the fundamental and second harmonic fields, when they are clearly coupled in the
Hamiltonian in Eq. (2)? 
5) Many variables and parameters are not explained or defined. The list is very long, but let me just point out to some: 
- The hopping coefficients J_k: which values have been used in all the simulations, and how these values were obtained? A
value for J_f is stated below Fig. 2, but I cannot find a value for J_s. 
- The phases phi_k: the formula for phi_k immediately below Eq. (1) introduces two new variables: n_eff, and d_y. The
variables d_y are never explained, as for n_efff, see my next comment. 
- n_eff: I understand that it is related to the propagation constant of the fundamental field in the ring resonator. But how it was
obtained, or which value was assumed in all the results presented? 
- The “frequency shift” Delta omega_0 in Fig.1(c) was never defined, and it is not clear what this plot shows. 
- The frequencies omega_0f and omega_0s, e.g. in the captions of Fig. 1d, are not defined. 
- What is actually plotted in Fig. 1(d) and how it was obtained? I am not familiar with the terminology “Hofstadter butterfly
spectra”, and I don’t think a general reader will be. 
- The parameters K_ex and K_in are never defined, and they are not a part of the model Hamiltonian in Eq. (1). 
- The definition of M(omega) is Eq. (3) is very vague: it is stated that it “describes propagation within individual resonators”.
But what is it? And how do you obtain it? 
All of the above issues makes it practically impossible for me to assess and validate the vast majority of the results
presented in this manuscript. I cannot recommend publication of the manuscript in its present form.
