Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Recommendation: Minor revisions 
Comments: In this submitted manuscript, the authors screened an effective Co single-atom catalyst for the electrosynthesis
of cyclohexanone oxime. More interestingly, an optimized flow-through configuration for flow cell was designed to
significantly improve the mass transfer issue. This configuration holds the high FE to 95% and further achieves the SPCE
over 95%. This work provides valuable new insights into the design of electrolyzers for the continuous electrosynthesis. After
the careful review, I suggest that it can be published in Nature Communications after the revision to addressing several
specific concerns. The following suggestions are provided for the authors. 
1. In this paper, the authors used various characterization methods to demonstrate that the Co single-atom catalyst maintains
its original properties after the electrocatalytic reaction. However, the phenomenon of metal ion leaching may occur during
the reaction process. I suggest the author to provide the concentration of cobalt element in the electrolyte after the reaction to
determine if metal leaching occurred. 
2. As is known, some organics could pass through the Nafion membrane in the operated electrolyzer. The authors should
confirm whether cyclohexanone and cyclohexanone oxime exist in the anodic counterpart. 
3. To enhance the comprehension of flow-through configuration performance, the authors need to provide LSV curves
obtained in flow-through electrolyzer. 
4. In this study, the authors present schematic diagrams and comprehensive descriptions of the electrolyzer. However, there
are no physical photographs included. It is highly recommended that the authors supplement the manuscript with the
photographs of the system to facilitate its broader adoption by other research groups. 
5. By reviewing this paper carefully, I find some formatting issues. Firstly, the “a” in Fig. 2a is hard to see. Additionally, the
authors need to unify the format of chart title (e.g., capitalization), such as the “Normalized Intensity” in Fig. 2b and
Supplementary Figs. 13-14. 
6. In my opinion, it’s necessary to investigate if different kinds of electrolyte have effect on the electrosynthesis performance. 
7. In the flow electrolyzer operation, why did the authors change the nitrite concentration from 0.03 M to 0.1 M for single-pass
conversion tests? 
8. Did the authors consider nitrate as the nitrogen source? 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors reported an interesting strategy to synthesize cyclohexanone oxime by a flow-through channel architecture. The
metrics of SPCE and FE at high current densities are impressive. However, I can’t support publishing this manuscript on
Nature Communications in the current form, mainly due to the lack of significance and novelty. Detailed comments as below:
1. The key novelty of this work is the flow-through channel design. However, the flow-through architecture is already widely
known in electrolyzer design. The idea that self-forced electrolyte convection through the electrode can facilitate mass
transfer is not a new idea. It might be the first time used in NOxRR, but it has been widely used in other electrochemical
reactions such as water splitting and CO2RR. The authors should at least discuss the prior arts about flow-through design,
and highlight how their architecture is different compared to previous reports.

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 2 -->

2. Given that the central idea of this work is almost entirely related to electrolyzer design, the authors should provide more
discussions about the engineering aspects. For example, how the mass transfer and electrochemical performance are
influenced by different flow plates (e.g. width of channels and walls), electrode thickness, catalyst loading, concentration of
nitrite & cyclohexanone, etc? The authors spend lots of efforts in catalyst characterization, H-cell electrochemistry and
surface mechanism study, which are nice but not novel. They should conduct more in-depth studies in mass transfer. 
3. The authors highlighted the significant improvement of FE in the flow-through design vs the single-flow-by design, which
serves as the ground for comparison. However, it is notable that the nitrite concentration is quite low (0.03 M), in particular,
considering the high current density. In other words, the authors ‘created’ a mass transfer challenge by using a low
concentration and then address the challenge by the flow-through design. I fully agree that the flow-through design will
enhance mass transfer, but the degree of enhancement may not be that significant if the nitrite concentration is reasonably
higher, e.g., > 0.1 M, which is the concentration in most literatures for oxime electrosynthesis. The SPCE will also be
influenced. The authors should test higher concentrations. 
4. The mass transfer simulations (Figure 4b and Figure S55) are unclear and superficial. It is a quite important part, if not the
most important part, in this work as the key novelty is about mass transfer. What are the authors simulating? Concentration
(then which species), or flow velocity? There is no unit shown for the y-axis. The authors should provide a more detailed
spatial distribution of nitrite and proton, and how those species can influence the rate determining step of the reaction. 
Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript reports a continuous-flow electrosynthesis of cyclohexanone oxime with >95% Faradaic efficiency and
strong long-term stability. The authors combine catalyst design (Co–N–C) with flow-field engineering in a membrane-
electrode assembly to achieve this performance. DFT calculations are used to rationalize the catalytic selectivity, while
COMSOL simulations support the design of flow-through architectures. There are several important assumptions in the DFT
and COMSOL parts are not clearly explained or justified, and some simplifications could affect quantitative interpretation.
Therefore, a major revision is needed and some issues should be addressed before publication. 
1. What are the spin states of the metal centers used in calculations, since Fe, Co and Ni atoms are the strong magnetic
metals. 
2. The inlet velocities and pressure conditions for the three flow architectures (given in the Methods) appear somewhat
arbitrary. For instance, a 10 mm s^−1 inlet speed was used for single-flow and flow-through cases, and 7 mm s^−1 + 3 mm
s^−1 for the two inlets of the binary-flow case. How to set them? If the authors have experimental flow rates, it would be
helpful to state that and relate it to the simulation inputs. 
3. The model implements nitrite reduction as a surface reaction with a “reaction frequency” constant of 5×10^−7 m s^−1 on
the electrode surface. How to determine it? 
4. The model does not incorporate mass-transfer coupled kinetics beyond a simple rate constant. At very high current
densities, kinetics on the catalyst might also start to limit the FE. The authors could comment on whether the Co–N–C
catalyst has ample activity such that mass transport was indeed the limiting factor in all cases. For example, did the authors
observe a mass-transfer-limited current plateau in experiments? 
5. While the manuscript demonstrates excellent long-term operational stability for the Co–N–C catalyst (110 h), the origin of
this stability is not discussed. The authors may consider briefly commenting on the possible structural robustness of the Co–
N4 moiety under reaction conditions. 
6. “*NHO is the thermodynamically stable product in all three models due to its lower ΔG compared to *NOH”, as shown in
Fig. 3c. I think ΔG is not enough to demonstrate it. The authors should calculate the reaction barriers for the formations of
*NHO and *NOH, respectively.

1. The key novelty of this work is the flow -through channel design. However, the flow -through

We thank the reviewer for  this constructive suggestion. As commented by the reviewer, the 
electrolyzer design and optimization have been widely studied for scalable electrochemical 
manufacturing, especially for water splitting and CO 2/CO reduction. For the water splitting, 
membrane electrode assembly (MEA) -based electrolyzers have been successfully  used through 
coating the catalyst onto the membrane, showing the merits of the  zero-gap configuration and 
minimal ohmic losses (Nat. Mater. 2020, 19, 1140; Nat. Chem. Eng. 2025, 2, 3). Given the reactant 
of water itself, the mass diffusion layer is deemed to be minimal and the mass transport limitation 
mainly arise from the release of gas product. In terms of CO 2/CO reduction, hydrophobic  gas 
diffusion electrode is typically applied to enable the accessibility of gaseous CO2/CO reactants on 
catalysts, in which the mass transport limitation can also be largely alleviated (Nat. Rev. Mater. 
2022, 7, 55; Nat. Energy 2022, 7, 130). In these established electrolyzers, the flow -by 
configuration is commonly used for the electrolyte owing to the structural simplicity.

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 12 -->

In comparison, the great challenges still exist in the case of the aqueous electrochemical reactions 
that involves liquid‒solid two boundary interaction. The diffusion coefficient of these dissolved 
reactants from bulk solution to catalyst surface is  generally two orders of magnitude lower  than 
that the gas states  (Nat. Energy  2022, 7, 978; Nat. Catal. 2025, 8, 771) . In addition, t he 
conventional flow-by configuration of electrolyte channel leads  to the long diffusion distance. In 
these circumstances , the mass transport is largely suppressed, restrict ing the over all reaction 
efficiency. To this end, the design of flow-through architecture is an effective way to accelerate 
the mass accessibility to catalyst surface. The electrode can be fully immersed into the solution to 
minimize the diffusion layer. More effectively, the forced convection can significantly strengthen 
the mass transport to enhance the liquid flux among the electrode surface. Such strategy has been 
recently reported in the applications of aqueous electrolysis, for example, nitrate reduction ( Nat. 
Chem. Eng. 2025, 2, 379; Nat. Commun. 2025, 16, 5716) and 5-hydroxymethylfurfural oxidation 
(Nat. Catal. 2025, 8, 771), which shows the great advancement. Yet, the current investigations are 
still preliminary, and the reported reactions are relatively simple as the target product is the finally 
reduced or oxidized one. In terms of oxime electrosynthesis in our demonstration, the reaction is 
more complicated. Firstly, the electrochemical process is NO 2‒ reduction to an unstable 
intermediate product of NH 2OH. Moreover, the reaction also consists of a non-electrochemical 
oximation process. Therefore, comprehensive mass transport and feeding balance on the catalyst 
surface should be fully considered. 
 
In this regard, we would like to emphasi ze that the novelty of our work is the subtle flow 
management to achieve the high FE and SPCE (both of >95%)  in the single -pass mode for 
continuous electrosynthesis , which involves the electrolyzer-structural optimization and 
electrolyte flow rate control. Therefore, we hold that the regulation of flow-through channel is 
one important but not the sole significance. In details, the flow-through architecture minimizes 
the diffusion layer and drives the self-forced electrolyte convection throughout the porous 
electrode matrix to strengthen the overall mass transport. This achievement significantly improves 
the FE of oxime production to over 95%. Meanwhile, the electrolyte flow rate is also optimized to 
balance the mass  supply between the CYC  feeding and the in situ generated NH 2OH in the 
electrolyzer, aiming to afford the high SPCE  (>95%), which has been rarely reported in the 
previous studies. 
 
According to the reviewer’s thoughtful comments, we have provided more  discussion about the 
development and challenge of flow -through electrolyzer design and reinforced the exhibition of 
the significance of our work in the Introduction section.

28. Why does the potential curve in Supplementary Fig. 56 fluctuate so much compared to

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 49 -->

We thank reviewer for raising this question to us. The potential  fluctuation is derived from the 
competing HER with H2 bubbles. In the single-flow-by operation, NO2‒ reduction is significantly 
limited, while HER become dominant. Therefore, the H 2 is generated very fast to produce 
substantial bubbles, which makes the small but highly frequent current fluctuation. For binary-
flow-by operation, NO 2‒ reduction is improved while the competing HER is still existing. The 
alleviated H2 generation in turn enables the accumulation in electrolyzer channel to form the bigger 
bubbles. Therefore, the bubble release is intermittent but has the apparent effect  of applied 
potential. In term of flow-through operation with dominant NO 2‒ reduction, the generation of H2 
is largely suppressed with neglectable gas bubble production. The applied potential curves thus 
become smooth. We have explained the potential fluctuation in the revised manuscript.
