Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors propose a pioneering non-solvent induced phase separation (NIPS)-triggered in situ interfacial polymerization
(IP) strategy for fabricating centimeter-scale asymmetrical COF-mixed matrix membranes (COF-MMMs). This approach uses
the polymer matrix as both a solid solvent and an IP platform, effectively addressing the long-standing issues of filler
aggregation and poor interfacial compatibility in traditional MMM preparation. However, the manuscript has several
weaknesses as follows: 
1. This manuscript lacks innovation. The textual description is rather ordinary, and no obvious innovative aspects are
identified. 
2. While the authors cite relevant studies on COF membranes, MMMs, and NIPS/IP methods, there are notable gaps in the

in situ polymerization strategies or advanced interfacial modification techniques, are not adequately discussed. Additionally,
the comparison with other emerging membrane materials (e.g., metal-organic framework (MOF)-based MMMs with similar or
superior performance) is insufficient. This incomplete literature review limits the authors' ability to fully contextualize their
work within the latest research landscape and highlight the unique advantages of their strategy over all competing
approaches.

Question 1 : The authors report an asymmetrical PES membrane with a COF top layer and 
embedded COF nanocrystals. I am not sure if we should refer to this type of membrane as a mixed 
matrix membrane. 
 
Response: Your prudent consideration is highly valuable. 
Mixed-matrix membranes (MMMs) are composite membranes in which a dispersed phase (e.g., 
nanofillers such as zeolites, MOFs, or COFs) is uniformly distributed within a continuous polymer 
matrix, thereby combining the advantages of both components (Chem. Soc. Rev., 2019, 48, 2665–
2681; Science, 2023, 381(6664): 1350–1356). The concept of MMMs has evolved significantly in 
recent years. Specifically, Jin et al. fabricated an MOF -MMM using a solid -solvent strategy for 
the first time (Science, 2023, 381(6664): 1350–1356). This MOF-MMM was composed of a thin 
MOF layer ( thickness less than 100 nanometers ) with high MOF loading (up to 80%) within the 
polymer matrix. When the MOF loading exceeds approximately 50 vol%, MOF particles will no 
longer be isolated but instead form interconnected MOF nano-channels within the polymer matrix, 
thereby dominating molecular permeation.  This demonstrates that even when the filler phase is 
highly continuous, the structure still belongs to the category of MMMs as long as a polymer 
matrix constitutes the continuous phase and forms a favorable interface. 
In our study, the membranes prepared using a NIPS-triggered in situ interfacial polymerization (IP) 
strategy featured a thin COF top layer ( 15–30 nm thick ) with well dispersed COF nanocrystals. 
The continuous COF membrane on the surface can be regarded as a special case of extreme 
enrichment and interconnection of the filler phase in the surface region.  In brief, the structure of 
membrane we developed is quite similar to that prepared by Jin et al. B ased on these similarities, 
we believe it is both accurate and consistent with current developments in the field to refer to the

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 8 -->

membrane in this study as a “mixed-matrix membrane.” The strategy developed here produces an 
asymmetric COF-MMM that combines t he high selectivity of pure COF films with the excellent 
mechanical stability typical of conventional MMMs.

Question 4: The article proposes a synergistic mechanism consisting of "Knudsen diffusion, 
selective adsorption, and interlayer molecular sieving". Please further elaborate on which 
mechanism plays the key role in achieving high H2/CO2 selectivity? 
 
Response: We thank the reviewer for this insightful question regarding the key mechanism behind 
the high H2/CO2 selectivity. 
Through gas separation experiments and simulation calculations, the proposed synergistic 
separation mechanism in this study comprises three components: Knudsen diffusion within the 
PES matrix, selective adsorption in COF regions, and molecular sieving through COF interlayer 
spacing. These three elements work collectively to achieve efficient H 2/CO2 separation. We 
contend that these mechanisms form an inseparable synergistic system where their individual 
contributions cannot be strictly prioritized; rather, they function complementarily to collectively 
enable the remarkable enhancement in membrane performance. 
Specifically, PES@GF membrane that rely solely on Knudsen diffusion demonstrate high H 2 
permeance (4111 GPU), yet th eir selectivity remains quite low (H 2/CO2 of 17.4), failing to meet 
efficient separation requirements. Should separation depend exclusively on the selective 
adsorption of COFs, the absence of both the rapid transport pathways provided by the PES matrix 
and the dynamic sieving effect of COF interlayer spacing would likely lead to a substantial 
reduction in overall gas permeance, making it difficult to balance high selectivity with high 
permeability.

GCMC simulations and structural analyses presented in this work indicate that variations in COF 
interlayer spacing induced by CO 2 adsorption (the molecular sieving effect) can partially hinder 
CO2 diffusion while simultaneously allowing rapid H 2 transport. This process is closely coupled 
with selective adsorption, ultimately creating a synergistically enhanced separation performance. 
According to your suggestions, the discussion has been revised and marked in red in order to 
improve the manuscript: “This separation mechanism achieved synergistic separation by 
combining Knudsen diffusion in the PES matrix, selective adsorption by the COF domains, and 
molecular sieving through the COF interlayer spacing to overcome “trade-off” effects” have been 
changed into  “This separation mechanism achieved highly efficient H 2/CO2 separation by 
combining Knudsen diffusion in the PES matrix, selective adsorption by the COF domains, and 
molecular sieving through the COF interlayer spacing. These three mechanisms are interrelated 
and work synergistically, collectively overcoming the “trade-off” effects in membrane separation 
processes, thereby enabling the COF -MMMs to simultaneously possess both high selectivity and 
high permeance” (Page 24, lines 467-473).

casting solution is immersed in the aqueous phase (non -solvent bath), rapid bidirectional diffusion 
between the sol vent (NMP) and non -solvent (water) begins. This process simultaneously brings 
Pa-1 monomers from the aqueous phase to the reaction interface and provides the driving force for 
the migration of Tp monomers toward the interface, thereby triggering interfacia l polymerization. 
Thus, at the onset, both processes occur simultaneously. However, the duration of these two 
processes differs. Liquid -liquid phase separation and polymer solidification are relatively rapid,

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 18 -->

whereas the interfacial polymerization and crys tallization of COFs proceed as a slower kinetic 
process. Therefore, interfacial polymerization continues for an extended period even after the  
polymer matrix network has largely solidified. 
The migration of Tp monomers is a “phased and constrained ” process, rather than a single, 
instantaneous fixation or free diffusion. We propose that the migration behavior of Tp monomers 
changes significantly before and after phase separation solidification, which can be specifically 
divided into two stages: 
Stage 1: During the initial polymer solidification, the system remains in a liquid state. At this point, 
molecular diffusion driven by the solvent/non -solvent concentration gradient is intense and rapid. 
In this liquid environment, Tp monomers can migrate relatively fr eely. A portion of the Tp 
monomers are carried by the rapid solvent outflow to the outermost surface, where they react 
swiftly with Pa -1 to form a dense top -layer COF membrane. Simultaneously, another portion of 
Tp monomers enters the inner surfaces of fin ger-like pore channels along with the solution. 
During this process, Tp monomers do indeed become instantaneously "fixed" on the forming inner 
surfaces of structures such as finger-like pores, providing sites for subsequent reactions. 
Stage 2: After the po lymer matrix solidifies, the fluidity of the entire system drops drastically. At 
this stage, the diffusion of Tp monomers trapped within the polymer chain network becomes 
extremely difficult. However, this does not mean the reaction stops. Tp monomers that  have 
already reached or are very close to the inner surfaces of the pore channels in Stage 1 can continue 
to react with Pa -1 monomers that persistently diffuse in through the aqueous phase, allowing the 
COF nanocrystals to gradually grow and mature. Meanw hile, Tp monomers encapsulated within 
the polymer network may undergo very short -range diffusion and rearrangement through local 
chain relaxation or within tiny pores, contributing to the formation of COF nanocrystalline grains.

The innovativeness of this article needs to be further refined, and performance data does not fall 
into the category of the article's innovation. 
 
Response: Thank you for your thorough review and valuable comments on our manuscript. We 
have carefully read and fully understood your point regarding the need to further refine the 
article's innovation and that performance data itself does not constitute innovation. We completely 
agree with your view. Performance data are not the innovation, but rather the results delivered by 
an innovative method. Accordingly, we have systematically refined and restated the 
“innovativeness” section, focusing on the originality of the method design, breakthroughs in 
mechanistic understanding, and synergistic strategies in interface engineering, rather than merely 
emphasizing performance metrics. 
We have now clearly summarized the innovative points into the following three aspects and 
highlighted them at the end of the Introduction and in the Conclusion section:

1. Methodological Innovation 
Proposed a “NIPS-triggered in situ IP” strategy, which organically combines non-solvent induced 
phase separation with in situ interfacial polymerization to achieve controllable and uniform 
growth of a COF membrane on the polymer matrix surface. 
Dual functionalization of the polymer matrix: It acts not only as mechanical support but also as a 
“solid solvent” and “reaction platform” addressing issues of filler agglomeration and interfacial 
defects common in traditional methods. 
2. Synergistic Innovation in Interface Engineering 
Constructed a seamless COF -polymer interface: Through in situ polymerization coupled with 
physical entanglement of polymer chains and hydrogen -bonding interactions (e.g., between PES 
and Tp), a defect-free interfacial structure was formed. 
Achieved multi-scale structural control: A dense, ultrathin COF layer (15 –30 nm) was formed on 
the membrane surface, while simultaneously, uniformly dispersed COF nanocrystals (4 –8 nm) 
were anchored on the internal pore walls, resulting in an asymmetrical multilayered architecture. 
3. In-depth Elucidation of the Separation Mechanism 
Proposed a “three-mechanism synergy” separation model: This model integrates Knudsen 
diffusion (in the PES matrix), selective adsorption (by COF domains), and molecular sieving 
(through COF interlayer spacing) to explain the gas separation mechanism. 
Through GCMC simulations and experimental validation, clarified the enhancing effect of pore 
size dynamic contraction induced by CO2 adsorption on H2/CO2 separation performance. 
We believe these revisions have more clearly articulated the scientific novelty and methodological 
contribution of our work. The performance data are now appropriately framed as outcomes of the 
innovative fabrication strategy. 
Thank you again for your valuable feedback, which has significantly improved the clarity and 
impact of our manuscript.
