(Remarks to the Author) 
General comment: 
Layered double hydroxides (LDHs) have been recognized to have great potential for the treatment of heavy metals in
wastewater and soil through various mechanisms. Up to now, many research on the adsorption performance and
remediation effect of As/Cd contaminated water and soil using various LDH such as CaAl-LDH, .FeMnCa-LDHs, .FeMnCa-
LDHs, Fe3O4@Mg/Al/Fe-LDH etc. 
has been carried out widely. Some published literature has shown that 
the adsorption
capacity of LDH/LDO reached 612.0 mg/g for AsIII and 625.0 mg/g for Cd2+. If some of these materials are used to
remediate the AsIII and Cd2+ coexisting polluted water and soil, the performance can be expected since the adsorption
capacity perhaps be enhanced obviously according to previous research. 
In this article, the MgMn-LDO prepared by the authors has excellent adsorption properties for both Cd and As, and the
feasibility of the application of MgMn-LDO in the composite contamination of Cd2+ and AsIII has been comprehensively
investigated in terms of material research and development, performance exploration, mechanism revelation, and production
and application. The adsorption mechanism and adsorption pathway of LDO on Cd2+ and AsIII were revealed. Overall, the
manuscript is good in language organization, results statement and mechanism analysis. The authors, however, suggest
that the novelty of the study is not enough, and the experimental design is not so clear, and the key contents are not
sufficiently discussed. Moreover, the remediation performance 
and function of materials are often affected by many factors
related to physical, chemical and biological activities as well as material aging process itself. The human health risk of
MgMn-LDO entering into crops through food chain should also be considered when it is applied into farmland.

Introduction 
The authors have explored the structure and characteristics of MgMn-LDO in detail in their previous studies
(https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2019.06.002), and have clarified its performance and mechanism for remediation of Cd2+
pollution. In this study, the authors only changed the target pollutant (AsIII/Cd2+) and deeply analyzed its mechanism and
pathway in the synergistic remediation of AsIII and Cd2+. Therefore, it seems that the authors did not develop the innovative
new material and the innovation of this article needs to be developed.

1. The authors made LDOs calcined at different temperatures, but the authors did not provide the calcination temperature of
which LDOs were applied in the subsequent experiments.

know more about the impacts in actual water bodies and soils. 
3. What is the right amount of material to apply in real water applications? How is material recycling achieved after use? Is it
reusable? 
4. For real soil applications, what is the long term stability of the material to immobilize soil As and Cd?

Reviewer #4 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This study presents a MgMn-layered double oxide (LDO) achieving exceptional adsorption capacities for As(III) and Cd2+ in
mixed systems. The remarkable performance stems from a four-step topological transformation that effectively decouples As-
oxidation from Cd-uptake processes. While the research offers timely and potentially significant contributions to water and
soil remediation technologies, several issues require attention before publication. Specifically, the manuscript needs
improvements in experimental validation protocols, data presentation clarity, and overall structural organization. The
absence of page and line numbering in the manuscript made it difficult for me to make more specific comments. 
The following areas require particular attention from the authors. 
Arsenic speciation analysis lacks quantitative solution data. While XPS identifies mixed AsIII/AsV states, HPLC-ICP-MS
analysis of both filtrates and solids is needed to properly track AsIII→AsV oxidation kinetics. 
Regeneration and stability testing is insufficient. Data provided only for Cd desorption/regeneration (Supp. Fig. 31) but
missing for the more toxic As(III). Testing should extend to at least 10 cycles with comprehensive evaluation of long-term
stability and potential leaching of all components (Mn, Mg, As, Cd). 
Mechanism validation relies solely on post-adsorption analysis. The study requires in-situ characterization during
transformation stages using TEM and spectroscopic techniques. No in-situ Raman/FTIR tracking of AsIII oxidation
intermediates is presented, and TEM evidence of layer expansion during Cd2+ substitution is limited. 
Real-world application testing is inadequate with only one sewage sample tested. The study should include varied real-
world samples across different pH levels and competing ions. Testing with organic co-contaminants common in wastewater
is limited, and As/Cd leaching evaluation under different environmental conditions is missing. 
Scalability and economics analysis is missing. The study should address cost-effectiveness and economic feasibility,
provide specific details on synthesis costs and energy consumption during calcination, and include comprehensive cost
analysis for large-scale production. 
Writing and organization require enhancement. Complex, multi-clause sentences should be simplified, transitions between
sections improved, and technical terms defined consistently. The conclusion needs strengthening to highlight broader
implications, and key methodologies from supplementary materials should be summarized in the main text.

2. Mg can dissolute from the layered framework. Why can Mn not be leached from

Author Response:  
This is an excellent question. It is precisely due to these inherent properties of 
LDH that mutual promotion can be achieved in co-adsorption. We will explain this issue

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 10 -->

5 
 
from two aspects respectively. 
2.1 Mg can dissolute from the layered framework 
All solid substances in the aqueous phase essentially exist in a state of  slight 
dissolution equilibrium, and LDH is no exception (ACS Appl. Mater. Interfaces 2022, 
14, 37823-37832; Chem. Eng. J. 2016, 303, 331-337). Therefore, in reality, the metal 
ions (Mg and Mn) in the layers of LDH in the aqueous phase are subject to 
a dissociation-crystallization equilibrium. When Cd2+ are introduced into the system, 
the system will  re-select the product formed  according to the  principle of 
thermodynamic stability (Adv. Funct. Mater. 2022, 32, 2106645; Chem. Eng. J. 2021, 
407, 127178 ). In our system, CdMgMn -LDH has a significantly lower  Gibbs free 
energy than that of MgMn -LDH (Fig. 1c, Fig. 4h ). Consequently, the replacement of 
Mg2+ by Cd2+ occurs. Similar phenomena have also been reported in the literature. Such 
as the mechanism of Cd2+ isomorphously substituting for Ca2+ in LDH laminate to form 
CdAl-LDH, previous studies have demonstrated through  similar ionic radii and DFT 
calculations that CdAl -LDH is  thermodynamically more stable. Likewise,  the ionic 
radius of Cd 2+ (0.95 Å) is relatively close to that of Mg 2+ (0.72 Å), which makes it  
possible for Cd 2+ to construct lamellae of LDHs by isomorphic substitution of Mg 2+. 
(Chem. Eng. J. 2023, 451, 138500; Chemosphere, 2024, 352, 141399) 
2.2 Mn not be leached from the layered framework 
The reason for the phenomenon that Cd 2+ substitutes Mg2+, instead of Mn 3+/4+ in 
LDH(AsIII), derives from their opposite free energy changes of these two processes. 
The substitution of Mg 2+ by Cd 2+ is thermodynamically favorable with Gibbs free 
energy changes ( ΔGs) < 0 eV (Fig. 4h) while the substitution of Mn 3+/4+ by Cd 2+ is 
thermodynamically unfavorable with ΔGs > 0 eV . Inspired by this comment of reviewer, 
the Gibbs free energy diagram of substituting Mn by Cd 2+ in LDH(As III) has been 
calculated and displayed below. The reaction formula of step 1 in Fi g. A  is 
Mg24Mn6O56H48(AsO2)(AsO4) + Cd 2+ → Mg24Mn5CdO56H48(AsO2)(AsO4) + Mn 2+. 
The chemical formulae of the other five steps are similar. The ΔGs of steps 1, 2, 3, 4, 
5, and 6 are 4.34, 1.53, 4.35, 4.25, 4.80, and 4.17 eV , respectively. All ΔGs of

1.2 Material innovation 
This is an excellent question, which makes us realize that the reviewer’s 
misunderstanding arises from our insufficiently clear expression. In fact, the materials 
we prepared are innovative in two aspects, including the ratio of layered metal element, 
and the calcination environment. Specifically:  
1) Synthetic layered metal element ratio. In our work, we employed MgMn-LDH 
(Mg:Mn = 5:1) as the precursor. In contrast, conventional LDHs typically form at ratios 
of 2:1–4:1 due to Paulingˈs rule. (Catal. Today 1991, 11, 173-301; The Periodic Table 
II. 2019, 182, 89-120), including the literature you proposed, the composition rate is 
2:1. The unique capability of synthesizing MgMn -LDH with Mg:Mn = 5:1 arises 
from structural adaptability and redox versatility of Mn. Firstly, the ionic radius of Mn3+ 
and Mn4+ are 0.65 Å and 0.53 Å, respectively, which  are close to the ionic radii of 
common LDH layer-metal ions, such as Al3+ (0.54 Å) and Fe3+ (0.65 Å). The matching 
of ionic radius result in Mn3+/Mn4+ to enter the la minate and form a stable LDH 
structure. Furthermore, the laminate of LDHs is formed by the substitution of a portion 
of divalent metal cations with trivalent metal cations, resulting in positively charged 
laminate. In terms of MgMn -LDH synthesis, the highly alkaline environment during 
preparation oxidizes part of Mn 3+ to Mn4+ (Mn-XPS analysis indicates approximately 
41.9% of Mn3+ is oxidized to Mn4+) (Fig. A). When high-valence Mn4+ replaces Mg2+ 
in the la minate, it causes a dramatic increase in positive charge within the la minate,

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 34 -->

29 
 
leading to structural destabilization. Therefore, in order to maintain structural stability, 
it is necessary to increase the proportion of Mg 2+ to dilute the high charge density 
without altering the total Mn content. Consequently, for the special element Mn 
entering the laminate of LDHs, the synthesis of high -ratio LDH of Mg/Mn is feasible. 
And to some extent, it increases the active sites for As III adsorption, promoting the 
adsorption process (Supplementary Information 58). Additionally, the synthesis process 
of LDH with a high Mg/Mn ratio is simple, requiring only the adjustment of metal salt 
molar ratios, and ensuring operational simplicity.

3.3 The regeneration performance of the MgMn-LDO was systematically 
evaluated via 10 consecutive laboratory cycling experiments. The experimental setup 
fixed the initial concentrations of As and Cd at 10 and 20 mg  L‒1, respectively, while 
50 mg L‒1 Na2CO3 solution was selected as the regeneration agent. As demonstrated in 
Supplementary Fig. 16, after 10 regeneration cycles, the removal efficiency remained 
at 90.2% for As and 100% for Cd, showcasing remarkable recyclability. The reason lies 
in that high-concentration CO32‒ in the regeneration solution displaces intercalated As 
anions through anion exchange, reforming CO32‒-intercalated LDH. This structural 
reconstruction allows the MgMn-LDO to reactivate its adsorption capacity for As upon 
subsequent use. In addition, t he high Mg content in MgMn -LDO (Mg:Mn = 5:1)  
enables sufficient isomorphic substitution of Cd within the lattice, while bulk 
hydroxylation reactions generate abundant surface hydroxyl groups. These hydroxyl 
moieties persist as active adsorption sites for both As and Cd across multiple cycles, 
maintaining consistent removal efficiency.

Author Response:  
The concentration disparity between AsV/Cd2+ and AsIII/Cd2+ systems is rooted in 
preventing premature As V-Cd2+ precipitation, which is critical for enabling LDO to 
drive ultra-stable mineralization rather than spontaneous precipitation. The rationale is 
structured as follows: 
AsIII is neutral form and exists as H 3AsO3, showing weak interaction with Cd2+,

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 57 -->

52 
 
allowing higher Cd2+ (200 mg/L) without precipitation. In contrast, AsV is anionic form 
and dissociates into H 2AsO4⁻/HAsO42⁻ (pKa1 ≈ 2.3, pK a2 ≈ 6.8, pK a3 ≈ 11.6) ( Int. J. 
Anal. Chem.  2019, 1, 3280840) , forming insoluble Cd3(AsO4)2 or related 
hydroxy/hydrated compounds (Chem. Eng. J., 2021, 424, 130410; Sci. Total Environ., 
2019, 666, 956-963) with Cd 2+. This precipitation occurs rapidly at high Cd2+, which 
interferes with LDO’s adsorption -driven mineralization.  Thus, during AsV/Cd2+ 
solution preparation, these compounds are difficult to dissolve, it is necessary to adjust 
Cd2+ initial concentration.
