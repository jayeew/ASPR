Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this study, Li et al. present a systematic optimization of two key components of the Fanzor2 system—MmeFz2 protein and
its cognate ωRNA—achieving substantial performance enhancement through a combination of structural prediction and
machine learning approaches. By rationally redesigning the RNA scaffold and engineering functional protein variants, the
authors developed a highly active and AAV-deliverable Fanzor2 genome-editing tool. Its therapeutic efficacy was
convincingly demonstrated in vivo using a humanized DMD mouse model. 
The work is distinguished by strong methodological innovation and comprehensive data, making it a valuable addition to the
current genome-editing toolkit. However, I have several concerns that need to be addressed. 
1. The authors selected endogenous loci with overlapping PAM sequences to enable a direct comparison of the editing
activity of IsTfu1, IsDge10, and engineered MmeFz2 variants. To enhance clarity, it is recommended that the authors provide
a schematic diagram of the target site sequences and their associated PAMs, emphasizing the common and unique motifs
recognized by each nuclease. 
2. The fusion of non-sequence-specific ssDBDs may carry the risk of increased off-target effects. However, the results
presented here demonstrate that HMG-D fusion not only enhances editing efficiency but also maintains specificity,
highlighting its strong potential for practical application. It is recommended that the authors emphasize this finding more
explicitly in the discussion section. 
3. While off-target rates of around 5–7% may be tolerable in experimental settings, they remain a concern for clinical-grade
genome editing. The authors are encouraged to discuss this point in the discussion section by outlining potential
approaches—such as further ωRNA optimization or the identification of high-fidelity variants—to minimize off-target activity
in future applications. 
4. The integration of AlphaFold and PLM-based approaches for optimizing nuclease systems represents a valuable
methodological paradigm. To enhance clarity, recall, and broader adoption within the research community, it is
recommended that the authors introduce a unified term or framework name for this strategy in the final version of the
manuscript. 
5. It is recommended that the authors expand the discussion to briefly address key considerations for clinical translation,
such as the potential immunogenicity of MmeFz2, long-term expression stability, and its complementarity to existing Cas-
based genome editing platforms.

Major: 
1. The authors state, "Three iterative rounds of prediction and experimental validation identified ten variants...". I suggest the
authors provide a more detailed procedure and illustration outlining the utilization of protein language models (PLMs) within
the iterative process for predicting and validating these variants. As this is a central innovation, a clear, step-by-step
explanation will offer valuable insights and serve as a practical reference. 
2. In the third paragraph, it is noted that beneficial mutations from both approaches stabilize critical interactions between
MmeFz2 and ωRNA. Could independent engineering lead to conflicts in their interactions? Specifically, mutations like
E326Q and E178H changing negative charges to positive or neutral may enhance interactions; clarification is needed. 
3. In section “Engineered MmeFz2-ωRNA system enables efficient genome editing in human cells”, why Drosophila HMG-D
is used? Is there a human homolog more suitable for in vivo applications? 
4. RT-PCR analysis of mRNA may not be a suitable experiment for quantitative identification as shown in Fig. 6d, I suggest
the authors show the sequencing data of genomic DNA. 
5. ωRNA design aims at structural stabilization and transcriptional enhancement. This can be validated through qPCR
detection of ωRNA variant abundance post-transfection in HEK293T cells. This could reveal the mechanism behind indel
efficiency enhancement. 
Minor: 
1. The statement, "we firstly screened efficient target sequences in HEK293T cells and found that DMD-guide3 exhibited the
highest indel efficiency at the SDS site of exon 50 (Fig. 4c)", is incorrect. Figure 4C shows DMD-guide3 had the lowest indel
efficiency. Maybe only the DMD-guide3 site stays in the SDS site of exon 50. The authors should refine this to reflect the
data accurately. 
2. The authors state that “PCR-based detection across the genomic locus confirmed successful splicing alteration...”. If this
refers to the resolution of DMD mRNA transcripts, it might be more appropriate to refer to the transcript rather than the
genomic locus in this context. Additionally, the gel image in Figure 6c appears to be slightly tilted in its current presentation. 
3. In fig.1f, the AT substitution should be AU.

engineering may exhibit conflicts when combined. Consistent with this possibility, such effects were indeed observed in several combinatorial mutants in our study. For example, although the individual E305N and E309R mutations both enhanced activity, their combination resulted in reduced functionality compared with the single mutants, likely due to structural conflicts arising from their close spatial proximity. In contrast, as shown in Fig. 3d, the C69K mutant is structurally distant from other mutations and exhibits favorable synergy when combined with any of them. Additionally, given that E178H and E326Q are sufficiently separated in space, we speculate that potential structural conflicts between them are minimal. 3. Previous studies have demonstrated that attaching the Drosophila HMG-D, a non-sequence-specific DNA-binding domain, to RNA-guided nucleases markedly improves genome-editing efficiency (PMID: 39096898 and PMID: 36457249). Guided by these earlier reports, we incorporated Drosophila-derived HMG-D into the MmeFz2-ωRNA system to further enhance its editing efficiency. We completely agree with your suggestion that using human-derived HMG-D could be more suitable for the in vivo application of the MmeFz2-ωRNA system. In future engineering studies, we aim to explore the impact of incorporating human-derived HMG-D into the MmeFz2-ωRNA system on its editing efficiency. 4.
