Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this study, the authors reveal the critical role of electrolyte surface tension (σ) in governing Zn nucleation and growth
through comprehensive theoretical analysis and experimental validation. Specifically, low-σ electrolytes facilitate dense
nucleation and fine-grained growth of Zn, even under harsh electrochemical conditions, owing to the reduced nucleation
energy barrier and growth resistance. This optimized Zn electroplating behavior enables not only dendrite-free growth but
also promotes the formation of a robust SEI, effectively suppressing side reactions on Zn metal anodes. This work reports an
effective low-σ strategy to enhance the stability and reversibility of Zn metal anodes at a practical level, advancing the
commercialization of zinc metal batteries. Moreover, this work underscores electrolyte`s σ as a vital consideration in future
electrolyte design for advanced metal electrodes. We believe this study will attract broad interest from the research
community. Therefore, we recommend the publication of this manuscript after a minor revision. Below are a few questions for
the authors to address: 
The study of Zn nucleation and growth from an energy perspective is interesting. To enhance understanding, could the
authors provide further clarification on the terms of "∆" "G" _"S" (surface energy) and "∆" "G" _"V" (volume energy)?

nucleation energy barrier and the critical nuclei radius. For the growth part, σ is linked to the growth resistance. Could the
authors provide more information about the growth resistance?

deposits and is related to the propensity for dendritic growth. Please explain more in the main text the relationship between

also affects the nucleation energy barrier during the nucleation process. We are interested in how this parameter would

Response: We sincerely thank the reviewer for this insightful comment. The surface roughness 
of the Zn plating layer is indeed a critical parameter, as it is  related to the propensity for 
dendritic growth. The relationship between particle size, plated capacity, and surface roughness 
can be explained as follows: in high-σ electrolytes, the formation of sparse and large zinc nuclei, 
coupled with high growth resistance, leads to preferential zinc deposition onto these limited 
sites. This results in  the formation of large Zn particles. These large particles stack unevenly, 
creating a coarse surface with high roughness. This roughness is further amplified with 
increasing plated capacity, as deposition continues to focus on these pre -existing protrusions. 
Conversely, in low -σ electrolytes, the high density of small zinc nuclei and low growth 
resistance enable uniform zinc deposition across the numerous available sites. This results in 
small particle sizes that form a dense, smooth layer with low inherent roughness. Furthermore, 
this homogeneous morphology remains stable even at high plated capacities, effectively 
maintaining a low surface roughness.

“These dramatic differences confirm that high-σ electrolytes promote sparse nucleation and 
uneven growth, leading to rapidly escalating surface roughness with capacity. Conversely, low-
σ electrolytes enable dense nucleation and uniform plating, maintaining a consistently smooth 
surface even at high capacities.”

Response: We sincerely thank the reviewer for this comment. The reviewer points out that the

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 9 -->

surface tension (σ) values of electrolytes of 5vol% G4, 5vol% G2, and 30 vol% EG fall within 
a narrow range (50–55 mN m−1), yet the corresponding Zn morphologies at a high capacity (50 
mAh cm−2) differ significantly (Supplementary Fig. S15b–d in the revised SI). This observation 
is indeed crucial, and we appreciate the opportunity to clarify the underlying mechanism. The 
effect of σ on the final Zn morphology is not a simple linear relationship; rather, it arises from 
the combined influence of σ on both the nucleation and growth processes. Moreover, the extent 
of σ's impact during the growth process varies substantially with the plated capacity. Below, we 
address this point from both theoretical and experimental aspects. 
Theoretical Aspect: The influence of σ on Zn electrodeposition is fundamentally nonlinear. 
σ governs the nucleation energy barrier and the size of the nuclei in the nucleation process, as 
well as the particle size in the subsequent growth process. Specifically, in the nucleation process, 
the nucleation energy barrier (∆𝐺ℎ𝑒𝑡
∗ ) is proportional to the cube of the surface tension (σ3), as 
described by Equation 2 and Fig. 1b in our manuscript. Thus, even a small reduction in σ can 
substantially decrease the energy barrier for nucleation, leading to a higher density of nucleation 
sites (as supported by Fig. 2a in our manuscript  and Supplementary Fig. S 14 in th e SI ). 
Moreover, a lower σ reduces the critical nucleus radius (Equation 3 and Fig. 1b), promoting the 
formation of finer initial nuclei. In the subsequent growth process, lower σ also helps maintain 
smaller Zn particle sizes by reducing the growth resistance  (Equation 4 in the manuscript and 
Equation 15 in the SI). This effect is especially amplified under high plated capacities (Fig. 1c 
in our manuscript), where prolonged growth exacerbates differences in morphology—as clearly 
observed in Supplementary Fig. S15b–d. 
Experimental Aspect: The strikingly different morphologies shown in Supplementary Fig. 
S15b–d are obtained at a high areal capacity of 50 mAh cm −2. Under such harsh plating 
conditions, the cumulative effect of σ on nucleation and growth is markedly amplified. As such, 
even modest differences in σ (e.g., ~5 mN m−1) translate into visibly distinct deposit structures. 
This result aligns well with our the oretical inference that the influence of σ on particle size 
becomes more pronounced under high plated capacity (Fig. 1c). 
To further validate this argument, we conducted additional experiments at  a lower plated 
capacity of 10 mAh cm−2. As expected, the differences in particle size among electrolytes with 
EG, G4, and G2 are much less pronounced at these low capacities  (Fig. R1). This capacity-
dependent effect reinforces the critical role of σ in governing deposition uniformity, especially 
under harsh electrochemical conditions. 
In summary, we acknowledge that the absolute variation in σ is small, but its nonlinear 
impact—coupled with the high plated capacity—explains the dramatic divergence in 
morphology.

Response: We sincerely thank the reviewer for their comment, which allows us to further 
clarify the conceptual advance of our work. We fully agree that low-ε cosolvents can suppress 
water activity, but this effect is typically concentration -dependent. Our work int roduces 
electrolyte σ as a previously overlooked, fundamental parameter that governs Zn deposition, a

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 11 -->

concept distinct from the weak-solvation paradigm. We address the reviewer's concern in three 
key aspects: 
First, indeed, low-ε cosolvents can effectively suppress water activity—but this generally 
requires high cosolvent concentrations (typically > 50 vol%). For instance, seminal works by 
Guo et al. (Energy Environ. Sci. 17, 4569-4581, (2024)) and Zhi et al. (Nat. Commun. 14, 1828, 
(2023)) employed 60 vol% G2 and 57 vol% sulfolane , respectively, to achieve this. In stark 
contrast, our strategy employs only 5 vol% TEP, a concentration too low to bind or constrain 
95 vol% water. Instead, this trace amount of additive does reduce the electrolyte's σ dramatically 
(Fig.1f), which directly regulates Zn nucleation and growth (Fig. 2, Supplementary Fig. 14, 15, 
26, 27). 
Second, the core of our work is to reveal how σ governs both nucleation and growth  
processes. While previous studies focused on solvation, they did not even mention or 
investigate the critical role of σ. In our work, we provide a comprehensive theoretical 
framework, derived from classical nucleation theory, that quantitatively establishes σ as the key 
parameter controlling both the nucleation energy barrier and growth resistance.  This is a 
substantial conceptual advance, offering a new design dimension for electrolytes beyond 
solvation chemistry. 
Third, to decisively decouple the effect of low  ε from that of σ, we prepared low-σ 
electrolytes using surfactants. The results are unequivocal: both surfactant -containing 
electrolytes, despite not being low-ε cosolvents, yielded identical outcomes—dense nucleation 
and compact Zn growth (Fig. R2, R3).  
Specifically, we selected a cationic surfactant, tetrabutylammonium chloride ( (Bu4N)Cl), 
and a non -ionic surfactant, triton x-100, for their short carbon chains and high aqueous 
compatibility. Two electrolytes were formulated by adding  0.2 wt% of each surf actant to the 
baseline solution, denoted as 0.2 wt% (Bu 4N)Cl and 0.2 wt% triton x-100, respectively. The 
addition of these surfactants significantly reduced the surface tension to 31.2 mN m−1 and 33.5 
mN m−1, respectively.  
Remarkably, both surfactant-containing electrolytes induce similar dense nucleation and 
compact growth of Zn as observed in our low -σ 5% TEP electrolyte.  At the beginning (3 
minutes) of electroplating with a high current density of 10 mA cm −2, the Zn deposits fully 
covered Cu foils, indicating the high density of Zn nuclei in these two electrolytes (Fig. R2a–
2c; Fig. R3a–3c). Extending the plating duration to 30 minutes , 1hour, 3 hour s, and 5 hours, 
corresponding to areal capacities of 5, 10, 30, and 50 mAh cm−2, respectively, the Zn deposits 
still maintain a dense, fine -grained morphology  (Fig. R2d–2g; Fig. R3d–3g). Besides, both 
electrolytes maintain low RMS roughness across all plated capacities, reaching only ~10.1 μm 
for 0.2 wt% (Bu4N)Cl (Fig. R2h, 2i) and ~10.5 μm for 0.2 wt% triton x-100 (Fig. R3h, 3i) even 
at 50 mAh cm −2—more than 97% reduction compared to 434 μm in 0% Addi. This evidence 
confirms that the dense nucleation and growth are a direct result of reduced σ, independent of 
the solvent's ε.

Motivation 
Mitigating dendrite growth and side reactions of Zn at a practical level is a persistent challenge.  
To date, various strategies have been reported to suppress these anode -related challenges, 
including anode engineering (e.g., artificial protective layers on Zn, three-dimensional (3D) Zn 
hosts, surface modification of plating substrates), as well as electrolyte design (e.g., 
concentrated-salt electrolytes, organic -water hybrid electrolytes). However, most of t hese 
approaches often work under  moderate electrochemical conditions (i.e., small areal currents 
and capacities) and very small depth of discharge (DOD, e.g., below 1%). Moreover, these

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 31 -->

strategies would compromise the overall energy density, safety, and cost of batteries. To address 
these issues, it is essential to come up with a balanced strategy that effectively mitigates dendrite 
growth and side reactions at a practical level (i.e., high areal current, high areal capacity, and 
high DOD) while maintaining cost-effectiveness, safety, and energy density. 
 
Notably, the electrodeposition of Zn—encompassing both nucleation and growth processes —
occurs directly at the electrolyte/Zn interface. This makes the electrolyte σ a particularly 
relevant parameter , as it significantly influences interfacial properties and should impact 
deposition behavior of Zn. However, a fundamental and quantitative understanding of how σ 
governs Zn nucleation and growth remains lacking. Traditional electrolyte design has primarily 
focused on suppressing water activity by using high organic solvent content (> 55 vol.%) or 
modifying the adsorption layer on Zn through functional additives in electrolytes. While these 
approaches have shown some improvement,  they have not established σ as a targeted design 
parameter, nor have they provided quantitative relationships between σ and deposition 
outcomes. Furthermore, the impact of σ on battery performance under practical conditions (high 
current, high capacity,  high DOD, Ah-level pouch cel ls) and its overall effects on dendrite 
suppression, SEI stabilization, and side reaction mitigation remain insufficiently explored. 
 
Idea / methods / Performance 
Herein, our study introduces a low-σ electrolyte strategy that enables dense Zn nucleation and 
fine-grained growth, even under harsh electrochemical conditions. Unlike all previous studies 
that overlooked the significance of σ, we establish a quantitative theoretical framework based 
on classical nucleation theory, demonstrating σ as the key parameter governing both nucleation 
energy barrier  (nucleation process)  and growth resistance  (growth process). The low -σ 
electrolyte is achieved by adding only trace amounts (5 vol.%) of low-polarity TEP into the 
reference electrolyte [2m Zn(OTf)2 in water with 1.5 wt.% zinc acetate dihydrate] . Low-
polarity solvents, characterized by their low ε, can effectively break the strong dipole-dipole 
interaction networks between water molecules, thereby lowering σ. Our result shows that t he 
addition of merely 5 vol.% TEP  can effectively lower both the σ (to 55% of the reference 
electrolyte) and  the nucleation energy barrier (to 17% of that in the reference electrolyte) 
without significantly compromising the ionic conductivity (still up to  90% of that of the 
reference electrolyte).  The as-obtained low -σ electrolyte fundamentally transform s sparse 
nucleation/coarse-grained growth into dense nucleation/fine-grained growth. In addition to TEP, 
this regulated nucleation and growth behavior is  universally observed in other low-σ 
electrolytes, achieved by adding merely 5 vol.% of other low-polarity solvents (e.g., G2, G4, 
and DME). 
 
This regulated nucleation and growth mode in low-σ electrolytes enables dendrite-free plating, 
stabilizes the SEI, and suppresses side reactions even under harsh electrochemical conditions. 
Consequently, ZMAs in low -σ electrolytes achieve  a high average CE  of 99.72% at 1 mA 
cm−2/1 mAh cm −2 over a record lifespan of 8,400 hours (350 days) —a 2 - to 40 -fold 
improvement over previous benchmarks  (typically 97.5–99.6% over 200–4,000 hours). Even 
under harsh electrochemical conditions , e.g., 10 mA cm −2/10 mAh cm −2, ZMAs still exhibit 
significantly extended lifespans of 2,200 hours (at 17% DOD) and 300 hours (at 85% DOD),

Besides, Ah-level pouch cells operating under lean electrolyte (11.4 g Ah−1) and low N/P ratios

Significance 
Our work establishes quantitative relationships between σ and Zn electrodeposition behavior, 
and demonstrates highly stable ZMAs in low -σ electrolytes even under harsh electrochemical 
conditions. These findings highlight σ as a crucial yet previously overlooked electrolyte 
property, introducing it as a new design criterion of electrolytes for high-performance practical 
RZMBs. Besides, l ow-σ electrolytes, achieved by the addition of merely 5 vol.%  of low-
polarity solvents , provides a cost -effective, scalable pathway toward commercially viable 
RZMBs without compromising safety or energy density.  The principles developed here could 
potentially be extended to other metal anode systems, such as Li and Na metal anodes.
