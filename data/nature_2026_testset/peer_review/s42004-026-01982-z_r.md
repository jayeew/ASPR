Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
I do not recommend publication of this manuscript in Communications Chemistry in its current form. While the chemistry is
useful, the work does not reach the level of conceptual novelty and mechanistic insight expected for this journal. 
The authors describe a Vilsmeier reagent–promoted, metal-free decarboxylative cyclization of 2-(heteroaryl)
tetrahydroisoquinoline-1-carboxylic acids to give THIQ-fused six- and seven-membered polycyclic heterocycles. The
reaction proceeds under mild conditions, has a reasonable substrate scope within this platform, can be performed on gram
scale, and selected products show antiproliferative activity in cancer cell lines. 
The reasons for this decision are as follows. 
1. Limited conceptual novelty 
The reaction design appears to be an application of known Vilsmeier reactivity (iminium formation, decarboxylation,
intramolecular trapping) to a particular THIQ-1-carboxylic acid scaffold. The manuscript does not clearly demonstrate a
fundamentally new activation mode or reaction concept, but rather a new example within established Vilsmeier chemistry. 
2. Mechanistic and synthetic impact below the level of Communications Chemistry 
The mechanistic evidence is qualitative (intermediate detection and racemization) and does not go beyond a plausible
scheme. The scope, although adequate within the chosen scaffold family, remains structurally narrow and is not clearly
positioned against existing routes to similar frameworks. 
3. Overstated claims and preliminary biological data 
The repeated description of the method as “green” or “environmentally friendly” is not well supported given the use of DCM
and stoichiometric Vilsmeier reagent. The biological results are preliminary screening data without mechanistic or SAR
analysis and do not substantially elevate the impact of the chemical work.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, the authors represent an environmentally friendly and efficient synthetic strategy for constructing a novel
tetrahydroisoquinoline (THIQ)-fused polycyclic heterocyclic framework via a Vilsmeier reagent promoted decarboxylative
cyclisation carried out under mild, metal-free conditions. Although there are many literatures concerning the synthesis of
tetrahydroisoquinoline fused six-membered heterocycle, the synthesis of seven-membered fused heterocycle in a facile
approach is rare. Here, the authors have not limited the methodology to the synthesis of fused seven-membered
heterocycles but also manipulated the strategy for the synthesis of fused six-membered heterocycles, which is appreciated.
The method shows broad functional-group tolerance, operational simplicity, and moderate to excellent yields, emphasising
its practical utility. 
The biological evaluation, which indicates notable antitumor activity of the synthesised derivatives, is
encouraging; however, more detailed experimental data and structure-activity relationship studies would strengthen these
claims. Overall, the study makes a valuable contribution to heterocyclic synthesis and medicinal chemistry. So, the article is
suitable for publication after addressing the following comments. 
1. In all the cases, the authors have implemented arylated tetrahydroisoquinoline-derived acids. Is the reaction working with
alkylated terahydroisoquinoline? 
2. The author should include scope of reacting partners with functional groups like amines and esters. 
3. Does pyrrole work as a reacting partner instead of indole? 
4. The author should perform a control experiment to show the aryl substitution with terahydroisoquinoline is pivotal for the
reaction to happen. 
5. In the mechanism, “CO” is released as the byproduct, which should be termed as ‘decarbonylation instead of
decarboxylation’. 
6. In the SI, although all the spectral data have been given systematically, but no NMR spectra has been incorporated. So,
please include the NMR spectra.

represent fundamental differences from the established reactivity of Vilsmeier reagent. 
J. Org. Chem. 2011, 76, 2880–2883 —reports a convenient one-pot synthesis of pyrimidin-4(3H)-
one and proposes a reaction mechanism involving sequential halogenation, formylation, and 
intramolecular nucleophilic cyclization.  Compared to the Vilsmeier reagent -mediated reactions 
reported by Dong's team, although both involve iminium formation and nucleophilic cyclization, 
the specific me chanisms and reaction conditions are entirely different. Firstly, in our developed 
method, the iminium ion is formed through a decarbonylation process of the substrate itself, 
facilitated by the Vilsmeier reagent, rather than the introduction of an iminium ion by the Vilsmeier 
reagent. Moreover, in the subsequent cyclization process, the Vilsmeier reagent does not act as an 
electron-deficient center to induce nucleophilic reactions. Instead, the cyclization is initiated by a 
nucleophilic attack on the imini um ion generated from decarbonylation by an intramolecular 
nucleophilic site. Therefore, our synthetic approach represents a fundamental conceptual departure 
from traditional methods. 
RSC Adv., 2023, 13, 26604  — presents synthetic schemes for the preparatio n of various 
heterocyclic compounds using Vilsmeier–Haack (V . H.) reagent. The Vilsmeier–Haack reaction is 
a classic example of the application of Vilsmeier reagent, which can convert electron-rich aromatic 
rings into aryl aldehydes. Conceptually, it involves the generation of the Vilsmeier–Haack reagent 
(i.e., the iminium salt) and the subsequent formylation reaction. In contrast, our method merely 
employs the Vilsmeier reagent to induce decarbonylation and does not utilize the Vilsmeier reagent 
as a carbon source in the subsequent reaction. There are fundamental differences in both mechanism 
and reaction type. 
These differences are summarized as follows: 
(i) Iminium Formation 
In traditional methods involving Vilsmeier reagent, the iminium formation is merely the insertion 
of the Vilsmeier reagent into the substrate molecule, rather than the formation of an iminium ion 
following decarbonylation of the substrate. In our reaction system, Vilsmeier reagent, promotes the 
substrate molecule itself to undergo decarbonylation to form an imine intermediate, and then the 
subsequent cyclization reaction occurs. This is fundamentally different from the formation of 
traditional imines. 
(ii) Decarboxylation 
Upon further investigation  of the reaction mechanism, it was observed that CO is released as a 
byproduct. Accordingly, we have revised the terminology from "decarboxylation" to 
"decarbonylation" throughout the manuscript. A thorough review of the existing literature reveals 
that there are no prior reports of Vilsmeier reagent being involved in or catalyzing decarbonylation 
reactions. In this context, our method represents a pioneering discovery, as it demonstrates that the 
reagent can induce decarbonylation of the substrates, subseq uently triggering the formation of key 
intermediates and products. This mechanistic insight introduces a novel dimension to the field.

(iii) Intramolecular trapping 
In prior investigations, the intramolecular trapping facilitated by Vilsmeier reagent has b een 
documented as a process wherein a nucleophilic site within the substrate molecule nucleophilically 
attacks the Vilsmeier reagent, thereby inducing cyclization and yielding the desired product. Within 
this framework, the Vilsmeier reagent functions as a  carbon source, with dimethylamine being 
released as a byproduct subsequent to the reaction. Conversely, the cyclization process inherent to 
the novel method unveiled in this manuscript transpires independently of Vilsmeier reagent 
involvement, thus marking a salient departure from conventional methodologies. 
(iv) Reaction applications 
This method applies the Vilsmeier reagent to tetrahydroisoquinoline  carboxylic acid derivatives 
with more complex structures, employing a reaction mechanism distinct from that of traditional 
Vilsmeier reagent applications. This method enables the efficient synthesis, under mild conditions, 
of a class of tetrahydroisoquino line-fused heterocycles featuring a novel seven -membered fused 
heterocyclic core that has been rarely reported; several of the synthesized compounds also exhibit 
potential biological activity. The substrates explored in the manuscript include 
tetrahydroisoquinoline-1-carboxylic acid derivatives and some aromatic heterocyclic derivatives. 
We are still exploring the scope of this method and have found that fully hydrogenated 
tetrahydroisoquinoline substrate  1z can also smoothly yield the desired products thro ugh this 
reaction, demonstrating that the method has the potential to be applicable to a wider range of 
aliphatic compounds. 
In the revised manuscript we have systematically categorized the existing decarbonylation strategies 
and incorporated them into the  Introduction (see the third and fourth paragraphs  of the revised 
manuscript). Additionally, a concise summary figure (see Figure 1C, in the revised manuscript) has 
been added to highlight the differences of the Vilsmeier reagent-promoted decarbonylation method. 
We hope that these additions will adequately address the reviewer's concerns regarding novelty.

structurally narrow and is not clearly positioned against existing routes to similar frameworks.
