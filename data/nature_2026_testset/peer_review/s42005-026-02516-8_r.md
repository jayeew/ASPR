Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In the manuscript by Pan et al. the authors report on their results of spatially- and time-resolved hole-burning dynamics of the
exciton halos observed in monolayers of WS2 at room temperature. They performed an extensive analysis of spatially- and
time-resolved photoluminescence concluding with the importance of reversible local doping effects and irreversible photo-
oxidation effects at intermediate and high pump densities, respectively. The study is overall well executed with careful
analysis of the experimental data, clear presentation, and well-presented discussion. The overall topic of non-linear and
non-equilibrium transport dynamics is important with the present manuscript serving as a helpful contribution to the
discussions in the field. Altogether, the manuscript should be suitable for publication in the Communications Physics journal,
albeit after a couple of points are taken care of, as outlined in detail below: 
1. Most importantly, the authors should connect their observations in terms of the explored density regime to previous work.
Fluence per pulse should be given and compared to those from earlier reports. This is particularly important given the
comparatively high laser powers up to a few mW used in this study as well as the implied claim that these results challenge
previous interpretations for lower density regimes. 
2. Related to the above, the effect of 
heating should be considered. In general, I would suggest to analyze the spectra after
the excitation in the middle of spot as a continuous function of the laser power. Then, it would also be easier to judge
whether the spectrum shifts as a whole or whether there is an exchange of PL intensity between the exciton and the trion.
Since this is arguably the key observable in this study, density dependent analysis should be given particular attention
including the data in the main manuscript. Moreover, one should consider that distinguishing exciton and trion spectra can
become challenging as the peak linewidth increases, due to either pump power or 
temperature. 
3. With respect to the main conclusions, I would suggest to present clear statements about the role of exciton-exciton-
annihilation and the exciton-to-trion transfer. It would be also helpful to the readers if the authors state all assumptions
underlying their interpretation, namely that the trion PL is much weaker and short-lived than that of the excitons (as far as I
understand the ). 
4. Considering the interpretation it would also be useful to make statements of whether the authors conclude that the exciton
and phonon Seebeck effects (Refs. 31, 32) are inadequate altogether or go hand in hand with the effects of excitons-to-trion
transfer proposed in this manuscript. I think it is good to challenge interpretations even if there has not been too much
controversy about them so far. It should only be made clear which parts are challenged and why. 
5. A few more minor points: 
- there is one more paper reporting the halos (see SI there): https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2053-1583/abbd51/pdf

In this manuscript, the transport properties of excitons in WS 2 are measured by ultrafast optical PL. With the 
increasing of optical pumping power, the strange ultrafast spatial hole burning effect and the re -brightening effect 
were observed. By means of time- and spatially resolved photoluminescence imaging spectroscopy, these two effects 
are well explained by the ex citon-exciton annihilation, efficient hole trapping by intrinsic sulfur vacancy defects, 
and laser -induced photo -oxidation. I think this explanation, derived from the experimental data, is sufficiently 
convincing. But some details still need to be discussed further. 
 
We thank the referee for his/her careful review of our manuscript and positive 
comments. 
 
i) The l aser power in the experiment is so high (up to ~1mW), and what’ s more the pumping source is a 
femtosecond laser. The heating effect seems to be huge and may affect the transport of the exciton. 
 
Thanks for suggestion. So far, we do not see any literatures reporting significant 
effects of laser heating on the transport of excitons. Regarding the control of 
exciton transport, three approaches have been demonstrated to be efficient: strain 
engineering, external electric field modulation, and dielectric disorde r control.  
Laser heating effect hasn’t been demonstrated to have significant effect on the 
transport of excitons. Actually, we have paid particular attention to the possible 
laser heating effect  during our measurements and analyses . We analysed the 
power-dependence of the neutral exciton PL peak but did not find any obvious 
peak shift ( see Fig. 1(e) of the revised manuscript ). This suggests that the  
heating effect is negligibly small in our work. Moreover, if laser heating had a 
pronounced influence on the ultrafast spatial hole burning process, one would 
expect to observe a gradual recovery of the spatial hole in Fig. 2(a)  as heat 
dissipates with  time. However, such behaviour was not observed.  Therefore,

effects of laser heating can be neglected in this work. 
ii) The re -brightening effect in the paper is explained by the laser -induced photo -oxidation at high power. It’ s 
expected to see this effect di sappear at high level vacuum. 1.5 Torr is not enough. Or it can be considered to 
conduct this experiment under an argon gas atmosphere. 
 
We agree with the referee that measurements under higher level vacuum could 
give more convincing support to our model o f the re -brightening effect . 
However, a s shown in Supplementary Fig. S4, a vacuum level of 1.5 Torr is 
already sufficient to significantly suppress the photo -oxidation effect at the 
centre of the laser spot.  For samples in ambient air, the re-brightening effect can 
be identified for pumping power a round 400 µW. However, for samples under 
vacuum level of 1.5 Torr, the re -brightening effect appears only for pumping 
power higher than 1200 µW (as shown in Fig. S4). This tells that t he threshold 
power under vacu um level of 1.5 Torr is at least three times that under ambient 
conditions. Due to the limited performance of the available instruments, 
measurements under higher vacuum are not available at this moment. However, 
the significant suppression of the re -brightening effect under 1.5 Torr is already 
convincing to support our proposed model . We would like to perform the direct 
measurements at higher vacuum conditions in the future when the required 
experimental conditions are available.

In their paper, Pan et al. investigated the mechanism behind the halo emission pattern observed in WS 2 under high 
pumping power. They proposed that at the halo center, an EEA process generates fre e electrons and holes, one of 
which becomes trapped at sulfur vacancies, leading to the formation of trions. These trions exhibit low radiative

offering spatial, temporal, and spectral resolution. A key piece of supporting evidence is the red -shifted emission 
peak at the pumping center,  which aligns well with the trion emission energy, whereas the outer regions exhibit 
emission at the characteristic excitonic energy. Additionally, the authors observed a re -brightening of the halo 
center at even higher pump powers, which  they attributed t o a photo -oxidation mechanism. This explanation is

The data looks nice and the explanation is convincing. I support their publication in Communication Physics if they 
can address my following questions: 
 
We thank the referee  for his/her careful review of our manuscript and 
recommendations. 
 
1. What is the typical sulfur vacancy density in the samples? It is important to rule out the scenario of very lo w 
vacancy density, as that would not yield sufficient trion population to account for the emission quenching. 
 
Based on reports from other groups , typical density of sulfur vacancies in 
monolayer TMDCs can be estimated to be  as high as 10 10 to 1013 cm-2 (Science 
364, 468 (2 019), ACS Appl. Mater. Interfaces 7, 11921 (2015) ). The intrinsic 
doping concentration in monolayer TMDCs is also on the order of 10 10 to 10 12 
cm-2 (Nano Lett. 21, 6773 (2021) ). Therefore, the density of sulfur vacancies in 
monolayer WS 2 is sufficient to i nduce spatially localized doping through the 
capturing of holes, which subsequently promotes the formation of trions. 
2. Why can the EEA process alone not explain the formation of the halo pattern? 
 
The EEA rate depends on the density of excitons . Due to t he Gaussian

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 12 -->

distribution of the laser spot, the EEA effect will be strongest at the spot centre, 
leading to rapid consumption of excitons at the spot centre. However, once the 
exciton density decreases to be the same as that in the outer region, the 
corresponding EEA rate will also become the same. This will finally lead to a 
"flat-top" exciton distribution, instead of a halo pattern. 
3. Could the authors provide a more detailed description in the main text of how the spatially resolved 
measurements in Fig. 3a were performed? 
 
The magnified real -space image of the sample, acquired through the micro -
photoluminescence spectroscopy system, is projected onto the entrance slit of 
the spectrometer. An adjustable confocal hole is also installed in  front of the 
spectrometer entrance slit . By adjusting the size and position of the confocal 
hole, we can select both the detection area and the spatial resolution . This 
information has been given in our revised manuscript. 
4. In Fig. 2a, most of the halo pattern appears asymmetric. Specifically, the left side is darker while the right side is 
brighter. What is the underlying reason for this asymmetry? 
 
Such asymmetry arises from the intrinsic spatial inhomogeneity (e.g., density of 
sulfur vacancies, crysta l quality)  of the sample. It depends on the specific 
detection position one selects. Other cases (e.g., the left side is brighter) can also 
be observed. 
5. There are minor inconsistencies in the labeling: in Fig. 2a, the power is indicated as 2.1 µW, whereas in Fig. 2b 
and the main text, it is given as 2.08 µW. 
 
Thanks for pointing out these inconsistencies. We have made corresponding 
corrections in our revised manuscript.
