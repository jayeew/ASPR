Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
I read the article by Chen et al. with great interest. The authors describe and evaluate a novel immunoprecipitation-based
mass spectrometry method for the characterization and quantification of Aβ species. The presented method, requiring only
one round of IP, is an improvement upon a previous IP-based MS method (PAβ V1.0), that required two rounds of
immunoprecipitation. The authors achieved this by testing a panel of binding buffers. The reliability of the technique is
demonstrated by both internal reliability analyses, using variable volumes of protein and plasma, and external, biological
validation in two cohorts. Importantly, the authors meticulously detailed the development of their method, accounting for
many parameters during optimization of the protocol. The proposed method could have important implication in the field of
Alzheimer’s biomarkers as it offers a scalable, affordable and reproducible alternative to immunoassay-based techniques.
Some additions could further enhance the evidence provided. We hope the following comments will help the authors during
revision. 
Major comments 
- Figure 2 and 3A: A possible interference peak can be seen at approximately 3900 m/z for some conditions of the PAβ V2.0,
but it is not noted on the graph. The authors should provide an explanation, if this is indeed an interference peak. 
- Figure 3B,C: the values presented in fig 3C do not correspond to the values in the graphs of figure 3B nor the text. It seems
that the columns PAβ V 1.0 and V 2.0 in Fig 3C have been accidentally switched. 
- Figure 5B: the top graph should display Aβ1-40/ Aβ1-40 IS based on the text (“…we observed overall better recovery when
using analyte specific IS to normalize peak area”) but is labelled as Aβ1-42/ Aβ1-40 IS. Please correct or clarify. 
- Page 10, Line 342: the S/N cutoff for Aβ1-40 seems to have been omitted, as a cutoff of 3 applies only to Aβ1-42. What was
the criterion for the selection of these cut-off values? 
- Results, Paragraph 3.5: Was there any statistics applied to show that the PAβ V2.0 assay retained a higher proportion of
Aβ peptides? Additionally, bar graphs should be substituted with dot plots, so that individual data points can be surveyed. 
- The authors should validate their assay against a state-of-the-art immunoassay, such as the Simoa, which they have
access to, in order to support the utility of their method. Furthermore, the advantages of the PAβ v 2.0 over other
measurement techniques than the previous version, should be discussed. 
- Results, Paragraphs 3.3 - 3.6: Data on other measured amyloid species (Aβ1-38, Aβ3-40, Aβ1-39, APP669-711) should be
provided for completeness. 
- Correlation of measured markers with continuous cognitive scores (MoCA, MMSE) apart from diagnosis would further
reinforce the biological validity of the results. 
Minor comments 
- Fig 4B,C: The authors should note the concentrations of analytes above the bar charts or using a color-coded legend.

 A signal to noise ratio of 3 was selected as the detection limit following what was done

average peak area for recovery calculations, hence the bar charts. We have clarified this

Simoa, which they have access to, in order to support the utility of their method. Furthermore,

We have now validated the assay against SIMOA immunoassays for Aβ42 and Aβ40 in a 
much larger cohort of cognitively normal participants (n=317) some of whom have 
biological evidence of Aβ pathology based on Aβ PET imaging. The improved IP-MS Aβ 
assay was more accurate than Simoa immunoassays (AUCs of 0.81 vs. 0.75) to identify 
abnormal Aβ PET scans, demonstrating potential utility for early Aβ pathology. 
Additionally, the plasma IP-MS Aβ assay showed good concordance with Aβ PET in a 
manner that suggested that soluble Aβ species, detected by this assay, become 
abnormal prior to being detectable on PET scans in aggregated forms.

We thank the reviewer for their favorable comments on the description and performance 
of the assay described in this manuscript. We beg to differ on their comment regarding 
novelty of the method. In the earlier version seen by the reviewer, we mostly reported 
analytical streamlining and simplification relative to other mass spectrometry-based 
assays. In the current version, we show that not only does the improved method have 
analytical handling advantages over the earlier assay version and existing mass spec-
based abeta assays on the market, but we also additionally demonstrate that the version 
2.0 assay has superior clinical performance than the original version and the Quanterix 
Simla assays. These findings substantially improve the novelty and significance of the 
manuscript which we are confident makes it worthy of publication in Nature 
Communications. 
 
Major Comments for the reviewer that will make this paper more impactful: 
1. Instead of focusing just on the method development (similar to the Nakamura paper, although 
the reviewer is pleased to see that it is indeed reproducible and provides similar sensitivity for 
the target analytes Aβ1-42 and Aβ1-40 in the plasma), the authors should compare their results 
with the Nakamura paper and their quantitative findings. The PITT-ADRC cohort size is

Given the limited sample size and neuroimaging data, we have decided to exclude the 
ADRC cohort from the clinical performance assessment and use it solely for correlation 
testing. Instead, we have incorporated an additional cohort (n=317) beyond the AGUEDA 
cohort (n=76) The new cohort includes 54 PET-positive cases, with many others with 
intermediate Aβ PET uptake. This focus on preclinical disease allowed us to evaluate the 
improved IP-MS plasma Aβ assay in the context of incipient disease.  
 
2. The plasma Aβ42/40-fold changes are very low between amyloid positive and amyloid 
negative participants. What are the AUCs for these analytes in both the cohorts? The authors 
should also comment on the small (10-15 %) change between normal controls and Aβ positive

We have added the AUC for the improved assay, which equals 0.81, using a new cohort 
of 317 cognitively normal participants. The fold change for plasma Aβ remains ~10-15%,

blood that might be produced from the brain. We have covered this in our previous work

3. The cohorts must have SIMOA/ Quanterix or other Aβ immunoassay data associated, how

and PET results were proportionately represented according to the cohort's 
demographics. The results showed an AUC of 0.8, which is not significantly different 
from the AUC obtained using 250 µL with the same samples.
