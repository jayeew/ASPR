Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript reports mixing BaHfO3 into (Sr,Bi,Na)TiO3 to tune the dielectric-maximum temperature Tm to room
temperature, and claims a combination of high recoverable energy density (16.2 J/cm2) and efficiency (92%). While the
reported values are impressive, the discussion about the physical mechanism is less convincing. Therefore, it would require
a major revision and a few pieces of supplementary data before the manuscript could be recommended for publication in
Nature Communications. 
1. Line 31 “a phase boundary between relaxor and superparaelectric phases” Line 63 “Below Tm, relaxor (RE) state …” Line
69 “In contrast, the superparaelectric (SPE) state appears above Tm …” 
The authors should be cautious when talking about a “phase boundary” at Tm. In relaxor ferroelectrics, Tm should be never
taken as an actual phase-transition temperature, as Tm strongly depends on measurement condition (i.e., probe frequency).
Instead, it has been established since the ‘90s that, in order to extract the actual transition temperature, the frequency
dependency of Tm must be fitted to Vogel-Fulcher law, which yields the actual phase-transition temperature, Tf. For an
original reference of the idea, please refer to [Viehland, Dwight, et al. "Freezing of the polarization fluctuations in lead
magnesium niobate relaxors." Journal of Applied Physics 68, 2916 (1990)]. For a modern understanding of phase transitions
in relaxor ferroelectrics, please read [Takenaka, Hiroyuki, et al. "Slush-like polar structures in single-crystal relaxors." Nature
546, 391 (2017)]. 
To be consistent with the mainstream understanding of relaxor ferroelectrics and to avoid confusion from future readers, the
authors should avoid using the term of “phase boundary” and instead use descriptions such as “the crossover from dynamic
to static/frozen relaxor states”. This adaption should be made not only at Line 31, 63, and 69, but throughout the manuscript. 
2. Line 85, “it exhibits faster dynamics and minimal akin to SPE state” This sentence is hard to understand. Some words
might be missing. 
3. Line 86, “relative to the RFE state”. Please use consistent abbreviations throughout the manuscript, either “RE” or “RFE”. 
4. Line 98, “relaxor degree” is not considered good English. Please consider switching to another expression such as
“strength of relaxor behavior”. 
5. Line 110, “the increased lattice dimensions provide greater spatial freedom for polarization enhancement”. This is an
interesting point. Is this supported by the polarization value extracted from DFT? 
6. Left, Fig. 1a. Is this illustration based on phase-field simulations? If so, what are the compositions of the materials
simulated, for the top and bottom panels, respectively? Please describe in the figure/legend. 
Right, Fig. 1a. Again, assuming this illustration is from DFT, please describe the material compositions for such calculations.
Plus, please provide a legend for the colors of the atoms. 
7. Top, Fig. 1b. Again, please specify which chemical compositions these simulations correspond to. 
Bottom, Fig. 1b. If this data is from experiment, it should be clearly labelled. Plus, the abbreviations ER and NER should be
defined.

Either way, the material compositions and measurement conditions (frequency, temperature) should be labelled in

9. Top, Fig. 1d. The lattice expansion is highly exaggerated. Please label the actual expansion percentage. Also, it would be

12. Fig. 2b-2c. The significant difference between x = 0.3 and x = 0.35 is unexpected. First off, there is only a small difference
in chemical composition. Second, BaHfO3 is a paraelectric and, naively, one would expect adding more BaHfO3 should
lead to decreasing Tm. Is there any structural difference (density, grain size, phase separation, etc.) that leads to this
difference? 
13. Fig. 2e. How is Burns temperature (TB) extracted? Please provide a plot of these fittings. 
All the dielectric data presented in this work are from temperatures lower than TB, but TB is only meaningful if extracted from
the high-temperature side. See, for example, [Bokov, A. A., and Z-G. Ye. "Recent progress in relaxor ferroelectrics with
perovskite structure." Journal of materials science 41, 31 (2006)].

displacement? This value defines the maximum polarization that can be achieved in this system via polarization rotation
under electric field. 
15. Fig. 4g. What is the connection between bandgap and breakdown field? If the logic is “large bandgap -> low leakage
current -> high breakdown field”, please show a comparison of the leakage current (I-V curve) in each case. 
In fact, bandgap would be a good indicator of leakage only if the conductivity is determined by intrinsic excitations. In
perovskite oxides, however, the case of intrinsic excitation is relatively rare, and in most cases the leakage is determined by
extrinsic factors such as doping or trap states. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This report outlines a strategy to simultaneously achieve an exceptional comprehensive energy storage performance, Wrec
of 16.2 J/cm3 and η of 92% at a high Eb of 68 kV/mm, by actively constructing a super-relaxor critical state—a phase
boundary between relaxor and super-paraelectric phases—through targeted compositional tuning and polarization
configuration control. The approach incorporates both phase-field simulations and first-principle calculations to explore the
influence of BaHfO3 integration into the Sr0.5Bi0.25Na0.25TiO3 relaxor matrix. 
The study presents promising result and
could make a significant contribution to the ceramic community if further questions are clarified and refined as follows: 
1. In the first paragraph of the Results and Discussion, the authors stated that the addition of larger substituent ions, such as
Ba2+ and Hf4+ can expand the volume of the Sr0.5Bi0.25Na0.25TiO3 unit cell, and the increased lattice dimension can
provide greater spatial freedom for polarization enhancement. 
This description is a problematic assumption without clear
evidence. 
Instead, the composition-driven enhancement induced by the strongly isotropic polar disorder via the BaHfO3
doping should be the main reason for providing greater spatial freedom for the polarization enhancement, rather than the
increased lattice dimension. 
The authors should give more evidences to support this conclusion. 
2. The temperature-dependent dielectric permittivity curves (Fig. 2d), Raman scattering spectra (Fig. S6, Supplementary
Information) and P−E loops (Fig. 2f), as well as the high-temperature impedance response (Fig. 4e) show different trends for
x = 0.35 composition. 
The authors should describe their correlations and give explanation for the observed change in trend.
Also, the authors should show the reliability of experimental data. 
3. To understand the relationship between the dielectric properties and phase structures in SBNT−xBH ceramics, please
provide the complete XRD refinement analyses for all compositions. 
Moreover, specifically explain the roles of the cubic and
tetragonal structures play in enhancing the energy storage characteristics, particularly in relation to the emergence of the
super-paraelectric/ super-relaxor state. 
4. Why is the c/a map of SBNT−0.3BH in the Fig. S8 (Supplementary Information) done along the [110]pc zone axis rather
than [100]pc zone axis? 
Is there any specific consideration? 
5. In the Nyquist plots (Z' vs. -Z'') plots as shown in Fig. 4e and Fig. S10 (Supplementary Information), the same intervals of
Z’ and -Z” should be applied for semicircle comparison and to estimate the resistances. 
What type of resistance contribute to
the semicircle (for example, grain bulk, grain boundary, or interface between electrode and ceramic)? 
6. Abbreviations should be given with full names or chemical formula while they appear first time in the text, such as DFT
(density functional theory) and BBT12. 
7. The label ‘Density grain’ in Fig. S7a (Supplementary Information) should be corrected to ‘Dense grain’.

a simple geometric lattice expansion, and this enhanced polar disorder underpins the coexistence of high recoverable 
energy density and high efficiency in the designed superrelaxor critical state. Therefore, we have revised our discussion 
on this local structure feature accordingly.
