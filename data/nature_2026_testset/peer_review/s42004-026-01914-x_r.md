Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, the authors introduce BIOSCE, a chemigenetic biosensing platform based on HaloTag that employs
steric confinement-induced emission (SCE) to track protein conformational changes in living cells. The authors synthesize a
chemical probe, MTPABP-Cl, and systematically demonstrate the utility of their approach across several model systems
(CaM, FKBP–FRB, and SNAP25) using live-cell imaging. This study highlights the potential of chemigenetic sensors in
directly capturing protein activation dynamics. BIOSCE addresses a central challenge in cell biology: the real-time
visualization of protein dynamics that underpin essential cellular events, from neural activities to signal transduction. This
method demonstrates robustness and offers a valuable alternative to current strategies such as FRET-based approaches,
circumventing limitations such as restricted transferability and considerable design complexity. Although the detailed
mechanism by which SCE transduces subtle conformational changes into detectable optical signals could be further
clarified, BIOSCE nonetheless represents a substantial methodological advancement and a valuable new tool for probing
cellular signaling and physiological processes. 
The work is of high quality. I recommend addressing the following minor points to further strengthen the manuscript. Here are
some minor points: 
1. The manuscript reports quantum-yield values but omits the measurement details, making the results difficult to reproduce.
Supplement the measurement method of fluorescence quantum yield in the method section. 
2. The current notation for scientific numbers and units is inconsistent, which may cause confusion or misinterpretation of
numerical values. For example, numerical representation of the association rate constant (Ka) uses an incorrect symbol in
line 129. 
3. The compound name appears in inconsistent forms across the text, potentially confusing readers and affecting clarity. For
example, the “MTPABP-Cl” is inconsistent with the previous text in line 651 and 699. 
4. Lines 676-679, the author seems to be describing the definitions of F0 and F during ΔF/F0 calculation rather than the
frame rate of acquisition. Lines 728-729, 738-739 also have the same problem. There is ambiguity in the expression, so it
should be revised. 
5. While the manuscript presents a compelling biosensing platform, the discussion would be strengthened by a more explicit
acknowledgment of current limitations. The Discussion briefly mentions future in vivo applications, but potential challenges,
such as probe delivery, tissue penetration, or phototoxicity are not analyzed. It is recommended that the authors add a
section to the Discussion addressing the current limitations of the BIOSCE platform, particularly concerning its applicability
for in vivo or tissue-level imaging, and outline realistic strategies to overcome these challenges. 
6. Some recently published related work may be of reference value, e.g., 10.1002/agt2.70073, 10.1002/agt2.637,
10.1002/agt2.550. 
7. It is recommended that the authors include the chemical structure of MTPABP-Cl in Figure 1 to facilitate better reader
comprehension.

Jia, H. et al., in their article titled “Steric Confinement-Induced Emission Probe for Monitoring Protein Conformations in Live
Cells,” describe BIOSCE, a Bio probe based on Steric Confinement-Induced Emission, which utilizes the HaloTag protein
and a HaloTag ligand whose fluorescence properties change in response to steric confinement. This approach enables the
monitoring of protein conformational changes during cellular processes. The concept of developing chemogenetic probes

development of a new HaloTag ligand, MTPABP-Cl, whose fluorescence changes are triggered by the conformational

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript Jia et al. claim that 
they "introduced a technique that enables tracking of individual protein conformation
change via steric confinement-induced luminescence switching". The technique relies on already well-established HaloTag
system. The only unique aspect appears to be a newly synthesised HaloTag ligand, MTPABP-Cl, which the authors state
exhibits an increase in quantum yield to 27.5% upon binding to the HaloTag protein, and further increase to 38.6% upon
binding to the HaloTag fusion protein. However, no experimental data determining the quantum yield are presented in the
manuscript; therefore it is impossible to assess whether the fluorescent ligand is reliable. The authors regard the HaloTag
coupled with MTPABP-Cl 
as "Steric Confinement-Induced Emission Probe", and named the technique "BIOSCE". Yet, no
convincing experiments were performed to confirm that the observed fluorescence change is indeed due to the steric
confinement. They introduced three potential applications of "BIOSCE", in which fluorescence intensity changes were
observed. However, the interpretation of these results are evidently speculative and lack objectivity. They also include
additional experiments unrelated to "BIOSCE", such as single-particle tracking, FRAP, and Western blotting. However, this
reviewer must point out that both the experiments and their interpretations fall short of the standards required for publication
in an international journal. Furthermore, experimental details are insufficiently described, and figure legends lack necessary
information. The descriptions in the main text are often abstract and subjective, which diminishes the overall reliability and
renders the study unconvincing. In conclusion the study presented in this manuscript fails to provide convincing evidence or
a solid experimental foundation, and therefore should be rejected by Communications Chemistry.
