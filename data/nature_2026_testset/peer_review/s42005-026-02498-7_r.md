Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript by Date et al. presents a high-quality and carefully executed study of the electronic structure of 4Hb-TaS2
using termination-resolved micro-ARPES. The authors convincingly demonstrate that while the T-terminated surface retains
partially filled metallic states with planar chirality, subsurface T layers beneath H terminations representative of the bulk are
fully gapped due to complete charge transfer to adjacent H layers. These findings provide strong evidence against cluster
Mott localization in this system and instead support a superconductivity mechanism based on Josephson-like coupling
between H layers. Overall, the manuscript is very well written, the data are compelling, and the conclusions are of broad
interest to the condensed matter physics community, especially given the growing attention on chiral superconductivity and
dichalcogenide heterostructures. However, some clarifications and minor revisions are required to strengthen the
presentation, refine certain claims, and better situate the results with respect to recent literature. Addressing these points will
improve the accessibility and impact of the manuscript without altering its central conclusions. 
- In page 4 (line 10-22), the authors claim that the Fermi surface observed on the T termination reflects partial occupation of
the flat band, consistent with theoretical predictions of charge transfer between 0.4-1 electrons per 13 Ta (Crippa et al. [24]).
However, no direct quantitative estimate of charge transfer based on the experimental Fermi surface areas is provided in the
manuscript. The authors are suggested to strengthen their claim by explicitly calculating and reporting the charge transfer
from the difference in Fermi surface areas between H- and T-terminated regions relative to the reference 2H and 1T-TaS2
phases. This calculation should include a clear description of the methodology and assumptions, allowing reproducibility.
and comparing the results from the work by Almoalem et al. [21]. 
- The manuscript would benefit from a clearer discussion of the microscopic origin of this charge redistribution. Is it primarily
driven by differences in work function, electrostatic potential gradients between layers, or a more subtle mechanism? A
clarification of this point would add depth and rigor to the interpretation. 
- There are several minor typographical and labeling inconsistencies in the manuscript text and Figure captions that should
be carefully checked and corrected. For example: 
1) Page 2, Fig. 1 caption: panel (d) is incorrectly referred to for the dispersion along the M-Γ-M direction on the T termination,
this should read panel (g). 
2) Page 3, Fig. 2 caption: panels (h, i) are described as showing DFT calculations at EF and E = EF-0.6 eV, whereas the
corresponding figures indicate -0.5 eV, leading to a mismatch that should be resolved. 
3) Page 3, lines 19: the phrase “the weaker intensity from the from the subsurface” contains a duplicated ‘from the’ and
should be corrected. 
The authors are recommended to carefully proofread the manuscript and Figures for such minor errors to ensure consistency
and clarity. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Authors use ARPES to study the electronic structure of 4Hb TaS2, a Mott/Sc composite. This topic is indeed interesting and

will appeal to wider CMP community. The data presented in the manuscript of of high quality, clearly showing the chiral
symmetry of the FS and relatively sharp 
bands. Authors were able to measure and identify two different surface terminations.
The conclusions based on these data are however less clear. Authors propose scenario of charge transfer that results in
almost rigid band shifts, as illustrated in Fig. 3g. There are certain aspects of the data that call for more in-depth analysis and
explanation, before one could move to justify proposed scenario. In Fig. 3a, b there seems to be two sets of bands present.
In 3a, the H termination, “u” shaped bands towards M point that cross Ef and “M” shaped bands near Gamma below -1 eV
are present. These are also present in 3b, i. e. T termination. In that data there is additional set of “M” shape bands above -1
eV and hole-electro-hole bands near the Ef. Authors claim they can identify the T bulk band, from the T layer under set of T-
H layers. If ARPES can obtain signal from two layers down, which is not impossible, then why the T-originated “M” shaped
bands are absent in H -terminated signal? This is especially important to resolve, because the “u” shaped bands likely
originating from H layer are clearly present in T-terminated data. 
Without clear understanding the the band assignment in for each termination, it is difficult to justify the conclusions presented
in the manuscript. It would be also helpful to have data for different photon energy plotted over the same momentum/energy
ranges to better understand the differences. The manuscript could be potentially published if the above issues are
satisfactorily resolved, or claim softened to match the level of evidence one can draw from the data.

Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors have addressed the earlier comments thoroughly. In particular, the newly added Supplemental Note 5 offers a
detailed and well-structured estimate of charge transfer based on a Luttinger-count analysis, which substantially strengthens
their central argument. The discussion is now quantitatively supported, and the comparison with previous work, including
that of Almoalem et al., is clearly presented. The authors also provide convincing evidence that the primary contribution to
charge transfer originates from the work-function difference between the H and T layers. The manuscript’s clarity, coherence,
and scientific rigor have been substantially enhanced. I recommend that the revised manuscript be accepted for publication. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The Authors addressed most of the concerns and questions outlined in the reports. The revised version of the manuscript is
suitable for publication.
