Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, the authors demonstrate that the layered PbI2 crystals support the propagation of low-loss and highly
confined hyperbolic phonon polaritons in deep terahertz range. Although there have been extensive studies on phonon
polaritons in the mid-infrared and far-infrared range, phonon polaritons with high FOM in the deep terahertz (THz) frequency
range are still lacking, which is crucial for THz nanophotonic devices. The experimental results are well supported by the
theory. I have no doubt about the significance and novelty of this work. Therefore, I can recommend its publication in Nature
Communications if the authors can address the following technical comments: 
1. The manuscript structure makes it difficult for readers to follow the story. For example, the figure description and its
interpretations are placed separately in the Results and Discussion sections. I recommend that the authors restructure their
manuscript according to the standard format used in other published papers in Nature Communications. 
2. In addition to the confinement factor and FOM values of phonon polaritons, the authors also employed the Lyddane-
Sachs-Teller relation to assess ionicity. Why is it necessary to extract the LST ratio in this study? The authors should
elaborate on the significance of extracting LST ratio for the study of phonon polaritons. 
3. How did the authors extract the FOM values of phonon polaritons from near-field measurements? I noticed they fit
experimental line profiles using an exponential decay function. The authors should be careful when employing this method,
as fitted values can vary dramatically when extracting line profiles from different locations in the near-field images.
Furthermore, they observe interference phenomena between polariton waves launched at different boundaries within the
microstructures (e.g., bright spots in Fig.2c, Fig.4b, and Fig.4d), potentially leading to overestimated lifetime values. The
authors should extract line profiles from positions distant from the cavity. 
4. When discussing polariton canalization phenomenon, the authors should cite highly relevant literature: Nano Letters 20,
5323 (2020); Nature Materials 19, 1307 (2020); Nature Materials 22, 867 (2023); Science Advances 11, eads0569 (2025). 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors report the experimental observation of long-lived hyperbolic phonon polaritons (PhPs) in layered PbI2,
demonstrating its potential as a new 2D material for deep-THz nanophotonics. Using room-temperature scattering-type
scanning near-field optical microscopy (s-SNOM), they achieve real-space imaging and broadband spectral mapping of
PbI2 flakes on various substrates. The observed polariton propagation shows an impressive figure of merit Re[q]/Im[q] > 15
and an extraordinary mode compression factor λ
₀
/λp ≈ 265, comparable to or even exceeding those of canonical mid-
infrared materials. 
This is a well-executed and timely study that significantly expands the material landscape of phonon polaritonics toward
longer (deep-THz) wavelengths. The experimental methodology is rigorous, the data are clearly presented, and the
comparison with theoretical models is compelling. In my view, the manuscript meets the journal’s high standards in terms of
novelty, broad significance, and technical soundness. Below are a few comments that may help the authors further improve
the scholarly presentation of their work. 
1) The novelty and importance of the work should be more clearly articulated. As the authors point out, no prior studies have

reported hyperbolic phonon polaritons in the deep-THz regime. However, this fact alone does not necessarily justify the

and explored. The authors are encouraged to provide a deeper discussion of the unique polaritonic characteristics of PbI2 in
the deep-THz range, connecting these to its distinctive crystal structure and anisotropy. Furthermore, the manuscript would

The LST ratio provides a direct and quantitative measure of the ionic character of the lattice

bonds, which governs both the LO–TO phonon splitting and the strength of the phonon–polariton interaction. Therefore, assessing the LST ratio helps establishing why PbI2 oRers an ultra-wide Reststrahlen band  that can host strongly conﬁned phonon polaritons in a large THz range, demonstrating that it is a promising material platform, rather than merely a favorable cavity geometry. From an application standpoint, we foresee several opportunities for PbI₂ to act as a key material, either by enabling new physical phenomena or paving the way toward compact, chip-integrated light circuits. Another compelling advantage is its ease of synthesis via hydrothermal growth. Unlike hBN, 2D crystalline PbI₂ can be readily produced in aqueous solutions at mild temperatures (from room temperature up to 90 °C), making it highly cost-eRective and, therefore, broadly accessible.

at the center of the PbI₂ ﬂake. While the phase peaks at νEu(TO), the blue curve representing the near-ﬁeld amplitude features slight detuning. How do the authors think this could be interpreted? In s-SNOM, the near-ﬁeld phase is directly related to the absorptive component of the tip–sample polarizability and therefore peaks at the phonon resonance, where ε''(ω) is maximum. In contrast, the amplitude follows the dispersive real part of the response, leading to a derivative-like line shape centered at the same optical phonon frequency. This behavior is characteristic of polar materials supporting optical phonons and associated phonon polariton response. This behavior is well-established in the mid-IR for materials whose dielectric response can be described by a Lorentz oscillator model. Since we also adopt a Lorentz-type model for the dielectric function of PbI2, the same principles apply to this class of materials in the THz regime.
