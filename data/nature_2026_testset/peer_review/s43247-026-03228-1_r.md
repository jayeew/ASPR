REVIEWER COMMENTS: 
Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
* What are the major claims of the paper? 
The major claim of the paper is that the 2017 Changdao earthquake swarm was driven by the migration of deep-seated,
CO
₂
-rich hydrothermal fluids, which reactivated a basement fault. This conclusion is supported by a newly constructed high-
resolution earthquake catalog that allows for better constraints on fault structures and seismic activity patterns. 
* Are these claims novel, and will they be of interest to others in the community? If the conclusions are not original, it would
be helpful if you could provide relevant references. 
To my understanding, the claim that the 2017 Changdao swarm was driven by deep fluid migration is novel for this specific
region. However, the general idea of attributing swarm-like seismicity to fluid involvement is not new—many studies have
proposed similar interpretations in other contexts. That said, relatively few have convincingly demonstrated fluid-driven
mechanisms with robust evidence. 
* Is the work convincing, and if not, what further evidence would be required to strengthen the conclusions? 
While the work lays out geological evidence suggestive of fluid involvement, it is not convincing in attributing the seismicity
to fluid-driven processes. Seismic swarms are commonly observed, but their underlying mechanisms remain poorly
understood. This uncertainty has led to oversimplified interpretations, such as using systematic sqrt(t) 
migration as the
primary evidence for fluid-driven seismicity. While such patterns were sufficient to support early interpretations of these
systems, they no longer meet the current standard for establishing a causal link between fluids and seismic activity. 
Furthermore, the statistical modeling using ETAS does not account for the time dependence of the background rate, which is
key to capturing the nature of swarm-like sequences. This omission undermines the interpretation, as the model is unable to
represent aseismic forcing, which is central to the proposed mechanism. To address this, the authors need to incorporate
time-dependent background rates into their modeling. 
In addition to refining the statistical approach, the study would benefit from independent evidence that more directly supports

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 3 -->

fluid-driven fault activation. For example, identifying a crack-opening mechanism through full moment tensor inversion would
provide a stronger case. To be considered cutting-edge, the work must go beyond simplified diffusion models and provide
more robust evidence that seismicity is indeed driven by fluids. 
* On a more subjective note, do you feel that the paper will influence thinking in the field in terms of either conceptual
understanding or technological capability? Please feel free to raise any further questions and concerns about the paper. 
The paper claims to address how basement faults respond to deep fluid pressure and other external stress perturbations,
which is a complex and still poorly understood issue. But the analysis mainly shows that the seismic events appear to follow
a simple fluid migration path. From this, the authors infer changes in permeability and conclude that a basement fault was
reactivated due to the depth of the events. However, this falls short of answering the broader question they raised. The
interpretation feels more like a surface-level correlation than a deeper investigation into the physical response of the fault
system. 
In brief, this paper will not incite a major response in the field of swarms being driven by fluids. However, since the catalog
employed in this paper is new, it may be useful for studies of that geographical area.

Response to Reviewer #1 
 
The major claim of the paper is that the 2017 Changdao earthquake swarm was driven by the 
migration of deep-seated, CO₂-rich hydrothermal fluids, which reactivated a basement fault. 
This conclusion is supported by a newly constructed high-resolution earthquake catalog that 
allows for better constraints on fault structures and seismic activity patterns. 
To my understanding, the claim that the 2017 Changdao swarm was driven by deep fluid 
migration is novel for this specific region. However, the general idea of attributing swarm-like

Reply: We are grateful to you for the thoughtful and constructive comments , which have guided 
substantial improvements to the manuscript.  Indeed, while fluid -driven earthquake swarms have 
been studied extensively worldwide, our study provides particularly compelling evidence for this 
specific intraplate setting. The Changdao area lies near the tectonic junction between the Tan –Lu 
Fault Zone and the Zhangjiakou –Penglai Fault Zone , located on the eastern margin of the North 
China Craton. This region is characterized by a complex network of deep -seated basement faults 
that merge at depths of approximately 7  - 13 km and by abundant hydrocarbon resources in the 
surrounding area. Recurrent earthquake swarms (e.g., in 1976, 2013, and 2017) and the occurrence 
of a large historical earthquake (M7.0 in 1548) make it an ideal natural laboratory to explore the 
role of deep fluids in fault reactivation and seismicity. However, its offshore location and limited 
instrumentation continue to pose challenges for comprehensive geophysical observations.  
In the revised version , we expanded the Discussion from L251 to L303 and established a multi-
proxy, time -dependent model for fluid -driven processes . As mentioned in replies to Editor ’ s 
Comments (page 2), we implemented firstly an ETAS model with a time-varying background rate 
λ0(t), quantifying that ~63% of the swarm was forced rather than cascade -triggered. Secondly, we 
added moment tensor results showing robust positive isotropic components (13 - 38%) in three ML 
≥ 3.5 events, consistent with shear-tensile failure under overpressure. Thirdly, we examined the b-
values throughout the entire swarm and within different phases , remaining higher than the 
background b-value from 2007 in the Changdao Island area (Figure S9a). Finally, we integrated low 
Vp/Vs tomography and regional geochemical constraints (CO 2-rich volatiles) to support a gas -
charged, overpressured environment. Additionally, we also clarified the physical interpretation of 
fault reactivation and permeability evolution within a fault -valve framework, linking diffusivity 
changes, b-value patterns, and non-DC components to physical processes of fracture opening, fluid 
migration, and sealing . We hope that these revisions  collectively provide more independent, 
convergent evidence for fluid -driven activation that substantially strengthens the manuscript’s 
conclusions.

To further investigate the instantaneous triggering effects of teleseismic surface waves on the 
Changdao region, we selected 12 Mw ≥ 6.5 global earthquakes recorded by local stations near 
Changdao Island. Following previous studies (Peng et al., 2010; Hill and Prejean, 2015; Liu et al., 
2017), we applied a 5 Hz high -pass filter to the original waveforms of these teleseismic records. 
This filtering process suppresses the low -frequency teleseismic surface waves while amplifying 
signals from local small earthquakes. Consequently, any potential t riggering phenomena would 
reveal previously obscured micro-earthquakes hidden beneath the large -amplitude surface waves. 
Additionally, spectrograms corresponding to the teleseismic waveform intervals were analyzed. 
These spectrograms clearly display the energy distribution across different frequencies upon the 
arrival of seismic waves: teleseismic energy is predominantly c oncentrated in the low -frequency
