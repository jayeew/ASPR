Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this work, Xi and co-workers reported a MOF-on-MOF S-scheme heterojunction for efficient photocatalytic urea
production. By integrating NU-1000 and Co-HHTP, dual Co and Zr active sites were constructed, which synergetically
promoted C–N coupling. However, this work lacks novelty because the MOF-on-MOF S-scheme heterojunction has already
been reported (Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 2025, 64, e202502009.). Meanwhile, the data presented in this article do not
substantiate the author's standpoint. Thus, the research work is still insufficient to meet the requirements of Nature
Communications. The reasons are as follows: 
Major issues: 
1. The analysis of the S-scheme heterojunction structure was paradoxical. Though the authors employed various
measurements to elucidate the charge transfer mechanism within the NU-1000@Co-HHTP heterojunction, the results do not
match each other, leading to potentially erroneous conclusions: 
(1) As semiconductors, the Fermi level is typically located between the conduction and valence bands. Thus, the energy of
the valence band, relative to the Fermi level, should be smaller than the band gap. However, the UPS spectrum (valence
band zone on the right side of Fig. S5) suggested that the energy of the valence band for both Co-HHTP and NU-1000 was
significantly larger than their band gaps. This discrepancy in the UPS data led to a misinterpretation of the work functions,
thus, the conclusion of the S-scheme heterojunction was not convincing. 
(2) The band structure obtained by Mott-Schottky tests was contradictory to the UPS findings. 
2. Though the authors performed systematic investigations to study the formation of interfacial Co-Zr dual sites, the
presented evidence remains inadequate. XPS technique does not have the fine structure resolution characteristics. Thus,
the peak at 530.69 eV in the O 1s XPS spectrum of NU-1000@Co-HHTP, positioned between the Co–O peak of Co-HHTP
and Zr–O peak of NU-1000, was more likely indicative of a signal overlap rather than the formation of Zr–O–Co chemical
bonds. 
3. The identification of in situ DRIFTS spectra was not rigorous. 
(1) During in situ DRIFTS tests, both H2O and CO2 existed. The adsorbed CO2 and H2O were commonly reported at 2300–
2400 cm

(2) The peaks observed at 1425 cm

1000 (Fig. 3i and 3j) were assigned to C–N and *CO intermediate, respectively. 
(3) No distinct signal corresponding to the *CO intermediate was detected in the in situ DRIFTS spectra of either NU-
1000@Co-HHTP or Co-HHTP. How does the CO product form during photocatalytic reactions? 
4. The distinction of reaction pathways is ambiguous. Reference 41, cited by the authors, identified the peak at 2075 cm

does this work propose *CO2NO as the key intermediate during the C–N coupling process, instead of following the *OCNO
pathway? 
5. The authors should compare the consistency of urea production detected by the different assays. 
6. The authors claimed that NU-1000@Co-HHTP exhibited superior carrier kinetics compared to other samples. However,
based on the PEC and OCVD results (Fig. 4f, g), the integration of NU-1000 and Co-HHTP actually decreased charge
transfer and separation efficiency of Co-HHTP. This result contradicted the findings from the PL and TRPL tests (Fig. 4h, i),
which suggested enhanced carrier dynamics. 
Minor issues: 
1. The noise-signal ratio in the 1H NMR spectra decreased as the concentration of the urea standard solution increased. To
avoid any misunderstandings, the internal standard should also be included in the 1H NMR spectra. 
2. The standard curves for NO2− and NH3 should also be provided. 
3. HHTP contains abundant chemical bonds between C and O atoms. I wonder why there was no signal corresponding to
O–C=O or O–C observed in O 1s XPS spectra of Co-HHTP. 
4. The relative Co2+ peak intensity to satellite (Figure S10b) significantly changed after cycling tests. Is there any other
oxidation state of Co formed? 
5. “Fig. 4k, l” in page 12 should be corrected to “Fig. 3k, l”. Besides, there are many spelling and grammar errors in this
manuscript (e.g., “donated as NU-1000/Co-HHTP”). The author should check the manuscript carefully.

Comment 3. The authors should provide more detail process about the conductive band potential calculation from Mott-

Comment 4. To conclusively trace the carbon source in urea synthesis, isotopic labeling experiments (e.g., using ¹³C-labeled

₂

the main text. The flat-band potentials of NU-1000 and Co-HHTP should be -0.60 and -0.7 eV, respectively. 
Comment 6. The manuscript only describes the co-reduction of CO2 and NO3- to synthesize urea, but does not identify the
oxidation process. 
Comment 7. The proposed "*CO2+*NO" coupling pathway is interesting. However, given that "*CO (from CRR) + *NO"

Reviewer #5 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The work by Chao Liu reports a MOF S-scheme heterojunction catalyst with dual Zr and Co sites that promotes efficient C-N
coupling for the photochemical synthesis of urea from NO3 and CO2. 
The authors perform synthesis and characterisation of
the material using TEM, elemental mapping and XRD. Optical properties of the materials are analysed with XPS and UV-vis
methods. The area of the catalyst was measured with N2 adsorption/desorption or BET measurements, and the adsorption
properties towards NO3 and CO2 were also evaluated. Phochemical and photoelectrochemical measurements were run to
assess performance. FTIR and NMR were used to address the reaction mechanism and product formation. This paper
shows a photocatalytic material with interesting properties toward urea production via the photosynthetic process, which I
think is valuable knowledge for the scope and the readership of this journal. However, I think different parts of the paper
could be discussed more. Below, I add my comments: 
1. I understand that the presence of dual sites or vicinal Zr and Co is key to catalysing the carbon-nitrogen coupling step that
is limiting for the production of urea. The need for dual sites for C-N coupling is also observed in electrocatalysis. However,
the other photocatalysts reported in this work, with single sites of Co or Zr, or a mixture of them (1000-Nu/CoHHTP), also
produce a significant amount of urea, despite not having dual sites. What explains the production of urea in the other
catalysts? 
2. Could urea be produced if there were either Zr dual sites or Co dual sites rather than Co-Zr? 
3. Figure 4 shows the photoelectrochemical current of the different materials toward different environments containing
KNO3, CO2, or both. How is the current in the blank electrolyte, I mean, without reactant (NO3 or CO2), only in Na2SO4? 
4. The authors determine the area of the catalysts with N2 adsorption, but is the electroactive area the same? Is it possible to
provide capacitance measurements of the catalysts to get information on the amount of Co and Zr sites available for
catalysis? 
5. What is the loading or amount of Co and Zr in each catalyst? 
6. The authors explain the presence of dual or vicinal Co and Zr sites that enhance the production of urea, but which
characterisation method confirms or supports that they form dual sites in the prepared material? 
7. The authors evaluate the catalyst structure after photocatalysis, but how long does it take to carry out each cycle, or for
how long is the urea synthesis carried out in hours? 
8. I suggest incorporating a table that compares the state of the art of photo and electrocatalysts for the production of urea.
How is the performance of this catalyst compared to other ones reported in the literature?

Comment 1. NU-1000 forms a S -scheme heterojunction with Co -HHTP. What is the

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 30 -->

difference between S-scheme heterojunction and Z-scheme heterojunction, and how to 
determine whether the material forms an S -scheme heterojunction instead of a Z -
scheme heterojunction? 
 
Response: We thank Reviewer 4 for the thoughtful comments.  Z-scheme 
heterojunction includes traditional Z -scheme, all-solid-state Z-scheme, and direct Z -
scheme heterojunction. Specifically, the traditional Z -scheme heterojunction is 
composed of two semiconductors with staggered band structure configurations and 
suitable intermediate couples (e.g., Fe 3+/Fe2+, IO 3-/I-, and I 3-/I-) in solution. The all -
solid-state Z-scheme heterojunction is consisted of two different semiconductors with 
a solid electron mediator (e.g. Au, Ag, Pt) at the interface between them. Direct  Z-
scheme heterojunction photocatalyst is directly formed by combining two different 
semiconductors in the absence of redox couples and electron mediators. The S-scheme 
heterojunction can be directly distinguished from the traditional Z-scheme and indirect 
Z-scheme heterojunction based on the different constitutions. The direct Z -scheme 
heterojunction follows the same charge transfer mechanism with S -scheme 
heterojunction. The concept of "S -scheme heterojunction" was proposed based on 
direct Z-scheme heterojunction by Prof. Jiaguo Yu, aiming to avoid the consequences 
of the failure of traditional Z -scheme photocatalysts and all -solid-state Z -scheme 
photocatalysts, and describe the charge transfer path more clearly (e.g. Chem 2020, 6, 
1543; Adv. Mater. 2022, 34, 2107668). To validate the S -scheme or direct Z -scheme 
mechanism, the techniques such as in situ irradiated XPS and Kelvin Probe Force 
Microscopy that are used in our study are recognized as the most efficient methods.
