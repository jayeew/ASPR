Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this work, the authors successfully prepared a nitrogen-doped carbon catalyst co-existing with cobalt single atoms and
nanoparticles (Co1+Con/N-C), for the transfer hydrodeoxygenation of vanillin using formic acid as a hydrogen source,
achieving >99% conversion and selectivity. They proposed a water-mediated dual hydrogen transfer mechanism that
significantly reduced the reaction activation energy, demonstrating excellent catalytic activity and stability. It is quite
interesting and innovative. I recommend that this manuscript be published after solving the following issues. 
1.In the synthetic route in Figure 1, the representation of nanoparticles in Con/N-C and Co1+Con/N-C does not have
subscripts. 
2.The texts in Figures 1b-d are very difficult to read. Please improve. 
3.The transport kinetics of reaction intermediates involved in the interface are related to the structure of the catalyst. The
interaction has been discussed in terms of its impact on the electronic structure and structural stability of the catalyst
(eScience 5, 100461 (2025)). By combining the progresses, the authors are encouraged to add the significance of interface
interaction at Co1+Con/N-C systems. 
4.While the hot filtration experiment can preliminarily rule out homogeneous catalysis, it is recommended to supplement it
with ICP analysis to detect cobalt leaching, thereby further verifying catalyst stability. 
5.How cobalt single atoms and cobalt nanoparticles synergistically promote the catalytic reaction should be further
explained by the authors. The mechanism requires further clarification and evidentiary support. 
6.The author should add a performance comparison, including more recent references. 
7.The stability test was conducted for only 5 cycles; it is suggested to extend the cycling tests or include post-reaction
characterization (e.g., TEM, XPS) to strengthen the persuasiveness of the conclusions. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The paper entitled "Synergistic cobalt catalysts for efficient hydrodeoxygenation of vanillin with formic acid: A study on
single atoms and nanoparticles" reported the synthesis of a nitrogen-doped carbon-supported cobalt catalyst, comprising
both single atom and nanoparticle forms (Co1+Con/N-C). The resultant catalyst exhibits remarkable efficiency for the
efficient catalytic transfer hydrodeoxygenation of vanillin to 2-methoxy-4-methylphenol utilizing formic acid. Additionally, the
possible reaction pathway for CTH of vanillin to MMP over Co-based catalysts was proposed. Therefore, the work is
recommended to be published. However, authors still need to address some concerns. The following suggestions are
provided for authors' revising manuscript. 
1.Catalyst stability is demonstrated via five recycling cycles, but post-cycling structural/chemical characterizations of
Co1+Con/N−C is absent. Characterizing the recycled catalyst would validate claims of "exceptional stability" 
2.The reaction pathway proposes vanillin → HMP → MMP, but there is no experimental evidence (e.g., HPLC/GC-MS
detection). Tracking intermediate concentrations over time would validate the proposed reaction sequence and support the
kinetic analysis of rate-limiting steps. 
3.There are some spelling errors needs to be corrected such as “Hotfiltrion”. 
4.The conversion of vanillin on Co1+Con/N-C catalyst (100%) is significantly higher than that of the single component
catalysts. Revealed the synergistic effect.

(Remarks to the Author) 
In this study, Dai and his co-workers synthesized a nitrogen-doped carbon-supported cobalt catalyst containing both single-
atom sites and nanoparticles (Co
₁
+Co

/N-C) via a sacrificial MgO-templated method. The Co1+Con/N-C facilitates the

utilizing formic acid as the hydrogen source, achieving a conversion of 100% with 99% product selectivity. Moreover, the
authors elucidated the underlying reaction mechanism through a combination of kinetic studies and isotopic labeling
experiments. This work presents interesting findings, but it requires substantial revisions and enhancements before it can be
considered suitable for publication. 
1. In this study, the authors did not provide a clear mechanistic explanation for the superior catalytic transfer hydrogenation
(CTH) performance of Co
₁
+Co
ₙ
/N–C compared to individual Co single-atom or nanoparticle catalysts, nor did they elucidate
the potential synergistic interactions between the two cobalt species in the reaction system. . 
2. The authors note that both water and formic acid serve as hydrogen sources in the reaction, but the proposed reaction
pathway schematic fails to explicitly illustrate the specific role of water. Should water indeed exert a significant influence on
the reaction, its amount and involvement warrant further systematic optimization. 
3. The TEM characterization of the spent catalyst should be supplemented to assess potential morphological changes after
reaction. Additionally, inductively coupled plasma (ICP) analysis is recommended to determine cobalt leaching by
comparing the Co content in the catalyst before and after the reaction. 
4. The structural characterization of the synthesized compounds requires enhancement. Specifically, NMR or GC-MS
spectra should be provided to verify product identity and purity. For isotope-labeling experiments, corresponding mass
spectrometry data must be included to substantiate the proposed reaction mechanism. 
5. The manuscript contains several issues that require careful revision to ensure accuracy and consistency: (1) The
description of the catalyst synthesis procedure in the Supplementary Information does not align with that provided in the
main text; (2) In line 133 of the main text, the term "catalytic" is omitted and should be inserted to maintain terminological
accuracy; (3) The catalyst labeling in Figure 5 is incorrect and needs to be corrected.

cobalt catalyst containing both single-atom sites and nanoparticles (Co₁+Coₙ/N-C) via a sacrificial

1. In this study, the authors did not provide a clear mechanistic explanation for the superior catalytic 
transfer hydrogenation (CTH) performance of Co 1+Con/N–C compared to individual Co single -

the two-cobalt species in the reaction system. 
Author reply: Thanks for this comment . We carefully responded the questions. In the isotope 
experiments, we investigated the CTH reaction using four distinct combinations  (Figure 6). The 
results indicate that the activation of water and the decomposition of formic acid are likely the rate-
determining steps in this CTH process. According to the literature (Nat Comm, 2024, 15, 8189), the 
use of single atom and nanoparticle catalyst, specifically Co-SAs/NPs@NC, significantly enhances 
both the adsorption and activation of the crucial monodentate HCOO* intermediate. Additionally, 
cobalt nanoparticles shift the d -band center of the Co single atoms, thereby facilitating the 
subsequent cleavage of the C –H bond. We also conducted KSCN poisoning experiments (Figure 
7a), which demonstrated a substantial reduction in the catalytic activity of single -atom catalysts, 
while the activity of nanoparticles remained largely unaffected. Furthermore, we use a single atom 
(Co1/N-C) supported cobalt nanoparticle catalyst to catalyze the CTH reaction (Table S3, entry 10), 
resulting in enhanced activity (from 52.4% to 92.6%,) compared to a mere physical mixture (68.3%, 
entry 4). This finding indicates that Co single-atom sites (Co-Nₓ structures) can effectively modulate 
the electronic properties of cobalt nanoparticles. The corrections were marked in page 7, highlighted 
by yellow color in the revised Supplementary Information.

the proposed reaction pathway schematic fails to explicitly illustrate the specific role of water.
