Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, the authors report a split system utilizing non-covalent recruitment of deaminases to Cas9 nickase via
coiled-coil heterodimers. They demonstrate that this system can improve or at least maintain the editing efficiency of ABEs
and CBEs with different PAM preferences in vitro. Additionally, they assess its in vivo application using dual AAV vectors to
edit the Pcsk9 gene in mice, achieving 33.84% on-target editing. While developing a flexible and universal split base editing
system is valuable, several limitations reduce the manuscript's impact. 
1. In Vitro Editing Efficiency 
Figures 1–6 present data from plasmid transfections comparing the control editor and different split versions. In some cases,
CC-BE significantly increased on-target editing efficiency. The authors speculate that coiled-coil heterodimers enhance
reconstitution, bringing UGI and APOBEC3 into closer proximity compared to the intact CBE, which may contribute to
improved performance. However, the study does not assess whether differences in editing efficiency arise from variations in
protein expression. Did the authors verify editor expression under different conditions? A Western blot analysis would be
informative in determining whether the observed differences are due to expression levels rather than an intrinsic advantage
of the split system. 
2. In Vivo Study Design 
The in vivo experiments involve delivering dual AAV8 vectors (sABE-P3P4, sABE-N5N6, sABE-ctrl or ABE) via
intraperitoneal (IP) injection at 1E12 vg total to 1-week-old mice, with liver harvest at 2 weeks post-injection. This design has
several concerns: 
o Efficiency and Timing: IP injection generally leads to lower transduction efficiency and a longer time to peak AAV
expression compared to intravenous (IV) injection. 
o High AAV Dose: A 1E12 vg dose in 1-week-old pups is extremely high and lacks translational relevance, particularly for
AAV8, which primarily targets the liver. 
o Duration of Expression: Harvesting the liver at 2 weeks post-injection may not be sufficient for full evaluation, especially
given the slower kinetics of IP delivery. 
3. Prolonged AAV-Mediated Expression and Off-Target Risks 
A major concern with AAV-mediated delivery is the prolonged expression of the editor, which can lead to cumulative on-
target editing but also increases off-target risks, a critical issue for in vivo gene editing. In contrast, Lipid Nanoparticles
(LNPs) provide transient expression, minimizing long-term off-target effects. While LNPs currently achieve high efficiency
primarily in the liver, AAVs have the advantage of transducing a broader range of tissues. Therefore, to demonstrate the
translational impact of the CC split system, the authors should show its ability to correct a pathogenic variant in a non-liver
tissue, where LNPs would not be an effective alternative. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Mu et al present a novel approach for splitting base editors, which already made a powerful impact in the treatment of human
disease, as several variants are already enrolled in clinical trials. Using coiled-coils (CC) as a non-covalent tethering option,
the authors show the applicability of using CCs to improve base-editing, demonstrated at several genomic loci and in
several cell lines. The functionality is additionally confirmed in vivo by editing the Pcsk9 gene. Although the authors provide
a substantial amount of editing data, confirmed by NGS, the paper lacks several important experiments.

Major concerns: 
1. The comparison of different designs may be slightly misleading. The constructs for CC-BE (for CBEs and ABEs) have
altogether 4 NLS, whereas the fused ABE or fused CBE (that CC-BE are benchmarked to) only have 2 NLS. The authors did
not even mention that in the text. In order to fully evaluate the potential improvement of the genome editing the authors
should conduct experiments with constructs (for both CBE and ABE) that have an equal number of NLS at several genomic
loci, since the slight improvement in genome editing might be due to the different nuclear translocation of CC-BEs. 
2. The authors also failed to mention in the text that the % of editing is presented on the sorted population of cells. Only when
looking at the Method section, the reader may comprehend that sorting was carried out. It would be important to know what is
the actual base editing efficiency in the bulk cell population. This goes for all experiments the whole paper (also for MEF,
PEF cells etc., and results presented in Fig.7C,E). The authors should provide the representative results of BE on bulk cell
population and should correct the figure legends and text. The gating strategy must be included in the paper. 
3. It was surprising that sCBE and sABE led to such a high editing efficiency. This could in principle pose a substantial
threat, due to the off-target effects so the authors should conduct a genome-wide determination of the deamination status as

“Off‐target effects of CC‐Base editor

the on-target base editing efficiency by amplicon deep sequencing (Supplementary Fig. 
11). Then, genome -wide single -nucleotide variants (SNVs) and small insertions or 
deletions (indels) were analyzed based on WGS data in CC-BE3 (sCBE-P3P4, sCBE-N5N6),
