Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript designs a Janus hydrogel through bionic structure, and significantly enhances the biomechanical strength
of the Achilles tendon, prevents postoperative adhesion, and restores basic motor function within 8 weeks, through the
functional coupling of antibacterial-immune synergy and intelligent regulation of exosome spatiotemporal delivery. This
study achieves "mechanical-immune spatiotemporal regulation" of infectious tendons, providing a beneficial strategy for
complex tissue regeneration, with relatively obvious innovative significance and reference value. However, the manuscript
still has the following issues that need to be explained, improved, or resolved: 
1. Some term abbreviations are too casual, which is not conducive to readers' understanding. 
2. The mechanical performance data of normal tendons (or Achilles tendons) are not provided, making it impossible to
visually compare with the relevant data of the hydrogel. 
3. The manuscript mentions that the degradation cycle and mechanical properties of the HAPP hydrogel well adapt to the
needs of the postoperative tendon repair and regeneration microenvironment, but the specific needs are unclear. 
4. The number of significant digits in some data is not standardized. 
5. Figure S11 is not referenced or explained in the main text. 
6. "LPS" is not clearly defined in the manuscript. 
7. The measurement time unit (ms) of the contact angle in Figures 3C and D seems to be incorrect. 
8. The Western blot analysis results in Figure 5C show that the exosome-specific markers (CD9, CD63, and TSG101) of
Nor-Exos and Hypo-Exos do not have significant differences, does this mean that their experimental effects are solely
dependent on the concentration of exosomes? That is, if the concentration of Nor-Exos is sufficient, will the effect be the
same as Hypo-Exos? 
9. The authors believe that lotus leaves induced polarity migration on the hydrogel surface, but lack supporting data from
surface chemical analysis. 
10. The authors believe through RNA-seq data that HAPP@H-EXO may drive macrophages to polarize towards the anti-
inflammatory M2 phenotype and secrete anti-inflammatory factors by inhibiting signaling pathways such as NF-κB, but lack
experimental evidence such as Western blot or IHC staining. 
11. The mechanism of hypoxic exosomes (Hypo-TDSC-Exos) relies only on phenotypic data, lacking supporting data for the
identification of key active molecules. 
12. The size and mechanical properties of rat Achilles tendons differ greatly from those of humans, and if a large animal
model could verify the feasibility of implantation and functional recovery, the effect would be better. 
13. Although using a lotus leaf mold is an innovation, compared to other methods of forming a hydrophobic interface, it has
relatively poorer operability and repeatability. 
14. The manuscript only indirectly infers stress dispersion through YAP immunofluorescence, lacking direct verification of
stress distribution at the hydrogel-tendon interface through finite element analysis or in situ mechanical testing, making the
depth of mechanism discussion in the manuscript insufficient.11. The mechanism of hypoxic exosomes (Hypo-TDSC-Exos)
relies solely on phenotypic data, lacking identification data of key active molecules. 
12. The size/mechanical environment of rat Achilles tendons differs greatly from humans. A large animal model to verify
implant feasibility and functional recovery would be more effective. 
13. Using a lotus leaf mold, although an innovative point, has relatively poorer operability and repeatability compared to
other methods for forming hydrophobic interfaces.

Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors aim to develop a Janus Hydrogel for optimal healing of infected tendon injury. The methodology of construction
and characterization of the hydrogels is detailed, with detection of fatigue resistance, self-healing, and antimicrobial
properties. Further, hypo- and norm-Exos from TDSCs were loaded with the hydrogel for better inflammation inhibition. The
study has a large workload. thus, the current research is of fine innovation. 
The graphs and images of the current manuscript are of fine quality. However, for publication in Nature Communications,
here are the deficiencies I would like to propose. 
1) Even though the study has a large workload, the current manuscript is lazily written with many typos and misuse of
spaces before and after punctuation marks. These obvious typos should be thoroughly revised before the next submission. 
2) In the Exosome uptake assay, 500 μL of TDSC-Exos suspension was applied, but no exact quantification of Exos has
been performed. Thus, the conclusion drawn from Fig. 5D-E is insufficient. 
3) In in vitro studies, cells were treated with extracts from HAPP3, HAPP@EXO, and HAPP@H-EXO. For precision, the
authors should add groups treated with merely EXO and H-EXO for comparison. 
4) Even though over-reactive inflammation impairs healing of tendons, excessive fibrosis also impairs tendon healing and
future limb function. Thus, after treatment, quantification of biomarkers of fibrosis should also be determined. 
5) The legend of Figure 9 includes group “Normal”, but there is no such group in the figure. 
Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This study introduces a biomimetic Arthur's hydrogel (HAPP@H-EXO), which can promote the regeneration of infected
Achilles tendons through mechanical immune time-controlled regulation. It demonstrates the potential of innovative design
and translational application. The authors effectively integrated the asymmetric adhesion topological structure, the dynamic
mechanism of bacterial capture and killing, as well as the stem cell exosomes (Hypo-TDSC-Exos) derived from hypoxic
Achilles tendons, thereby proposing a multifunctional method for Achilles tendon repair. However, some experimental
details and clinical relevance aspects still need to be further clarified. If the following suggestions can be addressed, I
suggest a major revision and acceptance. Specifically: 
1. As shown in Figure S9, real-time imaging data indicate a progressive decline in fluorescence from day 28 to day 35. The
specific fluorescent labeling dye used in this study requires clarification. Please elaborate on the methodology employed for
labeling the hydrogel with the fluorescent dye—was direct blending used? Additionally, kindly specify the exact
concentration of the fluorescent dye applied. Quantitative data on fluorescence degradation should also be provided.
Furthermore, given that in vivo animal experiments were conducted up to the sixth week, while fluorescence labeling was
completely degraded by day 35, it is important to clarify how the hydrogel continued to exert its therapeutic effects between
the fifth and sixth weeks. 
2. Could the authors specify the exact concentrations of the HAPP gel solution extract and the lotus leaf extract?
Additionally, please provide the mixing ratio between the HAPP gel solution and lotus leaf extract. Are there more detailed
data available to support these findings? 
3. Please specify the concentration and duration of LPS treatment used for macrophage polarization in the mRNA-seq
experiments. 
4. Several concerns have been raised regarding the cell adhesion experiment. Specifically, the duration of hydrogel
treatment with lotus leaf extract requires clarification. There also appears to be a discrepancy in the experimental
description: Figure 3E compares the lotus leaf extract treatment group with the pure hydrogel group; however, the control
group in the methodology is described as lacking hydrogel. We kindly request that the authors review and correct this
inconsistency. 
5. Concerning the modeling process, I remain somewhat unclear regarding the specific details of the lotus leaf treatment. It
would be greatly appreciated if the authors could provide a detailed visual demonstration or video documentation of the
procedure to enhance understanding. 
6. It is recommended to include commercially available control materials, such as fibrin glue or Interceed, in the experimental
design in order to rigorously evaluate and demonstrate the superior efficacy of the proposed treatment. 
7. To enhance the readability of the manuscript, it is recommended that the authors introduce and define the abbreviation of
the hydrogel within the main text upon first mention. 
8. The statement in the original discussion: "The increased swelling rate of the hydrogels may aid the removal of tissue
exudate from the injured tendon" appears inconsistent with the preceding content and the results presented in Figures 1E–

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 3 -->

1F. The authors are requested to revise the relevant discussion accordingly to ensure alignment with the experimental data. 
9. A comprehensive linguistic review of the entire manuscript is advised to correct spelling and grammatical errors—for
instance, “sweeling” on line 204 should be corrected to “swelling.” 
10. The authors successfully demonstrated that the superhydrophobicity of the lotus leaf surface induced asymmetric
adhesion of the hydrogel. I am curious about the tensile test results if the hydrogel were constructed using only hydrophobic
PE mold.

Answer: Thank you for your valuable comments on our manuscript. Your question 
regarding the compatibility between the HAPP hydrogel and the microenvironment for 
Achilles tendon repair and regeneration is highly insightful. During the postoperative 
Achilles tendon repair process, the requirements of the microenvironment are mainly 
reflected in two key dimensions:  healing time and mechanical properties. Firstly, the 
typical healing process of an infected Achilles tendon consists of three phases: the 
inflammatory ph ase (0–1 week), the proliferative phase (1–6 weeks), and the 
remodeling phase (6 weeks to several months) 1. The HAPP hydrogel achieves 
spatiotemporal regulation through a precisely controlled degradation cycle (over 24 
days). It not only maintains stable mechanica l support during the inflammatory and 
proliferative phases but also releases exosomes in a gradient manner via degradation, 
synergistically exerting multiple biological effects such as anti -inflammation, 
promotion of angiogenesis, and cell migration.  Secondly, in terms of mechanical

matching and mechanical compatibility, ensures that the HAPP hydrogel can provide 
optimal microenvironment support throughout the entire healing process.  We have
