Reviewers' comments:
 
Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
This manuscript presents a comprehensive investigation into the lattice dynamics and transport properties of the perovskite
solid electrolyte, addressing the role of strongly anharmonic soft phonons and local structural distortions on Lithium-ion
diffusion. The study offers an interesting and somewhat counter-intuitive contribution: contrary to the common heuristic that
soft phonons facilitate ion transport, the authors demonstrate that in LLTO, soft modes associated with rotational instabilities
actually suppress diffusion by creating static bottlenecks at lower temperatures. The work also provides insights into thermal
transport and its connection with phonon anharmonicity. I recommend for publication. 
Only have few comments: 
1. The author notes that in materials like argyrodites, polyhedral dynamics enhance diffusion. In LLTO, they hinder it. Could
the authors add a brief discussion distinguishing the geometric/steric reasons for this difference? Is it strictly a matter of
bottleneck cross-section area, or does the timescale of the TiO6 rotation relative to the Li-hopping attempt frequency play a
role? 
2. In Figure 6, the experimental spectra are quite broad. Is this broadening entirely due to anharmonicity and disorder, or is
there a contribution from the polycrystalline nature of the sample (grain boundaries/defects), or majorly from instrument
resolution? 
3. The author attributes the glass-like thermal conductivity to disorder and anharmonicity. It may be helpful to discuss how
typical glass-like thermal conductivity looks like, for example, the weak T-dependence. This can be beneficial for a general
reader. 
Minor: 
1. Sample preparation in the Supplementary should go before Fig.S1 
2. In the manuscript neutron structure factor S(Q,E), Q should be written as a vector. 
Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author): 
This work reports an interesting pheonomenon that restricting the O dynamics could enhance Li diffusion. Although most
parts of the manuscript is comprehensive and seems reasonable, a few technical questions needs to be explained. Thus, a
major revision is needed because of the following concerns. 
1. As the author mentioned, the finding of this work is different from many other reports. Could the authors explain in more
details the reason behind it? 
2. The methodology used in constrained MD simulations is questionable in my opinion. What is the condition to do the
simulations? NVT? I am not sure if constraining all O or part of O, or using average O positions from MD simulations could
safely lead to the conclusion (O dynamics suppress Li diffusions by reducing the bottleneck size). The comparison (if there
is) between similar results of LLTO and other normal systems will help. 
3. If the previous two questions are solved, what kind of other systems could display similar phenomenon? It will be more
convincing if the authors could provide a few more demo cases in addition to LLTO. 
4. It is interesting to see the anharmonic thermal conductivity behavior of LLTO. But the connection between the last section
and the previous parts on Li conductivity could be strengthened.

REVIEWERS' COMMENTS: 
Reviewer-1  
This manuscript presents a comprehensive investigation into the lattice dynamics and transport 
properties of the perovskite solid electrolyte, addressing the r ole of strongly anharmonic soft 
phonons and local structural distortions on Lithium -ion diffusion. The study offers an 
interesting and somewhat counter-intuitive contribution: contrary to the common heuristic that 
soft phonons facilitate ion transport, the  authors demonstrate that in LLTO, soft modes 
associated with rotational instabilities actually suppress diffusion by creating static bottlenecks 
at lower temperatures. The work also provides insights into thermal transport and its connection 
with phonon anharmonicity. I recommend for publication. 
We sincerely thank the referee for appreciation of our work and providing very useful 
suggestions and recommendations. We thank the referee for recommending our work for 
publication.  
Only have few comments: 
1. The author notes that in materials like argyrodites, polyhedral dynamics enhance diffusion. 
In LLTO, they hinder it. Could the authors add a brief discussion distinguishing the 
geometric/steric reasons for this difference? Is it strictly a matter of bot tleneck cross-section 
area, or does the timescale of the TiO 6 rotation relative to the Li -hopping attempt frequency 
play a role? 
We thank the referee for the suggestion. We have included the following in the revised 
manuscript.  
“Further, the difference in behaviour of phonon softening led Li hopping between many 
other solid electrolytes, such as argyrodites with perovskite structure LLTO, can be understood 
in terms of framework topology and underlying dynamics. Argyrodites consist of disjoint 
tetrahedral networks with additional “free” chalcogenides and numerous vacant cation sites 
[1-3]. Mobile cations occupy polyhedral cages formed by these units, and the cage faces define 
the migration bottlenecks. Low -energy modes mainly involve librations of tetrahedra and 
motions of free chalcogenides, which dynamically widen the bottlenecks. Softening of these 
modes, with increase in temperature, increas es their amplitude and enhances diffusion. In 
LLTO, Li bottlenecks are formed by oxygen atoms shared between corner -linked TiO 6 
octahedra. Low-energy phonons near the M point involve octahedral tilting. At low to moderate 
temperatures, these M -point instab ilities induce static rotational distortions in the lattice, 
reducing the bottleneck dimension, which conscripts the Li diffusion. However, at elevated 
temperatures, these M-point modes are anharmonically stabilized, reducing static distortions 
and dynamic ally widening the Li -bottleneck, which enhances Li conductivity compared to 
lower temperatures. A key difference between many other electrolytes and the perovskite LLTO 
is that in the former the polyhedron libration mode softens with increasing temperature  and 
supports the Li -diffusion, while in LLTO the polyhedron tilt -instability occurs at low 
temperature and hinders the Li -diffusion, and high temperature the tilt mode stabilizes and 
supports the Li-diffusion.”

2. In Figure 6, the experimental spectra are quite broad. Is this broadening entirely due to 
anharmonicity and disorder, or is there a contribution from the polycrystalline nature of the

We thank the referee for the query. The inelastic neutron scattering measurements were carried 
out at MARI Spectrometer which has energy resolution of 4% of incident energy. In Figure 6 
(a-b), we have shown the INS measurements with E i=20 meV (0.8 meV resolution), which 
show significant broadening well beyond the resolution confirms that the broadening is largely

may be helpful to discuss how typical glass-like thermal conductivity looks like, for example, 
the weak T-dependence. This can be beneficial for a general reader. 
We thank the referee for the suggestion. We have incorporated these suggestions in our 
manuscript. 
“In crystalline solid electrolytes the lattice thermal conductivity is largely governed by 
phonons, and shows inverse relation (~1/T) with temperature within three phonon scattering 
framework, whereas in glassy systems the temperature dependency becomes very weak [5]. We 
should mention that not only the temperature dependency, but also the magnitude of lattice 
thermal conductivity and associated transport mechanism are very different in crystalline and 
glassy systems [5-10]. In general, a solid electrolyte shows glassy -type lattice thermal 
conductivity due to various reasons such as strongly anharmonic phonons, vacancies, 
sublattice disorder, and phonon breakdown etc [2, 11-14]”.

We have also compared the effect of freezing the host -dynamics in other solid-electrolytes in 
superionic regime. We noticed that freezing the host -dynamics suppresses the cationic 
diffusion in Cu7PSe6 and Na3PS4 and sister compounds, while it shows contrasting behaviour 
in case of LLTO  (Figure S6 [4]). It may be noted that all these system exhibits presence of 
soft/strongly anharmonic modes. In case of Cu 7PSe6 and Na3PSe4 the low-E modes involved 
the coupled Cu/Na and host dynamics, which synergistically modulate the bottleneck 
dimensions and allow easy migration of cations. While in LLTO, the modes near M -point 
largely involved involve octahedral tilting that modulates bottleneck size. However, due to the 
interconnected framework, distortions that widen one bottleneck simultaneously narrow 
another. Since diffusion is limited by the smallest bottleneck, Li mobility remains slower.
