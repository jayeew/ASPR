Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, the authors prepared a series of aryl-substituted 1,3-dienes and carried out electrochemical and
photocatalytic oxidations to generate dicationic species. The resulting ethylene dications are highly stabilized by two or four
aryl groups, with primary stabilization from two thioxanthene ring systems. When done in the presence of anilines and
amines, the dication reacts to form a novel spirocycle from 4+1 cycloaddition (electrochemical oxidation). Photocatalytic
oxidation provides a spirocyclic ether from 4+1 cycloaddition with water. The redox characteristics have been characterized
by CV and the structures of the dications studied by DFT calculations. Interestingly, the optimized dication structure prefers a
cis-conformation (favored by 3.4 kcal/mol) over the trans. The cyclization products have been characterized by X-ray
crystallography. Yields of the aniline/amine spirocycles are ca. 50-97% while the water spirocycles are formed in 68-93%
yields. 
Mechanisms are proposed for the oxidation sequences and the cyclizations. 
In general, this is an interesting study of dicationic electrophiles. The authors are correct in noting that dicationic
electrophiles are usually generated under harsh conditions which precludes their use in reactions with strong nucleophiles.
This methodology allows such chemistry to be performed. The products formed are also rather unusual aryl spirocycles –
ones that should be further studied for in optoelectronic applications. 
The methodology would be more interesting (and wider in scope) if the authors could extend it to a 4+2 cycloaddition. In this
regard, the study should include a reaction with hydrazine or its derivatives. 
The authors should also include studies of chemical oxidants, especially those used previously in the generation of ethylene
dication systems (see Chapter 5, Superelectrophiles and Their Chemistry, Olah, 2008). For example, iodine has been
previously used to generate 1,2-ethylene dications. Will it work with this system? The authors’ use of electrochemical and
photochemical catalysis is outstanding. However, the results should still be compared to the chemical oxidants. 
The study would be further improved by direct observation of the dicationic species using low temperature NMR techniques.
Even more spectacular, it would be a great achievement to obtain an XRD of a crystal of the dication, although there are big
technical challenges to this type of work. 
The authors should try to develop a better hypothesis for the cis-conformation of the dication. It is more common to see
cationic rings stack with neutral rings, or two neutral ring stacking, but it seems rather remarkable to have two cationic rings
having pi-stacking. Indeed, the authors have noted that the ring are even parallel - suggesting a strong interaction or
bonding. Most dicationic systems tend to have strong charge-charge repulsive effects, which should tend to favor the trans-
stereoisomer. So why does this prefer otherwise? 
It is not exactly clear on the fate of the protons during the cyclization steps. Shouldn’t the excess aniline or amine be the
proton carrier? If so, can these ions provide the H2 gas upon reduction at the cathode?

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The Jin group reported an innovative solution for the synthesis of dispirocyclic π-systems featuring 13 central 2,5-dihydro-
1H-pyrrole or 2,5-dihydrofuran cores. Divergent synthetic routes were developed by either electrochemical or photoredox
methodology to accomplish the formal 4+1 cycloaddition. An important carbodication intermediate was proposed and
supported by DFT calculation. The reaction displayed a broad substrate scope, and mechanistic investigations were well
executed. 
However, my major concern is the significance of this manuscript. From the methodology point of view, this
reaction is predictable because hetero-nucleophile attacking a carbocation is a common decomposition pathway for
carbocation formed during proton-initiated electrophilic addition of alkenes, which has been well documented in classic
organic textbooks; examples of oxidative alkene activation via electrochemical and photoredox methods are also abundant.
In addition, I didn’t find the unique advantage of the carbodication intermediate for facilitating this transformation, which is
heteroatom-stabilized carbocation with attenuated reactivity. From the application point of view, although the authors
claimed these novel spirocyclic aromatic compounds may exhibit promising optoelectronic effect, no supporting data was
listed in this manuscript. Therefore, I will recommend rejection of this manuscript in its current form due to lack of
significance. If such optimal optoelectronic effect of these compound can be added, I would be happy to reconsider this
manuscript. 
In addition, regarding the DFT calculation, these questions should be addressed:

within Gaussian (e.g., implicit solvation model and relevant parameters).

We thank the reviewer  for this insightful comment and valuable discussion. As correctly pointed out,

A brief description of these findings has been added to the revised manuscript (page 5):  
“DFT analysis of 1a2+ in the presence of the PF₆ ‒ counteranion reveals that the cis conformer is 3.4

cycloaddition pathway (Fig. 2b, Table S7 and Fig. S7, SI). In contrast, when the PF₆⁻ anion is omitted, 
the trans-dication becomes 3.6 kcal/mol more stable than the cis form. These findings suggest that the 
PF₆⁻ counteranion mitigates charge–charge repulsion between the two cationic centers by cation -anion
