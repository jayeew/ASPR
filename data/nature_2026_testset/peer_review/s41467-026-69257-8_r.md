Response to referees’ letter  
 
Reviewer 1: The paper by Tejel, Geer, and coworkers describes divergent reactivity of BF4 and PF6 
anions at a cobalt(III) center. The work is very well done experimentally, characerizations and 
measurements are perfect, and the paper is well -written. It can probably be published 
in Nature Communications nearly as-is. However, it is the opinion of this reviewer that this chemistry 
is fairly low impact and general interest in it will be rather low. I do not think anyone is really

We thank the reviewer for the positive evaluation of the experimental work. We respectfully disagree, 
however, with the assessment that count erion activation is of low relevance. Beyond its specific 
chemical transformations, our work provides fundamental insight into how ion pairing and ligand -
anion interactions modulate anion activation at transition metal centers, a question of broad 
relevance to reactivity and catalysis.  Recent studies show that counterions exert decisive control 
over reactivity, selectivity, and even catalytic turnover through ion pairing, outer-sphere interactions, 
and supramolecular effects  [Angew. Chem. Int. Ed.  2018, 57, 13982; Angew. Chem. Int. Ed.  2004, 
43, 2066] . Far from being spectators, counterions frequently determine catalytic viability and 
mechanistic pathways. For example, Zhu et al. showed that the weakly coordinating anion [BArF 4]⁻ 
is essential for iron-catalyzed alkylzincation, increasing yields from trace to 92% and stabilizing the 
reactive cationic Fe(II)–alkyl species [Nat Commun 2025, 16, 7161]. Similarly, BArF 4⁻ undergoes C–
B activation in cobalt photocatalysis, and replacing it with NTf 2⁻ completely suppresses reactivity 
[Nat Commun 2025, 16, 6635]. 
Our study advances this area by showing how tailored ligand environments can modulate ion pairing 
and supramolecular interactions to control whether PF6⁻ or BF 4⁻ undergoes activation. The protic 
cavity of our ligand system enables subtle but decisive differences in reactivity, illustrating how 
ligand design can be used to modulate counterion behavior, an emerging strategy with broad 
implications for catalysis, supramolecular control, and reactivity design . We also note that the 
revised manuscript now includ es fluoride -transfer reactions that demonstrate the functional 
reactivity of the Co-F species generated upon PF6⁻ activation, highlighting that this anion activation 
enables downstream chemical transformations.

We thank the reviewer for this constructive suggestio n, which has indeed strengthened the 
manuscript. In response to the request for evidence of synthetically relevant reactivity of the Co -F 
species, we have performed new experiments that directly demonstrate the functional competence 
of the fluoride ligand formed upon PF 6⁻ activation. The revised manuscript now includes three 
representative transformations, included in the manuscript in the “Fluoride Transfer Reactivity of the 
Co–F” section: 
• Fluoride transfer to trityl tetrafluoroborate, forming trityl fluoride (Fig. 6 and Fig. S26). 
• Fluoride transfer to triphenylchlorosilane, forming fluorotriphenylsilane and converting the 
cobalt complex fully to [3][PF6]2 (Fig. 6 and Fig. S27). 
• Fluoride transfer to isovaleryl chloride, forming the corresponding acyl fluoride and yielding 
a new Co(III) acyl -chloride complex [8][PF 6]3, characterized by NMR and HR -ESI-MS (Fig. 6 
and Fig. S28).

These reactions show that the Co -F bond is not only isolable but also reactive toward a range of 
electrophiles, enabling the formation of C-F, Si-F, and acyl-fluoride products under mild conditions. 
We also tested isopropyl chloride as a simple alkyl elect rophile, but no reaction was observed, 
consistent with the nucleophilic reactivity profile of the Co -F species. Although C -H fluorination is 
therefore outside the scope of this system, the new results demonstrate that PF 6⁻ activation 
generates a cobalt bou nd fluoride which can be transferred to different electrophiles showing 
broader relevance.
