Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors present a novel and compelling concept for a conformal microwave absorber inspired by chainmail structures.
The originality and potential impact of this work make it a strong candidate for publication in this journal. However, to meet
the high standards expected by the journal, the manuscript would benefit from greater clarity and thoroughness in certain
areas. Please find below several suggestions aimed at improving the quality and rigor of the presentation: 
The term "absorbers" is used throughout the manuscript. As this word can apply to various domains (e.g., mechanical,
acoustic), it would be helpful to clarify early on—perhaps in the title or abstract—that the work specifically pertains to
microwave absorbers, unless "absorbers" is already an accepted convention in the field. 
Some of the quantitative descriptions would benefit from greater precision: 
In the Abstract, please clarify the bandwidth range corresponding to the reported EAB of 11.4 GHz (e.g., from XX GHz to XX
GHz). 
The term “cumulative bandwidth” is also used—please clarify whether this is a standard metric in the field, and if not,
consider redefining or rephrasing it for clarity. 
The manuscript makes several statements regarding the absorber’s robust advantages over conventional designs. While
some comparisons are made (e.g., in Figure 5 and the Materials & Methods section), a more comprehensive comparison
with results from existing literature—especially those involving conformal or rigid absorbers—would strengthen the authors’
claims. 
The manuscript frequently uses terms such as “significant” and “remarkable”. Consider limiting these descriptors unless they
are supported by quantitative comparisons or clearly defined criteria. 
Further discussion is needed regarding the mechanical strength results shown in Figures 2e–g. For instance: 
It is important to indicate the mass of Type-1, Type-2, and Type-3 designs, particularly as the plots compare absolute force
rather than stress. 
This information is critical to contextualize the mechanical performance improvements. 
The term “Reflectivity (dB)” is used in figures and text. In microwave absorber literature, the term “Reflection loss (dB)” is
often used when referring to measurements in decibels. Please confirm whether the usage here is consistent with field
standards. If “Reflectivity” is used, consider expressing it as a percentage; if the values are in -dB, “Reflection loss” may be
more appropriate. 
Please ensure that all dimensional values in the supplementary figures (e.g., Fig. S1, S2, S3) include units. For instance,

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors report a conformable and switchable microwave metamaterial absorber, which exhibits a broadband effective
absorption bandwidth of 11.4 GHz, while its interlocking topological design enables curvature adaptation without
deformation of the absorbing parts. And dynamic modulation of unit allows the absorber's operational frequency to be tuned
across 4.6-18 GHz. The authors experimentally verify the wideband and switchable performance. 
There are some issues need to be addressed before it can be further considered. 
1. Is the metal background plate (serving as a reflective surface) needed in this absorber design? If so, how does the metal
background deform together with the absorber? 
2. What is the relationship between the parameter optimization (Figs. S1-S3) and mechanical strength measurements? What
is the rationale for correlating the parameter optimization process with strength performance? 
3. The authors referred to an optimal balance for achieving wideband absorption through critical factors such as mechanical
strength and fabrication feasibility. Could the authors provide more details about the specific optimization technique
employed in this process? 
4. What is the indispensable role of pure-PLA in this absorber design? Is it primarily for structural support and tensile
strengthening? What impact does it have on electromagnetic wave absorption? 
5. Add xyz coordinate labels to Figure 2; otherwise, the results in panels 2e-2g cannot be properly aligned with the model.
Also, Fig. 3 lacks a scale bar. 
6. Fig. 3d demonstrates the oblique incidence performance at 8 GHz. How does the absorber perform at other frequencies
within the absorption band? And why is a ground plane introduced in Fig. 3e? 
7. Please provide the detailed structural designs of three comparative samples: Salisbury screen, metasurface, and
honeycomb (Fig. 5a). 
8. In switchable design, current mechanical reconfiguration mechanisms are constrained to 2D planes. What design
innovations would enable deployment on non-planar surfaces? 
9. Taking into account the UAV application scenario discussed in the manuscript, could the 3D printing technology utilized
in this paper enable mass production of the absorber? Does the absorber's specific density satisfy the weight constraints for
stealth UAV payloads? 
10. To ensure reproducibility, we request supplementary documentation of the manufacturing process, including a video
demonstration of the FDM printing process. 
Reviewer #4 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The work presents a chainmail-inspired microwave absorber formed by interlocking unit cells that embed a lossy CNT-PLA
crossed resonator inside a pure-PLA rectangular frame, produced in one step with dual-nozzle FDM printing. In the planar
(expanded) state the 5.5 mm-thick sheet delivers polarization-insensitive >90 % absorption across 6.2–17.6 GHz, which is
explained by Lorentz electric resonance and artificial magnetic modes extracted via effective-medium retrieval and
characteristic-mode analysis. Because the rigid absorbing elements merely rotate and translate, the sheet conforms to
cylindrical, saddle and doubly curved metallic surfaces with only ~4.9 % loss of average absorption while maintaining radar-
cross-section reduction exceeding 30 dB. Elastic bands that compress or expand the lattice provide reversible mechanical
tuning, shifting the main resonance from 8 GHz to 5.5 GHz and extending the aggregated absorption band to 4.6–18 GHz,
thereby bypassing the Rozanov thickness-bandwidth limit. Full-wave simulations closely match free-space and RCS
measurements of 3D-printed 8 × 8 prototypes, confirming a mechanically robust, ultra-wideband, conformal and switchable
absorber for advanced EMI-mitigation and stealth technologies. Overall, the work presents a substantial advancement in the
domain of metasurface absorbers. Thus, the Reviewer recommends that the paper be published if the following minor
concerns are addressed. 
1. Wide-angle stability is claimed, but only a single frequency slice (8 GHz) is shown in the final experimental demonstration
in Fig. 6. Thus, full-band angular sweeps are essential to substantiate broadband conformality. 
2. Practical deployment demands proof that thousands of conformal/switch cycles do not fatigue joints, delaminate CNT-
PLA, or alter absorption. 
3. It is more useful to show uncertainty of the performance in terms of the reflectivity with each expansion and contraction
length. 
4. Heating can detune permittivity and cause polymer creep. Therefore, thermal limits and mitigation strategies should be
specified. 
5. To provide a wider range of information on metamaterial absorbers in the Introduction section, the authors are suggested
to mention that there are many studies on metamaterial absorbers tuned by electric circuits, as shown below. Such
metasurfaces have passive/active tuning mechanisms as well as unique dependences not only on the frequency of the

Abstract: 
The rigidity of conventional microwave absorbers  impedes their reliable 
integration with complex curved surfaces and deformable components.  
To overcome these ch allenges, we present a chainmail -inspired conformal and 
switchable microwave metamaterial absorber, conveniently fabricated via 3D printing. 
Furthermore, we integrate the microwave absorber with elastic bands to achieve 
reversible switching between expanded and contracted states, extending the minimum 
operating frequency.  
This work establishes a new paradigm for developing ultra -wideband intelligent 
conformal microwave absorbers, demonstrating significant potential across diverse 
applications.

components present in these applications.

emerged as a promising research frontier for addressing EMC in highly integrated 
electronics and enabling low observability for target objects.

bands to fully exploit its fabric-like flexibility, enabling dynamic adjustment of the unit
