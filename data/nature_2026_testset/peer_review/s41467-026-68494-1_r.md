Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Overall Assessment 
This manuscript reports supramolecular gel systems that exhibit hierarchical helical assemblies and strong chiroptical
activity with a notably high |glum| value (~0.11). The work is visually appealing and potentially impactful in the field of chiral
photonics. However, the current version remains largely descriptive, with several key mechanistic aspects insufficiently
supported by quantitative or experimental evidence. Strengthening the correlation between molecular structure, hierarchical
organization, and optical response would considerably enhance the scientific depth and overall impact of the work. Given
the intended scope and audience of Nature Communications, I therefore recommend that the authors address these issues
before the manuscript can be considered for publication. 
Major 
1. Mechanistic elaboration of microstructure formation

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 2 -->

While the authors emphasize the helical structures, a more systematic discussion of how the microstructure can be tuned
would be scientifically valuable. The results and mechanisms related to Supplementary Fig. 5 should be expanded to
identify and rationalize the controllable parameters governing the assembly process. 
2. Optical property section too phenomenological 
The discussion of optical and chiroptical behaviors is largely descriptive and lacks mechanistic depth. Stronger evidence
linking molecular structure and supramolecular organization to the observed spectral shifts and CPL activity is needed.
Computational modeling, temperature- or solvent-dependent spectroscopy, or quantitative correlations between morphology
and |glum| would greatly enhance the credibility of the interpretation. 
3. In-situ optical microscopy study requires mechanistic justification 
The section describing the real-time optical microscopy observations is useful but remains phenomenological. The claim
that “the assembly was interface-controlled” (p. 9) requires quantitative support or experimental validation (e.g., temperature-,
concentration-, or viscosity-dependent studies). The authors should also clarify what the fitted kinetic curves (W(t) and L(t))
physically represent—specifically, what are the numbers of those two slops represent. Without a clear mechanistic
interpretation of these fits and supporting evidence, the conclusion regarding interface-controlled growth remains
speculative. 
4. Missing connection between molecular and microscopic scales 
The authors present molecular-level analysis and simulations; however, a clear experimental gap exists between the
oligomeric assemblies and the microscopic twisted structures. This limitation, likely due to the resolution of the in-situ
technique, should be explicitly acknowledged and discussed to provide a more continuous multiscale picture. 
5. Clarification of the physical picture of high |glum| 
Since the manuscript highlights a record |glum|, the authors should contextualize this result by listing comparable values
across related systems (e.g., small molecules, aggregates, gels) to quantify the advancement. As the large |glum| is a central
claim, a summary table or comparative figure with literature data would significantly strengthen the paper’s impact. 
Minor 
1. Misleading analogy on page 4–5 
The statement “The two long alkyl chains functioned analogously to DNA base pairs …” is inaccurate. DNA base pairs are
complementary and programmable, whereas hydrophobic interactions are nonspecific. This analogy should be revised or
removed. 
2. Clarify schematic representation 
The schematic drawings do not clearly illustrate how molecules pack into chiral structures. 
3. Reference accuracy and context 
Ensure that all kinetic and mechanistic claims (e.g., interface-controlled growth) are supported by appropriate literature
references and clearly compared with previous supramolecular systems. 
4. Detailed methodology of optical microscopy analysis 
The authors should describe the image analysis procedure in more detail—specifically, how the size of assembled
structures was measured, what software or custom scripts were used, and how many samples or replicates were analyzed. 
5. Overstated biomimetic analogy 
The comparison to botanical morphogenesis is primarily morphological. Since biological systems are non-equilibrium and
energy-driven, this “biomimetic” framing should be toned down or rephrased as a structural resemblance rather than genuine
mechanistic mimicry. 
6. Rearrangement of figures 
Figure 4 provides stronger evidence and simulations at the molecular level compared with Figures 2 and 3. If the latter
cannot be substantially improved through deeper analysis or discussion, rearranging the figures to highlight the molecular
insights earlier in the manuscript would improve narrative coherence.

promoting more efficient ICT.  The DFT calculations unambiguously suppor t this 
phenomenon (Figure R3). Upon alkylation, a significant decrease in the LUMO energy 
(-4.17 eV) compared to the HOMO ( -3.23 eV) is observed, with the HOMO -LUMO

strong, proportional relationship between growth rate and bulk monomer concentration.

growth rate to variations in bulk concentration C∞ is markedly weak, particularly in the 
higher-concentration regime studied here. The modest slowdown in growth rate despite
