Summary: This paper looks at velocity space structures captured in MMS data from its first crossing of the EDR in 2015.
These structures have not been measured by earlier spacecraft missions because MMS brings faster and higher resolution
measurements of particle distributions than were previously available. MMS data capture a crescent-shaped electron
distribution that is contracted in MMS3 when compared to MMS4. The gradient of a contracted crescent has a “smile-like”
appearance. PIC simulations of the EDR show a similar feature in the gradient of the electron distribution. Velocity-space
signatures are helpful tools for recognizing events, like reconnection, sampled by MMS and determining the position of MMS
relative to these events. Studying the 
of particle distributions is an important research area, and the authors are to be
commended for making insightful comparisons between MMS and PIC.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Review from Jun Zhong: 
This paper identified a visually compelling “smile”-shaped electron gradient distributions, that extend the well-known
crescent distributions, in the electron diffusion region of asymmetric magnetic reconnection on the magnetopause. The
interpretation of MMS observations is well supported by the high-resolution PIC simulations. The writing is generally clear,
the methodology is rigorous, and the implications for space and plasma physics communities are significant. 
The authors declare that such “smile”-shaped signatures may be unique to the electron diffusion region of asymmetric
reconnection. If this is true, the “smile”-shaped gradient distributions will be helpful to precisely identify the events that the
MMS spacecraft within electron diffusion region. My question is whether asymmetric magnetic reconnection (with small
guide field) necessarily produces such smile-shaped distributions. Could the authors check other similar MMS electron
diffusion region crossing events to see if similar observations exist? 
Additionally, I feel that the title is too broad, making it difficult for readers to associate it with the topic of the paper. 
Overall, I think this paper bridges spacecraft observations, kinetic plasma theory, and simulation, and may be aligned with
Communications Physics' scope.

“4.) The evolution of fe leading to the smile-like structure is discussed in lines 270-273.
What are possible physical mechanisms that cause this evolution? Why does the gyrophase
extent of the electron crescent population decrease? Is there a way to understand this de-
crease using a single particle motion description? ”
Reply:
Yes, the single particle orbits explained in, e.g., Bessho et al. [2016] [Ref 5]
(specifically their diagrams in Figure 2e and Figure 2f ) help to understand why
the gyrophase extent of the electron crescent population decreases. Specifically,
Bessho et al. [2016] states: “The crescent-shaped component is due to electrons
that cross the magnetic neutral line and are able to reach the point in the
magnetosphere where the distribution is taken.” Starting in the magnetosphere,
initially there is no crescent-shaped population observed – in other words, none
of the magnetosheath electrons’ gyro-orbits are observed. Gradually moving
toward the magnetosheath, eventually the first evidence of the crescent-shaped
electron population is detected as only part of the magnetosheath particles’ gyro-
orbits are captured. As one moves further toward the magnetosheath region,
more of the electron orbits are detected, and the observed statistical crescent-
shape begins to increase in gyrophase in velocity-space. Eventually, far enough
into the magneotsheath, electrons are detected from all parts of the gyro-orbit,
and the crescent becomes more ring-like as the observed gyrophases extend to
the full 360 degrees in velocity-space. This process happens in reverse when
moving in +z for the simulation results discussed in our manuscript: the crescent-
shaped population’s gyrophase extent decreases going from the magnetosheath
side (z <0) to the magnetospheric side ( z >0) of the layer.
It is more difficult to utilize the single-particle motion picture to understand
how the other velocity-space features of the gradient distribution form, e.g., the
red smile region of increasing and concentrating phase space density. Such inves-
tigations would certainly be interesting to conduct, and will be noted for future
work.
