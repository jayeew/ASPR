We thank the Referee for this observation. The apparent non-linear dependence of the FMR 
linewidth on frequency below ∼7 GHz in the YIG/YSGAG sample (Fig. 2(b)) is discussed in the

As explained in lines 121–127, the deviation from a purely linear Gilbert behavior at low frequencies 
originates from the presence of double or multiple FMR peaks caused by residual magnetic 
inhomogeneities in the YIG/YSGAG film. At low frequencies (≲5 GHz), these peaks are well separated 
and can be resolved and fitted individually, yielding very narrow linewidths. In the intermediate

Lorentzian. At higher frequencies (≳18 GHz), the peaks overlap almost completely, recovering the

Importantly, this effect is extrinsic and related to residual magnetic inhomogeneity of the YIG film

1.Besides the temperature -independent small diamagnetic susceptibility for YSGAG, why does the 
developed diamagnetic YSGAG/YIG show lower FMR linewidth as well as Gilbert damping compared 
to the GGG/YIG system? 
We thank the reviewer for this question. The reduced FMR linewidth and Gilbert damping of  the 
YIG/YSGAG system compared to YIG/GGG are primarily a low -temperature effect and originate from 
the absence of substrate magnetization and its coupling to the YIG film. As discussed in the manuscript, 
the paramagnetic GGG substrate becomes magnetized in an external field at cryogenic temperatures, 
producing a stray field that leads to an inhomogeneous internal field,  that shifts the FMR frequency. 
Moreover, the dipolar and/or exchange interaction  enables coupling between the two spin systems,

thereby in troducing additional damping (lines 47 –53; lines 102 –105). In contrast, the diamagnetic 
YSGAG substrate does not magnetize and therefore eliminates these substrate -induced dissipation 
channels, which is why it is ideally suited for low-temperature magnonics.

At room temperature, where the magnetism of the substrate does not play a role, the linewidths and 
Gilbert damping parameters of YIG/YSGAG and YIG/GGG are on the same level (within the error bars) 
as seen in Table 1  and (lines 109–113), confirming comparable intrinsic material quality.  As already 
mentioned in the answer to Reviewer 1, Comment 2, the narrower linewidths observed for YIG/YSGAG 
at low frequencies (< 4 GHz) at all temperatures are attributed to the splitting of the FMR mode into 
multiple peaks due to inhomogeneities. These peaks can be resolved and fitted separately, thereby 
revealing the true intrinsic damping of the YIG film (lines 121–125). When these peaks start to merge 
at higher frequencies for YIG/YSGAG, the resulting larger fitted linewidths no longer reflect the intrinsic

For YIG/GGG such peak splitting may not be resolvable at these low frequencies (< 4 GHz) due to higher 
effective homogeneity of the YIG film , leading to unresolvable multipeak and an effectively broader 
linewidth. In general, further improvement of the YIG film homogeneity in future studies is expected 
to equalize the damping levels in the YIG/YSGAG and YIG/GGG systems and to further reduce them  
both, potentially approaching the bulk YIG damping value, which represents the best achieva ble 
benchmark for YIG films. 
2.The effective magnetization of YSGAG/YIG is smaller than that of GGG/YIG at both low temperature 
and 300 K. The authors should provide the mechanism of the difference.  
We thank the reviewer for raising this important point. The reduced effective magnetization observed 
in YIG films grown on YSGAG compared to YIG/GGG at both room temperature and low temperatures 
originates from strain induced by the lattice mismatch between the substrate and the YIG film, rather 
than from a red uction of the intrinsic saturation magnetization. As discussed in the manuscript, the 
YSGAG substrate has a slightly larger lattice constant than GGG, which induces a higher tensile strain 
in the epitaxial YIG film (lines 80 –82). This increased tensile strain leads to an enhanced uniaxial 
anisotropy component perpendicular to the film plane (lines 82 –84). In ferromagnetic resonance 
analysis, this additional perpendicular anisotropy enters the Kittel equation through the effective 
magnetization M
eff, resulti ng in a systematically lower extracted Meff. Consequently, YIG/YSGAG 
exhibits a consistently lower effective magnetization than YIG/GGG across the entire temperature 
range, as also noted by the approximately 10% offset discussed later in the manuscript (page 6, lines 
181–184). This difference therefore reflects substrate -induced strain effects due to lattice mismatch 
and does not indicate inferior magnetic quality of the YIG/YSGAG system. 
To stress that the effective magnetization inherently includes the sa turation magnetization of YIG 
together with anisotropy contributions, we added the following to the manuscript text (page 3):  
…, which includes both the saturation magnetization of YIG and the relevant anisotropy contributions…

We thank the reviewer for this question. The low- temperature FMR measurements in the dilution 
refrigerator were performed using broadband transmission measurements of the S12 parameter with 
a vector network analyzer, as described in the Methodology section. To reliably extract the small FMR 
signal from the background in the presence of a long microwave transmission line and reduced signal-
to-noise ratio at millikelvin temperatures, a dual- reference-field background subtraction procedure 
was employed:  for each target magnetic field, additional measurements were performed at two 
reference fields offset by approximately ±20 mT, and the averaged reference signal was subtracted 
from the target-field signal to isolate the FMR response of the YIG film (lines  209–213). This method 
was developed and already used in previous publications [15,16]. The resulting FMR absorption spectra 
were then fitted using a Lorentzian lineshape to extract the resonance frequency and linewidth (lines 
213–216). To maintain thermal equilibrium at the lowest temperatures, the applied microwave power 
was limited to −25 dBm, which reduces the signal-to-noise ratio (lines 204–206). 
To further enhance the signal -to-noise ratio under these low- power conditions, the intermediate 
frequency bandwidth (IFBW) of the vector network analyzer was reduced from the standard 1 kHz to 
values as low as 10 Hz, allowing for longer integration times and more precise measurements. This 
enhanced measurement precision was particularly required for the weak and broadened FMR peaks 
of the YIG/GGG system at low temperatures, whereas the YIG/YSGAG system exhibited sharp and well-
defined FMR spectra that could be reliably extracted without requiring such significant  reduction of

4.The authors discussed the data from previous studies in Fig.3. However, the YIG thicknesses of 
previous systems (30  nm ~48 nm) are different from this study (140 nm/150 nm). Does the FMR 
linewidth show YIG thickness dependence? 
We thank the reviewer for this important question. The dependence of the FMR linewidth on YIG film 
thickness is indeed discussed in the manuscr ipt. As noted in the comparison presented in Fig. 3(b), 
sputtered and LPE-grown YIG films with thicknesses in the range of 30–48 nm generally exhibit broader 
FMR linewidths than the thicker 140 –150 nm LPE -grown YIG films investigated in this work. This 
behavior is consistent with previous reports showing that reduced film thickness can lead to enhanced 
magnetic damping due to increased relative contributions from surface roughness, interface 
imperfections, and structural disorder (see Ref. [29]). However, with films that are nearly structurally 
perfect in the range from 20 nm to 100 nm and negligible interdiffusion of substrate ions at the 
interface film/substrate, the FMR line widths are comparable and did not show any real thickness -
dependent broadening, as demonstrated in Ref. [33] and [12] for LPE films at room temperature. Only 
in films with a significantly greater thickness of several hundred nanometers was it found that the FMR 
linewidths can be narrower (see Figure 5 in Ref. [12]). At low temperatures, the FMR linewidths of LPE 
films with a thickness of approximately 50  nm also exhibit lower FMR line widths compared to thin 
films grown by physical deposition methods, as shown in Figure 3 in this report and Figure 5 in Ref. 
[30]. 
In addition, the manuscri pt explicitly points out that thinner YIG films, particularly those grown by 
sputtering, tend to exhibit higher linewidths and larger Gilbert damping parameters compared to 
thicker, high -quality LPE -grown films, even at room temperature (lines 161 –165). Th e improved 
linewidth performance of the thicker YIG films studied here is therefore attributed not only to the

choice of substrate but also to the higher microstructural perfection achievable in thicker LPE -grown 
films, which reduces surface- and interface-related relaxation mechanisms. 
To stress this more clearly in the manuscript, we modified the manuscript text as follows: 
The observed differences in room -temperature FMR linewidths between our samples and those 
reported in the literature can be attributed to several factors. First, microstructuring the YIG films into 
a stripe improves the material homogeneity under the measurement antenna, which can result in 
reduced linewidths. Second, the YIG film thickness in those studies is significantly smaller by a factor 
of three to five compared to our samples . However, as reported by Will -Cole et al. [3 0] for low -
temperature FMR linewidths of comparable 50 nm thin YIG-LPE films on GGG substrates, the values of 
these were also narrower than for physically deposited films. This is attributed to the sharp interface 
with a very thi n interdiffusion layer of ∼ 1 nm, which is significantly thinner than the interfaces 
described in the literature for sputtered films [30]. Finally, higher microstructural perfection of LPE 
films grown under near-equilibrium conditions can result in lower values for inhomogeneous linewidth 
broadening. 
At the same time, the comparison in Fig. 3(b) demonstrates that, despite differences in thickness, all  
high-quality YIG films exhibit a pronounced increase in linewidth upon cooling to cryogenic 
temperatures when grown on paramagnetic GGG substrates, whereas the YIG/YSGAG system retains 
low damping down to millikelvin temperatures. This indicates that, while film thickness can influence 
the absolute linewidth values, the dominant low-temperature linewidth enhancement is related to the 
substrate rather than to film thickness or quality . It is also important to note that several of the 
comparison samples  on d iamagnetic substrates  shown in Fig. 3(b) are grown on YSGG or YAG 
substrates, which exhibit a significantly larger lattice mismatch with YIG than YSGAG. This larger 
mismatch induces stronger strain, which can also be temperature dependent, and limits the achievable 
YIG film thickness to values below 100 nm.
