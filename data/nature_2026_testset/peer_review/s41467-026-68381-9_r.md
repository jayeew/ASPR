Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript reports a host–guest complex between acridine and a halogen-bonded organic framework (XOF), which
exhibits full-visible-spectrum piezochromic luminescence under pressure. The authors demonstrate that the rigidity of the
XOF matrix suppresses amorphization of the guest acridine under high pressure, leading to a pronounced emission shift
from blue to red. Such a pronounced shift would be the largest shift reported to date. The experimental characterization is
thorough and includes in situ X-ray diffraction, UV-vis absorption, photoluminescence, and IR spectroscopy under varying
pressure, along with DFT-based theoretical analysis. While the data is of high quality and the piezochromic behavior is
clearly demonstrated, I regret to conclude that the manuscript does not meet the novelty and conceptual impact required for
publication in Nature Communications. Several materials exhibiting large piezochromic shifts have already been reported,
and while this particular complex may achieve a record shift, such quantitative superiority alone does not constitute a
sufficiently novel conceptual advance. The design strategy, utilizing rigid porous frameworks to stabilize luminescent guests,
is not so unprecedented. One potentially interesting aspect is the observation of pressure-induced emission enhancement
(PIEE). However, the mechanistic discussion of this phenomenon remains speculative. For instance, the change in IR
spectral features near 1.2 GPa is used as suggestive evidence, but this alone is not convincing or sufficient to support the
claimed mechanism. Given these considerations, I recommend that the manuscript is not suitable for publication in Nature
Communications in its current form. However, the work could be of interest to a more specialized journal after appropriate
revision and clarification of the following points: 
1. To demonstrate potential sensing applications, only two pressure-release cycles is insufficient. At least ten cycles should
be conducted to evaluate the reproducibility and stability of the piezochromic response. 
2. To include the absorption spectral change of acridine alone under pressure in the Supporting Information would be
important. 
3. The manuscript does not explain why acridine was chosen as the guest molecule. A brief discussion of the advantages in
photophysical properties and why acridine is suited for the current study would strengthen the conceptual framing. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript presents a comprehensive investigation of full-visible-spectrum piezochromism achieved through a host-
guest halogen-bonded organic framework (3D-XOF) encapsulating acridine (AD) molecules. The authors demonstrate a
record-breaking photoluminescence (PL) red-shift of 237 nm under hydrostatic pressure, supported by an extensive suite of
experimental characterizations and theoretical calculations. The topic is highly relevant to the fields of piezochromic
materials, high-pressure physics, and optical sensing. The manuscript is well-organized, and the experimental data
convincingly support the proposed mechanism. The authors effectively integrate structural analyses (ADXRD, Hirshfeld
surfaces), spectroscopic measurements (PL, UV-vis, IR), and DFT calculations to elucidate the pressure-dependent
behavior of the system. 
However, the points raised below need to be addressed before the manuscript can be considered for publication.

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 2 -->

1) The correlation between the pressure-dependent chromic behavior of 3D-XOF and molecular aggregation phenomena is
unclear. 
2) The co-crystallization protocol is briefly outlined. Please provide information on the reproducibility and yield of the
synthesis. Additionally, are the cocrystals stable under ambient humidity and temperature over time? Please clarify whether
the XOF@AD samples can be handled in ambient atmosphere or whether special storage conditions are required. 
3) A more detailed structural visualization in Figure 2a is necessary to unambiguously demonstrate 3D-XOF, including its
elemental distribution and specific halogen bonding coordination environments. 
4) The reversibility of the piezochromic response is a key advantage. While two pressurization cycles are shown, more
detailed information on potential degradation is needed. Does the PL quantum yield fully recover after each cycle? Is there
any observable structural damage after multiple pressure cycles? 
5) The DFT methodology employed is standard and appropriate. However, the text should explicitly state whether dispersion
corrections were employed in the band gap calculations. Given the critical role of π–π interactions, the omission of
dispersion corrections could impact the accuracy of the results. Please clarify this point. 
Please give the functional(s) and basis set(s) employed in the DFT calculations of XOF@AD to facilitate better evaluation of
the computational methodology. 
6) The proposed mechanism, centered on the synergistic reinforcement of π–hole···π interactions and halogen bonding
maintaining structural rigidity and suppressing amorphization, is well-supported by Hirshfeld surface analyses and DFT
calculations. Nevertheless, the manuscript would benefit from a more quantitative discussion. For example, could the
authors provide explicit interaction energies or quantify the magnitude of the energy barriers preventing guest disorder? 
7) The font sizes in the Supporting Information figures should be increased to improve readability. 
Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this study, Yang et al. introduce an innovative host–guest strategy to tackle the challenge of achieving a broad and
controllable piezochromic response. By encapsulating readily amorphizable AD guest molecules within a rigid three-
dimensional, halogen-bonded organic framework, they preserve structural integrity under high pressure. The confinement
provided by the host framework effectively restricts molecular displacement and suppresses irreversible phase transitions,
thereby maintaining the guests’ piezochromic activity. Combining detailed experiments with DFT calculations, the authors
show that the host–guest architecture substantially suppresses amorphization of the AD guests under compression. This
mechanism yields a wide piezochromic tuning range spanning the entire visible spectrum. Moreover, the material exhibits
excellent reversibility, which is critical for practical applications. This work establishes the host–guest system as a robust
platform for full spectrum piezochromism and opens new avenues for developing smart, visible range pressure sensing
materials. The conclusions are supported by comprehensive data and a clear mechanistic rationale, representing a notable
advance in stimuli responsive optical materials. I strongly recommend publication in Nature Communications after
addressing the following minor concerns. 
1. How about the chemical stability of the studied XOF@AD? It will be important for future applications in piezochromic
applications. 
2. In Fig. 2b, the 3D PL spectra make it difficult to compare peak shifts. It is suggested that corresponding 2D PL spectra be
provided in the Supplementary Information for clearer comparison. Additionally, for the ADXRD patterns shown in Fig. 3a,
the authors should draw dotted lines through the peaks to highlight the changes for better understanding. 
3. The experimental details section should be expanded to include more comprehensive information. For example, what
type of samples were used in the measurements, single crystals or powder? What is the synthesis method for the 3D-XOF
material in Supplementary Fig. 7? Is the PL transition during compression instantaneous, or does it require a pressure-
holding duration to stabilize? 
4. The PL intensity is lower than its initial state upon complete pressure release from 22.8 GPa. Would the PL intensity be
higher than the initial state if the pressure is released from 1.2 GPa (the pressure at which PL is strongest)? 
5. The PL measurements were performed using the 355 nm laser line. However, the authors do not discuss whether any
changes occur in the optical properties under high pressure during irradiation. Please provide additional relevant PL
experiments. 
6. In Fig. 5d, the authors have presented the evolution of the absorption spectra during pressurization. Corresponding Tauc
plots should be provided in the Supplementary Information. 
7. There are some formatting and grammatical errors in the manuscript that need to be checked carefully. To name a few: 
(1) the subscripts of “non-radiative decay rates”, “radiative decay rates”, and “distance”. 
(2) The x-axis label of “Pressure (GPa)” in Supplementary Fig. 8 is incorrect. 
(3) In Page 14, Line 20, “is critically accounts for” should be revised to “critically accounts for”.

Author reply: We highly appreciate the reviewer for the insightful comments. We 
selected acridine (AD) as the guest molecule achieving full -visible-spectrum based on 
the following key considerations.  Firstly, AD is a representative fused aromatic 
molecule with an extensive π-conjugated system. Its structure comprises three linearly 
fused benzene rings, featuring a pyridinic nitrogen atom in the central ring. This planar 
molecule structure  contributes to enhanced rigidity and improved PL emission 
performance.1-3 The polycyclic structure  increases the overlap of π–π interaction 
regions, thereby contributing to the piezochromic performance under high press ure. 
Secondly, the selection of a PAH guest with an initial deep blue PL emission  is 
essential to realizing the full-visible-spectrum response , owing to enhanced π–π 
stacking interactions causing spectral red shift under high pressure. In studies of blue 
organic PL materials, molecules with aromatic systems and electron-rich systems (such 
as N, O, S) are the main focus because of their excellent stability and superior electronic

structure tunability. 4-5 Among these, AD represents one such typical molecule that 
possesses aromatic and electron-rich systems, exhibiting the required initial deep blue 
PL emission. Finally, the 3D-XOF formed by 1,4-diiodotetrafluorobenzene (1,4-DITFB) 
and 4-Phenylpyridine-N-oxide (PPNO) possesses well-defined nano channels with the 
diameter of less than 1 nm. Owing to its  suitable size , AD is an ideal guest for 
encapsulation within the nano channels of the 3D-XOF host (with the diameter of ~7.3 
Å). In summary, AD was judiciously chosen as the suitable guest material for realizing 
full-visible-spectrum piezochromism.

Author reply:  Thanks very much for the reviewer ’s insightful comments. To

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 24 -->

further elucidate the mechanism of the host -guest strategy for achieving full -visible-
spectrum piezochromism, we provided explicit interaction energies and quantified the 
magnitude of the energy barriers  under pressure.1-4 The π–hole···π interaction energy 
decreases from -3.00 kcal·mol -1 at 1 atm to -5.43 kcal·mol -1 at 10 .3 GPa , which is 
predominantly caused by the enhanced electrostatic attraction  (Figure R7 a, added 
Supplementary Fig. 23). Under further compression, the quantitative energy shows a 
progressive increase to -3.23 kcal·mol-1 at 22.8 GPa. This increase in interaction energy 
under high pressure can be ascribed to electron cloud repulsion, which arises from the 
excessively shortened intermolecular distances.  Notably, quantitative energy  of π–
hole···π interaction at 22.8 GPa is lower than at 1 atm, directly demonstrating that this 
strengthened inter action contributes to  enhance the structural stability  of 3D -XOF. 
Accordingly, the energy barrier increases from 1.30 eV at 1 atm to 1.40 eV at 22.8 GPa 
(Figure R7b ). This indicates that XOF@AD under high pressure must overcome a 
higher energy barrier for inducing structural disorder compared to that at ambient 
pressure. Meanwhile, the interaction energy of the halogen bond exhibited a continuous 
increase from  -26.55 kcal ·mol-1 at 1 atm to -6.62 kcal ·mol-1 at 22. 8 GPa during 
compression, demonstrating that the rigid hexagonal framework remained stable 
without structural collapse. 
Based on the above, we conclude that the structural rigidi ty of the 3D -XOF host 
under high pressure is preserved through strengthened π–hole···π interactions that 
lower its interaction energy. The resulting suppression of guest disorder maintains the 
enhanced π–π stacking interaction of the AD molecules, ultimately leading to band gap 
reduction. Therefore, pressure-modulated π–interactions (including π–π stacking 
interaction and π–hole···π interaction) are the underlying mechanism for the full -
visible-spectrum piezochromism.

yielding the final 3D-XOF product (yield: ~57%). 
In situ  high-pressure PL measurements were conducted using the DAC with

to the sample chamber by rotating the screws; pressure was calibrated using the  
fluorescence peak shift of ruby; and spectral acquisition was then performed. The total 
operational time for mechanical pressurization, pressure calibration, and spectral 
collection was approximately 30 seconds, with no intentional pressure-holding duration 
set during the entire process.
