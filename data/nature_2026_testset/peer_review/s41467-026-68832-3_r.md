Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Key results 
Overall, the authors demonstrate strong proficiency in molecular biology and present well-founded hypotheses regarding the
molecular mechanisms underlying ToCV pathogenicity and plant antiviral resistance. The study is original and holds
significant potential impact, as ToCV is a major viral disease that severely reduces tomato yield. Through a comprehensive
suite of molecular and biochemical experiments, including transformation, CRISPR-Cas9 mutagenesis, yeast two-hybrid,
co-immunoprecipitation, bimolecular fluorescence complementation, luciferase complementation imaging, and cell-to-cell
movement assays, the authors systematically elucidate the pathogen (ToCV) and host (tomato) interaction network. The
findings include: 
1. p59 Function: p59 is identified as a central pathogenic determinant essential for virion assembly, plasmodesmatal
targeting, and intercellular movement. It localizes to the plasma membrane and plasmodesmata, modulates callose
deposition to widen intercellular pores, and functions as a viral movement protein, thereby enhancing viral spread and
pathogenicity. 
2. SlAVE3 Role: The tomato E3 ubiquitin ligase SlAVE3 specifically recognizes and ubiquitinates the viral effector p59,
directing it to proteasomal degradation and restricting ToCV infection. The C-terminal RING domain of SlAVE3 and the
Leu255 residue of p59 are critical for this interaction, establishing SlAVE3 as a key antiviral regulator that limits viral
movement and enhances resistance. 
3. Regulatory Network: SlWRKY6 functions as a transcriptional repressor of SlAVE3 by binding to W-box motifs in its

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 3 -->

promoter. The SlAVE2–SlAVE3 ubiquitin–proteasome complex degrades SlWRKY6, relieving repression and forming a
positive feedback loop that amplifies SlAVE3 expression and fortifies antiviral defense. 
4. Redox Manipulation: p59 further promotes infection by hijacking and degrading the host antioxidant enzyme SlCAT1 via
the SlAVE2–SlAVE3 complex, disrupting redox homeostasis and creating a cellular environment favorable for viral
replication. 
5. Evolutionary Insight: SlAVE3 was subjected to selection during tomato domestication, producing three haplotypes from
two non-synonymous mutations. Among them, SpAVE3, derived from S. pimpinellifolium, exhibits stronger binding to p59
but weaker interaction with SlCAT1, designating it as an antiviral haplotype associated with enhanced ToCV resistance. 
Together, these findings establish a comprehensive interaction model (Figure 6) in which p59 drives ToCV pathogenicity by
facilitating virion assembly, intercellular movement, and host redox disruption, while the tomato AVE2–AVE3–WRKY6
regulatory module orchestrates an antiviral feedback mechanism that recognizes and degrades p59, thereby restoring
cellular homeostasis and conferring viral resistance. 
Validity 
Overall, the data interpretation is carefully executed and, in general, well supported by the presented evidence. The
experimental design convincingly demonstrates the proposed molecular mechanism of the interaction between ToCV and
tomato and the conclusions drawn from these data are appropriate. The integration of molecular, biochemical, and
phenotypic analyses supports and strengthens to the study’s main claims. 
One area of concern lies in the interpretation of selection and domestication analyses. The inference of a selection sweep
based on phylogenetic reconstruction is not methodologically robust for detecting signals of domestication. Phylogenetic
trees describe evolutionary relationships but do not quantitatively measure selective pressures or genetic diversity within
populations. To strengthen this aspect, the authors should focus on population genetic statistics, such as nucleotide diversity
(π), Tajima’s D, or the ratio of non-synonymous to synonymous substitutions (dN/dS), which would provide a more rigorous
and quantitative framework for evaluating domestication-related selection. 
Significance 
This study uncovers a previously uncharacterized antiviral mechanism in tomato that counters ToCV, a major threat to global
tomato production. The authors identify the viral effector p59 as a central determinant of virion assembly and cell-to-cell
movement, and demonstrate that the tomato E3 ubiquitin ligase SlAVE3 targets p59 for proteasomal degradation, thereby
restricting viral spread. The elucidation of the SlAVE2–SlAVE3–SlWRKY6 regulatory module reveals a self-reinforcing
antiviral feedback loop in which degradation of SlWRKY6 enhances SlAVE3 expression and fortifies host defense. The
study further shows that p59 manipulates host redox homeostasis through SlCAT1 degradation, linking viral infection to
oxidative stress responses. Importantly, evolutionary analysis identifies natural SlAVE3 haplotypes under selection during
tomato domestication, including an antiviral allele (SpAVE3) that confers enhanced ToCV resistance. Collectively, this work
provides a comprehensive molecular framework for ToCV–tomato interactions, highlighting the AVE2–AVE3–WRKY6 axis
as a critical regulatory node in plant antiviral immunity and offering valuable genetic insights for breeding virus-resistant
cultivars. 
Data and methodology 
While the experimental design is comprehensive, three methodological aspects would benefit from additional detail and
clarification to improve reproducibility and rigor: 
1. Quantification Using ImageJ 
The manuscript should specify the exact procedures and parameters used in ImageJ for image quantification. This includes
outlining which plugins or measurement tools were applied (e.g., area, intensity, or co-localization analyses), how regions of
interest were defined and whether background correction or normalization steps were performed. Providing this information
would enhance transparency and allow accurate comparison or replication by other researchers. 
2. Plant Material of Nicotiana benthamiana 
The genetic background of the N. benthamiana used in this study should be included. 
3. cDNA Libraries Used for Yeast Two-Hybrid Screening 
The manuscript should provide details about the cDNA libraries employed in the yeast two-hybrid assay. Specifically, the
origin of the library (species, tissue type, developmental stage, or stress condition), the vector system used, and the
coverage or complexity of the library should be reported. These details are important to assess the comprehensiveness of
the interaction screen and the biological relevance of the identified protein–protein interactions. 
Analytical approach 
The statistical analyses are appropriately conducted and effectively support the study’s conclusions by addressing the
research questions with sufficient rigor. 
Suggested improvements 
1. Considering that pathogen–host interactions function through complex molecular networks, it would be highly valuable to
investigate the associated gene regulatory network integrating RNA-seq analysis. Using transgenic materials that share a
more uniform genetic background with the wild type can minimize background variation between genotypes and provide
more reliable insight into network-level changes. In addition, it would be beneficial to propose or experimentally validate
candidate proteins that may participate in this network, such as other E3 ligases, transcription factors, or redox-regulating
enzymes interacting with the SlAVE2–SlAVE3 UPS, to strengthen the mechanistic model and broaden understanding of the
antiviral defense network. 
2. The study should also address the functional performance of heterozygous SpAVE haplotypes. In modern tomato
cultivars, which are typically F
₁
 hybrids, understanding whether SpAVE alleles act dominantly or recessively is essential for
practical breeding applications. If SpAVE exhibits dominance, the presence of one functional allele may be sufficient for the

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 4 -->

phenotype, whereas a recessive effect would limit its utility in hybrid cultivars. Experimental or genetic data addressing this
question would strengthen the manuscript and clarify the translational potential of these findings. 
Clarity and context 
Overall, the manuscript is clearly written and logically structured, presenting the research in a coherent and convincing
manner. However, the suggestions below would further improve the quality and readability of the paper. 
1. Clarification of the SlAVE2–SlAVE3 Complex formation 
The authors propose that the SlAVE2–SlAVE3 UPS complex mediates the degradation of SlWRKY6. However, the
molecular mechanism underlying the formation and functional dynamics of the SlAVE2–SlAVE3 complex remains
insufficiently explained. The manuscript would benefit from a clearer characterization of this complex, specifically, whether
SlAVE2 and SlAVE3 form a stable heteromeric complex or interact transiently during ubiquitin-mediated degradation. 
2. Importance of Effector Compartmentation for Pathogenicity 
The compartmentation of effectors within host cells is a critical determinant of pathogenicity. The authors have conducted
several localization assays to determine the subcellular positions of both effector and host proteins, which is commendable.
However, the discussion should better integrate how these localization patterns contribute mechanistically to virulence.
Specifically, clarify whether the spatial distribution of SlAVE2 and SlAVE3 affects their capacity to interact with SlWRKY6 or
to recruit the ubiquitin–proteasome machinery. Emphasizing this compartmentalization will help link the molecular data to
the overall pathogenic strategy of the effector. 
References 
The manuscript appropriately references previous literature. The cited works are relevant, up to date, and provide sufficient
context for the study’s rationale and experimental design. The authors effectively integrate prior findings to support their
hypotheses and discuss their results in relation to existing knowledge, demonstrating a solid understanding of the field.

UPS as an unstable complex is well -established. Accordingly, please find a detailed explanation

(1) Foundational Concept. Hershko and Ciechanover (1998; The ubiquitin system. Annual 
Review of Biochemistry, 67, 425 -479.) establish the overarching principle of dynamic interactions 
within the ubiquitin cascade. This review outlines the entire ubiquitination cascade. While 
foundational, it implicitly describes a system of unstable complexes: the ubiquitin thioester bond is 
a high-energy intermediate that is passed from E1 to E2 and then to the substrate, with complexes 
dissociating after each transfer step. It details the ATP-dependent formation of the E1~Ub thioester 
bond, establishing that the entire process is driven by energy consumption, which is a hallmark of 
transient, catalytic interactions rather than stable complex formation. 
(2) Mechanisms Underlying Ubiquitination. Berndsen and Wolberger (2014; New insights into 
ubiquitin E3 ligase mechanism. Nature Structural & Molecular Biology, 21, 301-307) delves into the 
molecular mechanics of ubiquitin transfer, explicitly highlighting the dynamic nature of E2 -E3 and 
E3-substrate interactions. It explains how E3 ligases, particularly RING types (SlAVE3 in this study 
is a RING type E3), often act as molecular scaffolds that catalyze ubiquitin transfer by stabilizing a 
reactive conformation of the E2~Ub complex only momentarily, following which the complex 
dissociates. This "proximity-driven catalysis" is a defining feature of a transient complex. 
(3) Structural Biology Evidence. Plechanovová et al., (2012; Structure of a RING E3 ligase and 
ubiquitin-loaded E2 primed for catalysis. Nature, 489, 115 -120.) provided a high -resolution 
structure of a RI NG E3 (RNF4) in complex with an E2~Ub (UbcH5A~Ub) conjugate, trapped in a 
"pre-catalytic state." The structure revealed a limited protein -protein interface between the E3 and 
the E2~Ub. Biochemical analyses confirmed that this complex is highly dynamic and  unstable in 
solution, directly providing structural proof of its transient nature. 
(4) Structural Insights into SCF -Mediated Ubiquitin Transfer. Duda et al., (2013; Structural 
insights into the role of the SCF ubiquitin ligase in catalyzing ubiquitin tran sfer. eLife, 2, e00884.) 
focuses on the structural mechanism of a large, multi-subunit cullin-RING E3 ligase complex (SCF). 
The structures show how the E2~Ub is recruited to a flexible, distal site of the cullin scaffold. This 
architecture functions as a d ynamic platform that brings the E2~Ub into close proximity with a 
substrate for a brief period, facilitating ubiquitin transfer before the complex rearranges for the next 
catalytic cycle. 
(5) Biophysical & Kinetic Evidence. Metzger et al., (2014; A conserv ed allosteric element 
controls the activation and polyubiquitination of Ube2S. Nature Structural & Molecular Biology, 21, 
876-883.) uses detailed biochemical and kinetic analysis to dissect the mechanism of the E2 
enzyme Ube2S. By measuring binding affinit ies (K d) and catalytic rates (K cat), the study 
demonstrates that E3 binding accelerates ubiquitin transfer by lowering the activation energy rather

than forming a stable E2 -E3-substrate complex. The rapid cycle of binding, catalysis, and 
dissociation is direct kinetic evidence for a transient complex. 
(6) Single-Molecule Observation of Ubiquitin Chain Assembly. According to Branigan et al., 
(2020; Single -molecule studies of ubiquitin chain assembly by the anaphase -promoting 
complex/cyclosome (APC/C). Nature Communications, 11, 1-14.), single-molecule techniques are 
ideal for observing transient events directly. This study visualizes ubiquitin chain formation by the 
massive APC/C E3 ligase in real time. The authors directly observe and time the brief binding and 
rapid dissociation events of E2 enzymes (UBE2C and UBE2S) with the APC/C. These events occur 
on a timescale of seconds or less, providing a visual and quantitative demonstration of the inherent 
instability of the E2-E3 complex.
