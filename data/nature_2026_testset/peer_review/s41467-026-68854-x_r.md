Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors present a detailed investigation of untwisted R-stacked WSe
₂
 bilayers and successfully identify the coexistence
of AB and BA ferroelectric domains. The results are scientifically intriguing and contribute meaningfully to the field of
twistronics. However, I believe the current version of the manuscript requires further clarification and supporting data before
it can be considered for publication. My concerns are outlined below: 
1. The reliability of the band alignment analysis critically depends on the quality of the R-stacked bilayer. While the authors
mention the use of the “tear-and-stack” method, the manuscript lacks sufficient characterization data to confirm sample
uniformity. Given the nature of the stacking process, the presence of bubbles or other inhomogeneities is likely. Therefore,
comprehensive data such as AFM topography across the entire sample, PL/Raman mapping, and representative spectra
should be provided to validate the sample quality. 
2. As illustrated in Fig. 1b, domains induced by stacking are expected to exhibit symmetry or regular patterns. However, the
KPFM data shown in Fig. S1 does not clearly demonstrate such regularity. The authors are encouraged to explain this
discrepancy and discuss possible reasons for the observed domain distribution. 
3. It is unclear which specific region of the sample was selected for the measurements presented in Fig. S1. Can identical
optical properties be consistently observed across all domains that exhibit similar contrast in Fig. S1? If so, please provide
supporting evidence. Furthermore, is the data reproducible even within the same domain? Clarification and corresponding
data are requested. Since multiple domains are visible, a more detailed justification for the choice of measurement location
would significantly enhance the credibility of the experimental results. 
4. The abstract mentions “establishing key parameters to understand twisted bilayers and enabling new ferroelectric and
excitonic device opportunities,” and a similar statement appears in the conclusion. To strengthen the impact of the
manuscript, the authors should elaborate on how their findings could be applied in practical device contexts, with specific
examples or mechanisms. 
In summary, while the manuscript focuses on deep scientific insights, additional explanation and supporting data are
necessary to improve its accessibility and impact. In particular, given the experimental complexity, the quality of the sample
plays a crucial role. I strongly recommend the inclusion of more thorough sample characterization to ensure the reliability of
the reported results. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript entitled "Highly tunable band structure in ferroelectric R-stacked bilayer WSe2" by Zhe Li et al. presents
interesting results on the analysis of key parameters affecting the electronic band structure of untwisted R-stacked bilayer
WSe2 using optical measurements. The device scheme involving a dielectric-sandwiched R-stacked bilayer WSe2 to
understand doping via the same polarity gating and ferroelectric built-in potential via opposite polarity gating is a novel
approach to understanding the complex type-II band alignment in R-stacked bilayer WSe2. Their experimental results and
the proposed model for understanding the band-gap difference, interlayer potential, and band offset in R-stacked bilayer
WSe2 are informative and convincing. Thus, I suggest minor revisions before this manuscript can be considered suitable for
publication in Nature Communications.

We thank the reviewer for this insightful question. 
The reviewer is correct that homobilayers with a small twist angle typically exhibit periodic, 
regular reconstruction patterns. However, this behavior changes dramatically as the twist angle 
approaches exactly 0° and the interlayer strain is minimized. In this near-ideal alignment limit, 
the domain size is expected to increase significantly, and the domain structure becomes less 
periodic and more irregular. In the most idealized scenario of a perfectly aligned, strain -free 
bilayer, the entire sample would form a single domain. These large irregular domains are often 
observed in naturally 3R-stacked MoS2 (e.g., Nature 612, 465–469 (2022)). 
In artificially stacked homostructures, some degree of residual strain or a minimal twist angle 
is often unavoidable, which typically results in the formation of periodic reconstruction patterns 
reported in many studies. Therefore, the observation of very large and irregular domains in our 
KPFM data ( new Figure S2) is not a discrepancy, but rather strong evidence for the 
exceptionally high quality of our sample, indicating a near-perfect 0° twist angle and minimal 
strain. 
We also thank the reviewer for pointing out that our original schematic in Fig ure 1b could be 
misleading. We have now revised Figure 1b to depict two larger, more distinct domains, which

We thank the reviewer for the valuable comments. The reviewer correctly notes that the built-
in potential for AB and BA R -stacked domains should theoretically have opposite signs. The 
positive potentials observed in our new Figure S2 are due to a significant systematic offset 
inherent in the KPFM measurement. KPFM accurately measures the relative potential 
difference between the domains, which is sufficient to confirm the existence of two distinct 
electronic states (AB and BA). Howeve r, the absolute potential value is highly susceptible to 
external factors such as stray electric fields from the substrate, ambient contaminants, and the 
precise condition of the tip. In our case, these factors introduced a large positive offset, shifting 
the entire potential map. We chose to present the raw, measured data rather than apply an 
arbitrary zero -point shift to force the values to be opposite.  We have also included th is 
discussion into new Figure S2. 
Regarding the second point, the reviewer is correct that KPFM data alone is insufficient to 
definitively prove the WSe2 bilayer is R-stacked. Our conclusion is based on the combination 
of KPFM data and our fabrication method. The KPFM images, by showing large-scale domains 
(micrometers) rather than a small -period moiré pattern, strongly indicate that the twist angle 
between the layers is very close to 0°. 
This near-zero twist angle is a direct consequence of our "tear-and-stack" fabrication technique 
[Kim, K. et al. Nano Letters 16, 1989–1995 (2016).]. This method involves tearing a single 
WSe2 monolayer flake and then precisely stacking one piece back onto the other. Because both 
layers originate from the exact same parent crystal, their relative orientation can be controlled 
with high precision. By stacking them without intentional rotation, we ensure their crystal axes 
are aligned (parallel), which define s the R -stacking configuration (near 0° twist angle). 
Therefore, the KPFM data and our fabrication method confirms the untwisted R-stacking.

iii) On page 6, the authors suggest a method for measuring the internal potential in R -stacked

Response 10:  
We thank the reviewer for pointing out the lack of clarity in our description. We apologize for 
the confusion and have revised this section in the manuscript (Page 6) to provide a more detailed 
explanation based on the underlying physical mechanism. 
Our method is based on tuning the spatial distribution of the doped Λ valley electrons using the 
external displacement field, D. The key physics is as follows: 
At finite electron doping, the neutral excitons (XL
0 and XH
0 ) interact with the Λ valley electron 
gas. This many-body interaction (e.g., screening) renormalizes the exciton energy, forming the 
attractive polaron (AP) states. This process manifests as an energy redshift ( Eshift= EX - EAP). 
The magnitude of this redshift Eshift is directly related to the strength of the exciton -electron 
interaction, which in turn depends on the spatial overlap between the exciton wavefunction

Based on the established type-II band structure of R-stacked bilayer WSe2, the conduction band

of an electron in the Λ valley and a hole physically localized within the LE layer.

pinpoint the LE layer's physical location. If we apply a D field pointing, for example, from the 
top layer to the bottom layer: 
1. If the top layer is the LE layer: The hole is in the top layer. The applied field will 
increase the hole's potential energy (relative to the electron in the middle). This will 
cause the IX energy to increase (a blueshift). 
2. If the bottom layer is the LE layer: The hole is in the bottom layer. The applied field 
will decrease the hole's potential energy. This will cause the IX energy to decrease (a 
redshift). 
Thus, by observing the sign of the IX Stark shift (a blueshift or redshift) in response to a known 
field direction, one can unambiguously determine whether the top or bottom physical layer is 
the LE layer. 
As shown in new Figure S11, we clearly observe these linear Stark shifts. The sign of this shift 
unambiguously determines whether the top or bottom physical layer is the LE layer within the 
measured ferroelectric domain.   We have added the following discussion into the caption of
