Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In the manuscript, the authors report an isotope-labeled operando NMR methodology for resolving proton-coupled electron
transfer (PCET) pathways in CO2 reduction reactions (CO2RR). The methodology addresses a significant challenge, as the
detection of proton pathways or sources remains difficult due to limitations in analytical techniques for light elements and the
inherent complexity of distinguishing such pathways via conventional 1H NMR spectroscopy. The authors employ dual-
isotope operando NMR (2H/13C) to differentiate between formate species (1H13COO- vs. 2H13COO-). The evidence
presented is robust, and the conclusions are well-supported. The method and findings are promising and worth publishing. I
would like to suggest the following revision comments for the authors to further improve their manuscript. 
1. The authors report that Li-modified BOC (BOC-Li) exhibits enhanced CO2RR performance for formate production.
Concerns regarding the structural stability of BOC-Li itself warrant additional evidence, such as characterization of the
material after CO2RR testing, to substantiate its stability. 
2. The authors state that BOC-Li exhibits stronger CO2 adsorption than BOC, as demonstrated by DFT calculations, and
propose this as one of the reasons for its enhanced CO2RR performance. Considering the significance of this claim, it is
recommended that the authors provide additional experimental evidence to support this conclusion. 
3. The authors demonstrate that BOC-Li significantly enhances CO2RR performance for formate production. To establish the
uniqueness of Li modification, it would be valuable to clarify whether other alkali metals yield similar effects through relevant
comparative experiments. 
4. It would be valuable to explore whether the methodology extends to other liquid products (e.g., C2H5OH, CH3COOH).
Additionally, investigating whether dual-isotope labeling of such C2 products yields insightful results could be considered for
future work. 
5. Several inconsistencies between the manuscript and Supplementary Information require verification. Specifically, the
coupling constant 2J[2H-13C] is reported as 29.87 Hz in the manuscript (Lines 346, 350, 368) but appears as 29.83 Hz in
Supplementary Fig. 10, 11, and 13. Additionally, discrepancies exist in the legend of Supplementary Fig. 10: the figure uses
purple triangles and golden stars to denote 2H13COO- and 1H13COO- respectively, whereas the accompanying legend
describes pink triangles and grey stars. These inconsistencies must be verified and corrected to ensure data accuracy and
documentation consistency. 
6. The manuscript requires verification for minor typographical errors, particularly spacing omissions. Specific examples
include "4f 7/2orbital" (Line 137) and "-0.53ppm" (Line 147), both of which lack required spaces. These errors should be
corrected to maintain formatting consistency. 
7. The authors attribute the facilitated H
₂
O dissociation to the presence of oxygen vacancies introduced by Li doping.
However, have the authors considered whether the Li dopant might also influence H
₂
O dissociation at other surface sites
that are not directly associated with the oxygen vacancy? It would be helpful to clarify whether the observed enhancement is
a localized effect at the vacancy site or if Li doping induces more general changes in surface reactivity.

Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In the present manuscript entitled “Operando NMR Decodes Alkali-Tuned Proton-Electron Relay Boosting CO2-to-Formate
Conversion”, a dual isotope-labeled operando NMR methodology for resolving proton-coupled electron transfer (PCET)
pathways in CO2RR is reported. This technique is reported to enable direct discrimination between formate species
(1H13COO- vs. 2H13COO-) through distinct ¹³C NMR chemical shifts, moving beyond reliance on peak intensity variations.
This investigation holds considerable significance for the CO2RR research community, particularly given the growing
emphasis on elucidating the proton source and transfer mechanism. In light of the demonstrated novelty and substantive
contributions, the manuscript is recommended for publication in Nature Communications after revisions. The detailed
comments are listed as follows: 
1) The enhanced CO2RR performance for formate production using BOC-Li is reported, with *HCOO (Fig. 4f, also denoted
as *OCHO) as the key intermediate. However, independent verification of the operando 1H/17O NMR findings is limited by
the absence of supporting in situ spectroscopic evidence (e.g., FTIR or Raman spectroscopy). Acquisition of such
complementary data is thus recommended to strengthen the mechanistic conclusions. Additionally, consistent use of the
conventional notation “*OCHO” instead of “*HCOO” is suggested. 
2) A dual-isotope operando NMR (2H/13C) methodology is presented, enabling discrimination between 1H13COO- and
2H13COO- species based on distinct 13C NMR chemical shifts. However, observed discrepancies in the chemical shifts of
these formate species across experimental datasets, specifically between Fig. 3e and 3f, as well as Supplementary Figures
10 and 13, require further clarification. A detailed explanation of this variation’s origin is essential for a comprehensive
understanding of the spectral differentiation mechanism. 
3) The stability assessment of BOC-Li (Fig. 2e) during CO2RR requires enhancement. It is recommended to extend
electrolysis duration (>24 hours) with periodic monitoring of formate Faradaic efficiency decay. 
4) Trace-level lithium loading (0.0065 wt%) is documented in Supplementary Table 1 (ICP-MS). However, exceptional
signal-to-noise ratios at this ultralow concentration are exhibited in the 7Li NMR spectra presented in Fig. 1g and
Supplementary Fig. 4. The origin of this enhanced detectability requires elucidation. 
5) In Figure 4f, the reaction free energy profile is presented. However, the manuscript does not provide sufficient information
regarding how entropy contributions were treated in the calculation of the free energy changes. Could the authors clarify the
methodology used to evaluate entropy for both adsorbed and gas-phase species? If any approximations (e.g., harmonic
oscillator model for adsorbates or standard gas-phase entropy values) were applied, please specify and justify their
applicability to this system. 
6) The proposed reaction mechanism involves the dissociation of H2O prior to its reaction with CO2. Have the authors
considered and tested a direct reaction pathway between molecular CO2 and H2O without prior H2O dissociation? If so,
please provide a comparison of the energetics. If not, please clarify why this alternative pathway was not considered and
whether it could be relevant under the studied conditions. 
7) Verification is required for inconsistency between the manuscript and the Supplementary Information. For example, a
discrepancy is noted where the 2J[2H-13C] is stated as 29.87 Hz in Lines 346 and 350 of the manuscript, while being listed
as 29.83 Hz in Supplementary Figs. 10, 11, and 13. 
Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
General Comments: 
This manuscript presents a mechanistic investigation into Li-doped bismuth oxycarbonate catalysts for CO2
electroreduction, highlighting the role of alkali metals in modulating proton-coupled electron transfer (PCET) dynamics. The
authors use dual-isotope (2H/13C) operando NMR and DFT calculations to support their claim of an alkali-tuned PCET
relay that boosts formate production. While the integration of isotope labeling and spectroscopy is interesting and the
catalytic performance is commendable, the central mechanistic claims—especially the synchronized PCET relay—are not
convincingly demonstrated. Several aspects of the experimental design, theoretical support, and data interpretation remain
weak or speculative. Therefore, I recommend reconsideration after major revisions. Detailed critiques are provided below. 
Specific Comments: 
1. The authors introduced trace amounts of Li+ (only 0.0065 wt%) and characterized its presence solely before electrolysis.
However, they did not provide sufficient justification for how such a minimal Li+ concentration—which could potentially leach
out during the reaction—could significantly influence the reaction kinetics. A more plausible explanation, supported by prior
studies (e.g., Nature Catalysis, 1, 111–119, 2018), is that trace dopants like Li+ may modify the intrinsic structure of active
sites rather than directly steering kinetics through bulk-phase interactions. 
2. Central mechanistic claim lacks kinetic validation. The core hypothesis that Li+ facilitates synchronized PCET is not
experimentally proven. There is no kinetic data—such as Tafel slopes, KIE measurements, or EIS—to evaluate whether
proton and electron transfer are indeed coupled or accelerated. Isotope-resolved NMR only shows the presence of HCOO-
species and their isotopic labeling, which does not suffice to establish PCET synchronization. 
3. Excessively strong or misleading terminology. Phrases such as 'decoding the PCET relay', 'synchronized transfer', or

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 3 -->

'mechanistically unlocked' are overstated relative to the evidence provided. These claims should be toned down unless
supported by more rigorous experimental validation. 
4. Dual-isotope NMR lacks direct correlation to PCET dynamics. The use of 2H and 13C labeling confirms the origin of
atoms in formate but does not allow resolution of when or how these species form during PCET. The data shows that Li+
leads to more 2H-HCOO-, but this could arise from surface affinity differences or water structure changes, not necessarily
PCET coupling. 
5. Missing critical control experiments. Key controls are absent—such as isotope labeling on undoped BOC, on Bi powder,
or in the presence of Na+/K+. These comparisons are essential to isolate the unique role of Li+ in the proposed mechanism. 
6. Theoretical model lacks clarity. The manuscript does not specify surface terminations, defect models, solvation or field
effects in DFT simulations. These omissions raise questions about how relevant the computed energetics are to real
electrochemical interfaces. 
7. Figure presentation and experimental detail need improvement. Some spectra lack sufficient resolution and annotation
(especially in the NMR section). Experimental conditions for NMR—such as method, electrolyte volume, or operating test
setup configuration—are not described in enough detail to ensure reproducibility. 
8. The reported stability of the system falls significantly short compared to similar studies in literature (e.g., Nature
Communications, 16, 1754 (2025); Nature, 626, 86-91 (2024)). Could the authors improve the current long-term stability
performance? 
9

 The mechanistic insights of CO2 reduction to formate presented in the manuscript are primarily supported by operando
NMR analyses in an H-cell-like setup. However, the electrochemical experiments were conducted in a flow-cell
configuration. These two systems differ significantly in terms of mass transport, catalytic site evolution, and interfacial
dynamics, which may influence reaction pathways and intermediate behavior. Therefore, it is not sufficiently rigorous to
extrapolate mechanistic conclusions from the NMR setup to the flow-cell environment. The authors are encouraged to justify
the relevance of the operando NMR experiments to the actual reaction conditions and to provide experimental evidence
validating the applicability of these mechanistic observations to the flow-cell system. 
Reviewer #4 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This study systematically elucidates how trace Li incorporation into the BOC structure modulates the electronic configuration
and reconstructs the interfacial reaction kinetics, thereby demonstrating that Li doping can effectively enhance both the
selectivity and efficiency of CO2-to-formate conversion. A high formate Faradaic efficiency (FEhcoo-) of up to 90% was
achieved at a current density of 1.5 A cm-2 accompanied by excellent operational stability. Moreover, the application of dual-
isotope operando nuclear magnetic resonance (2H/13C NMR) enables real-time tracking of proton sources and transfer
pathways during CO2 electroreduction, offering strong mechanistic insights into the proton-coupled electron transfer (PCET)
process in CO2RR. Nevertheless, the catalyst assessed in the article was only evaluated over a fleeting time scale of 8.5
hours, lacking long-term stability data and post-structural analysis, which is insufficient to support its claim of industrial
applicability. So, I regret to give my opinion that this paper is not suitable for publication in Nature Communications. The
following comments should be considered before submitting it to another journal. 
1. The paper points out that the Li content is only 0.0065 wt%, which has a significant impact on performance. However,
currently, only XPS and ⁷Li ssNMR provide indirect structural information (Fig. 1f–g). It is recommended to supplement with
synchrotron radiation XAS to further verify the Li doping sites and bonding environment. 
2. Although it is pointed out that Li3c has the optimal water dissociation activation energy (0.24 eV), its electronic structure
origin (such as PDOS or charge density difference map) is not explained in depth. It is recommended to supplement the
analysis with electronic state density (DOS/PDOS) to intuitively show the effect of Li regulation on the electronic structure. 
3. The study only used undoped BOC as the control group and lacked a lateral comparison with other alkali metal (such as
K
⁺
, Na
⁺
) doping systems. It is recommended to add comparisons with other alkali metal-regulated PCET systems to
enhance the persuasiveness of Li's advantages. 
4. Stability was only evaluated for 8.5 hours at a current density of 200 mA cm-2, which is insufficient to fully demonstrate its
industrial applicability. It is recommended to provide structural comparison data of the catalyst before and after deactivation
(e.g., SEM/XRD/XPS). 
5. Figure 3e introduces multiple spectra to analyze different proton sources, but the main text is too long and dense with
terminology. It is recommended to add a section titled “Key Information Summary” in the figure caption or main text.

Response: 
We sincerely thank the reviewer for their positive and constructive feedback on 
our revised manuscript. We are particularly grateful for the opportunity to further clarify 
the assignment of the operando infrared peaks, which has prompted us to perform 
additional control experiments and refine our interpretation. 
Regarding the possibility that the bands attributed to *OCHO might originate from 
interfacial bicarbonate or carbonate species, we conducted reference measurements 
using standard solutions of K 2CO3 and KHCO 3 in both H 2O and D 2O. These ATR-
SEIRAS measurements, as shown in  Fig. R1 and Fig. R2, indicate that bicarbonate 
(HCO3-) in H 2O exhibits characteristic bands at ~1358 cm -1, while carbonate (CO 32-) 
appears near ~1390 cm -1. The peaks we observed at 1438 cm -1 and 1600 cm -1 on the 
BOC-Li electrode under operando conditions are distinct from these reference bands 
and are consistent with reported vibrational modes for adsorbed *OCHO intermediates. 
Furthermore, the potential -dependent intensification of these bands specifically on 
BOC-Li, which correlates with enhanced formate production, supports their assignment 
as a reaction intermediate.  
Concerning the question of a Stark shift for the *OCHO species, we note that a 
pronounced potential -dependent peak shift is not uncommon for certain adsorbed 
intermediates like CO, but appears to be less characteristic for *OCHO in the formate

pathway. A survey  of literature on operando ATR -SEIRAS studies of formate -
producing catalysts, including Bi 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 , In7, 8, 9 , Sn10, 11, 12 , and Pb 13, 14 -based 
materials, frequently reports potential -dependent band intensification as the primary 
signature of *OCHO, without emphasizing a Stark shift. This observed behavior may 
be attributed to the adsorption configuration of *OCHO, which is often proposed to be 
bidentate, potentially resulting in a smaller change in the dynamic dipole moment 
normal to the electrode surface compared to linearly-adsorbed species. Thus, the strong 
potential-dependent intensity increase of the bands at 1438 and 1600 cm -1 on BOC-Li 
is considered a reliable indicator of the adsorbed intermediate in this context. 
Regarding the feature around 1660 cm -1, we agree with the reviewer that this 
region is typically associated with the O -H bending mode of water. Based on our 
reference spectra in H 2O, we have reassigned this feature to the δ (H2O) mode at 
approximately 1638 cm -1. This band is th erefore not associated with *OCHO or 
carbonate species.
