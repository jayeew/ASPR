Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this study, the authors report a N-Ni/NiCo@C catalyst for highly efficient and stable photo-thermal methane dry reforming,
which is distinct from the conventional metal oxide-supported catalysts. The structure-activity relationship investigation
reveals the synergistic effect among the precise catalyst structure, involved carbon layer encapsulation, C-N-Ni electron
transfer channels, and highly dispersed nano Ni/Co alloy particles, achieving high light utilization efficiency, performance,
and stability. Furthermore, in situ characterization reveals a CH4 activation pathway that is free of lattice oxygen. The
substantial O* released from CO2 is adsorbed on oxophilic NiCo alloy, and then effectively oxidizes the carbon species
(CH*) into CHO*, with the assistance of photo-generated holes. The findings in this work are interesting, offering a novel
strategy for producing long-term stable NiCo-based catalysts for photo-thermal methane dry reforming. Minor revision is
recommended, and the following comments and suggestions should be addressed. 
1. H2 atmosphere is a typical atmosphere for preparing metallic state catalysts. Why does this paper use the inert gas Ar?
Does the gas composition affect the metal dispersion? 
2. Given that the performance evaluation conditions for the photo-thermal DRM reaction vary in different literature and the
data on light utilization efficiency is often missing, how can the comparability of the data in this work (LTCEE) and those in
the literature be ensured? 
3. Using XRD, Raman, and XPS to observe the carbon deposits may not be precise enough. Employing TGA or TG-MS for
carbon deposit quantification is strongly recommended. 
4. Ni/Co catalysts supported on common metal oxides tend to agglomerate easily during DRM reactions. Imaging
technology is recommended to calculate the particle size of spent catalysts for further verification of sintering resistance. 
5. How was the existence of C-N-Ni sites determined? Is it possible that the NiN phase was formed and contributed to the
DRM reaction? 
6. The catalytic performance was measured at 600 °C in this manuscript, and it was proved that the carbon in the material
would not be oxidized to CO. However, traditional dry reforming of methane requires a temperature of 700-800 °C. What is
the maximum temperature at which this type of C-coated catalyst can be used?

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, a photothermal DRM reaction on a nitrogen-doped carbon-coated N-Ni/NiCo@C catalyst was reported.
Different from the traditional oxygen involved semiconductor-based catalyst, this material structure for solar DRM would be
new and the performance is good. Mechanism investigation and explanation would also interest a wide readship compared
to the conventional ‘lattice oxygen’ involving Mars-van Krevelen pathway. In addition, the experiments were systematically
and well conducted in most parts. A Minor Revision would be recommended and following comments and suggestions
should be properly addressed and corrected. 
(1) The manuscript attributes the remarkable 200-h stability primarily to the electronic effect of the C–N–Ni site, which is
proposed to suppress oxidation. This interpretation overlooks a more obvious factor: the physical confinement and
encapsulation provided by the graphitic carbon layer. Although the authors report a drastic stability difference between N-
Ni/NiCo@C and the NiCo/Al2O3 reference, these systems differ in at least two major respects: (i) the presence of the
proposed C–N–Ni site and (ii) the carbon surface itself. Hence, the enhanced durability cannot be unambiguously assigned
to the C–N–Ni site without decoupling these effects. 
(2) ICP–OES indicates severe Ni loss upon increasing N-doping (28.3 to 3.1 wt%). The authors should clarify the origin of
this depletion and reconcile it with the proposed stabilization mechanism. 
(3) The mapping shows clear O signals (Figure 1e), which conflicts with the “lattice-oxygen-free” claim as presently phrased.
Please (i) define the scope of “lattice-oxygen-free” (bulk vs. surface) and (ii) pinpoint the oxygen’s location—carbon-support
functionalities vs. an alloy oxide overlayer—to avoid a potentially misleading title. 
(4) The authors state that “the Ni 2p spectra show the Ni2+ peak (856.2 eV) shifts by +0.77 eV from N0 to N1.” However, the
plotted positions indicate a −0.77 eV shift (i.e., to lower binding energy) from N0 to N1. Please double check the sign and
report the absolute peak positions for both samples.

1. H2 atmosphere is a typical atmosphere for preparing metallic state catalysts. Why does this

Author reply: Thank you very much for your positive comments on our manuscript. 
We optimized the preparation conditions of the catalyst and found that the performance

prepared under an Ar atmosphere, as displayed in the following Fig. 1a. Therefore, Ar 
was selected as the optimal pyrolysis atmosphere. SEM and TEM observations (Fig. 
1b-d) revealed that the sample pyrolyzed in H2 featured a thicker carbon layer coating 
and blurry lattice. In contrast, the sample prepared via Ar pyrolysis exhibited more 
uniformly dispersed metal particles (Manuscript-Fig.1a-c), which is more conducive to

2. Given that the performance evaluation conditions for the photo- thermal DRM reaction 
vary in different literature and the data on light utilization efficiency is often missing, how can 
the comparability of the data in this work (LTCEE) and those in the literature be ensured? 
Author reply: We sincerely appreciate your consideration. Given the inconsistencies 
in temperature and light intensity used for performance evaluation in the literature, we 
recalculated the LTCEE values from all collected relevant studies to enhance 
comparability. In the calculation process, the thermal catalytic  reaction rate was 
deducted to compare the light  efficiency, and the ratio of LTCEE to light intensity 
(LTCEE/Light intensity) was adopted to evaluate the light utilization efficiency per unit 
light intensity.

The NETZSCH TG 209 F1 Libra TGA was utilized to determine the amount of carbon 
deposited on the DRM spent catalyst samples via temperature  programmed oxidation. 
The spent catalyst was heated at a temperature between 25 ℃ and 800 ℃ at a heating 
rate of 10 ℃/min with an air flow of 50 mL/min. The rate of weight change with

5. How was the existence of C-N-Ni sites determined? Is it possible that the NiN phase was 
formed and contributed to the DRM reaction? 
Author reply: The formation of C-N-Ni sites was confirmed by two lines of evidence. 
First, a characteristic peak of the N -C bond was observed in the N 1s XPS spectrum, 
and the signal of the N -Ni bond was detected via XAS measurements (Fig. 3 in the 
manuscript). Its N coordination environment was highly consistent with that of the 
nickel phthalocyanine standard sample, indicating that the sites possess a C -N-Ni 
structure. Second, supplementary confirmation was obtained through a control 
experiment. When only N -containing ligands (without oxygen- containing ligands) 
were added during catalyst preparation, no precipitation of metal crystals was observed.

Author reply: We sincerely appreciate your positive feedback on our manuscript. To

N ligands preferentially coordinate with Ni
2+ to form O -Ni-N type coordination 
precipitates. However, excess N ligands will form soluble Ni -N coordination species, 
which cannot be retained during subsequent separation processes and ultimately result 
in a reduction in the Ni loading of the catalyst. Benefiting from the strong selective 
coordination of rigid phenanthroline with Ni
2+, the as -prepared N -doped NiCo 
bimetallic catalyst exhibits excellent pyrolysis resistance.
