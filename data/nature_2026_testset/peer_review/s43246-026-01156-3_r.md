Reviewer #3 (Remarks to the Author): 
In this work, Komal and coworkers reported the synthesis of a dense, polycrystalline MIL-116(Ga) membrane grown on an
alumina substrate for He/CH4 separation. Through pure and mixed gas separation experiments, the authors demonstrated
the ideal He/CH4 selectivity of 78 and real He/CH4 selectivity (4:96 mixture) of 31 at a permeance of 2263 Barrer, the best
performing MOF membrane reported for He upgrading. The authors attribute this phenomenon to the molecular sieving effect
of MIL-116(Ga). 
The structural characterization of the membrane is thorough. The gas kinetic diameter-permeance relationship also clearly
shows a molecular sieving effect at play, although not as pronounced as expected. 
The major shortcomings of this work is
the lack of composition and pore characterization of MIL-116(Ga) after thermal activation (detailed below). Overall, this work
demonstrated enough novelty that will appeal to the audiences of Communications Materials. After addressing the following
questions, I believe the work is suitable for publication. 
• Granted that the previous work reported no N2, CO2 and CH4 adsorption in MIL-116(Ga), it is still critical to analyze the
composition of activated MIL-116(Ga) to quantify residual water molecules in the native pores of the MOF. Such
characterizations include TGA and elemental analysis. Also, water vapor adsorption isotherm will be a valuable addition to
the materials characterization to evaluate the overall accessible pore volume of the material (water has a smaller kinetic
diameter than both He and H2). 
• It seems that all the gas transport data are based on one sample. It is important to perform the same separation test on at
least three membrane samples to get an error bar for pure gas and mixed gas permeance and selectivity. 
• It is rather surprising that real He/CH4 (50:50) selectivity is much higher than ideal selectivity but real He/CH4 (4:96)
selectivity is substantially lower. For the first part, the authors attributed it to the higher surface barrier necessary for the
diffusion of adsorbing gases through MIL-116(Ga). However, the difference of energy barriers for the diffusion of different
gases 
is already reflected in the ideal separation factor. It cannot be used to explain the sudden increase of separation factor
for the mixed gas separation. For the second part, the authors attributed this phenomenon to the lower chemical potential of
He-diffusion. However, the equation for calculating separation factors has already taken into consideration of the difference
in chemical potential. I thus found these explanations not convincing. 
• It will be valuable to include a discussion on why H2 has a much higher permeance than He despite its larger kinetic
diameter. 
Reviewer #4 (Remarks to the Author): 
This manuscript presents an investigation of polycrystalline MIL-116(Ga) membranes for helium upgrading applications,
encompassing membrane synthesis, microstructure characterization, and helium separation performance. The authors
developed an effective method for fabricating dense, intergrown MOF membranes and conducted an analysis of their growth
mechanism and grain boundary structure. Through single- and mixed-gas permeability tests, they examined the underlying
factors contributing to performance variations among different gases. The study further benchmarks these results against
existing membrane materials to demonstrate the superior potential of MIL-116(Ga) for efficient helium separation, as
evidenced by its exceptional selectivity and permeance. The manuscript is lack of new science, i.e., mechanism. The
manuscript would benefit from including: theoretical calculations of gas diffusion pathways, molecular dynamics simulations
of gas-MOF interactions, and quantitative structure-property relationship analysis to strengthen the mechanistic
interpretation. To further improve the quality of the manuscript, some efforts should be done according to the following
comments/suggestions: 
1.The manuscript is lack of new science, i.e., mechanism. The manuscript would benefit from including: theoretical
calculations of gas diffusion pathways, molecular dynamics simulations of gas-MOF interactions, and quantitative structure-
property relationship analysis to strengthen the mechanistic interpretation. 
2.The authors should offer a Graphical abstract to show the key significance/concept/novelty of this work. 
3.In Introduction section, the authors should focus more on introducing MOFs and MIL-116 (Ga), explaining the existing
applications and challenges, highlighting the unique novelty of MIL-116 (Ga) for helium separation, and further explaining
the key gaps in existing technologies and how this work addresses these gaps. 
4.The membrane fabrication process should be described in more details, particularly regarding: reproducibility across
multiple batches, tolerance to minor synthesis parameter variations, and potential for large-area membrane production using
the developed methodology. 
5.The discussion of grain boundary effects is insufficient. A dedicated analysis should address: (1) defect impacts on
performance, (2) fabrication scalability, and (3) operational stability to better evaluate practical limitations.

3. The water molecule blocking effect in channels (Page 5 description) also warrants experimental validation.

performance degradation trends under continuous operation conditions. 
8.When comparing other materials with MIL-116 (Ga) in this article (including the data in Table S5), the comparison should
be extended to the cost analysis of membrane synthesis, stability data, and energy consumption comparison with low-
temperature distillation. 
9.The authors should strengthen the critical discussion by incorporating practical implementation challenges (module
design, high-pressure sealing, contaminant tolerance), environmental/economic assessments (carbon footprint, cost

The schematic diagram illustrates a proposed growth mechanism for polycrystalline MIL-116(Ga) 
film, based on SEM images. Crystallization theory as described by Ostwald and Miers always starts 
with spherical crystals, as in  an early stage of a seed no morphology is visible. The seed in our 
scheme is obviously a bit larger than it would be in reality. There are multiple theories that deal with 
1 or 2 step crystallization (see: 10.1021/ar800217x) before the ripening step. Crystallization is 
usually a process starting with the formation of a seed layer, continuing in a second step with growths 
(in our case  needle-like crystals) which continue growth and eventually start Ostwald ripening, 
forming larger crystals in another crystallization kinetic step. In our case, after 8 h synthesis time, 
we are still in the growth regime; after 24 h (as described above) we are in the crystal ripening stage. 
This behavior – and that it also describes MOF crystallization – has been demonstrated in literature 
several times, for instance by Cravillon in 2011 (10.1021/cm103571y) for the beautiful model system 
of ZIF-8. They also show that morphology changes during crystallization and growth. Other groups, 
for example Agrawal, reviewed the crystallization in thin films: 10.1021/acssuschemeng.8b05409 
Our diagram and sentence are based on knowledge on crystallization from a seed through growth 
into a polycrystalline film with crystalline domains and grain boundaries which can only be 
described by their morphology. We do not mean to dive too deep into crystallization kinetics, but 
we agree that MIL -116(Ga) is probably an interesting system. We will investigate detailed  
crystallization kinetics for this MOF in the near future.
