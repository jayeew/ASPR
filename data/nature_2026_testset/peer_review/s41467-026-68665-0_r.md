Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors proposed a universal strategy to construct a low-resistance oxide-based protection layer for practical Si-based
hierarchical photocathode by employing multiple carrier-tunnelling paths. This approach allows the protection layer to
realize the facile and low-loss photogenerated carrier transport, while providing a sufficient thickness for maintaining the
required durability. The authors further demonstrate the generality and feasibility of this strategy across various oxide-metal
systems. However, the manuscript lacks a comprehensive characterization of the stability for this multilayer film in
photoelectrocatalytic operation. Before the manuscript can be considered for publication, the authors are recommended to
address the following concerns: 
1. Comment: The core objective of this work is to address the corrosion issue in PEC catalysis through the design of a
surface protection layer. Therefore, stability is a crucial metric for validating the effectiveness of this approach. However, the
main text primarily presents catalytic performance data, lacking characterization of catalytic stability. We recommend
incorporating data on long-term stability into the main text. 
2. Comment: Figures S16 and S24 reveal a significant initial current decay within the first hour of operation. While the
authors attribute this phenomenon to three potential factors (line 282), to enhance the manuscript's rigor, we recommend that
targeted experiments be designed to quantify the relative contribution of each factor to the observed stability degradation. 
3. Comment: While Ti dissolution concentration was measured via ICP-OES in this study to infer potential stability, changes
in elemental content alone cannot definitively indicate stability performance. Structural evolution of the protection layer after
long-term operation—such as interfacial delamination or crystallinity changes in the oxide—provides direct insights into
surface structural stability. We contend that HRTEM-EDS mapping analysis of post-stability-test samples should be
supplemented to validate the stability mechanism of the multilayer architecture. 
4. Comment: Research on the use of Cu-based co-catalyst as the active site for the reduction of NO3- through
photoelectrocatalysis has also been reported recently (Energy & Environmental Science, 17(23), 9233-9243). It would be
informative to discuss such on role of co-catalyst in this system. 
5. Comment: Why is there such a large difference in ECSA among C(TF)nCSi (n = 1, 2, 4, 6, and 8) samples with similar
thicknesses, as shown in Figure S15? 
6. Comment: The article contains some formatting errors, such as the references [24, 
25], [43, 
44], [45, 
46], which should be
[24,25] [43,44], [45,46]. 
7. Comment: From the EIS plots in Figure 3d, it is evident that the shapes of the impedance spectra for C(TF)nCSi (n = 1, 2,
4, 6, and 8) differ, suggesting that their reaction mechanisms may vary. The authors should provide a more thorough
analysis and fitting of the impedance data, rather than merely referring to it briefly. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This paper demonstrates an interesting and feasible design of oxide-based protection layer for Si-based hierarchical
photocathode with high efficiency and good durability by multiple carrier-tunnelling paths. The approach is suitable for
various oxide-based layers, e.g., (TiO2/Fe)n and (CeO2/Pd)n. The design and results of this work are significant in the
photoelectrochemical field and will have a broad interest for the readers of the journal. I suggest to accept this manuscript

after addressing the following issues: 
1. A significant advance is the design of oxide-based protection layer with the introduction of thin metal unit layer (such as
Fe and Pd) to create the independent electron tunnelling path in the thin oxide unit layer. However, the metal layer is also
non-transparent, which could reduce the light harvesting of Si-based photocathode. The total thickness of the metal layer
used in this work is 18 nm, which is thick enough for affecting the light transmission. Could the authors provide a detailed
information about the influence of the thickness of metal unit layer on light harvesting? 
2. The conductive nature of metal unit layer will allow electrons access to the oxide unit layer, which are very important to
photoelectrochemical reduction reactions. Is it possible to give some discussion on the general rules needed to optimize the
thickness of metal unit layer by balancing the conductive and non-transparent nature of metal materials? 
3. The photocurrent density of >16 mA cm-2 and NH3 yield rate of >0.5 mg h-1 cm-2 for photoelectrochemical NO3- to NH3
at 0 V vs. RHE is very impressive, which arises mainly from the design of oxide-based protection layer and the presence of
multiple carrier-tunnelling paths. How about the HER performance of the Si-based photocathodes with such oxide-based
protection layer? The results might confirm the universality and feasibility of this approach. 
4. The authors used indophenol blue method to analyze and quantify the NH3 yield rate from photoelectrochemical NO3-
reduction reaction, the detection approach of NH3 might not be enough. To obtain more accurate results, other analytical
methods (such as NMR) should be used to evaluate the NH3 production. 
5. It also might be significant to show some composition and structure characterizations about (CeO2/Fe)n, (TiO2/Pd)n and
(CeO2/Pd)n, like XRD, FESEM, and optical measurements. 
Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript introduces a multilayer oxide/metal protection layer architecture designed to simultaneously optimize carrier
transport and corrosion resistance in PEC devices. By employing TiO_2/Fe (TF)_n, CeO_2/Fe (CF)_n, TiO_2/Pd (TP)_n,
and CeO_2/Pd (CP)_n stacks, the authors aim to create multiple carrier-tunnelling paths, thereby breaking the trade-off
between efficiency and durability. The study combines finite element simulations, systematic PEC tests, electrical
measurements, and structural characterization to demonstrate that (TF)_6 exhibits optimal performance, achieving high
photocurrent density and long-term stability. The work also extends this design to other oxide/metal combinations, claiming a
universal applicability. However, several key aspects of the mechanism are insufficiently supported, and the performance
benchmarking and physical interpretation of photoelectrochemical behavior require further clarification and experimental
supports. After revision and additional data as outlined below, the manuscript would be significantly strengthened. 
1. The manuscript attributes the improved charge transport to the presence of multiple carrier-tunneling pathways across
oxide/metal junctions. However, no direct experimental evidence for tunneling is provided such as temperature dependent
carrier injection/transport. The claim rests solely on WKB-based simulations. Moreover, detailed discussion on the tunneling
barrier height and effective mass assumptions used in simulations are needed. 
2. The claim that interfaces remain ohmic or low-barrier is central to the carrier transport mechanism. The manuscript would
benefit from direct characterization of energy level alignment via a more direct measurements such as Kelvin probe or UPS.
A schematic energy band diagram of the full multilayer stack, with alignment at each interface, would be helpful. 
3. The IMPS and EIS data are promising but are only qualitatively discussed. Please include quantitative fitting and transfer
time constants across samples to better support claims of faster interfacial kinetics. 
4. Although the oxide layers are thin, the inclusion of Fe or Pd layers may introduce parasitic optical losses. Please include
discussion of light absorption and reflection in the multilayer. 
5. In NO_3RR, species and yield of other by-products would also be discussed. 
6. The long-term stability claim is extrapolated over 12 h. While the method is valid, it would greatly strengthen the
manuscript to include longer durability tests. 
7. The main text lacks discussion comparing the PEC performance against recent state-of-the-art nitrate-to-ammonia PEC
systems 
8. Please clarify if any oxidation of the Fe interlayer occurred during or after deposition. This is especially important as it may
change the electrical and protective properties. 
9. Provide device-to-device performance variation or error bars for photocurrent and Faradaic efficiency to demonstrate
reproducibility.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The rebuttal further strengthens durability evidence by including additional data. However, there remain concerns regarding
the convincing demonstration of long-term protection, insufficient in-depth analysis of interface evolution at later hours,
limited mechanistic insight into electroreduction pathways and co-catalyst role, and marginal improvement in stability versus
state-of-the-art protected Si photocathodes. While the results are promising, major clarifications are necessary to justify
acceptance in Nature Communications. 
1. Although durability tests and ICP-OES-based dissolution analysis were added, the protective layer still loses total
thickness and co-catalyst fully disappears at longer periods. This indicates progressive degradation. Clarify whether the
observed lifetime is compatible with practical PEC system expectations. Provide contextual benchmarking vs state-of-art Si
protection layers. 
2. Modeling and calculation results support the concept, but experimental validation is not shown. Provide band-alignment
data to confirm ohmic vs Schottky interface behavior and tunneling barrier thickness evolution. 
3. Catalyst layer dissolves before the oxide shows visible damage, which introduces transient surface states and structural
changes. Catalyst degradation complicates interpretation of protection role. 
4. The rebuttal repeats data but still does not quantify contributions of bubble coverage, catalyst restructuring, and tunneling
interface modification. 
5. Since the manuscript positions the strategy as universal, other PEC demonstration (HER, CO2RR, etc) in an acidic or
neutral electrolyte would be highly valuable. 
6. Fe multilayer sputtering at 3 nm precision across the large scale surface is non-trivial. Further discussions are required. 
7. Authors need to provide statistics for stability tests.

the relatively stable operation for NO3RR. As shown in Figure S31, there was a nearly

to the relatively smooth current region in the J-t curves. As the reaction time was 
extended over 20 h, a drastic fluctuation in photocurrent density was obse rved on the 
C(TF)6CSi photocathode (see Figure S30c ). This phenomenon is attributed to the 
adsorption and desorption of the  bubbles on the photocathode surface via HER, 
reflecting the change of surface composition based on the reaction transformation. To 
further determine the composition and structure evolution in the PEC process, the post-
electrolysis C(TF)6CSi photocathodes at different reaction times were characterized by

Results and Discussion:  “(O/M)n protection layer/Si -based photocathodes (such as 
(TF)6CSi) were fabricated and used to direct estimate the lifetime of protection layer 
and avoid the interference of FeCu alloy layer. To quantify the loss of (TF)6 protection 
layer, the final Ti content in the electrolyte (1.0 M KOH-0.05 M KNO3) after 12-h PEC 
reaction was evaluated by inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectrometer 
(ICP-OES). The results revealed the dissolution of 23 ng  of Ti from the (TF)6CSi 
photocathode with a geometric area of ~0.22 cm-2 (Figure 3g and Table S4). Assuming 
that the photocathode surface is flat, with a mass loss of ~49-ng Ti per cm2 per day, 2.2-
nm TiO2 unit could be lost every day via operating continuously, meaning that the 
lifetime of (TF)6 protection layer is expected to be 196 h (see Figure 3g). Indeed, the 
actual surface area of (TF)6CSi photocathode with pyramidal structure is considerably 
larger than the geometric area (Figure S33a). Therefore, the actual loss of (TF)6 is much 
lower than the calculated value, namely, the actual lifetime is much longer than the 
expected value. In addition, according to the J-t measurements and corrosion rate 
calculations, the estimated lifetimes of Si -based photocathodes with different (O/M) n 
protection layers are over 100 h, as shown in Figure 3g. These results highlight that the 
low-resistance (O/M) n protection layer with efficient carrier transfer still maintains 
excellent durability with long lifetime, realizing the synchronized enhancement of 
conduction and protection functions.”

Q4) The rebuttal repeats data but still does not quantify contributions of bubble

Response: 
Thanks a lot for your intense interest on the durability of protection layer. 
According to the previous works (e.g., Electrochim. Acta 2025, 512, 145475; Chem. 
Eng. J. 2025, 512, 162513; J. Phys. Chem. C  2025, 129, 4383-4397), the presence of 
bubbles mainly influences the photocurrent of the photoelectrode in the PEC reactions 
(such as HER), including photocurrent fluctuation and the slight loss of light harvesting 
(~2% reported by Int. J. Hydrog. Energy  2019, 44, 10286 -10300). However, in our 
work, the low-resistance behaviors of (O/M)n protection layer were evaluated in PEC 
NO3RR with NH3 as main product and no bubble coverage. On the other hand,  in the 
long-term PEC measurements, H 2 bubbles were produced and covered on the surface 
of protection layer leading to the photocurrent fluctuation due to the failure of the FeCu 
alloy cocatalyst layer, as shown in Figure S30c.  The adsorption and desorption of the 
bubbles may slightly increase the mass transfer leading to the slight increase of 
corrosion rate for protection layer. But, in the PEC measurements, a high mass transfer 
has been carried out by magnetic stirring. Therefore, the effect of bubble coverage on 
the efficiency and durability assessments of (O/M)n protection layer can be neglected. 
On basis of systematic PEC measurements and composition/structure characterizations, 
the restructuring of FeCu cocatalyst layer is difficult to occur on the surface of the 
photocathode in this work. Because the corrosion/dissolution rate of FeCu is faster than 
its restructuring rate during PEC reaction under the strong alkaline conditions. The low

restructuring rate of cocatalyst layer is ascribed  to the low concentration of FeCu ions 
in the electrolytes. Assuming that the FeCu cocatalyst layer is completely dissolved into 
the electrolytes, the maximum value of the ion concentration (0.16 × 10-6 g/mL) can be 
roughly calculated by the volume of cocatalyst layer (layer thickness ( ~28 nm) × 
exposed C(TF)6CSi photocathode area (~0.22 cm2)) multiplying FeCu alloy density (8.5

for low-resistance behaviors. On the contrary, the dissolution of protection layer can
