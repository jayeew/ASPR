Reviewers' comments:
 
Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
In this work the Authors provide a temperature study of TADF OLEDs, and report significant performance increases at higher
temperatures. 
While I do not think this work will ultimately find direct applications, it is still a very interesting basic-science idea they have
investigated, and one that should be published. Overall the work is performed and presented well, however there are some
additional experiments the authors should perform or confirm to help build confidence in the temperature effects they
describe. 
Firstly, additional control devices are needed. The chosen exciplex host is itself likely TADF active, so these devices temp
variations cannot be fully attributed to the Y or G dopants without some additional devices. Either measurement of the non-
doped exciplex, or one doped with a purely fluorescent dopant should be checked for similar changes in EL and EQE with
temperature. Or, these Y/G TADF dopants should be checked similarly in 'normal' hosts like DPEPO or CBP that have no
parallel triplet harvesting activity. These will help confirm or disprove that the temperature effects are due to the dopant
TADF. 
Secondly, OLEDs are notoriously unreliable in aging processes. How did the Authors take this into account? Were separate
devices used for each voltage, held steady across the temperatures? Were the devices allowed to reach steadystate internal
temperatures by running them for a few minutes before collecting measurements? (as the Authors note, significant internal
heat can be generated). 
I suspect the best way to confirm that this is a real temperature effect and not a concurrent ageing effect is to re-run some of
these experiments, measuring the devices at randomised voltages (rather than in increasing order) and/or non-sequential
temperatures to make sure the trends reported are not actually a result of device ageing. Aging would be expected to be
most impactful at high currents and high temperatures, so this is very tricky to disentangle from the measurements of interest
here, but nonetheless is crucial for the main points of the work. This is especially important if it was the same device run
multiple times at different voltages and different temperatures, for which significant aging might have taken place over a long
measurement run. 
As a minor point, what are the EST values of the dopants themselves? A lot of activation energies are calculated, but it
would be useful to know the singlet and triplet energies of these dopants so that the changes in temperature (and thermal
energy) can be compared. 
Also, is any temperature decrease observed on the thermocouples when the device near the turnon voltage is initially turned
on? 
Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author): 
In this paper, the authors investigated the temperature dependence of OLED performance based on thermally activated
delayed fluorescence (TADF) emitters. They compared current, electroluminescence (EL) intensity, and external quantum
efficiency (EQE) at room temperature and elevated temperatures under the same bias voltages. 
Conversion of heat to visible light is a well-known feature of TADF, where lower-energy triplet states convert to emissive
singlet states via thermal activation. The operation of OLEDs at elevated temperatures to observe TADF effects has already
been demonstrated since the early development of TADF OLEDs (Adv. Mater., 21, 4802 (2009)). Nowadays, many efficient
TADF OLEDs with small singlet–triplet energy gaps (ΔE_ST) have been developed (Nature, 492, 234 (2012)), so
performance enhancement due to temperature is generally expected to be limited at room temperature. 
Nevertheless, the authors report interesting results — a very high enhancement in EQE (up to 7-fold). This is surprising for
their TADF system that already exhibits reasonably high EQE (>10%). From a quantum efficiency standpoint, such an
enhancement is unexpectedly large. 
Therefore, before recommending the publication of this article in the current form, I would like to suggest the authors confirm
their observations and provide a more robust explanation for such a significant efficiency enhancement as below:

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 3 -->

1) Can the reported 7-fold EQE enhancement be reproduced across multiple samples? Also, can you check the
enhancement be not due to irreversible thermal effects (e.g., changes in morphology, removal of traps, or defect
passivation)? 
I noted that the current range in the inset of Fig. 5(a) is smaller than in Fig. 3(d), possibly suggesting the presence of leakage
currents due to defective samples. 
2) Is it possible to measure emission spectra near the turn-on voltage? The emission intensity may be too low for a
spectrometer. Can you please provide raw EL spectra in this low-bias region? 
3) About the explanation of the enhancement, recent studies (e.g., Sci. Adv. 6, eabb2659 (2020)) have discussed exciton
quenching due to charge accumulation before device turn-on due to the spontaneous orientation polarization. If such
quenching is significant at room temperature, it is plausible that elevated temperatures accelerate RISC and suppress this
quenching leading to the enhancement of EQE. 
To support this hypothesis, I recommend to present EQE–J curves down to very low current densities where the
enhancement in EL is observed. Absolute EQE values are not essential, but a qualitative trend would be informative. 
Below are small things I recommend considering improving the articles. 
4) Page 2, line 70: Please add citations to prior works you found for the reader’s reference. 
5) Page 5, line 127: Did you use a 2.5 cm² OLED for all measurements, including those in Fig. 3(d)? The current density
appears too high for the OLED size. 
6) Page 7, Figure 3, axis label formats in panels c–f are inconsistent with the rest of the manuscript. Please revise for
consistency. 
7) Page 7, Figure 3 in panels d and f, the meaning of the symbols is unclear. Add arrows or improve the legend for clarity. 
8) Page 9, line 224: The text refers to the relationship between singlet and triplet populations being described by a
Boltzmann distribution. However, this should instead relate to the rate constant of the RISC process rather than the
population ratio. Please check this again. 
9) Page 9, Figure 4(a–b): Can you provide EL spectra up to 50 °C? 
10) Page 11, line 276 / Equation (2): The defined “dimensionless sensitivity” does not appear dimensionless based on the
form of Equation (2). Please check and revise accordingly. 
11) Page 17, Equation (6): This equation appears incomplete. It likely needs a perfector related to emission intensity. 
12) Page 18, line 451: The enhancement factors for power efficiency suddenly appeared. Are they for fixed voltages or
luminance? Why is the enhancement smaller than that of EQE if measured at constant voltage?
