Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors present a study on long-range exciton transport in π-conjugated polymer (πCP) films treated by solvent thermal
annealing (SVA), and investigates their potential for improving exciton diffusion lengths. The authors show significant
advancements, including submillimeter-scale spherulite formation, longer exciton diffusion lengths, and enhanced
electroluminescence in PLEDs. While the study has a wide range of characterisation methods covered, I regret that I cannot
recommend this manuscript for publication in its current form due to the following major concerns: 
1. The authors used photoluminescence quantum yield (PLQY) values in their calculations; however, these values are not
explicitly reported in the manuscript or supporting information. The measured PLQY values are essential for the comparison
between the spherulite and pristine film samples and to access the reliability of the calculation. 
2. The transient absorption (TA) measurements were measured at an intensity of up to 10 mOD, which is very high for
systems with good diffusion lengths, especially in films. Under these conditions, exciton annihilation is very likely
happening, making the kinetics faster and the comparison invalid. The authors should perform a fluence-dependent TA
series in both the pristine and spherulite films to identify a low-fluence regime where no annihilation occurs. In this regime,
the lifetimes should be compared. 
3. Regarding the response time of the experimental setup, the authors should clarify whether the 0-300 ns range, where the
diffusion constant is extracted, is within the response time of the system. If so, this time point may not be representative and
needs careful consideration. 
4. The authors extracted diffusion data for the first 300 ps despite the reported lifetime of 1.2 ns. However, Figure S7 reveals
a negative trend after 300 ps, which is not adequately explained. The authors should provide a clear justification for
excluding this part of the data although it is within the life time and address why the trend goes negative. 
5. The Gaussian fits used in the analysis should be included in the Supplementary Information to allow readers to evaluate
the robustness of the fitting procedure. 
6. While the manuscript focuses on singlet exciton dynamics, it does not address the potential influence of triplet excitons in
the devices. Optical excitation generates 100% singlet excitons, whilst triplet excitons only form via intersystem crossing
(ISC). In contrast, electrical excitation directly produces 75% triplet excitons. The rising electroluminescence (EL) intensity
trend observed in the spherulite films could be influenced by delayed triplet excitons contributing through triplet-triplet
annihilation, especially given the efficient diffusion reported. The authors should discuss the role of triplet excitons in detail. 
7. The authors should compare the PLQY and EQE values, as such a comparison can offer insights into the presence of trap
states or dark triplet excitons, which could influence the overall device performance. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This paper presents an innovative method to improve exciton transport in organic semiconductor materials. It involves
creating submillimeter-scale spherulite films through solvent vapor annealing (SVA). The authors report substantial
enhancements in exciton diffusion length and coefficients within π-conjugated polymer films, which show great promise for
optoelectronic applications, especially in polymer light-emitting diodes (PLEDs). The study is well-structured, featuring
comprehensive experimental methods, and the results offer valuable insights into how long-range ordered spherulite

structures can significantly improve exciton diffusion and optoelectronic properties. This research deepens our
understanding of the structural dynamics and performance of organic semiconductor materials. Therefore, this work can be
considered for publication after a minor revision by addressing the following comments. 
1. The paper suggests that the introduction of branched side chains, as opposed to linear side chains, leads to the formation
of large-sized spherulites. Why does the introduction of branched side chains result in the formation of spherulites, while
materials with linear side chains do not form this morphology? A comparative experiment should be conducted to explore
this issue. 
2. The description of “solvent vapor annealing” in the paper should be standardized, as there are currently two different
expressions being used, such as “solvent thermal annealing”. 
3. In Fig. 3b, why is the absorption edge of the spherulite higher than that of the pristine film? 
4. Page 8, line 27; “We obtained the width of the bandgap tail states (Eu) by fitting the photothermal deflection spectroscopy
(PDS). The Eu value of spherulite film is 48.1 meV, lower than the 52.9 meV of pristine spin-coated film (Fig. 3e).” In the
PDS test referenced in the paper, the data obtained indicates minimal differences. Might these data potentially contain
errors? 
5. Page 10, line 13; “As shown in Fig. 4a, the diameter of excitation spot (beam center) irradiated on the nanofiber bundles is
about 238 nm, covering several single nanofibers. ” This paper utilizes a laser with an excitation wavelength of 405 nm to
excite the sample. How is the diameter of the light spot determined to be 238 nm? In Fig. S6, what is the role of the flip
mirror? 
Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript studies exciton transport in a conjugated polymer by transient photoluminescence microscopy. Diffusion
coefficients reach in the same order of magnitude as those of other reports in literature. Based on a solvent vapour annealing
procedure of a spin coated film of branched side-chain modified polydiarylfluorene, the authors demonstrate improved
structural order and ehanced exciton diffusivity. When incorporated into polymer light-emitting diodes, these films yield deep-
blue electroluminescence with high brightness and improved color purity at low current densities. 
Although the manuscript provides a detailed analysis of the structural and photophysical properties of the spherulite films,
the core findings are not strikingly novel. The enhancement of exciton diffusion through improved molecular ordering and
reduced defects in π-conjugated polymer films is a concept that is established in the literature. While the work confirms
these relationships for the specific polymer under study, it does not introduce fundamentally novel insights or processing
strategies. I am not sure about the impact of this work. In summary, the study confirms established principles rather than
presenting research of great novelty that I would expect for papers in Nature Communications.

6. While the manuscript focuses on singlet  exciton dynamics, it does not address the potential 5 
influence of triplet excitons in the devices. Optical excitation generates 100% singlet excitons, 6 
whilst triplet excitons only form via intersystem crossing (ISC). In contrast, electrical excitation 7

Response: We thank you for this important comment. 12 
As you noted, electrical excitation generates approximately 75% triplet excitons and the rising 13 
electroluminescence (EL) in tensity trend could be influenced by delayed triplet excitons 14 
contributing through triplet -triplet annihilation. However, our devices are based on conventional 15 
fluorescent materials, and the emission originates exclusively from singlet excitons. Since no 16 
phosphorescent or thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF) materials are involved, triplet 17 
excitons generated under electrical excitation are non -radiative due to spin -forbidden transitions, 18 
and thus do not contribute directly to electroluminescence.  Although triplet-triplet annihilation 19 
(TTA) can, in principle, occur in systems with long-lived triplet excitons, it is not expected to 20 
play a significant role in our devices. First, the absence of delayed emission features in transient 21 
EL measurements suggests a negligible contribution from TTA. Second, our polymer system lacks 22 
the energy level alignment and molecular design typically required to enable efficient TTA 23
