Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this study, authors presents the Quebec Reference Genome Panel (QGP), a newly developed whole-genome sequencing
(WGS) reference panel based on a multi-ancestry cohort of ~30k individuals from the CARTaGENE biobank (30k for GWAS
genotyping and 3k for WGS). The QGP reflects the genetic diversity within Quebec, a Canadian province with complex
demographic history shaped by French colonial founders, British settlers, and recent immigrants. The panel improves
genetic imputation accuracy across ancestries, especially for low-frequency and population-specific variants, HLA variants,
and includes annotated data on rare coding variants and their geographic distributions. GWAS results for the set of the
phenotypes were presented. This resource serves as a critical tool for precision health research in Quebec and globally,
while acknowledging the importance of inclusivity in genetic references. This is a well-presented manuscript for the
population- and region-specific cohorts. 
1. Population demography of the cohort looks interesting. This reviewer is interested in more locus-specific story on the
target population, derived from the genome-wide natural selection screening. 
2. HLA genotype calling based on WGS is still methodologically under development and lacks accuracy. Validation of the
called HLA variants using other typing methods is recommended. Calling of non-classical HLA genes such as HLA-E/F/G is
generally still challenging, and as far as this reviewer knows, WGS-based calling is not accurate to date. 
3. Overview of the GWAS design and results (No. phenotypes for each category, No. identified loci) can better to be
presented in the main Figure. 
4. As always, there exist discussions on multiple comparisons of GWAS significance based on the No. phenotypes. This
reviewer does not request to strictly apply the threshold considering the multiple comparison burden, but it would be better to
indicate how the number of the significant loci would change after application. 
5. The value of the manuscript depends on data availability. Current statement ‘The CaG genetic and phenotypid data is
available from … after scientific and ethical review’ may not satisfy the Nature journal data deposit request. Direct deposit of
the data to public resources such as dbGAP or EGA would be necessary.

1.2) HLA genotype calling based on WGS is still methodologically under development 
and lacks accuracy. Validation of the called HLA variants using other typing 
methods is recommended. Calling of non-classical HLA genes such as HLA-
E/F/G is generally still challenging, and as far as this reviewer knows, WGS-based 
calling is not accurate to date.
Response: We thank the reviewer for highlighting this limitation in our analyses. We 
agree that short-read sequencing technology, even at >30X whole genome coverage 
as used in our study, has limitations in accurately resolving HLA haplotypes, and it is 
not suitable for clinical applications or for detecting ultra-rare genetic variants. 
However, using high-depth WGS data for HLA calling and imputation in population-
based biobanks is widely accepted, as demonstrated in studies such as PMID: 
34611364 (HLA calling from WGS), PMID: 37923823 (HLA calling from WES), and 
PMID: 35221124 (HLA imputation). In our analyses, we used HLA-LA (PMID: 
30942877) for calling, which is well-established and was thoroughly evaluated in its 
original manuscript and independent works (e.g., PMID: 36426357, PMID: 37161318) 
and is considered one of the most accurate HLA callers available; the authors report 
99.4% accuracy from whole-genome sequencing data. To minimize false positives as 
much as possible, we performed the following: (1) we applied the most strict 
thresholds on HLA-LA’s documented quality metrics; (2) we compared the WGS-
based HLA alleles frequencies against their frequencies in Hema-Quebec donor 
database to eliminate any outlier HLA allele with discordant frequency; (3) as the 
reviewer noted, the calling accuracy varies by HLA gene, and thus, we excluded from 
our analyses alleles in genes HLA-DRB3 and -DRB4 that are known to be highly 
problematic to call. Although non-classical genes (HLA-E, -F , -G) were not excluded
