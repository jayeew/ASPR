Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, the authors reported a permeable intimate membrane (PIM) electrode by casting ionomer emulsion
directly onto the CL to form AEM in-situ, creating a bonded interface with permeated channels for efficient ion transport. The
PIM-MEA achieves >90% CO selectivity across wide current densities in pure water, with 1.35 times higher energy efficiency
than conventional MEAs. Comprehensive characterization revealed that it is hydrogen-bonded network of interfacial water,
that significantly increased the proportion of strongly hydrogen-bonded water (sh-H2O), which greatly increased the
performance of ECR towards CO. Therefore, I recommend its publication in Nature Communications after addressing the
following points. 
1. The structure of constructed PIM needs to be discussed. How does the thickness of the catalyst layer and membrane
affect performance, or how can it be controlled to achieve the most appropriate state? 
2. Post-reaction structural characterization should be added to observe its structural evolution. 
3. In order to prove its universality, it is recommended to use different types of catalysts for testing, such as silver with
different particle sizes or other types of catalysts. 
4. Figure 2 provides a lot of repeated information. As shown in Figures 2a and 2b, only the sizes are different. It is
recommended to put one of them in the supporting information and keep the one with the most prominent features. 
5. The following related reported literatures on the topic of this study are suggested to be properly discussed and cited in the
introduction part, for example, Sci. Bull. 70, 2330-2346 (2025); Adv. Mater. 36, 2402979 (2024); ACS Nano 18, 33602–
33613 (2024); ACS Nano 18, 7192–7203 (2024); ACS Nano 18, 9823–9851 (2024). 
6. The caption of Figure 3 for ECR performance was mistakenly written as Figure 2. Such errors should be carefully checked
and corrected. 
7. It is recommended to add a table in the discussion section comparing key metrics (FE, EE, stability ...) of the PIM electrode
to recent state-of-the-art pure-water ECR MEAs from literature.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript presents an innovative study on the development of a permeable intimate membrane (PIM) electrode for
pure water-fed anion exchange membrane (AEM) electrolyzers for CO2 reduction. The work addresses a critical challenge
in the field by engineering an intimately bonded catalyst layer/membrane interface. The performance metrics, including
>90% CO selectivity over a wide current density range under pure water conditions, enhanced energy efficiency, and
impressive long-term stability, are noteworthy. The combination of electrochemical diagnostics, in situ spectroscopy, and
computational simulations provides strong evidence for the role of the PIM in restructuring interfacial water and facilitating
reaction kinetics. This work represents a step toward practical, electrolyte-free CO2 electrolysis. I will suggest a minor
revision. 
1. While the in-situ casting of the ionomer emulsion is central to the PIM concept, the manuscript would benefit from a more
detailed description of the process (e.g., emulsion composition, curing conditions, thickness control). A schematic or
supplementary video illustrating the fabrication steps could further improve clarity. 
2. Provide quantitative data on the interfacial resistance or adhesion strength between the CL and AEM in PIM vs.
conventional MEA designs. This would help readers better appreciate the improvement in interfacial contact. 
3. Briefly comment on the potential limitations of the PIM approach. A short discussion on the generalizability of this strategy
would be valuable. 
4. Specify the exact ionomer used (QAPPT is mentioned in the conclusion but not in the abstract or introduction). Clarify
whether the large-scale electrolyzer used for the 200-hour test is the same as the lab-scale cell or a different configuration.
Ensure all acronyms are defined upon first use. 
5. Check for consistency in terminology (e.g., ECR vs. CO2 reduction). Ensure that all figures and schemes are referenced in
the main text in logical order.

Comment 1 
1. The structure of co nstructed PIM needs to be discussed. How does the thickness of the catalyst

Response 1: 
 We sincerely thank the reviewer for this insightful comment. In response, we have now added 
a detailed discussion regarding the influence of both the anion-exchange layer (AEL) thickness and 
catalyst loading on the performance of PIM-Q electrode.  
1) The effect of AEL thickness 
 To investigate the effect of AEL thickness, we prepared PIM-Q electrode with four different 
thicknesses (13.4, 19.9, 25.6, and 34.4 μm), and the thickness values were confirmed by SEM cross-
sectional images (Fig. s16). These electrodes were subsequently evaluated under pure water fed 
electrolyzer. As the AEL thickness increased from 13.4 to 25.6 μm, the FECO improved significantly 
from 89.9% to 92.1% at 50 mA cm-2 (Fig. 2b, Fig. s17a -s17b). Further increasing the membrane 
thickness to 34.4 μm led to negligible changes in FE (Fig. 2b, Fig. s17c). Meanwhile, thicker AELs 
increased transport distances of ions across membrane, resulting in higher cell voltages (Fig. s17d). 
By comparing the EE at 50 mA cm-2, we observed a volcano -type trend, with the maximum EE 
achieved at an AEL thickness of 25.6 μm (Fig. 2b). Therefore, this thickness of 25.6 μm was selected 
as the optimal membrane structure for subsequent studies. 
2) The effect of catalyst loading  
 Based on the optimized AEL  thickness of 25.6 μm, we further examined the influence of 
catalyst loading within the PIM -Q electrode (Fig. s18, Fig. s19 ). Increasing the catalyst loading 
from 0.25 to 0.5 mg cm-2 enhanced the FE CO from 89.9% to 92.1%  (Fig. s20a -s20d). Further
