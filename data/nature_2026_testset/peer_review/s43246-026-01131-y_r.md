Reviewers' comments:
 
Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
The manuscript by Ishida et al. presents a study of Co3Mn thin films, where the Co atoms form a Kagome lattice and
magnetisation measurements indicate a ferrimagnetic state up to high temperatures. The authors include DFT calculations
and ARPES data to relate the magnetic properties of the films to the presence of flat bands near the Fermi level. 
Although I have no doubts about the experimental results, I found some of the arguments supporting their interpretation
unclear. 
The authors mentioned throughout the manuscript the important role of spin-orbit interactions. However, how this emerges
from their work is not clearly explained. The DFT calculations presented in the manuscript for a ferrimagnetic ground state
show very little modifications once SOC is included (Fig.1.(d)). 
In the discussion of the Pt-substituted films the authors argue that the higher perpendicular magnetic anisotropy (PMA) in
those films is stabilised by “increased spin-orbit interaction”. Could the authors explain how these two aspects are related
beyond the simple fact that Pt is heavier than Mo? 
Ab-initio calculations indicate that the ferrimagnetic ground state is the most energetically favourable. What is the energy
difference compared to other possible ground states? 
The ARPES data in figure 2 show only a few of the dozen of bands predicted by DFT for the same energy/momentum range.
Additionally some of the features, like the fast dispersing Λ band, shows a significant shift in energy compared to DFT. The
authors should comments on the possible reasons for these differences. 
This is an important point given that the magnetic properties of the films are interpreted based on the DFT DOS of the
ferrimagnetic ground states and the assumption that DFT gives a good description of the system. 
Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author): 
K. Ishida et al. report the discovery of room-temperature perpendicular magnetic anisotropy (PMA) in Co3Mo thin films and
attributes this behavior to kagome-lattice-derived flat bands located near the Fermi level. Through a combination of
calculations, ARPES, X-ray diffraction, XMCD, magneto transport measurements, and Pt substitution studies, the authors
propose that flat-band-enhanced DOS leads to a Stoner-type ferrimagnetic ground state with strong spin-orbit-mediated
magnetocrystalline anisotropy. They further argue that Pt incorporation enhances PMA and induces a transition from the
D019 to L12 symmetry. Overall, the study integrates high-quality experimental data with theoretical modeling and provides
valuable insights into magnetic kagome systems for potential spintronic applications. Nevertheless, several issues require
clarification or additional evidence before I can recommend the manuscript for publication. 
1.The manuscript emphasizes that kagome flat bands near EF lead to a high DOS that enhances Stoner magnetism and,
through spin-orbit coupling, produces PMA. However, the mechanistic link between flat bands and anisotropy is not
convincingly demonstrated. PMA originates from SOC-driven orbital moment anisotropy, and a large DOS alone does not
guarantee enhanced magnetocrystalline anisotropy; orbital character and specific SOC matrix elements are essential.
Moreover, the experimentally observed flat band lies ~0.25 eV below EF, raising questions about its direct contribution to
anisotropy. As presented, the explanation is qualitative and somewhat speculative, and the authors should either provide
quantitative support (e.g., orbital-resolved MAE calculations, SOC-induced band modifications, Berry-curvature analysis) or
temper the strength of the claim. 
2.The enhancement of PMA in Co3Mo1-xPtx is intriguing, yet the underlying mechanism is not clearly disentangled.
Although the manuscript attributes this trend mainly to the stronger SOC of Pt substituting at the Mo site, Pt incorporation
also modifies electron count, local bonding, lattice strain, and structural stability, all of which can affect magnetic anisotropy.
The abrupt transition into an L12 structure at higher Pt content further suggests that proximity to a structural phase boundary
plays a significant role. A more balanced discussion clarifying the relative contributions of SOC, doping, lattice distortion,
and structural instability would strengthen the interpretation. 
3.Several important statements about the orbital origin of the flat band, Dirac bands, and saddle-point states (lines 100–108)
as well as about the ferrimagnetic alignment between Co and Mo are not supported by explicit figures or data. Including the
relevant orbital-resolved DOS and band-projection results in the Supplementary Information would greatly improve
transparency and buttress these claims. 
4.In Figure 2a, the XRD pattern contains an unidentified peak near 65°, despite the text stating that no impurity phases are
present. This discrepancy should be addressed directly in the manuscript.

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 3 -->

5.The flat-band feature identified in Figures 2g and 2h is extremely weak. Applying second-derivative or curvature analysis
to the ARPES data could improve visibility and help substantiate the flat-band assignment. 
6.The XMCD signal for Co is small, and the Mo signal is stated to be negligible. To support the argument that the reduced
magnetization originates from the kagome Co network, the authors should include the Mo XMCD spectra, even if the signal
is weak, as a reference. 
7.Since Pt substitution is claimed to enhance SOC-driven anisotropy and modify the electronic structure, it would be
informative to track the evolution of the flat-band position with Pt concentration through both calculations and ARPES. Such
data would provide a more complete picture of how band-structure modifications correlate with the observed magnetic
behavior. 
8.All figures in the main text currently lack captions. 
In summary, the manuscript presents compelling experimental observations and addresses a timely topic---the interplay
between flat-band physics, magnetism, and magnetic anisotropy in kagome materials. However, several conceptual and
interpretive issues, particularly regarding the microscopic origin of PMA and the role of Pt substitution, require additional
evidence and clarification. Strengthening these points will significantly enhance the rigor and impact of the work.
