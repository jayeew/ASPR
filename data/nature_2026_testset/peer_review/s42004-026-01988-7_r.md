Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript by Angelo Fontana et al. describes the discovery and preliminary biological evaluation of jorumycidine, a
novel hexacyclic bis-tetrahydroisoquinoline alkaloid from a marine nudibranch–sponge symbiotic system. The study
identifies an intriguing new scaffold with potent nanomolar cytotoxicity, proposes a fascinating cross-kingdom biosynthetic
interplay, and provides a comprehensive bioinformatic re-analysis of the associated biosynthetic gene cluster. The topic is
relevant to the scope of Communication Chemistry. However, the manuscript in its current form contains several limitations,
primarily concerning the unequivocal structural elucidation of the new compounds and the overinterpretation of the
biosynthetic data. These issues must be adequately addressed before the manuscript can be considered for publication. 
Major Concerns: 
1.Incomplete and Unsupported Assignment of Absolute Configuration: 
The assignment of the absolute configuration for the key new compound, jorumycidine (4), is incomplete and insufficiently
supported. The sentence on line 169 ends abruptly ("…13S, 21"), which is unacceptable. More critically, the assignment
appears to rely heavily on biosynthetic analogy and NOE-derived relative configuration. For a novel scaffold with
unprecedented structural features, this is inadequate. The absence of computational support to corroborate the proposed
absolute stereochemistry is a major weakness. Although X-ray crystallography may not be feasible given the limited quantity
of compound, the calculated ECD absorption curves is essential and must be performed to provide credible evidence for the
absolute configuration. 
2.Overstated Conclusion Regarding Enzymatic Transformation in J. funebris: 
The conclusion on line 95, stating that the data support a specialized enzymatic transformation of renieramycins to
jorumycins, is premature and overly assertive. While the species-specific distribution pattern is suggestive, the claim of a
direct enzymatic conversion via transesterification is not proven by LC-MS/MS data alone. The authors must acknowledge
alternative biosynthetic possibilities. For instance, the PKS module (e.g., RenK) could possess substrate promiscuity,
allowing for the parallel incorporation of both acetate and angelate units from a common biosynthetic precursor. The
discussion should be toned down to reflect that the data are consistent with such a transformation, but that direct enzymatic
evidence is required for definitive proof. 
3.Insufficient Structural Data for Minor Compounds 5 and 6: 
While the proposed structures for the 21-deoxy analogues (5 and 6) are plausible, the extremely low isolated yields raise
concerns about the completeness of their characterization. The determination of their absolute configuration is particularly
vague. To ensure the structural assignments are reliable, the authors should provide, at a minimum, any available NOE data
that supports the relative configuration, and ideally, computational ECD data to support the absolute configuration,
especially since the hemiaminal group, a key pharmacophore, is absent. 
4.Deficiencies in NMR Data Presentation: 
The 1H and 13C NMR data (Figure S1, 3, 11, 12, 17, 22) provided in the supplementary information must list all chemical
shifts and coupling constants.

5.Concerns Regarding Compound Purity and Stability: 
The provided 1H NMR spectra for compounds 1, 2, 3, and 5 suggests the presence of minor impurity signals. This raises
significant concerns about the purity and chemical stability of these compounds. As the cytotoxic activities are a major
highlight of the manuscript, it is crucial to demonstrate that the tested samples were of high purity. The authors must address
these concerns by commenting on the stability of these compounds and providing evidence (e.g., HPLC purity analysis) that
the bioassay results are not compromised by impurities or degradation products. 
Minor Concerns: 
1.The cytotoxicity assays should include standard positive control drugs (e.g., doxorubicin, cisplatin) at relevant
concentrations in Table 1 to provide context for the potency of the tested compounds. 
2.Line 66: Please correct "C21" to "C-21" for consistency in structural numbering. 
3.The reference formatting requires careful checking and standardization. Please ensure journal title abbreviations, use of
italics for genus/species names, and capitalization style are consistent throughout the reference list. 
4.High-resolution images for the structural drawings of compounds 1-6 must be provided in the main text (Line 405) and in
the supplementary information (Figure S30).

We thank the Reviewer for this important and constructive comment regarding the structural elucidation 
and, in particular, the assignment of the absolute configuration of jorumycidine  (4). We fully agree that a

Major Concern 1: Incomplete and Unsupported Assignment of Absolute Configuration 
1. Incomplete and Unsupported Assignment of Absolute Configuration:  
The assignment of the absolute configuration for the key new compound, jorumycidine (4), is incomplete and 
insufficiently supported. The sentence on line 169 ends abruptly ("…13S, 21"), which is unacceptable. More 
critically, the assignment appears to rely  heavily on biosynthetic analogy and NOE -derived relative 
configuration. For a novel scaffold with unprecedented structural features, this is inadequate. The absence of 
computational support to corroborate the proposed absolute stereochemistry is a major weakness. Although 
X-ray crystallography may not be feasible given the limited quantity of compound, the calculated ECD 
absorption curves is essential and must be performed to provide credible evidence for the absolute 
configuration. 
We appreciate the reviewer’s comments. To address this concern, we have substantially revised and 
expanded the manuscript in two main ways: 
1. Expanded discussion of structural assignment. 
The discussion on the structural elucidation of jorumycidine and related bis-THIQ analogs has been 
significantly strengthened in the section “Isolation and Structure Elucidation of Jorumycin and other 
bis-THIQ analogs”. This revised section now provides a clearer and more complete rationale for the 
structural assignments, integrating NMR-based relative stereochemical analysis with biosynthetic 
considerations. 
2. New section on absolute stereochemistry using ECD. 
In direct response to the Reviewer’s legitimate request, we have introduced a new dedicated 
section describing the assignment of the absolute configuration of the new compounds based 
on experimental electronic circular dichroism (ECD) spectra combined with quantum-chemical 
ECD calculations. Calculated ECD curves were generated for the relevant stereoisomers and 
compared with the experimental spectra, providing independent and robust support for the 
absolute stereochemical assignments. 
Importantly, the ECD results are fully consistent with the previously proposed stereochemical model derived 
from biosynthetic logi c and NOE -based relative configuration. Thus, the new experimental and 
computational data strongly corroborate, rather than revise, the original biosynthetically informed 
assignment. 
The incomplete sentence previously noted has been corrected, and all stereochemical assignments are now 
clearly stated and fully supported by complementary experimental, computational, and biosynthetic 
evidence. 
We hope that these substantial additions satisfactorily address the Reviewer’s concern and provide a more 
rigorous and convincing foundation for the absolute configuration of this unprecedented scaffold.

Overstated Conclusion Regarding Enzymatic Transformation in J. funebris:  
The conclusion on line 95, stating that the data support a specialized enzymatic transformation of 
renieramycins to jorumycins, is premature and overly assertive. While the species-specific distribution pattern 
is suggestive, the claim of a direct enzymati c conversion via transesterification is not proven by LC -MS/MS 
data alone. The authors must acknowledge alternative biosynthetic possibilities. For instance, the PKS module

(e.g., RenK) could possess substrate promiscuity, allowing for the parallel incorpo ration of both acetate and 
angelate units from a common biosynthetic precursor. The discussion should be toned down to reflect that 
the data are consistent with such a transformation, but that direct enzymatic evidence is required for

We thank the Reviewer for this thoughtful and well -argued comment. We agree that the original wording 
was too assertive and did not sufficiently acknowledge alternative biosynthetic scenarios. 
We have therefore reconsidered and moderated the tone of this stat ement throughout the manuscript. 
While we still believe that the available evidence strongly supports our working hypothesis, we now frame it 
more cautiously. 
Specifically, our interpretation is based on the convergence of multiple, independent observation s: 
(i) the chemical analysis of individually purified metabolites; 
(ii) the LC–MS profiling of the complete metabolite pool in both mantle mucus and whole-body extracts; and 
(iii) consistency with previous literature reports on related bis -THIQ alkaloids a nd nudibranch –sponge 
chemical relationships. 
Taken together, these data suggest that the observed chemical patterns are compatible with a post -
biosynthetic modification occurring in  J. funebris. However, in line with the Reviewer’s comment, we now 
explicitly acknowledge that LC–MS/MS data alone cannot demonstrate a direct enzymatic transesterification, 
and that alternative biosynthetic possibilities , such as substrate promiscuity of the PKS machinery allowing 
parallel incorporation of acetate and angelate moieties from a common precursor, cannot be excluded. 
Accordingly, the revised text now states that the data are  consistent with, rather than demonstrative of, a 
specialized enzymatic transformation, and clearly notes that direct biochemical or enzymologic al evidence 
will be required to definitively establish the mechanism.

We thank the Reviewer for raising this important point regarding compound purity and stability, particularly 
in light of the reported cytotoxic activities. 
We acknowledge that, compared to synthetic compounds, exhaustive purity documentation (e.g., routine 
HPLC purity percentages for every isolated metabolite) is not always standard practice in natural products 
chemistry, especially when dealing with highly c omplex, low -abundance metabolites isolated in sub -
milligram quantities. Nevertheless, we pay close attention to compound purity throughout the isolation 
workflow. In this study, the purity of all isolated compounds was carefully assessed by repeated inspec tion 
of ^1H NMR spectra as well as high -resolution LC -MS data, which were used to confirm the absence of 
significant level of contaminating species. 
In response to the Reviewer’s specific concern and to further strengthen the manuscript, we have now 
provided additional chromatographic evidence in the revised version. In particular, to ensure the quality of 
jorumycidine, the key compound highlighted for biological activity, we performed an  analytical HPLC –MS 
purity analysis , which has been added to the Suppl ementary Information (Figure S48). This analysis 
demonstrates that jorumycidine is present as the predominant component, accounting for  more than 95% 
of the total chromatographic signal . While minor impurities or degradation products cannot be entirely 
excluded for such structurally complex and sensitive natural products, these data indicate that the bioassay 
results are not compromised by significant contamination. 
Furthermore, in the Supplementary Information we have also included the  HPLC profiles of the  
chromatographic fractions from which the remaining compounds were purified, showing sufficiently sharp 
and well-resolved peaks, consistent with a high degree of purity prior to final isolation. 
Regarding stability, we note that all compounds were handled under mild conditions, stored at low 
temperature, and analyzed promptly after purification. No major changes in NMR or MS profiles were 
observed during the time frame relevant to biological testing, suggesting that the compounds are sufficiently 
stable under the experimental conditions used. 
We believe that the additional HPLC –MS data and clarifications reported in the man text (“Comparative 
anticancer activity of jorumycidine “ and “Methods - Cell cultures and cytotoxic tests ” ) now adequately 
address the R eviewer’s concern and provide confidence that the reported biological activities can be 
attributed predominantly to the compounds of interest.
