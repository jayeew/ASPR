REVIEWERS' COMMENTS: 
Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
The questions raised earlier have been well addressed, and the manuscript has been restructured to present a clearer and
more coherent logical flow. 
I have two additional questions that I hope can be clarified: 
First, regarding the luminescence dating results. Given the limited information provided, no major issues are immediately
apparent (as is common in many paleoenvironmental studies, which often only report dating outcomes without detailed
methodologies—this makes comprehensive evaluation challenging). However, I contend that using the currently measured
water content for age calculation may be problematic. The yardangs are composed of lacustrine sediments, and the exact
timing when they emerged above the water table and became detached from the aquatic environment remains
unconstrained. Generally, a 1% increase in water content can introduce an approximate 1% error in the calculated age (i.e.,
higher water content leads to overestimated ages). 
Second, the relationship between the formation age of the yardangs and the age of the yardang-forming materials (original
lacustrine sediments) requires clarification. The age of the yardang surface layer does not necessarily represent the initiation
age of yardang formation, for two key reasons: 
It is unknown whether the surface of the entire lacustrine sedimentary sequence was eroded after lake desiccation, and if so,
the magnitude of erosion remains unquantified; 
Lake desiccation and the onset of yardang formation (i.e., aeolian erosion and incision of the original lacustrine sediments)
are not necessarily synchronous—aeolian erosion does not commence immediately following lake drying. 
Therefore, a conservative and rigorous statement would be: The measured luminescence ages of the yardang surface layers
can only represent the maximum possible age of yardang formation (i.e., yardang formation did not occur earlier than this
age). Furthermore, the samples collected near the yardang tops in this study are not from the actual surface layer. 
In conclusion, it can be inferred that a layer of material must have been deflated from the yardang tops by aeolian processes.
Coupled with the fact that the reported ages (from samples near the tops) do not correspond to the true surface layer, there

remains a significant discrepancy between these measured ages and the actual initiation age of yardang formation.
