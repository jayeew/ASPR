−𝑤  ∂𝑢/ ∂𝑧, it contributes a westerly acceleration in both shear zones. In the westerly 
shear region, 𝑤 < 0  and ∂𝑢/ ∂𝑧 > 0 , so −𝑤  ∂𝑢/ ∂𝑧 > 0 , which enhances

the descent of the westerlies, while the upward advection of momentum associated with 
easterly shear inhibits the descent of easterlies.” We therefore retain our original 
wording.

Response: We thank the reviewer for the comment. We acknowledge that under a large 
CO₂ loading scenario, many components of the Earth system may change, including, as 
noted by the reviewer in Comment #8, the annual cycle and the semiannual oscillation. 
As the reviewer pointed out, it is challenging to parameterize changes in the SAO and 
the annual cycle. We therefore followed the reviewer ’s suggestion and have clarified 
this limitation in the Methods section: 
“Here, we assume fixed periods of 360 days for the annual cycle and 180 days 
for the semiannual cycle, and the results from the one -dimensional QBO model 
should therefore be interpreted within this framework.” (Page 16, lines 370–372 in 
the revised manuscript.) 
In addition, the disappearance of the QBO under high CO₂ forcing is supported by 
both the CMIP6 simulations and our sensitivity experiments. In the sensitivity 
experiments, the imposed CO₂ increases were designed to follow the CO₂ trajectory 
under the SSP5-8.5 scenario. When CO₂ reaches four times its baseline concentration 
(approximately corresponding to SSP5 -8.5 around 2100), the QBO period is already 
substantially weakened ( Supplementary Fig. 4c,d). When CO₂ reaches six and eight 
times its baseline concentration, rou ghly corresponding to SSP5 -8.5 around 2150 and 
2200, respectively, the QBO disappears entirely ( Supplementary Fig. 4e –h). The 
mechanisms analysed in the manuscript are consistently supported by the CMIP6 
models, the sensitivity experiments, and the one-dimensional QBO model. We therefore 
conclude that the mechanisms discussed in the manuscript play an important role in the 
disappearance of the QBO. 
We note that, as the reviewer suggested, additional changes under high CO₂ 
loading (for example, changes in the annual cycle and the semiannual oscillation) may 
also influence the QBO and could contribute to its disappearance. However, given the 
scope and length constraints of the present manuscript, and the complexity of 
quantifying these effects, we do not pursue a detailed discussion here.
