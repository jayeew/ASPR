Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author): 
Spring Phenology of the Arctic Ocean Shelf Production System 
General about the paper: 
The current study uses a numeric biophysical model to further explore the seasonality of the key components of the Arctic
marine ecosystems. It provides a closer look at the seasonal phenological co-development and interaction between the

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 3 -->

physical processes and key biological players as ice-associated algae, a copepod, and Polar cod fish. 
The paper is very well written, the methods and the support for the choice of methods is well explained. In my opinion, the
paper just need a minor revision, where the authors need to better highlight the strengths and novelties of their research ,
and thus, the contribution of this paper to a vast literature on phenology of the Arctic ecosystems. 
Below, 
I provide more details to help the authors address this issue, and make a few minor corrections. 
Abstract 
Lines 12-18. Here you mention only briefly that you have used a biophysical model and what the model showed. My
question is: didn’t we know it from before that ice algae growth begins in early May and intensifies in late May and so on? I
understand that your model mimicked the real ecosystem so well that its results match the reality, then please, highlight it
here in the Abstract. What I think you miss is to add sentences saying that the model’s results matched the theory (and you
don’t need to describe all the phenological details right here), and then how the model results contributed something new,
how they were informative given that there are quite a few papers on arctic phenology 
already published? 
Lines 23-24. You say your finding offer insights into how climate-drive changes may disrupt the ecosystems. And this is the
crucial result of your whole research. So what are these insights? Can you give an example or two, here, in the Abstract? 
I hope I explained myself well, I simply think that you can improve the Abstract so that it becomes more eye-catching (many
readers look only at the Abstract, you know). 
Introduction 
Lines 66-70. This is a passage about the physical oceanography of the Barents Sea, which I know is important for the
ecosystem’s biological processes as well. But I miss the connection, or a transition from the facts about oceanic currents
(down to the line 66) and C. glacialis distribution. You mention it later in the sentence, but I think the logical flow will be
better if you add something like “the oceanic water transport has a direct impact on the extent of key zooplankton species—
C. galcialis”, and then you may explain what this impact is. 
Line 69. I think the verb “deflected” should be in the present tense as “deflects”to be parallel with a verb “exports”. 
Line 72. As far as I recall, composite adjectives are written with a hyphen, to make “ice-covered pelagic production”.
Otherwise, it may be misread as ice has covered the production of the Arctic ocean. I have seen the same mistake multiple
times through the paper, please, check for the other occurrences of “ice covered”. 
Line 77.” Under ice” primary production should be written as “under-ice”. You can see in Google scholar that other papers
use “under-ice” for the same meaning as in your paper. For instance: 
https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2015JC011183 
Line 90. Shouldn’t it be “individual-based model”? 
Line 93.” Ecologically important copepod” in place of 
“ecological important”. 
Line 94. The months are written in capital letters in English, so it should be March, not march. 
Line 102. “As an example of the Arctic Ocean ...” 
Material and methods 
Line 133: “”the -u and -v components”… You may explain what are these components, since the paper will be read also by
ecologists that know little about circulation models. 
Line 151:you say that “to hypothesize that spawning may be triggered …” but then you insert a reference to Søreide et al. So,
that spawning is triggered by the first sunlight is your hypothesis or the one Søreide et al. has proposed? Please, clarify. 
Line 164: YOY Polar cod in BESS. I have not seen the explanation of what is YOY and BESS earlier in the paper, would you
add an explanation here? 
Line 170: a reference to Ben Laurel – should be round parentheses here? 
Line 240: “stage-specific” with a hyphen. Check also the other occasions of this phrase. 
Lines 245-248. Here you explain the results of the binomial GLM. First, what was the effect of 1 degree temperature change
on the odds of CV (the log of beta)? This log of beta parameter is the temperature effect, but you don’t report it here. 
You mention the 26% decline in CV proportion but how have you arrived at that number? You may estimate the probability of
CV for each given temperature using the model equation (and then, I assume, you interpret probability as proportion), but
how you estimated a decline in the probability/proportion? 
Please, check this section and add beta parameter. Check also

Line 291. “The pre-adult CV” not C5 since you used CV throughout the text. 
Results

Line 420: “toward the end of May”. You often do not write the article “the” in the similar phrases, please, check the whole text.

Overall evaluation 
This manuscript is well written and valuable, as it addresses the ice associated production, and related development of polar
cod larvae in the Arctic Barents Sea. The adult polar cod (Boreogadus saida) has been a key forage fish of Northeast arctic
cod (Gadus morhua) in the past. The Barents Sea is experiencing unprecedented warming and related loss of sea ice and
the decrease of many key fish stocks, including polar cod. Hence, this study is timely. I recommend publishing the model
driven study in Communications Earth & Environment. The manuscript is accepted with minor changes. 
Reviewer: Padmini Dalpadado 
General comments 
Some structuring of the manuscript is needed. Under the Material and method section, explanations given on the different
model layers are imbalanced e.g. a couple of sentences on the 1st layer (physical drives) followed by a lengthy 2nd layer on
the primary production. Furthermore, I suggest adding subheadings particularly to the lengthy section addressing “biological
models”, for the reader to easily follow. 
The following subheadings are proposed: “ice algae development; predicting
Calanus glacialis biomass; Egg production, mortality and vertical positioning of C. glacialis; Polar cod egg incubation, drift
and swimming capabilities of larvae” 
In the introduction there should be a short paragraph on climatic changes in the marginal ice zone ecosystem and its impact
on the higher trophic levels, including polar cod and cod.

Alse see Årthun et al (2015) for transport mechanisms,
likely impacting C. glacialis.
