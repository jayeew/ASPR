Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Comments: 
Functional group migration offers a powerful approach for precise molecular editing and regiocontrol, yet strategies that
achieve exact stereochemical control remain rare. Professor Jian Xu and co-workers have introduced a photoenzymatic
cyano migration that enables precise stereocontrol. By employing stereocomplementary enzymes, both enantiomers of the
product can be accessed—a selectivity rationalized through mechanistic studies and molecular dynamics simulations. This
work significantly broadens the scope of asymmetric transformations achievable via photoenzymatic catalysis and presents
a compelling strategy to address long-standing challenges in organic synthesis. Overall, this reviewer finds the MS highly
exciting and looks forward to future advances from the authors. 
1. Regarding the substrate scope, electron-withdrawing substituents lead to diminished yield and enantioselectivity. The
manuscript merely suggests accelerated radical quenching as a possible cause, which does not sufficiently account for the
significantly low yields. A mass balance analysis is recommended to clarify the fate of the substrates. 
2. In the radical trapping experiment, the reaction yield remains at 32% despite the addition of 4 equivalents of TEMPO. Any
discussions would be helpful. 
3. Additional review could be cited, such as Chin. J. Chem. 2024, DOI: 10.1002/cjoc.202400656 
4. The Supporting Information lacks detailed procedures for the substrate synthesis and enzymatic reactions. 
5. For racemic product 2o in Table S65 (line 990), the high-resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS) deviation exceeds 0.001
Da. Re-measurement is suggested. 
6. Several NMR spectra in the SI are misinterpreted. For example, in the spectrum of racemic 2g (S92), the aromatic region
is incorrectly integrated to 2.77H, and an extra hydrogen is accounted for in the aliphatic region. Similarly, in the spectrum of
2h (S93), a water peak appears to have been misassigned as a product signal. A thorough re-examination of all spectral
assignments is suggested. 
7. Minor errors were noted in the SI: inconsistent font type at S3 line 44, "standard" misspelled in the table on S17, and
inconsistent abbreviation of "eq" in the first reaction scheme on S21. A full proofreading is recommended to ensure
consistency and accuracy throughout. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript by Wang and Xu describes a photoenzymatic 1,5-cyano transfer reaction catalyzed by flavin-dependent
ene-reductases. While these types of cyano transfer reactions are well known in the photoredox literature, they have not
been realized within a photoenzyme. The ERED platform is well established for photoenzymatic reactivity and can control

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 2 -->

the delivery of a hydrogen atom to prochiral benzylic radicals with excellent enantioselectivity, making it a sensible catalyst
for the reported reaction. The yields and enantioselectivities are good with a range of substates. Mechanistic studies confirm
that radical initiation occurs via excitation of charge transfer complex. Overall, I would describe this work as novel but not
particularly surprising as it relies on many well-established features from the ERED photoenzymatic literature.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This work introduces a photoenzymatic strategy for stereocontrolled translocation reactions, with cyano translocation taken
as a model system. It combines experimental data with computational analysis aimed at rationalizing the stereoselectivity. At
this stage, however, the computational component does not appear sufficiently strong, as it is not entirely clear whether the
sampling depth and methodological rigor of the analysis are adequate to support firm mechanistic conclusions.
Nevertheless, if the results can be maintained and strengthened after addressing the following comments, the study may
reach the level required for publication. I would like to see the revised version of the manuscript. 
1) The simulations need to be extended and at least a second replica needs to be performed for each systems. 
2) Rather than showing distances from a single representative configuration (such as those between the substrate and the
polar atoms of the active site, or between the benzyl ring and hydrophobic active-site residues), the authors should present
the distribution of each distance over the course of the MD simulation, separated for the pro-S and pro-R basins. 
3) One of the main conclusions drawn from the simulations is the following: 
“Notably, the benzyl group formed additional parallel (with W100) and T-shaped (with F269) π-π stacking interactions in the
pro-(S) binding form in CsER and the pro-(R) binding form in GluER, respectively, providing extra binding stability. In
contrast, the pro-(R) binding form in CsER and the pro-(S) binding form in GluER showed much more exposure of the 2a•
hydrophobic group to polar water molecules, indicative of an energetically unfavorable binding mode.” 
This could be an interesting observation but should be corroborated by specific analyses. 
a) Concerning the distances between the benzyl group and hydrophobic residues, the authors should also consider the side
chains of residues that could for pi-pi interactions in the “wrong” pro-chiral basin as for example: 
-distance between the benzyl group and Y336 or Y66 in CsER (pro-R) 
-distance between the benzyl group and W66 and W342 in GluER (pro-S) 
b) Concerning the comment on the larger exposure to water molecules of hydrophobic groups, this should be shown with
appropriate analyses 
4) Concerning the OYE3 enzyme, the authors claim: “the dihedral distribution and structure characteristics in the binding
complex of OYE3(Y196F), FMNsq, and 2i• were in accordance with the reduced stereoselectivity for the dicyano-substituted
compounds (Fig. S6).” 
I might agree with this conclusion, but not for the reasons provided by the authors. In fact, for OYE3-Y196F the negative
values do not appear to be pro-S but rather achiral, as they lie too close to –180° to define a clear prochiral preference.
Similarly, for the positive values the average dihedral angle may also be not too far from 180°, which again implies an
unclear chiral assignment. The authors may therefore wish to reconsider and revise their interpretation.

Reviewer #4 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Xu et al reported an interesting cyano translocation reaction with high stereoselectivity under the photoenzymatic catalysis
with ene reductases. But it seems this reaction only works with three carbons, limiting its synthetic applications. It did not
work with four carbon atoms, possibly due the stability of six-membered cyclic radical intermediate. It is not clear whether it
works with other numbers of carbon atoms, that may be dependent on the ability of forming the cyclic intermediate and the
stability of the intermediate. The computational analysis may address this point. 
Table 2, CsER or GluER is noted in the footnote, but it is not noted which one under each product. It should be noted under
each product for clarity.
