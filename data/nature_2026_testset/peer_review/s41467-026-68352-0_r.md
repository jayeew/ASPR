Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors of this manuscript present the synthesis and study of electrochemically reconstructed PtPdBiSn nanoplates
catalysts for ethanol electro-oxidation. It is shown that the reconstruction-induced confined transformation causes an
interfacial electron flux redistribution, resulting in a noble metal mass activity of up to 28.17 A mg-1 for EOR, 18.4 times
higher than that of commercial Pt/C. Computational studies confirm the enhanced activity due to optimized adsorption and
reduced energy barriers. The characterization of the materials is exceptional, with very high-quality AC-TEM and in situ
IRAS studies. The reported performance far surpasses that of other catalysts documented in the literature. The authors
demonstrate the significant advantage of this unconventional structure in enhancing the ethanol oxidation reaction. 
The article is suitable for publication in Nature Communications after addressing the following issues. 
1. For Zeta potential measurements, the small difference observed over a relatively wide measurement range for Zeta
potentials should be validated by at least three experimental replicates. 
2. The authors propose that Sn dissolution promotes the heterophase transformation. While the catalyst’s initial composition
is provided, ICP-MS analysis of the post-reconstruction solution is recommended to further verify elemental changes during
reconstruction. This is common in experiments for electrochemical reconstructions. 
3. For Fig. 5e-g, potential mislabeling of the first and second cycles in the CO stripping. Additionally, the origin of the
catalyst’s exceptional CO tolerance requires clarification. 
4. While detailed XPS data (including deconvolution plots) are provided in the Supplementary Information, a brief analysis of
XPS results should be incorporated into the main text.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript reports the design and electrochemical evaluation of a novel PtPdBiSn nanoplates catalyst that undergoes
a confined heterostructural transformation from an ordered hexagonal close-packed (HCP)/face-centered cubic (FCC)
configuration to a disordered non-close-packed hexagonal (H-nCP)/FCC structure via electrochemical reconstruction. This
transformation reverses and amplifies interfacial electron flux, strengthening orbital hybridization (Bi-6p/Pt-5d/Pd-4d), shifting
the d-band center closer to the Fermi level, and enhancing catalytic activity for ethanol oxidation reactions (EOR). 
1. The claimed innovation of confined structural transformation lacks substantial novelty as similar electron modulation via
heterostructural design has been previously explored in Pt-based systems. 
2. The distinction between HCP and H-nCP phases is subtle and not convincingly differentiated with unique crystallographic
evidence; FFT patterns and lattice spacing overlap with common phases. 
3. The interfacial electron flux redirection mechanism is speculative, relying heavily on DFT and charge transfer models
without direct in situ validation. 
4. There is no operando spectroscopy (e.g., XAS, XANES) to verify electronic structure evolution during real-time
reconstruction, weakening the central mechanistic claim. 
5. The synthesis involves precise precursor ratios and multiple high-temperature and electrochemical steps, which are
impractical for industrial scale-up. 
6. The manuscript benchmarks only against Pt/C and Pd/C, omitting relevant comparisons with advanced non-Pt catalysts or
Pt-alloy systems that may offer better cost-to-performance ratios. 
7. The authors suggest a “universal paradigm” based on a single alloy system, without extending the concept to any other
bimetallic or trimetallic systems experimentally. 
8. The rationale for choosing Bi and Sn as co-alloying elements lacks depth. Bi/Sn stability and toxicity concerns are not
discussed. 
9. Although the catalyst retains activity over 4,000 cycles, this benchmark is moderate compared to other state-of-the-art
EOR catalysts which exceed 10,000 cycles in recent literature. 
10. All performance claims are limited to half-cell tests in alkaline electrolyte; no data from full-cell DEFC configurations are
presented. 
11. Key electrochemical parameters such as Tafel slopes, electrochemical surface area (ECSA)-normalized activities, and
impedance spectra are missing or superficially discussed. 
12. CO stripping data lacks quantification of charge densities or peak integration to fully support claims of superior CO
tolerance. 
13. Claims of structural transformation reproducibility and performance consistency are made without error bars or statistical
analysis across multiple samples. 
14. Chronoamperometry is limited to 4,000 s, which is insufficient to establish long-term operational robustness under
steady-state conditions. 
15. The claim that Sn leaching drives phase transformation is not directly correlated with thermodynamic or kinetic driving
forces in the manuscript. 
16. While DFT provides insight, many conclusions (e.g., orbital coupling, d-band shifts) are not experimentally confirmed
with techniques like UPS or EELS. 
17. The IR/SEIRAS results are qualitative and lack quantitative peak deconvolution to support claims of intermediate
suppression or enhanced oxidation pathways. 
18. The manuscript overly relies on d-band theory without discussing other potential descriptors of activity, like charge
transfer kinetics or local geometric strain. 
19. The catalyst's activity may be influenced by diffusion effects in the thick electrode film, which are not controlled for or
addressed. 
20. The manuscript’s readability suffers due to repetitive technical jargon and over-explanation of standard concepts.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors have been diligent in performing numerous additional experiments (ICP-MS, long-term stability tests, XAS,
DEFC tests, etc.), which is commendable. However, this "data-heavy" response often sidesteps the core conceptual
weaknesses pointed out by the reviewers. The responses frequently substitute quantity of data for quality of mechanistic
insight, and key issues regarding novelty, evidence, and overstatement persist. The fundamental concerns regarding the
significance, mechanistic evidence, and practical impact of the work are not addressed. More detailed comments: 
1. The authors' defense centers on their claim that achieving a bulk phase transition within a platinum-based hexagonal
phase-change core is a "first." However, this is a subtle technical distinction. The basic concept—altering the core through
electrochemical reconfiguration to enhance electron-shell interactions—is not new. Their response fails to elevate their case
from an incremental materials advance to a conceptual paradigm shift, as is standard for Nature Communications. 
2. While they provide more AC-TEM images and FFT patterns, the distinction between their "H-nCP" and a heavily
disordered or defect-rich HCP phase remains ambiguous. The provided lattice spacing (0.35 nm) is not sufficiently unique to
definitively prove a new, unconventional phase, as it falls within the range of known HCP intermetallics (e.g., they note Pb
HCP has a=0.35 nm). The evidence remains suggestive, not conclusive. 
3. The authors cite KPFM, Zeta potential, and XPS shifts as experimental validation. However, these techniques measure
consequences (changes in surface potential, binding energy) of an electronic redistribution, not the dynamic flux itself. The
central claim of "flux redirection" is still a model inferred from these static, post-reconstruction measurements and DFT,
lacking direct experimental proof of the dynamic process. 
4. The authors' demonstration of a 5x scale-up is a minimal response. Scaling from lab-scale (mg) to true industrial
relevance (g/kg) involves vastly different engineering challenges. Their response acknowledges the procedure's complexity
and essentially defers the issue, stating it's "optimized for fundamental research," which underscores the work's limited
practical applicability. 
5. Adding a comparison table is an improvement, but it is selective and includes many similarly complex, lab-made
multimetallic catalysts. It fails to address why a simpler, more cost-effective catalyst could not be more practical. The
comparison feels curated to show their catalyst in the best light rather than providing a balanced assessment of the field. 
6. Showing HAADF-STEM images of three other quaternary systems (PtPdBiSb, PtBiSnTe, PtPdBiTe) with similar elements
is not a demonstration of a "universal strategy" or "universal paradigm." It merely shows that the same synthetic approach
can be applied to a very narrow class of chemically similar, complex multimetallic systems. 
7. The authors provide a post-hoc rationalization ("Sn has the lowest oxidation potential... creates vacancies") but no direct
thermodynamic or kinetic data to substantiate this as the driving force for the specific phase change to H-nCP. The
explanation is plausible but speculative and lacks the quantitative experimental or theoretical validation requested. 
8. The authors attempt to contextualize the ~80% activity retention after 20,000 cycles by comparing it to other catalysts in a
new table. However, the significant initial activity drop in the chronoamperometry test (from 28.17 to ~1.136 A mg

fully rule out this alternative interpretation.
