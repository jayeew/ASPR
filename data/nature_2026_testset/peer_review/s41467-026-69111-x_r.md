Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript uses linear regression and mechanistic models, and Brazil's publicly available dengue surveillance data
from 2000-2024 to analyze the role of changes in maternal dengue seroprevalence over time in the incidence of neonatal
and infant dengue, both severe and non-severe cases. 
The data and models show an evolving bimodal curve of dengue and
severe dengue in infants during the first year of life, the later peak explained by maternal antibody dependent enhancement
(ADE), and the first explained by the immature neonatal immune system protected when maternal dengue antibody is
present and susceptible to dengue without maternal antibody. The observation of severe dengue in infants with waning
maternal antibody is well established, and thus the increased incidence of severe dengue in infants with increasing viral
transmission in the Americas is not unexpected. The manuscript provides new information on the incidence of dengue in
neonates. 
The methodology and interpretations appear generally sound. 
However, certain aspects of the modeling
approach would benefit from clearer justification and explanation, particularly regarding model selection, equation structure,
and convergence diagnostics. Additionally, the figures and tables would benefit from clearer descriptions and titles, more
detailed legends, and consistent use (and definition) of acronyms to improve interpretability.

REVIEWER COMMENTS 
 
Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
 
The manuscript uses linear regression and mechanistic models, and Brazil's publicly available dengue 
surveillance data from 2000-2024 to analyze the role of changes in maternal dengue seroprevalence over 
time in the incidence of neonatal and infant dengue, both severe and non-severe cases. The data and 
models show an evolving bimodal curve of dengue and severe dengue in infants during the first year of 
life, the later peak explained by maternal antibody dependent enhancement (ADE), and the first explained 
by the immature neonatal immune system protected when maternal dengue antibody is present and 
susceptible to dengue without maternal antibody. The observation of severe dengue in infants with waning 
maternal antibody is well established, and thus the increased incidence of severe dengue in infants with 
increasing viral transmission in the Americas is not unexpected. The manuscript provides new 
information on the incidence of dengue in neonates. The methodology and interpretations appear 
generally sound. However, certain aspects of the modeling approach would benefit from clearer 
justification and explanation, particularly regarding model selection, equation structure, and convergence 
diagnostics. Additionally, the figures and tables would benefit from clearer descriptions and titles, more 
detailed legends, and consistent use (and definition) of acronyms to improve interpretability.  
 
Technical comments: 
1) Main text (introduction), line 51 and references - Strongly suggest include seminal reference on 
maternal antibody and ADE, Kliks et al, Am J Trop Med Hyg. 
DOI:https://doi.org/10.4269/ajtmh.1988.38.411 
We have added the Kliks reference. 
 
2) Why weight the linear regression models (and why by square root of infant cases)? 
We appreciate this question. Weighted regression accounts for heteroskedasticity, which is present 
here as different numbers of infant cases occur in each state year, but we have reduced each state-
year to a single data point. However, at the suggestion of Reviewer 2, we have now removed this 
linear regression analysis, Figure 2, and Table S1 and 2. 
 
3) Some of the methods are a little difficult to follow as written. Before presenting the equations, can the 
authors give a brief justification (or state the equation in less technical terms) for using them? 
We appreciate this comment. We have made efforts throughout the methods to provide extra 
explanation or wording to justify and provide intuition for model equations. 
 
4) Line 462 - Please cite the existing model that was adapted. Can the the authors provide further 
justification for why this model was chosen and adapted.  
We have added the reference and some text to broadly explain the need for extensions: “We 
adapted an existing model for estimating dengue FOI from case data in settings where the four 
dengue virus serotypes co-circulate endemically to better suit Brazilian data. Specifically, we 
extended the model allowing FOI of each of the four serotypes at any time point to differ to reflect 
their asynchronous (re-) emergence in Brazil and the dominance or absence of specific serotypes 
over long stretches of time.” 
 
5) Mechanistic model, lines 148-149 - It is unclear to this reviewer how the authors estimate "behavioral 
protection" to neonates. What are the prior justifications for such an estimate? 
We have updated lines 148-149 to “through “behavioural” protection, i.e. reduced risk in all 
neonates due to reduced exposure to mosquitoes, that has been hypothesized to lead observed low 
incidence of infections in neonates”. We have added a reference here to a paper by Chau et al that 
supports this finding. We have also added the following sentence for clarity: “In both seronegative

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 9 -->

and seropositive children, this protection leads to reduced hazard of infection at birth, and the 
protection decays exponentially as the infant ages.” 
 
6) Lines 487-490, convergence - Why use 12 chains (seems like a lot) and only 2000 iterations (doesn't 
seem like very much)? Can the authors please further show evidence of posterior convergence, i.e.,
