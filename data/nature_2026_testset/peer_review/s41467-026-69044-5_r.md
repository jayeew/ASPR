Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, the authors developed an innovative use of homologous carbon dots to optimize the interfacial layer
between cellulose triacetate and polyamide through homologous matching. It increases both salt rejection from 96.5 % to
99.1 % and permeate flux from 15.2 to 18.3 L·m−2 h−1, far breaking through the “trade-off” in conventional cellulose
membranes. It is interesting and can be consider for publication after the following revisions. 
1. Suggest shortening abbreviations, for example, O-CCDs-0.1, M-CCDs-0.1, P-CCDs-0.1 can be revised to O-CDs-0.1, M-
CDs-0.1, P-CDs-0.1. 
2. Remove the parentheses from the serial numbers of Figures to match the publishing style of NATURE
COMMUNICATIONS. 
3. The dimensions of O-CDs, M-CDs, and P-CDs should be the average size, please be precise. 
4. Some of the fonts in Figure 2f should be enlarged for easier viewing. 
5. Introduction is too lengthy and thus should be rewritten concisely. 
6. It is not clear why CTA is used as a substrate rather than conventional polysulfone and polyethersulfone supports. 
7. How to obtain the particle size distributions of O-CDs, M-CDs, and P-CDs needs to be specified. 
8. More discussion should be carried out in terms of the homologous matching effects of M-CDs on CTA/M-CDs/PA RO
membranes. 
9. The unit of pressure (Mpa) in the text should be corrected to "MPa". 
10. Figure 5, the particle size is recommended to be expressed as the average particle size. 
11. There are some writing errors in this article, for example, “This indicates that the CTA base surface successfully forms a
PA layer, with CDs embedded within the layer”. 
12. More discussion should be carried out in terms of the enhanced salt rejection and permeation mechanism of M-CDs in
CTA-based membranes.
