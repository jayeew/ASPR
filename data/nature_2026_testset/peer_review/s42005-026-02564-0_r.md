Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Peter T. Orban and co-workers report on the transport measurements on the chiral superconductor Mo3Al2C and the so-
called Josephson junctions. The authors report diode effects in both right-handed/left-handed and right-handed/right-handed
devices, but not in single crystals. I acknowledge that investigating superconducting diodes in chiral superconductors or
derived Josephson junctions is a novel and timely topic, which motivated me to carefully review this manuscript. However, I
find that the authors have overstated their conclusions based on the present experimental evidence, which could leave a
misleading impression on readers. 
For example, they claim that a Josephson junction can be formed simply by pressing two crystals together. However, key
signatures of a Josephson junction, such as Shapiro steps or reliable Fraunhofer patterns, are missing. I recognize the
technical difficulty in fabricating Josephson junctions from this material and understand why the authors employed this
uncommon preparation method. Nevertheless, clear evidence such as Shapiro steps (e.g., ref. 18) is a prerequisite before
interpreting their observations as Josephson-junction-based phenomena rather than weak-link superconductivity. 
In genuine Fraunhofer patterns, the critical current is defined as 
Ic(B)=Ic0sin(πphi/phi0)/ (πphi/phi0), where phi=BAeff is the magnetic flux threading the junction area and phi0 is the flux
quantum. This expression predicts a vanishing Ic when (phi/phi0)=nπ, as demonstrated in refs. (11) and (18). In contrast, the
data presented here show no oscillatory behavior of Ic with external magnetic field, which strongly suggests weak-linked
superconductivity rather than a Josephson junction. Based on the current experimental results, I do not find this manuscript
suitable for publication in Communications Physics. 
Additional comments: 
1. The definition of “zero field” is unreliable. In many cases, the maximum Jc can occur at a finite magnetic field due to
collective vortex pinning effects (see Phys. Rev. Lett. 131, 027001). 
2. The authors claim that the applied magnetic field is smaller than Hc1, thus excluding vortex penetration and the possibility
of a vortex-diode effect. This claim is not correct. The reported Hc1 is extracted from diamagnetism measurements without
transport current, reflecting higher superconducting phase stiffness. Under applied current, the phase stiffness is strongly
suppressed, so the effective Hc1 depends on the transport conditions. Even at real zero field, vortex–antivortex pairs can
penetrate and suppress the self-field Jc. 
3. If indeed no vortices are present, then no Josephson effect should be observed. From the formula above, the relevant field
scale H0 should be consistent with the junction size, but such analysis is missing. 
4. If the symmetry breaking originates from chirality, rotational symmetry constraints dictate that only the I
⋅
B term is allowed
in nonreciprocal transport (see ref. 7, Nat. Commun. 9, 3740). Why, then, is the diode efficiency nearly absent in Fig. 12
when I parallel to B? 
5. In lines 125–127, the comparison should be made in terms of current density Jc across different devices, rather than total
current Ic. 
6. The figures and data presentation could be significantly improved. More clearly organized figures, along with careful and
professional data analysis, would considerably enhance the quality of the manuscript.

(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript entitled “The superconducting diode effect in Josephson junctions fabricated from structurally chiral

Mo3Al2C” from P. T. Orban et. al. describes observation of supercurrent diode effect (SDE) in bi-crystal Josephson junctions
between two Mo3Al2C single crystals with the same structural chirality or opposite structural chiralities. Josephson diode is
an emerging research topic which, compared to conventional semiconductor p-n diode, demonstrates unique I-V
characteristics that are of broad interest in low energy consumption electronic device applications such as quantum
computers. However, after reading carefully through the manuscript, I feel that many crucial questions remain unanswered,
and thus the main conclusion of the work is not sufficiently supported. Therefore, I would not recommend potential
acceptance of the present manuscript to be published in Communications Physics. 
In the following, I will describe the details of my concerns and comments. 
1. The authors normalized the applied field to H0 which is associated with the junction area. However, the modulation field
in a Fraunhofer pattern is usually normalized to the quantum flux Phi0. I understand that the penetration depth of the
superconductor may not be well understood, but a reasonable estimate of that (~100 nm according to various reports) would
provide more information regarding the quality (esp. uniformity) of these bi-crystal contacts. Given the description of junction
fabrication method, I believe that these Josephson junctions are in the large/wide junction limit, so the supercurrent flowing
between the two single crystals is very likely not uniform. 
2. Following the comment above, for junctions in the large/wide junction limit, the critical current is often asymmetric in
opposite directions (Ic+ and Ic-) at a fixed magnetic field, including zero field. However, such kind of asymmetric behavior in
the critical current is not considered as SDE. 
3. In Fig. 5b, it seems like that the critical currents (blue and pink dots) are very symmetric at any given low field, but the light-
blue dots and the solid curve indicate an asymmetric percentage up to 5%. 
The authors may check if the values are correct. 
4. An important aspect for Josephson effect is the product of Ic and Rn (the normal state resistance) of Josephson junctions.
Due to the quasi-4 terminal method adopted here, the I-V curves always have a slope at zero current which corresponds to
the contact resistance. However, we can still estimate the Rn from the change in the slopes below and above the critical
current. It is even more obvious in Fig. 3, where the decrease in the resistance near Tc is Rn. Given Rn values of ~ 10 Ohm
and ~ 25 Ohm for device 2 and device 3, respectively, the zero-field IcRn would be roughly 0.6 mV for device 2 and 4.0 mV
for device 3. These are very high values (especially for device 3) given the Tc of the superconductor is only ~ 9 K. The
authors should be aware of this unusually high IcRn product. 
5. 
The authors claim that the low-field peak observed from device 3 broadens as the field is rotated out of the plane in SI
Figure 10b. Yes, when the field rotates away from the interface plane, the modulation of the critical current should gradually
vanish following a cosine functon(the angle should be the field direction with respect to the plane). However, given the
measurement uncertainty, the broadening of the peak is not obvious at all. In addition, in SI Figure 10a, the modulation
features became completely undistinguishable when the field rotates only 20 degrees away from the in-plane direction,
rather than showing an expected 6% reduction in their amplitudes.

Report of 2nd round review 
Both rectification/diode effect and chirality induced spin selectivity (CISS) are manifestations of symmetry breaking but arise
from very different mechanisms. There is little overlap between these two topics, likely limited by the availability of suitable
chiral materials. By invoking CISS induced time reversal symmetry breaking, the authors realize a superconducting diode
effect without an external magnetic field. This is the key message this work brings to the community. While further
investigation in other systems is desirable, this study bridges two important phenomena and opens a new direction to
achieve zero field superconducting diodes. 
The authors have straightforwardly and carefully addressed all reviewer comments from the first round, making this revision
much more reliable and attractive than the previous draft. I appreciate the honest replies and the substantial effort. Therefore,
I withdraw my previous negative opinion and now support publication in Communications Physics. I believe no additional
experiments are required. However, the main text and figures deserve more attention before formal acceptance. 
1. Since this paper will be included in the collection of superconducting diodes, it would be better to state explicitly in the first
sentence of the abstract that time reversal symmetry is broken. Note that spin rotation symmetry SU(2) can be broken by
Rashba SOC while time reversal symmetry is preserved. CISS induced breaking of spin symmetry is not necessarily the
same as time reversal breaking, as the authors already note in lines 271–275. 
2.The logic flow is clear to me after several careful readings: superconducting diodes are observed only in junctions
fabricated from opposite chirality and are absent in single chiral crystals or junctions made from the same chirality. However,
it is redundant to present 8 main text figures and 2 tables to support such a concise story. The authors also include 25
supplementary figures in the SI, which makes review unnecessarily burdensome — especially when done over the
weekend. For example, what does “Check notebook” mean in SI Table 3? Please remove meaningless or unnecessary
figures such as SEM images 24 and 25, and reduce repeated raw I–V transition data in the supplementary material. More
importantly, consider moving less essential main figures (for example, Fig. 3) to the Supplementary Information. A more
concise, cleaner presentation will increase the paper’s readability and impact. 
If the above points are addressed, I strongly recommend publication of this manuscript.
