Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This study presents the fabrication of loosely structured macrocycle-assembled membranes as a promising approach for
effective dye/salt separation in high-salinity organic wastewater treatment. The authors systematically evaluate the optimal
concentrations of MPD and TACA, along with reaction time, to optimize membrane formation. The separation performance of
the fabricated membranes is subsequently assessed, revealing high rejection rates for a broad range of organic dyes (most
exceeding 99%). Furthermore, molecular-level mechanisms are provided to substantiate the observed separation behavior.
Overall, the research strategy is well-conceived, and the methodology is clearly described. The results are thoroughly
presented and interpreted, offering strong support for the conclusions. I recommend this article for publication after revisions.
Detailed comments are as follows: 
1. In lines 216-219, it is mentioned that “A negative surface charge is observed across a wide pH range for the TACA-MPD
membrane (Fig. 3g), attributed to the electron-rich cavity of TACA and its phenolic hydroxyl groups. This negative charge
can promote strong electrostatic repulsion against negatively charged organic molecules, such as organic dyes, and
enhance their rejection.” Given that the surface charge becomes positive below pH 5, does this imply that the TACA-MPD
membrane should be operated at pH ≥ 5 to maintain a negatively charged surface and thus achieve effective electrostatic
exclusion of anionic organic dyes? 
2. In Fig. 3h and 3j, the effects of varying MPD and TACA concentrations on membrane performance are examined. To
improve clarity and practical applicability, it is recommended that the authors specify the optimal concentration ranges of
MPD and TACA identified in this study. Providing these ranges would help readers better understand the design window for
achieving favorable membrane performance. 
3. In Fig. 4d, the long-term filtration performance of the membrane is evaluated. Although the permeance and rejection of the
mixed solution demonstrate exceptional long-term operational stability, could the authors clarify whether any
physicochemical or structural changes occurred in the membrane material after prolonged operation? Specifically, were
there any post-filtration characterizations (e.g., SEM, FTIR) conducted to assess potential fouling, degradation, or structural
integrity loss? 
4. While the fabrication of TACA–MPD membranes demonstrates excellent dye/salt separation performance, both MPD and
TACA (or their precursors) may pose potential risks to human health and the environment due to their chemical toxicity and
reactivity. Have the authors considered the environmental and occupational safety aspects associated with the use of these
monomers, particularly in terms of residuals, disposal, and potential leaching from the membrane? 
5. In lines 404–406, the conclusion states that “This combination enables energy-efficient diafiltration without the need for
high hydraulic pressures commonly required in traditional processes.” However, the manuscript does not present any
quantitative or statistical data on energy consumption to support this claim. Could the authors clarify or provide relevant data
to substantiate this conclusion?

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This article reports development of macrocyclic water treatment membranes via a new diffusion-assisted interfacial
polymerization (IP) method. Nanoporous films containing calixarene macrocycles with intrinsic internal cavities exhibited a
noteworthy combination of high dye rejection and high permeances for separation of aqueous salt and dye mixtures (Fig
4E). While incorporation of aligned macrocycles into polymer membranes has been previously reported for similar small
molecule separations, this work establishes new methods of controlled film formation with enhanced control over membrane
microstructure and increased separation performance. The use of a Kevlar hydrogel as a substrate for interfacial
polymerization is innovative, and the authors clearly demonstrated how directional polymer growth can be controlled at the
organic solvent interface. These materials could have potential for use as high performance sieving membranes for current
and emerging water treatment applications. 
Experimental and simulation methods are sufficiently detailed to aid reproducibility. 
The conclusions that these materials exhibit high separation performance are supported by evidence provided. Conclusions
about how macrocycle incorporation and diffusion-assisted IP affect molecular diffusion pathways and how those in turn
affect macroscopic transport still require additional supporting evidence. 
Recommended revisions or clarifications: 
1. It would help the reader to clarify the difference between “unidirectional” diffusion for interfacial polymerization (IP) and the
conventional method of IP which also primarily occurs via growth in one dimension perpendicular to the membrane’s
surface. The authors observed nodular structures on TACA membranes – how do these compare with nodular structures of
other IP membranes? 
2. Does TACA remain as nanoparticles during IP or is it fully soluble at the concentrations used? If so, is there a solubility
limit? 
3. Can the authors comment on the reversibility of dynamic imine linkages and the impact on mechanical and chemical
stability in harsh operating conditions (e.g., acidic or basic solutions, exposure to bleach during washing and regeneration)?
Why are these linkages expected to be stable under practical operating conditions based on previous literature or new
observations? 
4. The study would benefit from more clear comparisons with control materials to better establish the mechanism of
separation improvement. How do the permeances and rejections of membranes formed by “unidirectional diffusion-assisted
IP” differ from those formed either (A) from the same monomers by conventional IP using other substrates like PES or (B)
from conventional monomers such as TMC+MPD using the diffusion-assisted Kevlar hydrogel substrates (refs 32 and 33)?
Can the authors quantify any flux enhancement for macrocycle membranes formed by diffusion-assisted IP compared to
similar membranes without macrocycles or using conventional IP? This would provide strong evidence that the resulting
structures are distinctly unique. 
5. Additional support is needed for the existence of “high-speed water transport pathway”s through the macrocycle internal
cavities which was mostly speculated. Simulations showed network interconnectivity and porosity affected small molecule
transport (around the macrocycles), but that is a distinctly different diffusion pathway from transport through the macrocycles.
How many of the molecular trajectories in MD passed through the TACA macrocycles and how many went around them? Is
this consistent with any observed flux enhancement compared to a control material without internal macrocyclic cavities? 
6. Please add citations for the structures shown in Figure 4E. 
7. Please clarify if “permeances” in Figure 4 subplots are solute or water permeances.

Response: Thank you for your insightful comment. The rejection of dye molecules by the 
TFC membrane is primarily attributed to the synergistic effects of size sieving and the

has a relatively large MWCO of 11480 Da and a pore size of 3.4 nm, the negative charges

retained by the membrane pores through enhanced size exclusion. Both experimental 
results and molecular dynamics simulations confirm the efficient rejection of various dye 
molecules by the TFC membrane. We have rewritten this part to make our description and 
explanation much clearer.

6. Please provide more details on the relationship between partition coefficients, 
hydrophilicity, and monomer diffusion or reaction regions. 
Response: Thank you for your valuable comment. The partition coefficient refers to the 
ratio of the equilibrium concentrations of a compound in the organic and aqueous phases, 
denoted as log P. It reflects the compound's relative affinity for the organic and aqueous 
phases and is widely used to describe its lipoph ilicity or hydrophobicity. A higher log P 
value indicates a stronger affinity for the organic phase (oil), whereas a lower value 
suggests a stronger affinity for the aqueous phase (water) (Sun, C. (2018). Partitioning and 
Partition Coefficients.; ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces 17, 47659-47671 (2025); Anal. 
Chem. 91, 10371-10375 (2019)). 
The log P of TACA between dichloromethane and water is 3.33, indicating strong 
lipophilicity and poor water solubility. In contrast, MPD has a log P value of -0.45, 
demonstrating good hydrophilicity. According to the calculation formula of log P, MPD 
also exhibits certain solubility in the oil phase. As a result, MPD can diffuse from the 
aqueous phase to the oil-water interface and into the oil phase, while TACA is directionally 
distributed at the oil -water interface, where it reacts with MPD through a Schiff base 
condensation reaction. During the interfacial polymerization between MPD and TACA, 
the reaction is confined to the oil-water interface. Furthermore, during the diffusion process 
of MPD, the abundant hydrogen bonds in the Kevlar hydrogel effectively regulate its 
diffusion rate, thereby enhancing the controllability of the interfacial polymerization. In 
response to the reviewer’s suggestion, we have incorporated additional details as presented 
below. 
Page 8, Line 251-254:  
“The partition coefficient ( logP) is a measure of the relative distribution of a compound 
between an organic phase and an aqueous phase. A higher logP value corresponds to an 
increased lipophilicity and preferential partitioning into the organic medium, while a lower 
value denotes enhanced aqueous solubility and a dominant affinity for the aqueous phase.”

Response: Thank you for your insightful and constructive suggestions. The differences 
between the two methods and their unique features are outlined below. 
Traditional interfacial polymerization is the primary method for fabricating polyamide 
TFC membranes. In this process: (i) the support membrane first adsorbs the aqueous phase 
and retains a certain amount of amine monomer. It then directly contacts the organic phase 
to form a liquid -liquid interface. (ii) Highly reactive monomers rapidly diffuse to the 
interface and undergo random, irreversible cross-linking reactions. Once the polymer film 
begins to form, it restricts further diffusion of monomers, resulting in a self-limiting effect 
that inhibits additional growth of the polymer film ( Chem. Commun. 59, 13258 -13271 
(2023); Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 59, 21840 -21856 (2020) ). This typically results in the 
formation of a relatively thick and dense selective layer wi thin a very short time ( Chem. 
Soc. Rev. 50, 6290-6307 (2021)). 
Unidirectional diffusion assisted interfacial polymerization uses a diffusion cell 
device where a base membrane is secured between two chambers, each containing aqueous 
and organic phase monome r solutions. This method relies on the directional diffusion of 
monomers to facilitate interfacial polymerization ( J. Membr. Sci. 663, 121043 (2022); J. 
Membr. Sci. 604, 118090 (2020)). In this process: (i) The porous support prevents direct 
contact between the two phases, slows the monomer diffusion rate, and thereby makes the 
reaction process more controllable. (ii) Compared to traditional in -situ interfacial 
polymerization, the unidirectional diffusion -assisted mode ensures a more sustained and 
sufficient monomer supply for film formation. (iii) Longer reaction times promote self -
healing of the membrane structure, significantly minimizing defect formation and 
facilitating the formation of defect-free thin films.

Response: Thanks for your constructive suggestions on this manuscript. As a dynamic 
covalent bond, the imine bond strikes a balance between reversibility and stability. While 
higher reversibility facilitates defect repair and structural rearrangement in the network, 
driving it towards a thermodynamically stable state, excessive reversibility can 
compromise the stability of the material (Chem. Soc. Rev. 49, 8469-8500 (2020); Advanced 
Science 6, 1801410 (2019); ACS Nano 19, 29934 -29960 (2025)). Because of the unique 
reversibility and structural stability of imine bonds, these bonds exhibit promising 
applications in membrane separation. 
 Numerous studies demonstrate that imine -based membrane materials possess 
excellent mechanical and chemical stability under harsh operating conditions. Wang et al. 
prepared imine COF membranes via Schiff base reaction of tetrakis(4 -formylphenyl)-
methane wi th 3,3' -Dihydroxybenzidine ( Nano Lett. 21, 8355 -8362 (2021) ). Due to the 
reversibility of the Schiff base reaction, COF microcrystals tend to coalesce with 
neighboring crystals, forming larger crystals through the breaking and rebinding of imine 
linkages. The simultaneous deposition of crystals and microcrystals onto the substrate 
facilitates horizontal growth of the COF, leading to continuous film formation. The COF 
films exhibit outstanding flexibility and exceptional tolerance to a wide range of extreme 
conditions (e.g., hot organic solvents). Wen et al. fabricated cross-linked crown-ether-based 
polyimine nanofilms through interfacial polymerization of p -phenylenediamine and 
4,4′,5,5′-terabenzaldehyde dibenzo-14-crown-4 (Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 62, e2023001 67 
(2023)). The incorporation of flexible crown ether units into the rigid aryl conjugated 
skeleton chains forms a “flexible-in-rigid” structural framework within the polyimine 
membrane. The membrane exhibits both robustness and flexibility, demonstrating excellent 
mechanical properties (Young's modulus ca. 8.2 GPa). Zachary P. Smith and colleagues 
used triaminotriptycene and tetraformylspirobifluorene as building blocks to fabricate 
microporous polyimine TFC membranes via interfacial polymerization (Science 388, 839-
844 (2025)) . By incorporating molecular units with rigid and contorted structures, the 
microporosity and hydrophobicity of the membrane were significantly enhanced. The 
polyimine membrane achieved exceptionally high swelling and plasticization r esistance, 
remarkable operational stability, and great potential for crude oil fractionation processes. 
The studies cited above collectively demonstrate that polyimine membranes maintain 
excellent mechanical and chemical stability even under harsh conditions.
