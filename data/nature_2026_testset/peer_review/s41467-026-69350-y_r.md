Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript offers significant and innovative insights into how tumor metabolism influences immune evasion through TIL
ferroptosis mediated by STING and HO-1. Zhe et al. show compelling data indicating that TME lactate production drives the
activation of STING signaling in tumor-infiltrating CD8+ lymphocytes (CD8 TILs). This leads to the upregulation of TOX and
downstream repression of HO-1 in TILs, which subsequently results in ferroptosis of TILs and reduced tumor control. The
study has numerous strengths, including elegant gain- and loss-of-function experiments demonstrating the feed-forward loop
between STING and TOX, adoptive cell therapy data showing the advantage of using TILs with low levels of STING to
achieve better tumor control, combinatorial therapy experiments illustrating the benefit of combining this ACT modality with
checkpoint blockade or chemotherapy, and human data suggesting the prognostic value of HO-1 levels in patients’ tumors.
Although the studies are mechanistically robust and the data is of value to the field, the significance of this study could
benefit from clarifying some points. 
Major Concerns: 
1. Although the experiments in Fig. 5 demonstrate a link between lactate dose and ferroptosis, they do not show that lactate
is a major factor driving T cell functionality or survival in the tumor environment. A recent report by Peralta et al., Nat.
Immunol. 2024, demonstrated that reducing lactic acid uptake by exhausted T cells enhances their function and decreases
tumor burden. Furthermore, T cells uniquely induce the expression of MCT1, and blockade of this receptor inhibits lactate
uptake in TILs, resulting in improved tumor control. It would be important for the authors to investigate whether such a
blockade in vivo recapitulates the mechanism described by the authors in the manuscript. 
2. The authors show that deletion of STING or TOX in CD8 T cells improves their effector function, reduces exhaustion, and
increases T cell survival. However, as noted in point 1, it is unclear whether the reduced tumor burden associated with low
levels of STING and TOX is due to enhanced TIL function, increased TIL survival within the tumor, or greater TIL recruitment
from the draining lymph node to the tumor. It would be important to determine whether the changes in ferroptosis and TIL
survival observed in vitro occur over time in vivo as tumor burden decreases. Differentiating between increased recruitment
and improved survival is important because it could indicate whether reducing STING and/or only TOX leads to a higher
frequency of tumor-specific CD8 T cells with a progenitor phenotype that promotes continuous recruitment, or whether it
enhances the survival of existing effector T cells within the tumor. Do TILs in the tumor show increased expression of
survival markers? Does the number of TILs in the tumor remain constant over time? Does it increase because they
proliferate in the tumor or because there is an increase in recruitment? 
3. The authors mention previous studies implicating STING and ER stress-mediated apoptosis in T cells. A recent study by
Quaney et al. indicated that activation of STING signaling in T cells induces apoptosis through ER stress and CHOP. Since
CHOP, ER stress, and ferroptosis are interconnected, the authors should at least incorporate this information into their
discussion to provide a more comprehensive view of the mechanisms involved. 
Minor but important concerns:

5. Fig. 6: As a minor point, it would be helpful to specify whether the low tumor burden values are maintained before 21 days

(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript submitted by Zhu et al. investigates the interconnection between STING signaling and Transcription factor
TOX in CD8 T cells and how this modulate the antitumoral effects. This is a nice continuum from a prior publication by the
senior authours focusing on STING and FOXP3 in CD4 T cells (JITC 2022). Overall, the manuscript has multiple interesting
findings and the linkage between STING and TOX regulation in T cells and the mechanism of cell death driven by
ferroptosis is highly novel. But based on the current quality and amount of data presented in the manuscript I do not find it
adequate for publication. 
Generally, I encourage the authors to softening the language, so statements are less bold and try not to make attempt to use
data to make overstatements. 
There are numerous data panels that lack significant controls to support the findings. Also, the use of DMXAA as a STING

(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors present a novel mechanism by which the STING and TOX signaling pathways promote ferroptosis in CD8
⁺
 T
cells within the tumor microenvironment, thereby limiting their antitumor function and contributing to immunotherapy
resistance. Through a combination of genetic models, transcriptomic profiling, and in vivo therapeutic interventions, the
study proposes the STING–TOX–HO-1 axis as a key regulator of CD8
⁺
 T cell fate and highlights its potential as a
therapeutic target in cancer immunotherapy. The study is novel and employs state-of-the-art techniques and methods. The
data are relevant and scientifically sound, with clear clinical significance. However, there are several concerns that need to
be addressed by the authors before the manuscript can be accepted for publication in the journal. 
Major comments 
1. The manuscript should also address why CD8
⁺
 T cells, in particular, are highly susceptible to ferroptosis compared to
other immune cells. Previous studies have shown that myeloid-derived suppressor cells are also highly sensitive to
ferroptosis. Therefore, the authors should analyze other immune and stromal cell populations in the TME to determine the
specificity of the proposed STING–TOX–HO-1–ferroptosis axis. 
2. It is of utmost importance to assess the functional consequences of ferroptotic T lymphocytes on the TME. How do these
dying cells reshape or modulate the immune landscape of the tumor? A thorough analysis of their immunological and
inflammatory impact is needed. 
4. The authors should provide mechanistic evidence on how lactate is sensed by CD8
⁺
 T cells. Additionally, it is unclear
why CD8
⁺
 T cells are particularly sensitive or responsive to lactate concentrations within the TME. 
5. It is not clear from the study how generalizable these findings are or whether the described phenomenon applies to other
cancer types. Most of the work is conducted on a single tumor model (TC1). To strengthen the main conclusions, the authors
should validate their findings in additional cancer models, ideally of different tissue origins.

updated analyses ( Fig. 1c and Extended Data Fig. 2c ), the frequency of stem -like 
(PD1⁺TIM3⁻CXCR5⁺) CD8 ⁺ TILs were increased, whereas that of exhausted precursor

proliferating CD8⁺ TILs relative to WT mice, suggesting a more proliferative intratumoral CD8⁺ T-
cell compartment, special in Tox-cKO mice. To clarify whether the accumulation of CD8 ⁺ T cells 
in the tumor microenvironment (TME) of Sting1- and Tox-deficient mice results from  enhanced

involved in CD8⁺ T-cell trafficking. Transcriptomic analysis revealed that Cx3cr1, Ccr5, and Cxcr6

recruitment in these cKO mice. To further delineate the temporal dynamics of CD8⁺ TILs from TC-

sequencing and flow cytometry. Sting1- and Tox-cKO mice exhibited markedly reduced tumor 
burden at days 20 and 30, with a non -significant decreasing trend at earlier time points ( New Fig.

reveal significant differences in CD8⁺ T-cell activation or proliferation between WT and these cKO 
mice (Extended Data Fig. 5a).  
Collectively, these results indicate that the increased accumu lation of CD8 ⁺ T cells in tumors of 
Sting1- and Tox-deficient mice is primarily driven by enhanced resistance to ferroptosis rather than 
by augmented recruitment or proliferative capacity within the TME.
