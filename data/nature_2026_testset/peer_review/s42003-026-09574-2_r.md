Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors demonstrated that 1) beta3 adrenoceptor (beta3 AR) in the brown adipose tissue (BAT) was increased
significantly in male chronic social defeat stress (CSDS) model susceptible (SUS) mice, 2) peripherally administered beta3
AR agonist (CL316243) caused depressive-like behaviors such as increase of immobility during forced swimming test and
confirmed by beta3AR antagonist, 3) SDS induced IL-6 in the BAT, and BAT may be source of serum IL-6, 4) anatomical
study by viral tracing from the BAT revealed that neural pathway between the brain and the BAT are involved in the PVH,
LH, DMH and PAG, 5) chemogenetic approach to the PVH that expressed hM3Dq activated by CNO showed that PVH
activation cause depressive-like behaviors via beta3 AR and IL-6. This study is very interesting and well-designed to
combine with behavioral study and molecular study. However, there are several points that should be clarified as below. 
1. Although beta3 AR is expressed abundantly in the BAT, beta3AR is also expressed in the other tissues. It should make
clear why beta3 AR in the BAT is focused in this study. 
2. In general, BAT is known to be involved in the regulation of body temperature (BT). Did you measure BT in this study? 
3. How do you explain the relationship between IL-6 production in the BAT, serum IL-6 and depressive-like behaviors? 
4. The PVH is heterogenous and divided into the several sub-divisions, such as the magnocellular neurons projecting to the
posterior pituitary, parvocellular neurons projecting to the internal layer of the median eminence, and the pre-autonomic
neurons project to the brainstem and spinal cord. In this study, CNO may also activate these neurons and cause secretion of
posterior pituitary hormones and activate HPA axis. This point should be clarified. 
5. In this study, sodium pentobarbital was used for anesthetizing, however, this is not standard recently. 
6. Page 3, line 54: live should be liver. 
7. Page 4, line 81: the paraventricular hypothalamus should be the paraventricular hypothalamus (PVH). 
8. Page 15, line 320: administrated should be administered. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This paper presents evidence for a brown adipose tissue, beta-3 adrenoreceptor-induced IL-6 secretion that is involved in
social stress-induced behavioral effects seen in stress-susceptible but not stress-resilient mice. Stress susceptibility was
associated with an increase in b3AR expression in BAT and pharmacological b3AR activation and chemogenetic
stimulation of hypothalamic paraventricular nucleus neurons induced depression-like behavior and increased BAT b3AR
expression and IL-6 secretion in the blood. The findings reveal an important hypothalamic control of depression-like
behavior in the mouse via regulation of b3AR control of IL-6 secretion, which provides an interoceptive mechanism to
regulate affective behavior. The study has many positive and interesting elements, but I have several general concerns that
limit my confidence in the main interpretation of hypothalamic PVN control of depression-like behavior via BAT b3AR
activation. 
Major criticisms: 
Three main concerns: 1) insufficient distinction between central vs. BAT b3AR roles in depression-like behaviors; 2)

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 2 -->

activation of PVH outputs is non-specific; 3) PRV jumps synapses, so the presynaptic structures shown can be mono- or
polysynaptically connected to the BAT; PVH efferents cannot be responsible for activating b3AR’s in the BAT. 
1) The pharmacological stimulation of depression-like behaviors with a b3AR agonist that crosses the blood-brain barrier but
the lack of inhibition by a b3AR antagonist that does not suggests a significant role in the depression-like behavior of central
adrenergic signaling, i.e., that does not involve the BAT. This goes against the overall premise of the study. There needs to
be a clear distinction of the peripheral BAT role from the central role of noradrenergic signaling, which is well known to
regulate depression-like behaviors. 
Since 6-OHDA and the b3AR antagonist reversed the social defeat-induced IL-6 increase, this effect was due to peripheral
NE activation of b3AR. But the effect of b3AR agonist CL on social behavior was probably central, since the antagonist that
does not cross the BBB had no effect on the behavior. The fact that it also caused the increase in IL-6 through a BAT-
dependent mechanism suggests that it acts peripherally on the BAT b3ARs and centrally on depression-like behavior. Was
the effect of the agonist on depression-like behavior tested for its dependence on the BAT by performing BATectomy? 
2) The PVH houses a heterogeneous population of pre-autonomic and neuroendocrine cells that project to multiple targets
in the brain and pituitary and are involved in the regulation of multiple stress-like behaviors. The chemogenetic activation of
PVH neurons is non-specific and should cause multiple phenotypes if all the PVH cells were infected. There is no
consideration given to the heterogeneity or complexity of this system and its outputs. The PVH chemogenetic stimulation of
depression-like behavior that is reversed by 6-OHDA is not causally linked to BAT b3AR activation other than by the
increase in IL-6 levels. This causal link between the PVH and b3ARs in the BAT is insufficiently made. 
3) The PVH does not contain any nor/adrenergic neurons, so it cannot be responsible for the direct stimulation of the BAT
via b3AR activation. The pseudo-rabies virus jumps synapses, so it must have retrogradely traced a multi-synaptic
projection from the PVH. There is no mention of the LC or of the other main source of NE, the NTS, in the presynaptic
structures labeled with the PRV. Were these structures not labeled? If not, how do the authors propose that the PVH
activates b3AR’s in the BAT? Also, the PRV is toxic over time. How much cell death was there after 5 days of infection and
how might this affect outcomes? 
Was there any way of distinguishing monosynaptic vs. polysynaptic connections in your
results? At the very least, the issue of poly- vs. monosynaptic connections with the PRV needs to be addressed. 
Minor concerns: 
Is the CD1 aggressor mouse in the CSDS paradigm a different mouse or the same CD1 mouse every day? 
Provide the commercial source of the PRV. 
On line 182, if the CNO was injected IP, this needs to be stated. Was there a negative control for the effect of CNO? 
Line 188, the composition of the ACSF needs to be defined. 
Figure 3G does not accurately illustrate the paradigm. The days of CSDS that included SR injection should be depicted. 
It is not clear how the results of the GO analysis in Fig. 4A show that genes related to IL-6 production are upregulated more
than the others. Similarly, in the gene set analysis in Fig. 4B, it is not clear how IL-6 is implicated. I see positive regulation of
IL-1b and Mmp8, but not IL-6. In fact, IL-6 does not appear to be in the graph at all. How was the conclusion of an increase in
IL-6 come to? Were the CSDS-resilient mice run in this comparison, too, and if so, did they differ from the susceptible mice?
Assuming they exist, these data should also be presented. 
It would be informative to include the full Western blots instead of cutting out the bands. This could be included in the
supplemental figures. 
Are all the structures labeled retrogradely by the BAT PRV injections listed? What part of the cortex was labeled? Was the
LC or NTS labeled? Other nuclei of the brainstem than the PAG, e.g., the RVLM? If there is selective listing of the
presynaptic structures, what are the criteria for making it onto the list? Again, mono- vs. polysynaptic connections? 
The action potentials in Fig. 5 are only a few mV in amplitude, and the afterhyperpolarizations are about the same size. This
appears to be an extracellular recording and not a whole-cell recording. The Methods say that cell-attached patch recordings
were performed. This needs to be clarified in lines 393-95. 
How was mRNA measured following CNO activation, by patch-seq, RNAscope ISH, qPCR, etc.? 
Repeat 6-OHDA administration details on lines 413-14, i.e., route, amount, timing with respect to CNO injection. 
In the Discussion, “We also identify a neural input from the PVH as a key brain region target that regulates depressive-like
behaviors by targeting BAT β3AR and its downstream IL-6 signaling.” This seems implausible, since the PVH input to the
BAT is not adrenergic.

Du et al demonstrated that the SNS-β3AR pathway in BAT plays a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of depression. Elevated
levels of TH and β3AR proteins were observed in BAT of susceptible mice following CSDS. Activation of β3AR is
responsible for increased peripheral IL-6 levels and promotes susceptibility to depressive-like behaviors. Excitation of the
PVH-BAT axis enhances SNS-β3AR-IL-6 pathway and social avoidance behaviors. Collectively, these findings provide
direct evidence for a brain-BAT axis that detects social stress, leading to increased peripheral IL-6 levels and heightened
vulnerability to social stress. However, there are still several problems in this study. 
1. Lines 311-314 “Consistently, our immunofluorescent staining and immunoblotting data demonstrated a significant

their controls (CTRL) or the CSDS-resilient (RES) mice (Fig. 1F and G).” As far as I know, sympathetic activity is marked by
norepinephrine or epinephrine, but not TH. How about the expression of NE/NA? 
2. Du et al showed that Pharmacological activation of β3AR facilitates depressive-like behaviors. But β3AR agonist
CL316243 (CL, 1 mg/kg) was given by intraperitoneally injection. Of course, these results suggest that activation of β3AR
induces depressive-like behaviors in naive mice, and promotes susceptibility to CSDS-evoked depressive-like behaviors.
But, Intraperitoneal injection has affected many peripheral organs, how can it be proved that BAT is playing a role? 
3. “Pharmacological inhibition of β3AR did not affect depressive-like behaviors”, Please explain in more detail the possible
reasons for this result in the discussion. 
4. IL-6 were detected by WB, but as a secret protein, ELISA may be a better method. 
5. Lines 371-372 “and western blotting experiment found that SD induced increase in IL-6 was significantly reversed 6-
OHDA” instead by “……reversed by 6-OHDA” 
6. Lines 369-370 
“6-hydroxydopamine (6-OHDA), an extensively used agent for eliminating SNS activity in BAT without
affecting the central nervous system, was intraperitoneally injected into SD mice”. Why is intraperitoneal injection only aimed
at BAT? Additionally, experimental evidence is needed to prove that it indeed eliminates the effect of sns, such as detecting
changes in the content of NE/NA. 
7. The result of c-fos is better placed after PRV-tracing, as it increases the evidence for your choice to do PVH. Then you use
chemical genetics to manipulate this brain region, discovering changes in behaviour and molecules.

(Remarks to the Author) 
Du et al have revised their manuscript by adding critical experiments that significantly improve the study and provide
convincing evidence for PVH regulation of depression-like behaviors via control of SNS activation of BAT via a polysynaptic
projection and beta-3 adrenoreceptors and secretion of IL-6. The additional BAT-ectomy experiments greatly increase
confidence that the observed effects are indeed due to PVH-induced peripheral noradrenergic activation of BAT. The
authors have addressed all my major concerns, and I have only minor concerns/remarks remaining. 
The fact that the beta-3 AR antagonist did not block the behavioral effects caused by social defeat stress is still confusing,
and the explanation provided in the Discussion in reference to the antagonist crossing the BBB – “…may explain why
intraperitoneal injection of the β3AR antagonist SR59230A failed to induce antidepressant-like behaviors” – does not make
sense since one would expect it to block the peripheral effects of b3 AR activation nevertheless. This discrepancy in their
findings deserves more explanation than has been provided, since the authors tout their findings as a possible inroad into
alternative therapeutic treatment of depression. 
No controls for the chemogenetic experiments were performed, either CNO injection in a control AAV without the Gq-
DREADD or saline injection in a DREADD-expressing mouse. 
The sentence in the Abstract “Our retrograde tracing data demonstrated a neural innervation of the brown adipose tissue by
the paraventricular hypothalamic nucleus” implies a direct projection, so I suggest changing to “a polysynaptic neural
innervation”. 
Clarifications concerning the PVH - HPA axis activation is mediated by parvocellular neurons and not magnocellular
neurons (lines 478-80), and the parvocellular neurons project to the external layer of the median eminence, not the internal
layer. 
The HPA activation was not very remarkable, as stated in line 486, but rather modest compared to stress activation.

Yes, the connection between PVH and  BAT is likely polysynaptic. To support this notion,
