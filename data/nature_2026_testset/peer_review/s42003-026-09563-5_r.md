Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Summary: 
This study is looking at resting state EEG measurements from individuals diagnosed with Autism and Controls in three
different phases of drug administration of a GABA agonist drug: Placebo, Low and High dose. Building on past task-based
research the authors are hypothesizing that GABAergic differences, reminiscent of the Excitation-Inhibition imbalance
hypothesis, will be evident in sensory areas, however the analysis extends in the entire brain, in the form of neuroimaging
networks. From EEG measurements the metric PAC is computed which is a proxy of GABA related Excitation-Inhibition
processes and is referring to the modulation of high frequency bands from low frequency bands. 
There are differences between almost all network PAC values at the Placebo condition, in case-control comparisons, with
individuals diagnosed with Autism showing greater PAC values, suggesting greater Excitation-Inhibition imbalance.
Networks FPN, VAN, SMN, VN, show a return of PAC values to levels similar to those of the control group after a high-dose
of the GABA agonist drug, showing a rescue of Excitation-Inhibition imbalance in these extended cortical regions. 
There is
no change in PAC values of the control group during the low or high dose of the drug. PAC values are calculated in four
different ways for the network LN, and a similar rescue of Excitation-inhibition imbalance during the high dose in the Autism
group is evident when PAC values are calculated with beta-alpha waves. When PAC values are calculated with beta-theta,
gamma-theta, gamma-alpha waves Excitation-Inhibition rescue is evident during the low-dose of the GABA agonist drug for
this network. 
Then the study examines aberrant connectivity, in case-control comparisons between networks connectivity. There is a
global reduction, that is all between-network connectivity differences between groups are disappearing in the Low-dose
condition. In this part of the statistical analysis it is evident that the High-dose of the drug changes aberrant connectivity
differences in comparisons to the Placebo condition. 
This study has implications for pharmacological treatments in individuals diagnosed with Autism Spectrum Disorders.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
I commend the authors for conducting a well-designed study to experimentally probe the effect of manipulating the
GABAergic system on an EEG measure of network processing and drafting a well-written manuscript. I only had a few minor
comments that I believe would help the readability and comprehension of the findings. 
1. Though they administered R-Baclofen (a GABA-B receptor agonist), undoubtedly it has effects on other receptors as well
either directly or indirectly. Is there any more direct measure of GABA-ergic effects of the drug as the authors specifically
state that PAC "PAC can arise from various configurations of nested excitatory-inhibitory networks 27-29 or from interactions
within purely inhibitory populations of GABAergic interneurons 30." Thus their primary outcome, is a bit unclear about how
much of it is tied to GABA versus other neurotransmitter systems. It would be helpful to explain that better in both the
introduction and discussion (limitations) sections. 
2. It seems that some people did not complete all three visits. They do say that if a participant experienced a more than
moderate SE, they would break the blind so as not to expose them to a higher dose of the drug, but it is not clear if that is
why these individuals did not complete all visits or if there were other reasons for drop out. It would be helpful to understand
how many people completed all three visits versus only one or two and the reason for drop outs. 
3. The results are very complicated and hard to digest with so many variables/levels of each variable. The authors reference
different brain networks (7), different conditions (4 I believe) (baseline, placebo, low and high dose of the drug, although
perhaps placebo and baseline are the same, but again this is confusing), and different frequency bands PAC (beta-theta,
gamma-theta, gamma-alpha). It is hard to understand the main take home and why certain levels of certain variables were
significant when others were not. For example why beta-theta for some findings and gamma-theta for others? Why certain
networks and not others. The authors tried to explain it in the discussion in terms of number of receptors in different brain
regions, but it was all just a lot to wrap my head around. I suggest that the authors work to restructure or organize their
findings in a way that is easier to follow and digest. I believe they appropriately used statistical methods to address the
multiple comparisons, it is more of an issue of understanding the main take home in the weeds of all of these variables and
levels. 
Reviewer #4 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript presents exciting findings of single-dose arbaclofen effects on PAC in autistic individuals, furthering the
field understanding of the effect of GABA dysfunction in ASD. The study sample size is admittedly on the small side, which
is acknowledged appropriately by the authors, but still serves as an important step in understanding potential influences on
clinically heterogenous response to GABAergic drugs in ASD. There are however several points I would like to see clarified
in order to be able to fully evaluate the validity of the results. 
Abstract and throughout: 
Most published papers in the field adopt the use of labeling PAC by the phase first, then amplitude (as in phase-amplitude
coupling), so instead of beta-theta, most papers say theta-beta PAC. Although it's clear what the authors mean by beta-theta,
gamma-theta, etc. for consistency with the literature it would be helpful to reverse these labels. 
Introduction: 
PAC has been investigated in autism before. While this is mentioned somewhat in the discussion, it would be helpful to
include a short summary of the most common findings here to support this being a probable abnormality to investigate in
ASD. 
Similarly, the authors provide a history of their own work with arbaclofen to make the case that this is a good choice as a
probe of GABA-ergic regulation. However there are many other MHRA approved medications that have clinically-supported
effects on GABA in the brain, including baclofen itself. Why did the authors choose arbaclofen over these, when it is still in
clinical trials in the UK? STX209 was donated by the Simons Foundation; did they have any further influence on the study
design? 
Results:

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 3 -->

A consort diagram would be helpful here to document missing data and the reason for that data to be missing at each phase
of the study. It is important to understand why some participants did not provide data for each phase - was it side effects?
Data quality? They missed a visit entirely? Especially important is why so few controls participated in the high dose visit
compared to autistic participants. The methods mention that unblinding could occur in order to discontinue high dose if a
person had moderate side effects at low dose, but no numbers are reported for how often that occurred. Is that the reason for
the reduction in control number in high dose? regardless those occurrences should be reported. 
It is also important to note here in the results or preferably in the methods if behavior was monitored carefully in the EEG
sessions for all participants. Sitting still with eyes closed for an entire minute is difficult for many autistic individuals.
Differences in eyes closed EEG may arise from real neural differences or potentially reduced compliance with keeping the
eyes closed. 
The authors report no effects on any networks for theta-beta PAC in controls. Could this be due to dropout from individuals
experiencing moderate side effects? This would be clarified by the reporting requested above. 
Most importantly for this study, there is considerably more heterogeneity in the ASD data for LN, compared to controls and to
most of the other networks - do the authors have a hypothesis as to why that might be the case? The drug findings for LN are
pretty inconsistent - some findings for low dose but not high dose, then a finding for controls but not ASD - it seems possible
that these irregular effects may simply be due to the variability in this particular measure in LN for ASD. There are a lot of
possible explanations provided by the authors in the discussion, but it would be helpful to address directly why the authors
feel this is a real set of drug effects rather than spurious finding given the distribution of the data. 
Methods: 
In the study design, please specify that is is a cross-over study. 
For the EEG processing there are several items that need to be clarified to understand how data was managed: 
1. Were data segments that contained remaining artifact after ICA left in the data? Typically ICA aimed at removing muscle,
ocular, and ECG does not completely remove large body movements and significant muscle, which could impact PAC and
source analytics. If segments with remaining artifact were removed, what was the minimum number of seconds retained to
qualify as useable data? If segments with remaining artifact were not removed, then this also causes concern for findings in
eye-closed EEG only, where behavioral differences between autistic individuals and controls during a more difficult section
of the study may contribute to differences in overall data quality between groups. 
2. Please specify the maximum number of interpolated channels 
3. The authors briefly mention that ICA artifacts were identified and removed using automatic processing. What algorithm
was used for automatic removal of ICs? Was there any quality control on the automation? I have noted in my own work (as
have others) that some of these algorithms are better than others at identifying artifact in neurodivergent samples, where
baseline brain activity may differ from the "Expected" training set for these algorithms. They all need some level of
monitoring to verify validity of IC removal decisions. 
4. Some would say that 64 channels is bare minimum for general source localization, much less accurate parcellation into
400 ROIs. Can the authors justify their choice of source analyses to alleviate this concern? 
5. In the first mention of PSI on page 24, there is a typo (should be slope, not slop) 
6. For the within-network PAC evaluation section, it is stated that "The 400 ROIs in the source space were assigned to Yeo’s
7 functional networks based on their spatial distributions". Can the authors provide a bit more detail here on how that was
done? Was this based on a set of coordinates from an atlas or just by visualization, or some other method?
