Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The present manuscript is interested in the beneficial impact of 40 Hz flicker towards nonarteritic anterior ischemic optic
neuropathy (NAION), a cause of vision loss in the elderly. This interesting work demonstrates that 40 Hz (but not other
frequencies) flicker before (but not after) ischemic onset mitigates retinal ganglion cell loss and function as well as reduces
retinal inflammation. This beneficial impact seems related to adenosine signaling since, as already shown earlier by this
group, 40 Hz flicker enhances adenosine levels. Also, the protective effect of the flicker is blocked by treatments with
dipyridamole, an ENT blocker, and DPCPX, an A1 receptor antagonist. 
The paper is clearly written, experiments nicely done and results of interest. I only have minor comments that are
suggestions to improve few points of the manuscript: 
1-Dipyridamole as an ENT blocker is often associated with an enhanced extracellular level of adenosine. On the other hand,
DPCPX blocks A1 receptors. Both might be seen as paradoxical observations and further discussion would be welcomed. 
2-What about A1 receptor levels in the model of NAION +/- flicker ? 
3-The data regarding CD68 are interesting but would deserve a bit more discussion. In particular, CD68 is present in
microglial lysosomes and therefore might be involved in RGC phagocytosis. This could be also discussed. 
Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The study of Su and colleagues represent a novel and interesting twist in the field of preconditioning and in the potential
application of 40 Hz flickering. In fact, even if after reading the scope of the study it is logical to understand its rationale, it is
never the less a brillant breakthrough to have made this link and to explore it. Therefore, based on the novelty of the concept
explored in this study, I can only be fully supportive of its rapid publication. The reviewing process may allow providing some
suggestions to strengthen the quality of the manuscript, as detailed below. 
1-Although the scientific interest of this study is undeniable, the clinical translation is somewhat unclear. In fact, by definition,
PC needs to be administered shortly before the catastrophic event. If one cannot guess when the catastrophic event will take
place, it seems difficult to time when to trigger PC. Moreover, some key points are: i) to know how long does PC last; ii) to
know if repeated periods of PC (i.e. spaced for instance 1 week) still maintain their benefit (given that one would expect a
desensitization of A1R). This might be useful to be very briefly mentioned as future (required) prospects before planning a
translational study (to avoid killing a good idea by lack of data to support an adequate planning of the intervention). In this
context, introducing the manuscript by claiming that it is necessary to develop ‘new and effective treatments for NAION’
seems rather at odds with the prophylactic-like strategy pursued in this study.

be of interest to know if it is mandatory to carry out 3 repeated flickering session in 3 different days (where, eventually, each
session might decrease A1R-mediated impact of the subsequent session) or if one session would be enough and actually
optimal to get a maximal PC. 
15-A small note might be required in the Discussion to point out that NAION is characteristically found in elderly patients,
whereas this study opted to use young adult mice. It should be warned that this PC-like effects of 40Hz light flickering
remains to be documented in aged animals.

We thank the Reviewers for the overall positive comments on the manuscript and for the excellent suggestions to improve the manuscript. In 
response to the reviewer’s comments and suggestions, we have performed six additional sets of the experiments and obtained following supportive 
data: (1) To define the effective time window of 40 Hz flicker preconditioning, flicker stimulation was applied 12, 24, 48, or 72 h before NAION 
induction. The strongest protection was observed at 12 h, with a moderate effect at 24 h, whereas preconditioning at 48 or 72 h showed no 
significant protection (Figure 1). (2) The protective effect of 40 Hz flicker preconditioning persisted for at least 4 weeks after NAION induction

conditions (Figure 3). (4) Pretreatment with dipyridamole alone, in the absence of 40 Hz flicker preconditioning, did not affect RGC survival or 
visual function c ompared with vehicle -treated mice (Figure 4) (5) Similarly, pretreatment with DPCPX alone, without 40 Hz flicker 
preconditioning, had no significant effect on RGC survival or visual function (Figure 5). (6) After increasing the sample size, 40 Hz flicker  
preconditioning significantly reduced microglia density compared with NL NAION mice, whereas DPCPX partially attenuated this effect without 
reaching statistical significance (P = 0.0813, Figure 7). These new experimental finding s further validate our previous findings and support our 
conclusions and have been included in the revised manuscript. We have also carefully revised the manuscript to fully address  the reviewer’s
