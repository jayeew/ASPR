Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors address the important question of how the most lethal human malaria parasite commits to sexual development,
a process that is critical for parsite transmission to mosqutitoes. Prompted by the discovery of a spontaneous missense
mutation in ap2g, a gene encoding a known master regulator transcription factor for commitment to sexual development, this
work adds new detail to our understanding of how ap2g operates downstream of another known factor, GDV1. In particular,
the data demonstrate that GDV1 controls ap2g expression early, before an autoregulatory loop is thought to lock ap2g in the
on-state, which is then thought to turn on a larger number of target genes. 
Conceptually, this work is not entirely novel but, importantly, it consolidates and corroborates previous data in a way that I
find compelling and suitable for publication in Nature Communications. This is technically challenging work in a difficult
system which uses many new conditional lines and reporter systems in carefully designed and well-executed experiments. 
Particular strengths an innovative approaches include (1) the careful comparison of inducible lines for GDV1 and AP2G-L
and -V and the inclusion of F12 as another loss-of-function mutant, (2) the independent analysis of both transcript
abundance, translation through a luciferase fusion gene and protein level through an mScarlet fusion, (3) the use of different
phenotypic readouts ranging from gametocyte conversion rates to early marker gene expression and MSRP1 expression. 
I find the manuscript well written. The analysis is thorough and the work is well executed. The statistical analysis is
appropriate and methods are largely described clearly. 
I think Figure 3 and related text passages need some improvement. The biological significance of where an algorithm for
motif identification draws a domain boundary is not clear to me. The statement that most amino acid substitutions affect
where a particular algorithm draws the boundary is therefore difficult to interprete. They are not saying that the substitution

legend. Or did they just take an existing structure and drew the different amino acids into it? I.e. is this a cartoon or data? I
see how the substitution is in the DNA binding site, but can they model how the substitution would affect folding and
binding? 
Figure 3c: Is there evidence that the binding is sequence specific? Have they for instance competed with the same or an
unrelated unlabelled oligo? What is the evidence the expressed domain is present in the same quantity and has not

Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author): 
 
 
The authors address the important question of how the most lethal human malaria parasite 
commits to sexual development, a process that is critical for parsite transmission to 
mosqutitoes. Prompted by the discovery of a spontaneous missense mutation in ap2g, a gene 
encoding a known master regulator transcription factor for commitment to sexual development, 
this work adds new detail to our understanding of how ap2g operates downstream of another 
known factor, GDV1. In particular, the data demonstrate that GDV1 controls ap2g expression 
early, before an autoregulatory loop is thought to lock ap2g in the on-state, which is then 
thought to turn on a larger number of target genes.  
 
Conceptually, this work is not entirely novel but, importantly, it consolidates and corroborates 
previous data in a way that I find compelling and suitable for publication in Nature 
Communications. This is technically challenging work in a difficult system which uses many new 
conditional lines and reporter systems in carefully designed and well-executed experiments.  
 
Particular strengths an innovative approaches include (1) the careful comparison of inducible 
lines for GDV1 and AP2G-L and -V and the inclusion of F12 as another loss-of-function mutant, 
(2) the independent analysis of both transcript abundance, translation through a luciferase 
fusion gene and protein level through an mScarlet fusion, (3) the use of different phenotypic 
readouts ranging from gametocyte conversion rates to early marker gene expression and 
MSRP1 expression. 
 
I find the manuscript well written. The analysis is thorough and the work is well executed. The 
statistical analysis is appropriate and methods are largely described clearly.  
 
I think Figure 3 and related text passages need some improvement. The biological significance 
of where an algorithm for motif identification draws a domain boundary is not clear to me. The 
statement that most amino acid substitutions affect where a particular algorithm draws the 
boundary is therefore difficult to interprete. They are not saying that the substitution would affect 
folding of the domain, right? How did they model the structures in 3a? This is not clear from 
methods or figure legend. Or did they just take an existing structure and drew the different 
amino acids into it? I.e. is this a cartoon or data? I see how the substitution is in the DNA 
binding site, but can they model how the substitution would affect folding and binding?  
 
Response: The original structure in Fig 3a was modeled using Rosetta software with the two 
different amino acid sequences. We have replaced this with Alphafold prediction AF-Q81519-F1 
and used the MutationExplorer web tool to evaluate changes due to the V2163L mutation. The 
model predicted changes in several amino acids located close to L2163 in the AP2 DNA binding 
domain. To follow up we also used Alphafold to predict the structure of the recombinant proteins 
used in the EMSA. Again, the core AP2 domain structure was not disrupted, consistent with the 
recovery of soluble recombinant protein that was used in the EMSA. 
The MOTIF search webtool provides the AP2-G motif boundary which is only prediction, but the 
MutationExplorer also predicts changes in the AP2 domain that could alter DNA binding.  The 
MOTIF search tool doesn’t measure protein characteristics such as folding. It only provides a 
motif/domain prediction if the query protein sequence shows significant similarity with their 
annotated database. The definition of AP2 domain and their boundaries in ap2 genes including 
ap2-g, has been described earlier (PMID: 16040597 and 30959972). We have modified results 
section to better describe these predictions (Lines 155-169).

Figure 3c: Is there evidence that the binding is sequence specific? Have they for instance 
competed with the same or an unrelated unlabelled oligo? What is the evidence the expressed 
domain is present in the same quantity and has not precipitated? Do they know it is folded? 
 
Response: AP2-G binding motif was previously defined using a protein binding microarray 
(PBM) with wt recombinant AP2-G protein (rAP2-G) that was generated using the same protocol 
used in this study (PMID: 21060817). Based on that work, we used an oligonucleotide that 
contains a consensus GnGTAC sequence which binds wt PfAP2-G and also rAP2-g from the 
distantly related rodent parasite P. berghei. The wt and mutant PfAP2-G recombinant proteins 
contained the same amino acid region (aa 2150-2220) and were purified as GST fusion proteins 
from the soluble/supernatant fraction of sonicated E. coli material. There were no signs of 
misfolding. The protein was present in the soluble fraction and based on the protein 
quantification we used the same quantity of both wt or mutant AP2-G in two different dosages (3 
mg and 6 mg). The results and methods section have been revised to include this additional 
description (Lines173-174, 598-600).
