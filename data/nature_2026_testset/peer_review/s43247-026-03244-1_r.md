REVIEWER COMMENTS: 
Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
This study proposes a spatial conservation planning framework that integrates ecological resilience and ecosystem
services, with the Loess Plateau serving as a case study. The research identifies the spatial mismatch between
improvements in ecosystem services and declines in ecological resilience, which holds significant scientific value for
achieving sustainable land management. However, there are some issues in the manuscript. Here are specific comments. 
1.In lines 73-75 of the Introduction, the authors' statement that "most current conservation planning efforts primarily focus on
biodiversity hotspots and/or ecosystem service, while often overlooking ecological resilience" appears to be based on
selective citation rather than a comprehensive literature review. 
2.A fundamental methodological concern arises regarding the use of kNDVI as the primary indicator of ecosystem state for
assessing ecological resilience across the entire study region. While kNDVI has proven useful in forest-specific resilience
studies and vegetation change detection at local scales, its ability to capture resilience dynamics across diverse
ecosystems, requires stronger theoretical and empirical justification for large-scale, multi-ecosystem applications. 
3.Water supply evaluation spans 2001-2020, whereas carbon sequestration and soil conservation assessments cover 2000-
2020. This one-year offset creates an inherent temporal misalignment that could compromise the validity of comparative
analyses and spatial overlay assessments, particularly when calculating service-resilience coupling metrics. The authors
must: (1) provide explicit justification for this temporal discrepancy in data availability or methodological constraints; and (2)
demonstrate that the missing 2000 data does not significantly affect the integrated analysis outcomes. 
4.There are a few spelling errors that need to be addressed (e.g., 'trad-offs' → 'trade-offs' on line 278; 'ecosyste' →
'ecosystems' on line 269). The authors should conduct a thorough proofreading of the entire manuscript to eliminate such
errors and ensure professional presentation qualit. 
5.While the authors effectively identify the critical finding that "areas with high ecosystem service supply often coincide with
resilience loss, revealing mismatches between service gains and resilience decline", the discussion lacks concrete
management strategies to address this critical finding. The authors should expand the practical implications to include
specific restoration approaches that can simultaneously enhance service supply and resilience. 
Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author): 
This article takes the Loess Plateau in China as a case study to evaluate and explore the matching and balancing of
ecosystem service supply and ecological resilience capacity in the process of ecological restoration, which has important

[Reviewer #1 Comment 1] 
1. In lines 73 -75 of the Introduction, the authors' statement that "most current 
conservation planning efforts primarily focus on biodiversity hotspots and/or 
ecosystem service, while often overlooking ecological resilience" appears to be based 
on selective citation rather than a comprehensive literature review. 
Response: We appreciate the reviewer ’s valuable comment. We agree that the

The revised paragraph now reads as follows (Line 76-91 in the revised manuscript): 
“Spatial conservation prioritization frameworks provide practical and cost -effective 
tools for supporting targeted and adaptive ecosystem management 10. Traditionally, 
many existing frameworks have focused on biodiversity conservation and ecosystem 
service optimization as their primary objectives 11-13. In recent years, however, the 
importance of incorporating ecological resilience into conservation planning has been 
increasingly recognized 14,15. Despite this growing attention, the development of 
operational frameworks that explic itly integrate resilience remains methodologically 
limited, largely due to the conceptual ambiguity and the inherent difficulty of quantifying 
resilience16. Advances in satellite observations and critical slowing down (CSD) 
indicators—such as temporal autocorrelation and variance—offer promising means to 
measure resilience and assess ecosystem stability2,5,17. These indicators detect slower 
recovery rates and increased fluctuations as systems approach critical transitions 18, 
providing a quantitative basis f or integrating resilience into spatial conservation 
decision-making. Linking ecological resilience with ecosystem services thus enables a 
more comprehensive and robust foundation for identifying priority areas under 
uncertain environmental conditions.”

[Reviewer #1 Comment 2] 
2. A fundamental methodological concern arises regarding the use of kNDVI as the 
primary indicator of ecosystem state for assessing ecological resilience across the 
entire study region. While kNDVI has proven useful in forest-specific resilience studies 
and vegetation change detection at local scales, its ability to capture resilience 
dynamics across diverse ecosystems, requires stronger theoretical and empirical 
justification for large-scale, multi-ecosystem applications. 
[Response] We appreciate the reviewer ’s insightful comment. We agree that the 
theoretical justification for using kNDVI as the primary indicator of ecosystem state 
needs to be clarified. Specifically, kNDVI was chosen because it is a kernel -based 
extension of NDVI that enhances sensitivity to vegetation greenness in both low and 
high biomass conditions (Camps-Valls et al., 2021). Moreover, previous studies have 
demonstrated that vegetation indices derived from satellite observations (e.g., NDVI, 
EVI, kNDVI) can serve as reliable proxies for ecosystem functional states, from which 
resilience metrics such as temporal autocorrelation and variance can be derived (Smith 
& Boers, 2023).  
To further strengthen the justification, we have added a short introduction of kNDVI 
and supporting citations. The added sentences read as follows (lines 397-401 in the 
revised manuscript): “The kernel normalized difference vegetation index (kNDVI) was 
used as a proxy for vegetation state. It shows stronger correspondence with primary 
productivity and improved robustness to saturation, bias, and noise across space and 
time compared with conventional indices such as NDVI and near -infrared reflectance 
of vegetation (NIRv)51.” 
Reference: 
Camps-Valls, G. et al. A unified vegetation index for quantifying the terrestrial 
biosphere. Science Advances 7, eabc7447, doi:10.1126/sciadv.abc7447 (2021). 
Smith, T. & Boers, N. Reliability of vegetation resilience estimates depends on biomass 
density. Nature Ecology & Evolution 7, 1799 -1808, doi:10.1038/s41559-023-02194-7 
(2023).

3.Water supply evaluation spans 2001 -2020, whereas carbon sequestration and soil 
conservation assessments cover 2000-2020. This one-year offset creates an inherent 
temporal misalignment that could compromise the validity of comparative analyses and 
spatial overlay assessments, particularly when calculating service -resilience coupling

[Response] We appreciate the reviewer’s careful observation. 
(1) The one-year offset arises from data availability limitations of the PML-V2 (Penman-
Monteith-Leuning Version 2) model, which we used to derive water yield (as

evapotranspiration and runoff components). Although this dataset provides high spatial 
resolution, physical consistency, and comprehensive energy -water balance  closure 
advantages over other global products, its temporal coverage starts from February 26, 
2000. Consequently, to ensure consistency in annual aggregation, the first complete 
year available for water yield calculation is 2001. Carbon sequestration (fro m NPP) 
and soil conservation data, however, are available from 2000-2020. 
(2) To verify that the absence of 2000 data does not bias the analysis, we recalculated

added a clarification in the Methods section to explicitly explain this temporal 
discrepancy and its justification. In addition, three supplementary figures

between the 2000-2020 and 2001-2020 datasets, demonstrating their high spatial and 
statistical consistency.  
The added sentences read as follows (l ines 479-489 in the revised manuscript): “The
