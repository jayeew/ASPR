Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript presents a highly significant and innovative work on the development of a monolithically integrated, dual-
band (NIR/SWIR) infrared focal plane array (FPA) using solution-processed PbS colloidal quantum dots (CQDs). The novel
p-i-n-i-p unipolar barrier heterostructure design, achieving bias-switchable spectral discrimination with exceptionally low
crosstalk and high detectivity, represents a major advancement in low-cost, high-performance multispectral infrared imaging.
The successful demonstration of a functional 128×128 FPA prototype with real-world imaging applications underscores the
technological impact of this work. The manuscript is well-written, logically structured, and supported by comprehensive data.
It is suitable for publication after addressing the points below. 
1.While operability is mentioned (Fig. S23), quantitative data on pixel-to-pixel uniformity (e.g., histogram of responsivity or
dark current across the array) for both bands should be provided, which would strengthen the assessment of FPA
manufacturability. 
2.The ROIC readout speed (frame rate) for the demonstrated FPA is not stated. Providing this would help contextualize its
suitability for real-time applications. 
3.Page 6 mentions the PbS-I layer thickness limits NIR absorption, contributing to residual crosstalk. Was thickness
optimization performed? Could a slight increase further reduce NIR→SWIR crosstalk without compromising SWIR response
or speed? 
4.The ligand exchange process is critical for performance. Briefly commenting on the reproducibility and stability of the
exchanged films (e.g., shelf-life, environmental stability) would be beneficial. 
5.The energy band diagrams (Fig. 1f, S4) are schematic. Adding estimated values (e.g., from UPS/IPES or simulation) for
the conduction/valence band offsets and Fermi level positions would provide deeper insight into the carrier
blocking/extraction mechanisms. 
6.How do the reported detectivity and dark current values for each band in the dual-band device compare to state-of-the-art
single-band PbS CQD detectors? This would highlight the efficiency of the integrated design. 
7.The 1000-cycle test (Fig. S19) shows excellent short-term stability. Commenting on potential degradation mechanisms
(e.g., oxidation, ion migration) and any preliminary data on longer-term stability under ambient or operational conditions
would be useful for assessing practical viability. 
8.The ROIC schematic (Fig. S20) is mentioned but not described. A brief explanation of its key features enabling dual-bias
operation would be helpful. 
9.The methodology in Fig. S15 should be briefly summarized in the main text or caption of Fig. 3b for clarity. 
10. Define "LDR" (Linear Dynamic Range) upon first use in the main text (Page 9).

Reviewer #4 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Submitted paper entitled “Dual-Band Infrared PbS Colloidal Quantum Dot Focal Plane Array” presents a dual-band infrared
imaging system based on a vertically stacked configuration of oppositely oriented near-infrared (NIR) and short-wave
infrared (SWIR) absorbing PbS CQD layers. The authors report dark current densities on the order of 10
⁻
⁶ A and EQE of
~25%, followed by dual-wavelength imaging demonstrations. While the experimental realization is technically competent,
the manuscript, in its current form, falls short of the scientific depth expected for a publication in Nature Communications.
The work reads more as a mid-level engineering report/demonstration than a contribution of fundamental scientific insight. 
I recommend either submitting this manuscript to a more engineering- or imaging-focused journal, or doing more
experimental designs and substantially revising it to clearly articulate and support a meaningful scientific question or
hypothesis. Below, I outline several concerns and suggestions: 
1. The use of PbS CQDs for infrared detection and imaging has been extensively explored and commercialized. The device
architecture, including the hole/electron transport layers and charge blocking strategies, appears to rely on well-established
approaches that have been in the literature for over a decade. 
2. While PbS CQDs are widely considered the gold standard among CQD materials, their intrinsic ionic character leads to
well-known issues with operational and environmental instability. The device architecture employed here has previously
been shown to suffer from performance degradation under electrical bias and prolonged operation. However, the manuscript
does not address these stability concerns, either through experimental investigation or discussion. 
3. The authors state that the concept of dual-band CQD-based imaging has been previously demonstrated across several
architectures. It is unclear what the central innovation of this work is. If the novelty lies in the monolithic integration on a focal
plane array (FPA), this aspect is confined to a brief discussion centered around Figure 4 and does not sufficiently elevate the
scientific significance. 
4. The manuscript does not clearly articulate the scientific challenge being addressed or the fundamental insight offered by
this study. As a result, the broader implications for the CQD or imaging community remain unclear. 
5. The proposed architecture may face limitations for real-world imaging applications. Notably, the system appears to require
relatively high bias voltages to achieve meaningful contrast, which can negatively affect device stability, induce ghost
imaging artifacts, and slow response times. 
6. Due to the opposing orientation of the stacked junctions, the internal electric fields generated upon illumination in each
spectral band are in opposite directions. This raises the possibility of electrostatic coupling or cancellation effects, whereby
excitation in one junction perturbs the band alignment and detection fidelity in the other. This undermines the assumption of
independent dual-band detection and should be thoroughly investigated. 
7. Several figures contain data that appear inconsistent. The authors are encouraged to carefully review their
measurements, ensure statistical significance, and include reproducibility assessments. 
8. The images presented in Figure 4 are difficult to interpret. Aside from panel c, the differences among the panels are subtle
or unclear. 
9. The experimental section lacks sufficient detail on the fabrication of both the devices and the integrated FPA. More
comprehensive information is needed to ensure reproducibility and to assess the scalability or practicality of the proposed
approach.

Reviewer #4 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The revised manuscript shows clear improvement, and the authors have made a substantial effort in addressing the
reviewers’ comments. Still, I have a few concerns that should be resolved before publication: 
1. The manuscript conflates optical stability, electrical stability, and shelf-life stability. These are distinct concepts and should
be treated separately. 
• Optical stability must be evaluated under continuous illumination, not by measuring absorption after storage under a
particular environment. 
• Electrical stability requires extended operation under applied bias, not short experiments involving chopped illumination. 
The current ~2 h measurement with intermittent light exposure is not representative of realistic detector operation. Moreover,
the manuscript does not provide experiments that identify the origin of instability, and several interpretations appear
speculative or unsupported by systematic data. The suggestion that encapsulation would resolve the instability is an
unverified extrapolation and should be avoided unless backed by dedicated experiments. 
I recommend revising the entire stability discussion to remove speculation, avoid misinterpretation of limited data, and
clearly state that stability assessment and mitigation require further comprehensive studies. 
2. Figure 2 remains unclear. From panels 2b and 2c, the EQE of the 9xx nm band under positive bias appears higher than
the 16xx nm band under negative bias, yet this trend is not reflected in panels 2d and 2e. To clarify the device behavior, the
authors should report EQE spectra across the full wavelength range (e.g., 500–2000 nm) under both positive and negative
biases (for example, ±0.5 V and ±1 V). 
Additionally, providing EQE measurements under broadband white-light illumination would help contextualize the device
performance for more realistic operating conditions. 
3. The figure captions remain incomplete and do not provide sufficient context for readers. Please revise all captions to
include essential experimental details, such as applied voltage, device architecture under test, illumination intensity, and
measurement conditions. These elements are necessary for reproducibility and proper interpretation.

Regarding environmental stability, we investigated both optical and electrical
