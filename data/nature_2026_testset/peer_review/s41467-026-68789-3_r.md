Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, the authors demonstrate that unflanked R-loop structures in the PAM-distal region facilitate trans-
cleavage activity when Cas9 targets short double-stranded or single-stranded DNA substrates. They further show that
extending guide RNA spacers beyond the canonical 20 nucleotides, even by only a few bases, markedly reduces this
collateral cleavage activity. The authors provide evidence that catalytic activity in the HNH domain has a significant impact
on cleavage efficiency. Finally, these findings are supported by structural analyses of relevant Protein Data Bank (PDB)
entries. However, the following points should be addressed: 
Major comments： 
1. The authors introduced five systems. However, the descriptions provided in the main text are vague and overly general,
making it difficult for readers to find their differences. These systems are repeatedly mentioned throughout the Results and
Discussion section; the differences among them can only be identified by referring to the supplementary figures. I
recommend presenting these systems directly in the main figures to improve clarity and accessibility.
2. In lines 101–106, the authors examined crRNAs with imperfect spacers for systems 1 and 2 and reported that a three-
nucleotide mismatch occurred at positions 4, 5, and 6. I suggest that the authors provide detailed information on these
nucleotides in Figure 2; otherwise, readers are unable to identify the location of the mismatch. Furthermore, the rationale for
beginning with positions 4–6 and then moving to position 13 is unclear. What about the positions between 6 and 13? Can

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 2 -->

Cas9 tolerate single-nucleotide mismatches at these untested positions? In addition, the authors tested single-nucleotide
mismatches at positions 5 and 13, as well as a three-nucleotide mismatch at positions 4–6. However, the effects of other
possible variants, such as single mismatches at positions 4 and 6 or a two-nucleotide mismatch at positions 4 and 5, remain
unexplored. 
3. In this manuscript, the authors reported that a flanked R-loop, an elongated spacer, and an inactive HNH domain can
impede the trans-cleavage activity of Cas9. They further conclude that these findings support advances in nucleic acid
detection. I recommend that the authors provide a real-world example demonstrating how their findings could improve the
design of the CRISPR/Cas9 system for nucleic acid detection, thereby strengthening the practical relevance of their study. 
Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this article, Montagud-Martínez, R. et al. investigate that the formation of flanked R-loops, an intervening sequence
exceeding 20 nucleotides, and the inactivation of the HNH domain can all result in a decrease in the trans-cleavage activity
of Cas9. The author investigated the trans-cleavage activity of Cas9 using a series of different target DNAs and analyzed the
existing structure of flanked R-loop and an elongated spacer impeding trans-cleavage by Cas9. Despite rigorous
experimentation, there is a lack of direct and clear evidence to support the arguments. Therefore, this work does not meet the
standard for publication in Nature Communications. 
1. When the authors compared the cis- and trans-cleavage activities with system#1~5, there was basically no significant
difference in the trans-cleavage activity of the system #3/4/5 regardless of the extension of the target sequence. But the
trans-cleavage activity of Cas9 with system#5 is always worse, could the authors explain this discrepancy? 
2. In Figures 3 and 4, some experiments only increased the spacer sequence length of crRNA, while others also increased
the sequence length of the target DNA. It is hoped that the authors can add schematic diagrams to make the distinction more
obvious for readers' understanding. 
3. The author believes that HNH domain activity is an important prerequisite for the activation of Cas9's trans-cleavage
activity. In previous studies by Chen et al., it was also proposed that the inactivation of the HNH domain reduced the trans-
cleavage activity when use target dsDNA, which presents a certain degree of repetition of arguments. Furthermore, the use
of nicked dsDNA targets to activate Cas9 mutants does not affect the trans-cleavage activity, which is somewhat different
from the TS breakage caused by conformational changes in the HNH domain. Can this mechanism be explained more
deeply from a structural perspective? 
4. The molecular diagnostic application based on trans-cleavage activity of Cas9 is a topic of concern. Has the author
evaluated nucleic acid detection based on relevant research and provided a better detection method? 
5. The author analyzed the effect of R-loop and active HNH domains on trans cleavage activity based on existing structures,
but it seems that there is still a lack of clear explanation. 
6. When the author used nicked dsDNA to investigate the effect of HNH domain on trans-cleavage activity, only system#2
was selected, and there was a lack of experiments on other systems. The author seems to lack some logic and coherence
when selecting different target DNA for experiments.
