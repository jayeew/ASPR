Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This paper, "Functional variants at 1p36.23 confer risk of schizophrenia through modulating RERE," investigates how
specific genetic variations contribute to schizophrenia (SCZ) risk by affecting the RERE gene. The study characterizes
previously identified functional variants, rs159961 and rs301792, located in the intronic regions of the RERE gene at the
1p36.23 risk locus. These variants exhibit allele-specific enhancer activity. The risk alleles of both variants lead to higher
enhancer activity, which is associated with increased RERE expression in human brain tissues. The risk allele of rs159961
reduces the binding of the transcription factor REST. The risk allele of rs301792 increases the binding of POLR2A. Both
mechanisms result in stronger enhancer activity that subsequently regulates RERE expression. The study concludes that
the functional variants rs159961 and rs301792 contribute to schizophrenia risk by upregulating RERE. This upregulation, in
turn, affects neurogenesis, dendritic spine density, and synaptic transmission, thereby increasing SCZ risk. These findings
provide a framework for translating GWAS findings into an understanding of disease biology and offer insights into SCZ
pathogenesis. While the paper is comprehensive, it is fairly difficult to follow- especially how new versus reanalyzed data
are presented is not made clear in the results or figure legend text. The methods are also lacking and critical experiments
(e.g. possible synergistic effects of the two alleles) are noticeably missing. 
Major comments

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The study by Liu et al aims to characterise functional variants underpinning a schizophrenia genome-wide significant
common variant locus at 1p36.23, and provide insights into how these variants impact schizophrenia pathophysiology. The
paper presents a series of functional genomics experiments that implicate two functional variants (rs159961 and rs301792)
at this locus. TWAS, eQTL and CRISPR/Cas9 knockout experiments suggest these variants are associated with increased
expression of RERE. Mouse neural stem cells (mNSCs) models suggest overexpression of RERE impairs proliferation of
NSCs and neurogenesis, and also abnormalities in dendrite complexity and dendritic spine density. A transcriptomic
analysis of RERE overexpressing NSCs identified 213 differentially expressed genes that were enriched for those with
functions related to glutamatergic, cholinergic, and GABAergic synapse. Western blot and qPCR analyses also found RERE
overexpression resulted in a decreased expression of the NMDAR subunit GRIN2A, which has been consistently been
implicated in schizophrenia by common and rare genetic studies. 
The manuscript is well written, and although a large number of analyses and results are presented the paper is easy to
follow. The findings provide novel insights into the schizophrenia pathophysiological mechanisms associated with common
risk alleles at 1p36.23. Since my background is in psychiatric genetics, my review specifically focusses on the TWAS, eQTL
and differential gene expression elements of the paper. I have the following comments regarding these analyses. 
1. The authors claim their findings support the neurodevelopmental hypothesis of schizophrenia, but I did not see any data
from the eQTL or TWAS analysis implicating REME in early neuronal development. The authors show in Supplementary
figure 5 that REME is expressed prenatally, but are the eQTL/TWAS analyses based on postmortem adult brain tissue?
Since published schizophrenia TWAS based on human fetal brain did not find any significant associations with REME
(PMID: 32366953, Supplementary Table 2), could the authors please clarify whether their eQTL/TWAS findings implicate
specific development timepoints in schizophrenia, and if not comment on the conflicting TWAS findings for REME reported
in Hall et al 2021 (PMID: 32366953). 
2. The transcriptomic analysis identified 213 differentially expressed genes in RERE overexpressed mNSCs, but I did not
see outputs of these experiments presented anywhere other than in Figure 5b. It would be helpful if the full results for each
gene were presented as supplementary data, as it is not easy to identify which specific genes are differentially expressed
from the figure. For example, I could not determine whether GRIN2A was differentially expressed from the figure. This
comment is also relevant for any other analyses in the paper where results are only presented in figures; the raw results
should also be available in table format where applicable. 
3. In the transcriptomic analysis, genes with adjusted P-value <0.05 were considered differentially expressed genes. I could
not see what method was used to adjust P values, could the authors please clarify? 
4. The discussion opens by stating the "Neurodevelopmental hypothesis SCZ posits that SCZ is caused by a combination of
environmental and genetic factors”. I think this sentence should end by saying that these factors impact brain development. 
5. In the largest schizophrenia GWAS to date, RERE was prioritised through fine-mapping as the credible causal gene

Response: We thank the reviewer for this helpful comment. According to the suggestions of the

comprised 50 variants in FINEMAP analysis. Similarly, SuSiE analysis yielded a 95% credible set 
that includes 55 SNPs. CARMA ﬁne-mapping identiﬁed 102 SNPs, which comprise the 99% 
credible set. Notably, both rs301792 and rs159961 were included in reside the credible sets in all 
three analyses, with modest posterior probabilities (rs301792 (PIP of SuSiE: 0.0073, CARMA: 
0.0052, FINEMAP: 0.0066) and rs159961 (PIP of SuSiE: 0.0066, CARMA: 0.0046, FINEMAP:

variants. We have incorporated ﬁne-mapping results into the revised manuscript. Page 6, lines
