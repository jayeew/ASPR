Reviewers' comments:
 
Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
The manuscript by Hong et al reports a comparative RIXS study of the infinite layer nickelates PrNiO2 and cuprates
SrCuO2. The low energy part of the RIXS spectra is fitted by five components, including elastic lines, two phonons, magnon
and background. The magnon dispersion is fitted by Heisenberg Hamiltonian using parameters U/t/t'/t", including a
momentum dependent renormalization factor Zk. The fitted values U/t are compared across different compounds. 
The topic of the study is highly relevant in experimentally evaluating the similarity between the electronic structure of d9 Ni
vs Cu compound. Given the recent experimental experimental controversy (RIXS, REXS, ARPES, etc) despite wide
experimental efforts, this manuscript provides useful information to compare both systems side by side that will be of interest
the nickelate community. 
I have couple of questions for the manuscript before it can be published: 
1. For the main dataset, i.e. Fig 2e, the fits could be improved. The 3 spectra near the (0,0) does not fits well. Moreover, how
is the elastic line aligned? The maximum of the elastic signal in Fig 2e have non-monotonous offsets up to ~10meV. I
wonder if the authors can reconfirm the energy alignment. 
2. The authors use hopping parameter t'/t=-0.4 and t"/t'=-0.5 taken from LSCO214. This should be justified, especially
because there is an additional rear earth electron pocket in PNO while absent in SCO. The absence of apical oxygen should
also have effect of the ratios (see PRB 72, 054519 (2005)). 
3. Can the author justify the use of renormalization factor Zk? I find many aspects of the results are omitted. i.e. Can the
authors explain why is the renormalization factor stronger in SCO than PNO? The Zk factor has more dispersion in SCO
compared to PNO? Why there is a maximum in Zk around (0.15, 0.15) in SCO? 
Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author): 
1. What leaps out in this study is a quite different interpretation of rather similar data in [18]. Most fundamentally, I think that
this suggests that these RIXS measurements are insufficient to uniquely fit Hubbard models. Since the magnon energy is of
order $4t^2/U$, this can be fit by varying either 't' or 'U' and resolving the right decision is a 
complicated and subtle extraction
of information associated with some boundary dispersions and higher order exchange processes. In this light, I have some
comments: 
a) The work needs to prominently acknowledge in the abstract that this is not a complete experimental extraction of t/U. It
needs to assume specific ratios of the higher order hoppings. 
b) I think this sentence should be changed. "Given the isostructure of of SrCuO2 and PrNiO2, we here assume that the ratios
of square lattice hopping parameters t'/t and t''/t' are constant". The isostructure does not prove or even strongly imply that
these ratios should be constant. 
c) In commenting on the DFT by Botana and Norman and the use of their observation to the ratio of these are similar. 
I think
it's important for fairness to add that the actual values obtained for the nickelate are different by a substantial margin. 
2. The statement that Fig 1. demonstrates homogeneity and high crystallinity seem inappropriate. The measurements shown
have very little sensitivity to the crystallinity and quite modest sensitivity to the most pertinent inhomogeneity, which is
excess oxygen. The best solution here would be to simply remove these claims.

Reviewers' comments:
 
Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
The authors addressed my questions and concerns successfully. I have no further technical concerns for the paper and
believe the quality is worthy of publication in Communications Materials. 
Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author): 
While I thank the authors for the changes, the alterations don't actually capture my comments very well and I think the text of
the manuscript is not a good representation of what's really been achieved in more than one important place. The key

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 4 -->

comment I made previously is: "these RIXS measurements are insufficient to uniquely fit Hubbard models". Some of the
language in the revised text presents what are in reality sensible presumptions as if they are definitive and supported by
prior studies. 
1a) Appropriate text would be "physically plausible assumptions" and "estimates of t/U". i.e. 
"Using a single-band Hubbard model and including physically plausible assumptions about higher-order 
exchange interactions, we estimate the correlation 
factor U/t for both compounds. A key finding 
is that despite the prediction of a smaller Coulomb repulsion U, PrNiO2 exhibits a correlation strength U/t that is 20%
stronger than that of its isostructural 
cuprate counterpart SrCuO2." 
Of course the authors can choose their own wording, but the work here is too heavily dependent on assumptions to justify
the current wording. The current wording would require direct empirical evidence of many of the input constraints, such as
the ratios of hoppings. 
b) The text concerning [5] inappropriately implies that DFT is likely to predict accurate ratios even when the authors consider
the absolute values wrong by a substantial margin. Text like this would be suitable: 
An identical and significant t′/t ratio has been suggested for infinite-layer nickelates 
and cuprates by first-principles calculations [5], although the magnitude of hopping amplitudes (and hence the energy scale)
is inconsistent with our final values. 
Again, authors can choose the wordings, but the text needs to be clearer that while they take the ratio from this paper, there
is a consistency problem with the absolute scale. In my opinion, there is no reason why DFT should get the ratio right if it
gets the absolute magnitude substantially wrong.
