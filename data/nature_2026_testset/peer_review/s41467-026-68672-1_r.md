Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Thanks for the manuscript and the interesting read. The text also appended as attachment. 
The authors present a Large Language Model based framework (iDesignGPT) developed to address the nature of early
phase conceptual design. Evaluated on publicly available design challenge authors used available (published-, and known)
metrics based on questionnaires that enable quantitative evaluation of useability and impact when used. Results suggest
that the proposed tool/framework, iDesignGPT, performs better than two existing Large Language models used for similar
tasks. The set up of the framework is sound. The four core components (Agents, Knowledge Base, Toolkit and Web front-
end) to a generic four phase design relevant for a LLM supported design process makes a lot of sense, and the idea to set
up the framework with a co-pilot, and an agent- mode, to bridge the gap between the “fuzzy” Design context and the LLM
Agent domain is also sound. As such the authors present a solution that advances knowledge in an interesting gap where a
theoretical and practical understanding needs to be advanced to benefit in design from the rather new advancements in
LLM’s. Another strength is that both the targeted design (noun) is explored together with the process of design (verb),
although the level of resolution of the study address engineering systems design rather than engineering design (implying
more details in product representation). The single most interesting construct presented is the division into the co-pilot and
agent- modes that reflect the nature of the targeted design process phase well. 
Validity. The nature of design studies, being largely ill-defined and un-structured, is difficult to study rigorously and
quantitatively. The choice of using open accessible design challenges makes sense, and the approach clearly a trade off
between making a systematic evaluation and a problem that is sufficiently realistic and complex. There are some aspects
where the manuscript can be clearer to understand what is in, and out of, scope. 
- Scope and coverage of Engineering aspects. As the ambition is to address Engineering Design as a discipline, the
problems addressed in the “engineering” side are less well positioned as a more general “design” approach. The case and
reasoning presented, seems to be before classical engineering design become applicable, and rather address early stakes
of engineering systems design, rather than engineering design? As an example, a classic and relevant problem for LLM
support is to related to geometrical representations of a product definition, which is not discussed (as resolution stop at the
identification of “modules”. In the text, design representation seems to be limited (down-) to shapes and forms (images etc),
such can be generated using e.g. Dall-E, but not product geometries necessary to conduct engineering analysis of product
models (CAD models e.g.) that is typical for product engineering design. What authors address-, and not-, in the conceptual
engineering design studies targeted would need to be more clearly bounded. It becomes important as authors claim (Row
92) to address AI-assisted engineering design by integrating advanced analytical tools; and in engineering design such
“advanced analytical tools” is too open for a general claim. What (type of) analytical tools are included and what are not? 
- The component “knowledge base” (e.g. Row 119) claim “real-time access to domain knowledge … ultimately facilitating the
generation of optimized design solutions”. In line with the above, it is not clear to what degree this is actually supported and
used in iDesignGPT and not? 
- The complexity of the problem posed, and the design concepts used, opens for several questions on detail level. As one
example, in the derivation of requirements (a part of the design process), it is explained that the down-selection of

Originality and significance. 
- To the reviewers knowledge the work is original and provides new insights into an important field. Relevant references are

Conclusions

critical eye to results generated, something that typically requires a high degree of expertise? Ultimately, who is accountable
of the results generated?

Clarity and context:

section is important. 
The manuscript would benefit from displaying a more clear example of the concepts being generated, to enable the reader to
get a better sense onto what level concepts are being generated and evaluated. This is present, but embedded in text and in
too small figures.

technologies from their impact onto the design process predominately in my own research.

Key Results 
The paper describes a framework using large language models to support engineering design. 
The paper claims a number
of advances including improving the novelty of solutions and diversity of solutions as well as an ability to uncover hidden
requirements and fostering creativity, among others. 
Validity 
The paper makes many claims based on the interpretation of results from a series of test cases, for example the Go Aero
Design Challenge. 
In my view the claims are completely unverified by the presented paper and supplementary documents. 
For example, the
paper as presented claims a number of times to be more creative, and to have improved the novelty of solutions. 
But there
are no results from the actual designs to support these claims. 
In the case of the Go Aero Challenge contestants are asked to
design a vehicle that meets a complex set of requirements and cope with multiple mission scenarios. 
There are no results
presented as to what this design looks like with the iDesignGPT framework. 
Moreover, this kind of vehicle has been
developed many times in competitions across the globe, so there is a reasonable data set of existing designs which could
be used to demonstrate novelty or improved performance. 
Additionally, the measure of novelty as presented in
supplementary note S2 describes a set of generic equations and makes no reference to the actual data used. 
This is a
critical point. 
The actual data set, which should include both initial design parameters and corresponding performance
metrics must be described and shown to ensure the novelty measure is comparing like with like. 
The design of an air vehicle is a complex process and typically requires the use of a number of software and analytical tools
(a toolchain) to generate the data and provide input to design decisions. 
The paper makes no mention of any of these tools
or whether they are simple spreadsheets for example or even sophisticated simulation packages (e.g. Abaqus FEA,
ANSYS, CFD...). 
Without the details on which package was used and where it was used it is impossible to have confidence
in the results of any process. 
The complete lack of data presented in this submission is a fatal flaw in my view, a renders any conclusions drawn to be
invalid. 
Significance 
The ability to instantly and autonomously design a complex vehicle or equivalent high value product would be immensely
significant. 
However, noting the points above, this cannot apply to this work as the conclusions cannot be verified. 
Data and Methodology 
The presentation of the AI technology at a high level is generally clear and well presented. 
But the specifics of the
iDesignGPT system are not presented. 
So, although the approach as shown mostly in the early figures appears logical it is
not clear at all how this is shaped into a real design process that produces well defined concept designs. 
The approach and method itself also raise questions. 
For example, 6 different design challenges are used, and the six
people participating each did one. 
The experts that then review novelty are basing this completely on their experience and
judgment. 
I would have expected an approach where each example is explored by several people so that meaningful
measurements and comparison can be drawn for each. 
In my view the basic methodology for comparing the designs is flawed and a major issue in the work. 
This coupled with the lack of real data on each design and how they compare is a fatal flaw. 
Analytical Approach 
Following on from the data & methodology above the analytical approach is weak because it does not use a comprehensive
data set on the actual designs and processes and as so many different examples were studied it reads to me like each has a
sample size of one. 
Again this is a major flaw. 
Suggested Improvements 
This is an exciting technical area and one with much potential for the field, and it is important that this field is explored and
developed. 
Some suggestions for improvement are: 
1. Significantly reduce the breadth and scope of the paper and focus on only one or two aspects. For example novelty. 
2. Include real design data and show how the individual solutions differ in both their design parameters (e.g. Wing span) and
their performance measures (e.g. Speed). 
3. Develop several solutions to produce at least a 1-sigma study. 
4. Clearly define and show how the metrics e.g. Novelty are defined and show how this applied to at least one of the design
solutions.

5. In describing how the system works show how all the real engineering calculations are carried out or approximated and
show all the various software tools that are used in this process. 
Clarity and Context 
The results are all presented as claims of capability in the AI space. The context for the design and the design process are
absent and therefore obscures the potential value and capability of the work. 
The introduction does make many good
observations but this should be clearer. 
For example, TRIZ is mentioned as a design method and appears in the
functionality of the framework. 
However, TRIZ in itself is not a direct tool, although there are some tools there that help. 
Also,
QFD as a method only helps at one part of the design process, and moreover is not universally adopted in engineering
design. 
How do all these tools work together as a toolchain? 
And how does this fit into the iDesignGPT framework? 
References 
There is a good and long list of references many of which are contemporary and therefore up to date. 
The challenge is to
have a set of references that are really relevant to the problem in hand and in this the references could be more
comprehensive and more targeted at the specific technical gaps or opportunities being explored in the paper.

I lead a number of major research programmes in engineering
design that are using AI tools and addressing challenges in complex engineered systems such as aircraft. I have published
extensively in the aircraft design field.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This papers presents iDesignGPT, an LLM-based design support tool, that is applied in engineering design contexts in order
to support designers. The development of iDesignGPT may have a valuable contribution to the field of engineering design,
however the manner in which it is reported in the paper makes this not possible to discern. My recommendation is therefore
that the paper is rejected and the authors thoroughly re-structure and re-write the paper such that a clear contribution can be
evidenced and articulated. Reasons for this conclusion are detailed in the following paragraphs. 
The paper has a very odd structure, with the results beginning in section 2, immediately after the introduction. As such there
is no clear research gap and no formal review of similar work. Whilst LLMs are novel in their application in engineering
design, there is some work already in this space (see Ege et al https://doi.org/10.1017/pds.2024.202). In fact, a whole
engineering design conference was run on this topic last year (https://www.designsociety.org/1337/NordDesign+2024).
Whilst LLMs are relatively new, expert systems, knowledge based systems and design support tools are not, and much
exists in this space. Further to this, an established research area is the Democratisation of Design, which seeks to up-skill
designers in Engineering Design tasks. iDesignGPT builds on all of these concepts and, as such, an article presenting it
needs to consider and review the areas upon which it is built. 
There is no methodology section. Some methods are mentioned later in the paper but isn’t collated into a clear section that
provides cohesive overview of the work undertaken. Some noteworthy omissions from a methodology section are that: 
1. there is no clear design research methodology (e.g. DRM) followed; 
2. there is no selection of the types of engineering design problems iDesignGPT is applied to - it isn’t possible to evaluate
this holistically so some representative areas need to be chosen; 
3. there is no clear method of appraising functional success of iDesignGPT - what does success look like?; and, 
4. there is no clear method of evaluating user success - how can you evidence it has supported their design process?. 
Moreover, results presented in the paper are descriptive, and lacking objective measures. I think this is largely due to
absence of methodology and means of validating and evaluating the iDesignGPT tool generated. 
Further to these substantial structural issues in the paper, figures in the paper are too small to be readable (thus they don’t
provide valuable results) and the writing style is very verbose. Much of the text feels AI generated. 
In terms of re-working the paper it strikes me that it should be separated into at least two separate publications. The first
would be a technical presentation of the iDesignGPT with validation in 2-3 representative engineering design case studies.
This paper could answer the research questions of i) can iDesignGPT provide technically valid solutions to engineering
tasks; and, ii) can it outperform a general LLM? The second paper would present a user centred evaluation (with 20-30
participants) of the iDesignGPT demonstrating how it is able to support non experts to improve their design processes. This
research question could be about can iDesignGPT support / up-skill designers in engineering design problems? To try and
answer both of these questions in a single paper is overly ambitious and I don’t believe both can be covered well.
