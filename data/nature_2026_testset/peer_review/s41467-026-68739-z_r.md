Reviewers' comments: 
In this manuscript, Yin et al report ampere-level CO2 electroreduction to multi-carbon oxygenates in acidic electrolyte
through surface microenvironment reconstruction. Suggesting IL@Cu achieved a 22 Faradaic efficiency (FE) of 82.7%
toward C2+ products at a current density of 2.0 A cm−2 in 0.5 M K2SO4 (pH = 1, adjusted with H2SO4), and the
displacement of K+ can enhance C–C coupling over Cu electrode surface via facilitated the incorporation of oxygen-
containing intermediates into the hydrogen bond network. The obtained IL@Cu delivers good multi-carbon oxygenates yield
rate and high ethanol Faradaic efficiency. In all, the results are interesting and the conclusion is fully supported with solid
evidences. However, there are some significant concerns must be well addressed, and some of the conclusions are
unconvincing due to the lack of insufficient experimental evidence. Importantly, Cu with [Bmim][ClO4] ionic liquid has been
widely studied in C2+ products electrosynthesis from CO2RR, and the novelty/impact of this work does not meet the high
standard of nature communication. Therefore, I think the manuscript is not suitable for publication on Nature
Communications. My detailed comments are as follows: 
1.Limited novelty – The concept of employing ionic liquids (e.g., imidazolium-based species) to tune the Cu catalyst surface
microenvironment for CO
₂
 electroreduction has been reported in numerous prior works, including in Applied Catalysis B:
Environment and Energy 359 (2024): 124508; ACS catalysis 13.12 (2023): 7812-7821; Angewandte Chemie International
Edition 136.1 (2024): e202312163; Angewandte Chemie International Edition, 61.13 (2022): e202200039; Advanced
Functional Materials, 34.41 (2024): 2404102. 
2.Mechanistic evidence remains indirect – The central mechanistic claim that Bmim
⁺
 repels K
⁺
, promotes C–C coupling,
and reconstructs the interfacial hydrogen-bond network is based primarily on indirect spectroscopic signals (SERS, ATR-
SEIRAS) and AIMD simulations. More direct, quantitative in-situ or operando evidence is needed to substantiate these
conclusions. 
3.Reproducibility concerns for performance data – The reported performance at pH = 1 with 82.7% FE for C2
⁺
 products and
60.3% selectivity for C2
⁺
 oxygenates at 2.0 A cm
⁻
2 is significantly higher than most known systems. However, no statistical
analysis, error bars, or multiple independent electrode batches are presented to confirm reproducibility. 
4.Inadequate comparator systems – The comparison is limited to IL@Cu versus a KHCO3-derived Cu electrode. More
comprehensive benchmarking against state-of-the-art Cu-based catalysts (oxide-derived Cu, nanocrystalline Cu, other
surface-modified Cu) would better establish the relative advantage of the proposed material. 
5.Insufficient stability evaluation – The “long-term” test is only 24 h, which is insufficient for industrially relevant acidic, high-
current conditions. Longer durability studies with detailed post-characterization are required to support claims of excellent
stability. 
6.Single-pass conversion efficiency and industrial relevance underexplored – Although a high SPCE is reported (78.5%),

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript presents an interesting catalytic system in which an ionic liquid-modified Cu electrode (IL@Cu) achieves
high C
₂⁺
 oxygenate selectivity in acidic CO
₂
RR at ampere-level current densities. The focus on double layer modulation by
ionic liquids rather than the more commonly discussed surface reconstruction is novel and potentially important for the field.
However, the mechanistic evidence currently provided is not sufficient to fully support the main conclusions. Additional
experiments and analyses are necessary to strengthen the mechanistic claims before the manuscript can be considered for
publication. I therefore recommend major revision. 
1. Many previous studies have reported enhanced CO
₂
RR in ionic liquids, often attributing the improvement to ionic liquid
induced surface reconstruction. In this work, the authors attribute the enhancement mainly to double layer modulation. How
do the authors exclude or decouple the possible contribution of surface reconstruction to the observed activity/selectivity
changes? This distinction is critical to substantiate the novelty of the proposed mechanism. 
2. Please clarify whether the ATR-SEIRAS results in Supplementary Fig. 27 were obtained under in situ conditions. If they
were, why do the signals differ from those in Supplementary Fig. 32? If they were ex situ results, how can the authors confirm
that the ionic liquid remains adsorbed on the catalyst surface under working conditions? It is also possible that the IL
desorbs into the electrolyte during operation and readsorbs upon drying after the reaction. 
3. Can the authors extract more precise adsorption configurations of the Bmim
⁺
 cations from the spectra, or by combining the
spectral data with AIMD simulations? Such information would significantly aid in understanding the catalyst surface state
and its role in activity enhancement. 
4. Please provide the initial and equilibrated configurations of the AIMD simulations in the Supplementary Information for
clarity and reproducibility. 
5. The current AIMD preequilibration time of only 6 ps is insufficient for such a mixed electrolyte system to reach equilibrium.
It is recommended to first preequilibrate the system for ~100 ps using classical molecular dynamics, and then proceed with
AIMD for more accurate sampling. 
6. The molecular illustration in Fig. 4f is overly schematic, with the IL cation appearing smaller than the hydrated K
⁺
 ion. The
authors should provide a more realistic structural representation based on AIMD results.

(Remarks to the Author) 
Overall Evaluation 
This study presents a significant advancement in acidic CO2 electroreduction (CO2RR) by demonstrating an ionic liquid-
modified Cu electrode (IL@Cu) that achieves remarkable current densities (>1.2 A cm-2) and Faradaic efficiencies (82.7%
for C2+ products) for multi-carbon oxygenate synthesis. The mechanistic insights into interfacial microenvironment
reconstruction are compelling. However, several critical issues require clarification and additional experimental validation to
fully support the claims. 
1 Stability of Bmim+ Coordination Requires Further Validation 
The authors provide initial evidence of Bmim+ coordination to Cu via XPS (Fig. 1e) and ATR-SEIRAS (Fig. 1f). 
Key concerns:

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 3 -->

1) There is a lack of post-stability characterization as Figure 2f demonstrates stable Faradaic efficiency over 24 hours yet
provides no data confirming Bmim+ retention following prolonged electrolysis. 
2) Insufficient in situ monitoring is indicated by the absence of Bmim+ specific peaks in operando ATR-SEIRAS (Fig. 3d-e),
which limits assessment of its chemical stability. 
Recommendations: 
1) Perform TEM-EDS elemental mapping (focusing on N distribution) and zeta potential measurements after 24 h
electrolysis, comparing results to initial data (Fig. 1c and Supplementary Fig. 8). 
2) Adjust the testing parameters of in situ ATR-SEIRAS to successfully resolve the Bmim+ characteristic peaks (e.g., ~1300–
1600 cm-1) during CO2 reduction reactions. 
2 Experimental Details Need Clarification 
1) Electrode Fabrication and Reactor Design: 
The catalyst loading area (2 cm x 1.5 cm, Methods) contradicts the 1 cm2 geometric area used for current normalization, so
the exact active area should be specified, and it is further necessary to justify why electroreduction occurred in an H-cell
(Supplementary Fig. 2) rather than the flow cell used for testing (Supplementary Fig. 18). 
2) Product Collection Methodology: 
Clarify how gas/liquid products were quantified during the 24 h stability test (Fig. 2f). Was sampling continuous or batch-
wise? 
3) Instrumentation Limitations: 
The CHI 660E workstation (250 mA limit) cannot support 2.4 A cm-2 currents. Confirm use of a current booster and specify
its model. 
3 Additional Data Required to Support Claims 
1) Supplementary Figure 2f should be supplemented with post-test SEM/XPS data of IL@Cu to validate its surface integrity
and Bmim+ retention. 
2) To demonstrate the specificity of Bmim+, its performance should be compared with that of other ionic liquids (e.g.,
EmimClO4) to highlight its unique role. 
3) To enhance the reproducibility of experimental procedures, replace schematic diagrams (including SAXS-XAS in
Supplementary Fig. 9, CO2 reduction setups, in situ Raman setups, and in situ ATR-SEIRAS setups) with actual photos of
the corresponding experimental apparatuses. 
4 Minor Suggestions 
1) Extend stability testing beyond 24 h (e.g., 48 h) to assess industrial relevance. 
2) How was the Faraday efficiency of the products calculated? 
3) I was wondering, under such a high current density, whether any bubble formations were observed at the cathode
electrolyte outlet?

Reviewer: 1 
 
This work achieves selective electroreduction of CO2 to multicarbon products (C2+) 
under acidic conditions at very high current densities (2A/cm 2) on copper electrodes 
modified with an ionic liquid (IL, B mimClO4). The results are of high interest as 
reaching both high currents and C2+ products are notably difficult to obtain under acidic 
conditions. The high selectivity towards C2+ is attributed to the presence of the IL at the 
copper surface where it changes the microenvironment of the electrochemical interface 
by pushing the water molecules and K+ cations of the electrolyte further away from the 
electrode surface; these mechanisms are considered to disturb the H 2O hydrogen 
bonding network and to promote C-C coupling needed to obtain C2+ products.  
While the experimental results are very promising and interesting, the molecular level 
mechanistic understanding contains multiple issues. In particular, the analysis on how 
the IL controls the microenvironment at the atomic level should be clarified and 
strengthened significantly.

(b) The authors report that IL@Cu achieves higher electrochemical surface area (ECSA)

Response: We thank the reviewer very much for the comment. The method for 
calculating ECSA and ECSA-normalized jC2+-oxygenates followed previously established 
protocols (Nat. Commun. 2025, 16, 1843; Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 2024, 63, e202409563;

was determined from double-layer capacitance (Cdl) measurements, derived from scan-
rate-dependent cyclic voltammetry (CV). CV was conducted within a potential window

(c) It is written that "The Stark tuning slope of Cu –CO over IL@Cu was greater than 
that over Cu (...), indicating stronger binding energy between *CO and IL@Cu". What 
is the basis for this statement? Usually the Stark slope is used as a measure for the 
electric field strength at the interface rather than as a measure for the binding/adsorption 
energy (e.g. https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.0c03316). It is possible that the presence 
of the IL changes the electric field at the interface rather than the CO adsorption energy. 
Response: We thank the reviewer very much for the comment.  We agree with the 
reviewer that the Stark slope quantifies how sensitively the vibrational frequency of an 
adsorbate responds to the changing electric field at the electrode interface. A larger 
absolute value indicated a stronger electronic interaction between the adsorbate and the 
electrode surface, as the electron cloud of the adsorbate is more effectively polarized 
by the electric field. Therefore, the Stark slope can also serve as an indirect descriptor 
of intermediate adsorption strength (Nat. Commun. 2024, 15, 5172; Nat. Nanotechnol. 
2021, 16, 1386–1393; J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2024, 146, 15917–15925; J. Am. Chem. Soc. 
2023, 145, 8714–8725; J. Phys. Chem. Lett. 2020, 11, 8307–8313). A larger Stark slope 
for *CO was observed on IL@Cu than on Cu, suggesting that the IL enhances the local 
electric field at the electrode surface. This enhancement led to a greater sensitivity of 
the *CO vibrational frequency to the applied potential, reflecting stronger electronic 
coupling between *CO int ermediate and the IL@Cu surface. As suggested by the 
reviewer, in the revised manuscript, we have emphasized this by “A larger Stark slope 
of Cu–CO was observed on IL@Cu than on Cu (Supplementary Fig. 3 7), suggesting 
that the IL enhanced the local electric field at the electrode surface39. This enhancement 
led to a greater sensitivity of the *CO vibrational frequency to the applied potential, 
reflecting stronger electronic coupling between *CO intermediate and the IL@Cu 
surface”. Please see page 10 in the r evised manuscript. The literature provided by the 
reviewer has been cited to the revised manuscript (Ref. 39).

conclusively confirmed that the IL played a critical role in promoting water dissociation.

Referee report on the manuscript "Ampere-level CO2 electroreduction to multi-carbon
oxygenates in acidic electrolyte through surface microenvironment reconstruction" by Yin et
al on August 15, 2025.
This work achieves selective electroreduction of CO 2 to multicarbon products (C 2+)
under acidic conditions at very high current densities (2A/cm2) on copper electrodes modified
with an ionic liquid (IL, BmimClO4). The results are of high interest as reaching both high
currents and C2+ products are notably difficult to obtain under acidic conditions. The high
selectivity towards C2+ is attributed to the presence of the IL at the copper surface where
it changes the microenvironment of the electrochemical interface by pushing the water
molecules and K+ cations of the electrolyte further away from the electrode surface; these
mechanisms are considered to disturb the H2O hydrogen bonding network and to promote
C-C coupling needed to obtain C2+ products.
While the experimental results are very promising and interesting, the molecular level
mechanistic understanding contains multiple issues. In particular, the analysis on how the
IL controls the microenvironment at the atomic level should be clarified and strengthened
significantly.
Major issues
1. Experimental studies
(a) The authors report a high Faradaic efficiency towards C2+ products. However, it
is unclear how the Faradaic effiency and selectivity towards different products
were measured and quantified. Please clarify.
(b) The authors report that IL@Cu achieves higher electrochemical surface area
(ECSA) normalized current densities. However, it is not clear how the ECSA is
obtained from the experiments. Please clarify.
(c) It is written that "The Stark tuning slope of Cu–CO over IL@Cu was greater
than that over Cu (...), indicating stronger binding energy between *CO and
IL@Cu". What is the basis for this statement? Usually the Stark slope is used as a
measure for the electric field strength at the interface rather than as a measure for
the binding/adsorption energy (e.g. https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.
0c03316). It is possible that the presence of the IL changes the electric field at
the interface rather than the CO adsorption energy.
(d) The authors write "In situ SERS (...) showed that the intensity of the Cu–CO peak
at 310 cm−1 over IL@Cu was greater than that over Cu, indicating a higher *CO
coverage over IL@Cu". What is the basis for this statement? The intensity can
depend also on the local field which in turn may be controlled by the presence of
the IL.

(e) The SERS peaks are attributed to different adsorbed species without providing

2. Simulations:
(a) AIMD simulations were used to study the distribution of H2O/K+ near the Cu
and IL@Cu electrodes, and it was concluded that the IL repels both water and
K+ from the surface. However, the used simulation time is only 24ps which
is too short for obtaining converged structural information and the results will
likely depend very sensitively on the initial conditions/geometries used in the
simulations. For instance, the water rotational and librational timescales are on
the order of 5 and 10ps while K+ diffusion would take about 1ns (https://doi.
org/10.1016/j.electacta.2023.142095), and the diffusion/reorganization
of the IL would likely take even longer. To achieve converged results for the

(b) Besides changing the water/K+ concentration near the interface, the IL will likely
change the water orientation. I ask the authors to provide to study and report the
orientational properties of water in the Cu and IL@Cu systems.
(c) It is written that "The distance of 5 Å between K+ and the Cu(111) surface,
corresponding to that between K+ and IL@Cu, results in a Gibbs free energy for
C-C coupling of -0.41 eV , which is much lower than that observed with distances
between K+ and the Cu(111) surface that are either closer or more distant (Fig.

authors compute the C-C coupling thermodynamics in the presence of the IL on
the surface rather than placing K+ far away from the surface (where it is likely
highly unstable).
(d) The DFT calculations for reaction thermodynamics do not consider the influence
of the water network on the reaction steps. This is a significant omission as the
authors consider that the water environment, especially the hydrogen bonding
network and the presence of K +, have a notable influence on the activity and
selectivity. To support the results drawn from the experiments, I consider that it
is necessary to study the reaction thermodynamics and kinetics of key reaction
steps through the use of enhanced sampling AIMD simulations.
(e) The influence of K+ on the CO and H2O adsorption thermodynamics is obtained
by using a model where the K + is solvated by two or three water molecules.
However, AIMD simulations (e.g. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41929-021-00655-5)
has more water molecules in its first solvation shell. To obtain more reliable
results, a more complete solvation structure of K+ on both the surface and away
from the surface should be used.
(f) The choice of computing the thermodynamics for forming ethanol in the pres-

facilitates the C-C coupling steps, it would be more relevant to simulate how the
IL changes the reaction thermodynamics of different C-C coupling pathways and
intermediates; I ask the authors carry out such calculations.
