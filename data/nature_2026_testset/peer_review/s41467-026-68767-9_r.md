Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript presents an intriguing and mostly experimental study on magnetic exciton-polaritons in a CrSBr
metasurface. Unlike prior works where CrSBr serves solely as a source of magnetic excitons coupled to external photonic
structures or Fabry-Pérot modes in bulk, this work employs CrSBr itself as both the excitonic medium and the photonic
lattice. This dual role enables in-situ engineering of the photonic modes and offers a compelling route for tunable exciton-
polariton properties via external magnetic fields and photonic lattice design. The authors demonstrate tunable reflectance
around the BIC and observe exciton-polariton modulation by magnons using transient angle-resolved reflectance
spectroscopy. These results are likely to attract broad interest and make meaningful contributions to the study of hybrid
magnon-polariton systems in magnetic semiconductors. 
However, several points require clarification or further support before the manuscript can be considered for publication: 
1. Figure 3e shows the experimental results of reflectance at θ=0 to illustrate the brightening of dark BIC mode with applied
magnetic field. This observation appears inconsistent with the statement that "the robustness of the BIC is protected by the
C
₂
 symmetry, which holds even in the presence of magnetic field." This inconsistency should be addressed more clearly.
Moreover, although the role of the off-diagonal dielectric tensor element εab is invoked to explain the effect, simulations are
only shown at a finite angle (θ=2.3). What is the predicted effect of εab at Γ (θ = 0°)? Does it agree with the experimental data
shown in Fig. 3e? 
2. In Fig. 4f, the authors present two very different k-space distributions for the pump: one as the "Fourier transform of the
real-space pump pulse" and another as the "measured normalized k-space distribution of the pump light." The notable
discrepancy between these two curves is confusing and should be clearly explained. Additionally, the authors should clarify
how this relates to their argument on the impact of heat/strain and direct momentum transfer mechanisms. 
3. The FFT amplitude of the optical and acoustic magnon modes shows different dependence on momentum. Could the
authors provide a more detailed physical explanation of why the optical magnon exhibits stronger k-dependence and why
acoustic magnon does not? 
Minor Comments and Suggestions 
1. The text description of Fig. 3e seems to refer to Fig. 3f. Please check and revise accordingly. 
2. The color bar scale in Fig. 3f uses only "max" and "min" labels. Providing numerical values would better illustrate the
absolute reflectance change attributed to εab. 
3. Consider plotting optical and acoustic magnon magnitudes separately in Supplementary Material to more clearly reveal
their different k-dependences.

(Remarks to the Author)

grating. Formation of polaritons with the grating bright and dark (BIC) modes have been experimentally extracted with large
coupling strength of 167 meV, consistent with previous reports in this material. The authors then demonstrated tuning of
these polariton modes with an applied external magnetic field in both static and pump-probe measurements. While the
attempt of fabricating nanophotonic devices that engineers the photonic band structures is applaudable, there are some
experimental observations that are potentially intriguing and worth further investigation, the data quality and analysis of the

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Overall, this manuscript presents a timely and original contribution to the emerging field of spin–photon interfaces based on
van der Waals magnetic semiconductors. By employing a CrSBr metasurface, the authors demonstrate that high-Q
resonances and exciton polariton bound states in the continuum (BICs) can be utilized to substantially enhance magnon–
photon interactions. The combination of steady-state optical spectroscopy and transient angle-resolved reflectance
measurements provides convincing evidence for magnon–exciton–photon hybridization and reveals mode- and momentum-
dependent dynamics that highlight direct momentum transfer from pump photons to magnons. These findings advance the
fundamental understanding of the interplay between spins, magnons, and photons in two-dimensional magnetic systems
and establish a promising platform for tunable, spin-dependent photonic and quantum devices. 
That said, the current version requires further refinement before it can meet the publication standards of Nature
Communications. In particular, aspects of the experimental methodology, the theoretical interpretation, and the
contextualization within the broader literature should be clarified and strengthened. 
1. Quoting “The CrSBr metasurface hosts an exciton polariton (EP) bound state in the continuum (BIC). Owing to the
magneto-electronic coupling effect of CrSBr and photonic singularity of the BIC mode, both the energy and the reflectivity of
the half-light, half-matter state can be efficiently tuned with an external magnetic field.” This statement in the Introduction
appears imprecise and somewhat misleading. Specifically, based on the one-dimensional CrSBr grating, the authors
investigate polaritons arising from the strong coupling between high-Q quasi-BICs and magnon–excitons in momentum
space, rather than a generic BIC-hosted polariton. To improve clarity, it would be important to provide a concise but accurate
introduction to the nonlocal BICs physics, with emphasis on how quasi-BIC resonances enhance light–matter interactions. In
particular, since exciton polaritons represent a local response while grating resonances are inherently nonlocal, the authors
should expand the Introduction to highlight the content of from local to nonlocal high-Q metasurfaces, supported by relevant
literature, so as to place this work more convincingly in the broader context of recent advances in photonic and polaritonic
metasurfaces. 
2. Continuning my last Question. Also, NOT “bound state in the continuum (BIC)”, but “bound states in the continuum (BICs)”.
3. In line 86, the authors mention that the relative permittivity along the b-axis is derived from the reflectance spectrum shown
in Fig. S1. Near the exciton state X, three distinct resonant valleys are visible in the reflectance spectrum; however, the real
(ε
₁
) and imaginary (ε
₂
) parts of the relative permittivity in Fig. 1 do not exhibit multi-order oscillations near the exciton state X.
Please explain this inconsistency. 
4. I am wondering the size of the fabricated sample. No information about the grating size. 
5. The authors use a coupled oscillator model to explain the strong coupling between the FP mode and the exciton in a
uniform 150 nm-thick CrSBr flake (Figs. 2 and S3). However, does the quasi-BIC mode in the one-dimensional CrSBr
metasurface also form strong coupling with the exciton? Although the energy of the quasi-BIC mode is far from that of the
exciton mode—making its coupling features susceptible to interference from other modes closer to the exciton and difficult to
directly observe experimentally—the authors should provide supporting theoretical analysis to substantiate the claim of

3. The FFT amplitude of the optical and acoustic magnon modes shows different 
dependence on momentum. Could the authors provide a more detailed physical 
explanation of why the optical magnon exhibits stronger k-dependence and why acoustic 
magnon does not?  
Response: 
The different k-dependence of the optical and acoustic magnon modes is rooted in the mode 
character. Fig. R3c and d show the precession of the sublattice spins for the acoustic and 
optical magnon modes for bulk CrSBr in the absence of magnetic field (𝐻௘௫௧=0), which is 
obtained by solving the Landau-Lifshitz equation. Here, 𝛿𝑚ሬሬ⃗ଵ(𝑡) and 𝛿𝑚ሬሬ⃗ଶ(𝑡) are time-
varying part of the sublattice spins 𝑚ሬሬ⃗ଵ(t)=𝑚ሬሬ⃗ଵ+𝛿𝑚ሬሬ⃗ଵ(𝑡) and 𝑚ሬሬ⃗ଶ(t)=𝑚ሬሬ⃗ଶ+𝛿𝑚ሬሬ⃗ଶ(𝑡). The 
change of the angle between 𝑚ሬሬ⃗ଵ(t) and 𝑚ሬሬ⃗ଶ(t) leads to the temporal modulation of exciton 
energy in bulk CrSBr, which is given by Δ𝐸∝𝑚ሬሬ⃗ଵ(t)⋅𝑚ሬሬ⃗ଶ(t)/|𝑚ሬሬ⃗ଵ(t)||𝑚ሬሬ⃗ଶ(t)|. At 𝐻௘௫௧=0, 
one can tell from Fig. R3c and d that neither acoustic or optical magnon can modulate the 
exciton energy at the corresponding magnon frequency 𝜔. This is simulated in Fig. R3e 
where the time evolution of Δ𝐸 does not have frequency component at 𝜔 for both acoustic 
and optical magnon excitation. Although weak higher order frequency component at 2𝜔 can 
be identified. When an out-of-plane external magnetic field is applied, 𝑚ሬሬ⃗ଵ and 𝑚ሬሬ⃗ଶ is tilted 
away from 𝑎−𝑜−𝑏 plane, which breaks the symmetry and initiates the coupling between 
the exciton and the optical magnon. This is simulated in Fig. R3f, where a strong oscillation 
of Δ𝐸 at the corresponding magnon frequency 𝜔 is observed only for the optical magnon. 
Whereas, the acoustic mode remains decoupled at frequency 𝜔. Such distinct “selection rule” 
for acoustic and optical magnon is observed in our measurement on unetched CrSBr flakes as 
shown in Fig. R4 c and d, where the acoustic mode is absent in the FFT spectra at all angles. 
The observation of disallowed acoustic magnon in the FFT spectra from the CrSBr grating 
(Fig. R4 a and b) then must arise from the etched edges and sidewalls which have low 
symmetry. At such localized site, the acoustic magnon-exciton polariton interaction therefore 
does not conserve k and naturally leads to a insensitive dependence on angle. On the other 
hand, the selection-rules-allowed optical mode requires match of momentum owing to the 
preserved translation symmetry and therefore leads to an angle-sensitive behavior for both the 
pristine CrSBr flake and the CrSBr grating.
