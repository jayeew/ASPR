(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript “Nonlinear Dynamics and Fermi-Pasta-Ulam-Tsingou Recurrences in Macroscopic Ultra-low Loss
Levitation” by M Sourki et al. reports long-lived nonlinear oscillatory dynamics 
in millimetre-scale diamagnetically levitated
dielectric (quartz) particles. Major strengths of the current manuscript are: clear demonstration of long-lived coherent
nonlinear motion in a macroscopic, passive magnetic trap—a notable complement to the rapidly developing field of levitated
optomechanics; quantitative characterization (Lyapunov exponent, dissipation limits, acceleration sensitivity) that positions
this platform for sensing and fundamental studies; connection to statistical-mechanical phenomena (FPUT recurrences) is
attractive and broadens the potential impact.

levitodynamics: using macroscopic, passive levitation platforms to access long coherence times and novel nonlinear
regimes. The manuscript is clearly written with only a few typos. Overall I find the study interesting and the conclusions
plausible.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript presents a fascinating study on a macroscopic diamagnetic levitation system, demonstrating ultra-low
dissipation and exploring nonlinear dynamics, including features reminiscent of Fermi-Pasta-Ulam-Tsingou (FPUT)
recurrences. The achieved experimental parameters, such as the exceptionally low damping rates and high acceleration
sensitivity, are impressive and potentially of significant interest to the community. The platform itself is promising for
fundamental studies in nonlinear dynamics and sensing. 
However, in its current form, the manuscript suffers from several major organizational and presentation issues that
significantly hinder the clarity of the narrative and the ability to assess the experimental rigor. A major revision is necessary
to address these concerns before the manuscript can be considered for publication. 
Major Concerns: 
1.Organization and Narrative Focus: The manuscripts structure is currently unclear and undermines its core message. The
title and abstract emphasize nonlinear dynamics and FPUT recurrences, yet a substantial portion of the "Experimental
Results" section is dedicated to acceleration sensitivity and displacement noise measurements. While these are valuable
performance metrics, their prominent placement disrupts the flow towards the main claimed novelty. Similarly, the
"Discussion" section mixes future applications with results interpretation. The authors must restructure the entire manuscript
to create a coherent narrative. I strongly recommend focusing the results section squarely on establishing the nonlinear
character of the system and presenting the evidence for FPUT-like behavior. The impressive acceleration sensitivity and
other sensor metrics could be consolidated into a separate, succinct subsection or moved to a dedicated section on "Sensor
Performance" after the core physics has been established. The discussion should then clearly separate the interpretation of
the presented results from the outlook on future applications. 
2.Lack of Experimental Details for Key Measurements: The "ring down measurement" is cited multiple times as a source for
crucial parameters like the dissipation rate (e.g., Page 8: "From the ring down measurement, we have observed a dissipation
rate..."). However, the manuscript lacks a clear description of how these ring-down experiments were performed. Which
excitation method (mechanical or magnetic) was used for which data set? How was the amplitude decay tracked (which
detection method)? Providing representative time-domain data for a ring-down, even in a supplementary information section,
is essential to validate the claimed ultra-low damping rates and to understand the challenges posed by nonlinear
recurrences during decay. 
3.Insufficient Description of the Optical Detection System: The measurement of the particles motion is critical to all presented
results. While several methods are listed (quadrant detector, interferometry, camera, single-pixel detection), the description
is too brief. For the key datasets presented in the figures, it must be explicitly stated which detection method was used. A
more detailed description, possibly with a schematic diagram, of the primary optical setup (e.g., the non-interferometric
single-pixel detection) is necessary for the reader to understand the measurement principles, potential noise sources, and
calibration methods. 
4.Inadequate Figure Presentation: The figures require significant improvement to be scientifically informative. 
oFigure 1(a-b): The 3D illustration and COMSOL simulation lack essential details. A scale bar is missing. The different
components (permanent magnets, metallic core, disk) should be clearly labeled and/or distinguished using different colors or
patterns. 
oFigure 1(c-e): The text description on Page 4 discusses the plots in the order (c) z-axis, (d) x/y-axis, (e) angular
displacement. However, the figure panels themselves are not explicitly labeled as (c), (d), (e) in the provided PDF, making

matches the figure layout precisely.
