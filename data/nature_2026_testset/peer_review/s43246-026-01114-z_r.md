Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
In this manuscript, Ahmad et al. present a comprehensive theoretical and experimental investigation of van der Waals
(vdWs) heterostructures based on VP/PdSe2 for photodetection applications. The authors reveal that the VP/PdSe2
heterostructure exhibits a type-I band alignment, enabling efficient broadband photodetection across the visible (VIS) to
near-infrared (NIR) spectral regions. 
To further enhance device performance, the authors introduced a graphene contact layer (forming a Gr/VP/ PdSe2 stack),
which significantly improved the photodetector’s performance. Remarkably, the optimized device achieved a responsivity of
111.3 A/W and an external quantum efficiency (EQE) of 26,001%, representing an enhancement of nearly three orders of
magnitude compared to the bare VP/ PdSe2 configuration. 
In addition, the engineered photodetector demonstrated excellent operational stability over 100 measurement cycles and a
rapid photoresponse time of approximately 10 ms. The device also exhibited strong polarization sensitivity with an
impressive dichroism ratio maintained across a wide spectral range, further supporting its suitability for advanced photonic

Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author): 
Summary: This manuscript reports photodetection in VP/PdSe2 vdW heterostructure. The authors calculated the band
structure of a VP/PdSe2 heterobilayer using DFT and demonstrated devices based on multilayers of VP and PdSe2, with
and without a graphite contact on VP. They report improved photodetector performance and stability, and they examined
polarization dependence. 
General assessment: Although the authors present a comprehensive study of VP/PdSe2 structure from theory to experiment,
there are fundamental methodological issues and internal inconsistencies that prevent a reliable attribution of the reported
performance improvement to graphene contact engineering. The key comparisons are made across devices with
uncontrolled differences in thickness and geometry. Additionally, some data interpretations are not fully explained. I
therefore recommend rejection.

Major comments: 
1. Model-experiment mismatch: In Figure 1, the authors calculated band structures, interfacial charge redistributions, and
band alignments in monolayer VP/monolayer PdSe2. However, all devices are built from multilayers. In introduction, the
authors mentioned strong thickness-dependent bandgap of PdSe2 ranging from 1.3 eV to 0.3 eV. As a result, the monolayer
band alignment cannot be used to explain the physics of the multilayer devices without additional information. 
2. Effect of graphene is not clear: The main results of this manuscript – two orders of magnitude increase in photocurrent
upon adding graphene contact – is not a controlled comparison. According to Supplementary Figure 2 (optical microscopy
images), both VP and PdSe2 are substantially thicker in Gr/VP/PdSe2 device than in the VP/PdSe2 device, and channel
lengths also differ. Because PdSe2 bandgap and absorption change strongly with thickness, and scale with geometry, the
observed current/responsivity increase can be simply explained by thickness and geometry alone. Moreover, Fig. S2
suggests a possible Gr-PdSe2 contact, which could create an unintended conduction path. To claim a graphene-contact
effect, the two devices should have identical thickness (identical bandgap) and channel length (or at least normalization). As
presented, the central claim is not supported. 
Minor comments: In addition to these two major comments, which weaken the points the authors mainly demonstrated in the
manuscript. There are many points in the manuscript that are not clearly elaborated or comprehensive understood. 
1. The reported PL at ~685
 
nm (1.83
 
eV) is assigned to “exciton recombination of VP,” but the cited refs [17,
 
40] are not VP
PL papers. The peak is not reconciled with the VP bandgap values discussed in the introduction 
2. Only ~50% PL quenching is reported for VP/PdSe
₂
 relative to VP. In many vdW heterostructures with efficient charge
transfer, PL quenching often exceeds an order of magnitude. This consistency is not addressed. 
3. The author showed KPFM map (Fig. 2e) as evidence of strong interfacial coupling. However, the CPD difference between
PdSe2 and VP is only 2uV, which is significantly small, compared to their band alignment in Fig. 1e (conduction band offset
is 500 meV). This consistency is not explained. 
4. The fabricated VP/PdSe2 photodetector shows clear p-type behavior, but no explanation is given. 
5. The fabricated VP/PdSe2 photodetector shows larger responsivity for 532 nm than both 405 and 808 nm, however, the
mechanism is not provided. 
6. No I-V curves are provided for Gr/VP/PdSe2 devices. 
7. The faster decay time in Gr/VP/PdSe2 compared to VP/PdSe2 is attributed to “efficient recombination”. However, it is
unclear how Gr (introduced as a contact) influences recombination of photocarriers in VP or PdSe2. This might be just due
to different thickness in VP and PdSe2. 
8. Different thickness of VP and PdSe2 should have different absorption, however, it is not considered in the analysis. 
9. In Fig 4, the author showed improved stability of VP by adding graphite as a capping layer, however, it may be simply
reflect the thicker VP used in Gr/VP/PdSe2 device. 
10. In Fig. 5, the reduction of dichroic ratio upon adding graphene is attributed to uniform carrier transport, but this
explanation is not compelling.

Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author - please also see the report attached as pdf, for better understanding): 
In this manuscript, Ahmad et al. present a broadband, polarization-sensitive photodetector based on a VP/PdSe2 van der
Waals (vdWs) heterostructure and systematically investigate its characteristics from both theoretical and experimental
perspectives. The authors demonstrate that the VP/PdSe2 heterostructure forms a type-I band alignment that confines
photoexcited carriers in the active region, thereby enabling efficient photodetection from the visible (VIS) to near-infrared
(NIR) spectral regions. Furthermore, by introducing a graphene contact layer to form a Gr/VP/PdSe2 structure, they achieve
a substantial enhancement in device performance. The device exhibits a responsivity (R) of 111.3 A/W and an external
quantum efficiency (EQE) of 26,001%, corresponding to an improvement of nearly three orders of magnitude compared with
the bare VP/PdSe2 configuration. The proposed photodetector also maintains stable operation over 100 cycles and exhibits
a rapid photoresponse on the order of 10 ms, together with polarization sensitivity characterized by a dichroism ratio of about
2.2 at 532 nm. 
Despite these promising results, several important aspects are not fully substantiated. In particular, three points remain
insufficiently supported: (i) a quantitative discussion of the carrier confinement mechanism in the PdSe2 layer based on the
reported band offsets, (ii) PL analysis that corresponds to the full claimed broadband detection range, and (iii) clarification of
the origin of light-induced degradation in VP and the stabilizing role of the graphene capping layer. In addition, the notation
and formatting used throughout the manuscript (e.g., use of italics, spacing, subscripts/superscripts, and wavelength labels
in figure captions) are not fully consistent, which detracts from the overall readability and the perceived quality of the work.
Taken together, these issues preclude recommending this manuscript for publication in Communications Materials at this
time. 
1. Line 180-184: The authors state that band offsets of ΔEc = 0.5 eV and ΔEv = 0.38 eV effectively confine photoexcited
carriers within the PdSe2 layer. However, the manuscript does not provide sufficient evidence to assess whether these
offsets are indeed large enough to ensure such confinement. Please include appropriate literature references or additional
discussion to substantiate this interpretation. 
2. Figure 2d: In the caption, the term “of” is repeated unnecessarily. Please correct this to improve clarity. 
3. Figure 2e: In the PL mapping panel, the axis values and labels remain noticeably smaller than in the other panels, which
reduces readability. Please revise this panel to improve clarity and visual distinction. 
4. Figure 2f: Only the PL spectrum under 532 nm excitation is presented. Since the manuscript emphasizes that the
VP/PdSe2 photodetector operates over a VIS-NIR range, it would strengthen the argument to include additional PL analysis
(e.g., at 685 nm and 808 nm) or to clarify why such data are not available. 
5. Supplementary Figure 4a: The material VP is partially labeled as “V” in the figure. To avoid confusion, please revise the
labels so that the material name is consistently indicated. 
6. Line 283: The authors state, based on Supplementary Fig. 5, that Iph and Pin exhibit a linear relationship. However, this is
not clearly supported by the data as currently presented. To clarify the claimed linearity, please provide the corresponding
fitting result. If, as described later (Lines 345-346), the relationship is in fact sublinear, the description in this section should
be revised accordingly to maintain consistency. 
7. Line 302-304 and elsewhere: The notation used for parameters in the equations and throughout the manuscript is not fully
consistent. For clarity, please revise the manuscript to ensure a uniform notation scheme (e.g., italicized variables, proper
use of subscripts/superscripts, and consistent spacing in mathematical expressions). 
8. Supplementary Figure 9: The wavelength is labeled as 1064 nm, which is not consistent with the 808 nm wavelength
mentioned in the manuscript. Please verify the actual wavelength and correct the label in Supplementary Figure 9. 
9. Line 389-392: The authors state that VP undergoes degradation under prolonged light exposure, and that this degradation

Optical Materials 11.15 (2023): 2300167]. The result is a more isotropic photoresponse, where 
the current from “normally weak” polarization orientations is b oosted relative to the no -

polarization states by providing a uniformly excellent pathway for all carriers. We have added
