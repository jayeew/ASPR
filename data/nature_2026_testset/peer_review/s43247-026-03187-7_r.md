Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author): 
In this manuscript, the authors have studied future trends in snow drought conditions using CMIP6 models. The analysis
shows that the intensity, frequency and duration of snow droughts are projected to increase. The manuscript is
comprehensive, well-written and presents interesting results. However, there are some points that need to be improved and
discussed. 
Introduction 
The Introduction section could be enhanced by providing a more thorough background and review of previous research
related to snow droughts. Specifically, it would be beneficial to briefly discuss how snow droughts have changed historically
and how they are projected to change in the future based on existing research. You mention that Cowherd et al. have
studied future snow droughts (line 65). It would be helpful to briefly summarize the main findings of their research rather than
just stating that the biases are significant. 
Sect 3.3 (Annual mean climatology of future snow drought) 
This section would be more insightful if the results were compared to the current situation, rather than only presenting future
climatological values. Readers may find it challenging to interpret the future values without knowing the current or historical
values. Therefore, I suggest adding figures that show climatological values for the current/historical climate and comparing
them with the future values. This addition would strengthen the manuscript and enhance the analysis. 
Inter-model variability 
The paper primarily focuses on ensemble mean values. However, Fig. 3 indicates that there are differences between the
models even after bias correction. It would be beneficial to include more discussion on the variability between the models
and whether the trends in snow droughts are consistent across different models. 
Other comments 
Table 1 and 2: I suggest showing spatial resolution instead of spatial dimensions. Please also add a reference for each
dataset. 
Eq. 4: Is there a typo in the equation? I am wondering whether “u” should be “X” instead? 
L244-245: “a decrease of 93.2%”. How did you calculate this value? 
L293: Are all the trends mentioned throughout the text calculated for the ensemble mean? 
Fig. 3: Please add the ensemble mean. 
Fig. 4e: This figure should be presented in the Methods section. Additionally, you mention (line 334) that abbreviations are
shown in Table 3, but that table displays the drought categories. Please clarify the reason for dividing the study area into
sub-regions and explain why some parts (e.g., northern Siberia and areas south of Alaska) are excluded. 
Fig. 4. (line 332): The phrase "Regions with oblique lines" is unclear as no oblique lines are visible in the figures. 
Fig. 5b, d, f: The color maps used in these figures are confusing. They suggest both positive and negative values, which is
not the case. For example, in Figure 5b, the blue colors might be interpreted as negative values and red as positive. Please
consider using more appropriate color maps. 
Fig. 6: I recommend dividing this figure into three separate figures, with each row presented individually. Additionally, the
maps (c, f, i) should be larger to make the spatial variability easier to interpret. 
Reviewer #3 (Remarks to the Author): 
The manuscript authored by Hu et al. investigates snow droughts (deficit of snow amount on land surface) and their future
evolution as projected in CMIP6 climate models and for different SSP scenarios. Inspired by two publications that are cited
in the manuscript (Huning and AghaKouchak 
2020, Cowherd et al. 2023), the authors used SWEI (snow water equivalent
index) to assess the snowpack. SWEI is a probability-based index defined as an inverse transformation of the cumulated
density functon(CDF) applied to the snow water equivalent (SWE). With this normalized or standardized index SWEI, the
authors assess the northern hemisphere snowpack climatology and future evolution in an ensemble of multiple models
(CMIP6 participants) and multiple scenarios (SSP), with focus on frequency, intensity and duration of snow droughts. The
manuscript is generally well written and the presented results also seem coherent with what intuitively expected. But I have
a major concern on the methodology employing an algorithm of bias correction. I don’t object to using the CDF-t technique
(which is indeed one of the best algorithms in correcting univariate geophysical variable). But I think it is not a good idea to
apply the CDF-t to SWEI which is no more a physical variable, but a standardized index. I think the authors need to justify
their practice with convincing evidences.

Comment 7: L244-245: “a decrease of 93.2%”. How did you calculate this value? 
Response: Thank you for  raising this question and allowing us to clarify the 
calculation of the 93.2% discrepancy reduction. 
 
The value was calculated by comparing the mean absolute bias against observations 
before and after the application of the CDF -t downscaling technique. The specific 
calculation steps are as follows: 
 
(1) We first computed the mean absolute bias for the en tire Northern Hemisphere and 
across the entire validation period (1999 -2014) before downscaling. This is the 
average of the absolute differences between the original CMIP6 multi -model 
ensemble mean SWEI and the observed SWEI for all grid cells and months. Let us 
denote this value as Bias_original. 
(2) We then computed the same mean absolute bias for the SWEI data after 
downscaling, denoted as Bias_corrected. 
(3) The percentage reduction in discrepancy was then calculated using the following

formula: 
Percentage Reduction = [ (Bias_original - Bias_corrected) / Bias_original ] × 
100% 
 
This calculation quantitatively demonstrates that the CDF -t method successfully 
reduced the systematic bias in the simulated SWEI by 93.2% relative to the 
pre-downscaling level. 
 
In response to your comment, we have revised the relevant sentence in the manuscript 
to explicitly state the metric used, enhancing the clarity for readers. The revised text

Comment 8: L293: Are all the trends mentioned throughout the text calculated for the

Response: Thank you for this comment, which helped us to improve the manuscript's 
readability. Yes, you are correct. The trend values we report throughout the text  (e.g., 
-0.049 ± 0.041 decade ⁻¹) are structured to provide a comprehensive view:  1. The 
central value (e.g., -0.049) represents the best estimate of the trend, derived from the 
ensemble mean; 2. The ± uncertainty (e.g., 0.041) represents one standard deviation 
of the inter -model variability around this ensemble mean trend. It is calculated from 
the trends of the individual models and quantifies the spread and consistency among 
them. A smaller standard deviation indicates higher model agreement. 
 
This approach is standard practice as it provides a clear "best estimate" from the 
collective model signal while simultaneously conveying the level of consensus or 
disagreement among the models. To enhance clarity, we will make a minor revision in 
the Methods or Results section where the trend analysis is first introduced. We will 
add a sentence such as (or Lines 294-296): 
 
“Linear trends are calculated from the CMIP6 multi-model ensemble mean time series. 
The reported uncertainty (±) represents one standard deviation of the inter -model 
variability in the trend estimates.”
