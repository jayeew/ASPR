Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript reports a rationally designed gas diffusion electrode (GDE) architecture that combines a non-fused
particulate-packed catalyst-binder interface with a hierarchical wettability and pore gradient. This configuration enables self-
breathing H2O2 electrosynthesis under ambient air without external oxygen supply. The study further integrates three-
dimensional structural reconstruction and high-resolution Lattice Boltzmann simulations to visualize gas-liquid-solid
distributions at the mesoscale, providing mechanistic insights into interfacial transport phenomena. Of particular note is the
engineering of a modular 400
 
cm² flow-through stack that incorporates electronic, thermal, and fluidic subsystems. This
platform achieves industrially relevant performance and cost benchmarks, underscoring the translational potential of the
proposed design. The work represents a notable step forward toward decentralized and sustainable oxidant manufacturing.
Overall, this manuscript is well-organized with robust performance and systematic mechanism elucidation. As such, I
recommend the publication of this manuscript in Nature Communications after addressing the following issues.

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 3 -->

1. Quantitative Validation of the QSGS-Reconstructed Structures. The QSGS method is employed in Figures 2g and 4e/g to
generate representative 3D catalyst layer morphologies for simulation. However, the manuscript does not provide sufficient
quantitative validation of how well these synthetic structures reflect the true morphology obtained via FIB-SEM
reconstruction. To enhance the credibility and generalizability of the simulation results, the authors are encouraged to
compare key structural parameters between the real and simulated structures. Providing such a comparison in the form of a
figure or table would strengthen the justification for using the QSGS model and support the relevance of the simulation
conclusions. 
2. Applicability and Limitations of LBM Fluid Assumptions. The LBM simulations assume an ideal, incompressible fluid to
model electrolyte intrusion and gas-liquid interface dynamics. However, in real electrochemical environments, factors such
as H2O2 accumulation, ionic strength variation, and charge transport may influence capillary behavior and wettability. While
simplifications are necessary for computational feasibility, the authors should briefly discuss whether these assumptions
might affect the validity or scope of the conclusions derived from the model. 
3. Clarification of the Inverted Performance Trend in PPE-D with Increasing PTFE Content. 
In Figure 5b, the authors examine the effect of varying PTFE content in the first layer of the PPE-D electrode. Although the
introduction of a gradient structure is expected to improve electrode performance, the observed Faradaic efficiency does not
increase with higher PTFE loading. Instead, enhancing the gradient appears to diminish performance. This counterintuitive
trend has not been sufficiently discussed. The authors are encouraged to provide a more detailed explanation of the
underlying mechanism, which would help strengthen the theoretical foundation of the gradient optimization strategy. 
4. Quantitative Characterization of the Gradient Structure. The manuscript introduces a gradient wettability and porosity
design in the CL (e.g., Figure 5a), which is central to the proposed directional transport mechanism. However, the current
evidence provided is primarily qualitative. It is recommended that the authors include more quantitative characterization of
the gradient structure, such as spatially resolved measurements of contact angle, PTFE content distribution, or porosity
variation. These data would help to validate the existence and effectiveness of the proposed gradient and support the
conclusions drawn from it. 
5. Inclusion of the Most Recent Studies. In Figure 6c, the authors benchmark the performance of their electrode against
representative studies published over the past decade. While this comparison is valuable, the dataset would benefit from the
inclusion of more recent studies—particularly those published in 2025—that address similar challenges such as high-
current-density operation, ambient-air electrosynthesis, or long-term stability. Incorporating these up-to-date references
would better contextualize the novelty and competitiveness of the present work within the current state of the field.

Response: We sincerely appreciate the reviewer’s insightful and constructive comments. We fully 
agree that a plausible alternative hypothesis indeed exists: the performance enhancement of the

hydrophobic bottom layer acting as a superior physical barrier that increases breakthrough pressure

to decouple their respective contributions, aiming to make our mechanistic interpretation clearer

significantly higher FE. This counter-intuitive result suggests an additional contribution beyond 
passive flooding resistance.

“Hierarchical Gradient CL for Liquid Repellence and Guided Product Transport 
Building on previous findings, a hierarchically structured CL with porosity and wettability gradients 
was designed to guide electrolyte transport. To systematically assess the influence of gradient design, 
we fabricated electrodes with dual-layer (PPE-D) and triple-layer (PPE-T) gradient architectures in 
addition to a single-layer reference (PPE). For PPE-D, a PTFE-rich ink was first coated onto the gas 
diffusion layer (GDL) and sintered to form a dense, highly hydrophobic layer with fine pores, 
serving as the first CL 49. A second catalyst layer composed of non-fused particulate packing was 
then deposited on top, yielding a dual-gradient structure (PPE-D) with controlled PTFE content 
(1.8-3.6 mg cm-2) (Fig. S28). Cross-sectional EDS mapping (Fig. 5a) qualitatively confirmed that 
the bottom layer is denser and richer in fused PTFE, establishing a clear porosity gradient. Mercury 
intrusion porosimetry (Fig. S29) quantitatively confirmed a porosity gradient, with a marked 
decrease in pore volume toward the PTFE-rich bottom layer, consistent with densification and 
partial pore blockage from sintering. Because contact-angle measurements are unreliable for rough 
porous surfaces, PTFE loading was used as a quantitative indicator of hydrophobicity. The higher 
PTFE content in the bottom layer indicates stronger electrolyte repellence, confirming coupled 
porosity–wettability gradients across the CL. 
For PPE-T, while adding another low-PTFE catalyst layer could further steepen the gradient, such 
multilayer assembly would complicate fabrication and hinder scalability. Instead, oxygen plasma 
etching provides a rapid, controllable, and catalyst-independent approach to tune surface 
hydrophilicity without additional coating or structural damage 50. We therefore applied plasma 
etching to PPE-D, enabling a simple and scalable enhancement of surface wettability. The treatment 
introduces oxygenated groups (–OH, C=O, C–O–C) that raise surface energy and reduce the top-
layer contact angle from 158° to 30 ° (Fig. S30). XPS and EDS analyses verified the incorporation 
of these oxygenated groups, and the plasma parameters were optimized to preserve catalyst activity

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 14 -->

and pore morphology (Fig. S31- S33). The resulting electrode, PPE-T, thus exhibits a continuous 
porosity–wettability gradient throughout the CL. 
We next compared the performance of PPE, PPE-D, and PPE-T to evaluate the effect of gradient 
steepness. The dual-gradient design (PPE-D1.8) maintained 83% FE after 5 h —43% higher than 
PPE (Fig. 5b and Fig. S34). The same performance hierarchy of FSE < PPE < PPE-D was 
maintained under both acidic and alkaline electrolytes, confirming that the structural design 
principles are generally applicable across different pH environments (Fig. S35). The introduction of 
a surface-modified hydrophilic layer further amplified this effect: PPE-T outperformed both PPE 
and PPE-D, maintaining nearly 100% FE during the first 3 h (Fig. 5b). Across 0-500 mA cm-2, PPE-
T exhibited a near-linear increase in H 2O2 production rate, indicating that oxygen-transport 
limitations were fully resolved (Fig. S36). These results demonstrate that introducing multi-layered 
wettability gradients via scalable surface modification strategies can substantially enhance GDE 
performance under practical operating conditions. 
To elucidate the origin of the performance enhancement, LBM simulations were combined with in-
situ experiments to assess electrolyte breakthrough behavior. Gradient and non-gradient structures 
were generated using a modified QSGS algorithm (Fig. S37). The simulations showed that, at the 
same timestep, PPE-D restricted electrolyte intrusion to localized gas-liquid regions, preserving 
oxygen pathways, whereas PPE was completely flooded (Fig. S38). These trends were further
