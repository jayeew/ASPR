Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Referee report: “Superconductivity of Bad Fermions: Origin of Two1Gaps in HTSC Cuprates” 
Summary: The paper examines the role of NNN hopping (t') in the square-lattice single-band Hubbard model at various
doping relevant to the cuprate superconductors. The authors use two dual-fermion-based approaches: (i) DQMC and (ii)
single-impurity DMFT. The work focuses on understanding whether pseudo-gap (PG) phenomena and d-wave
superconductivity (d-SC) are friends or foes. The authors demonstrate that the PG exists at the anti-nodal points for optimal
doping and t' value at high temperatures. They ascribe its origin to scattering from local magnetic moments (LMM) and
antiferromagnetic correlations between them. On introducing a d-SC field, the authors find that the d-SC coexists with LMM
(or with the PG phase). The authors further conclude that while spectral weight is pulled into forming the LMM, thereby
weakening d-SC, the AF correlations among them enhance the superconducting correlations. They also demonstrate that
the spin fluctuations that aid SC are mainly due to AF fluctuations of the LMM state and rule out paramagnon contributions
(by comparing with single site DMFT). 
Strengths: The paper addresses a long-standing open question. While it does not conclusively solve the problem, it offers
clear and strong hints at the cooperative role of PG and AF correlations in the LMM phase for stabilizing d-SC and offers a
microscopic reason for it. From a technical standpoint, the paper employs cutting-edge strong-correlation approaches, and
the results appear sound. 
Weaknesses: The paper requires some revisions regarding the presentation. While technically sound, the abstract neither
conveys the question nor the conclusions the authors offer in a transparent manner. While such an abstract would be OK for
a focused journal, it should be rewritten for a broader audience, as the present journal has. Similarly, the final discussion in
the paper can be sharpened. 
Minor technical comments: 
1. It will be helpful to comment in the introduction on the nature and origin of the PG phase at half-filling, where it is known to
produce a sector-selective Mott transition (with change t’) [PRB 80, 245102 (2009)]. 
2. A clarification on why Higgs's field and the d-wave field must be added? to break the symmetry of the formalism? 
Typos: 
1. “to a huge enhancement of the binding energy fo two holes,” 
2. Pg 3 of Supplementary materials: displays enhanced intensities at momenta corresponding to the Fermi surface shifted by
the AFM Q vector, i.e. G(k + Q)….."Something missing?" …. but also to the anomalous S part of the self-energy, through the
scattering of electrons on an effective classical AFM mode that originates from the Higgs field.

In summary, the work particularly stands out for using the novel dual-fermion approach and the insightful comparison with
single-site DMFT to strongly suggest the cooperative role of AF spin fluctuations from local moments in the PG in aiding
superconducting fluctuations. Thus, it suggests the cooperative role of PG in stabilizing superconducting order. I will
recommend acceptance of the paper once the above concerns/comments are addressed. 
Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This paper by Stepanov et al. presents a comprehensive theoretical study on the origin of the two-gap structure observed in
high-temperature superconducting cuprates. The authors employ state-of-the-art approaches combining strong-coupling
Green's function expansion with exactly solvable reference systems to investigate the spectral properties of the doped t-t'
Hubbard model. 
They reveal a strongly renormalized flat band feature with a pseudogap around the antinodal point in the electronic spectral
function of the doped t-t' Hubbard model. They show that the superconducting response exhibits a pseudogap at the
antinodal point, related to the "bad-fermion" behavior in the normal phase, and a d-wave-like structure in the anomalous
Green's function. They propose that the formation of local magnetic moments and their antiferromagnetic correlations are the
key microscopic mechanism underlying high-temperature superconductivity. 
The conclusions presented in the paper are based on solid theoretical calculations and simulations. 
The authors have
demonstrated that their approach can capture the key features observed in experiments, such as the pseudogap and the d-
wave superconducting gap. There are several works that investigate related aspects, such as the role of antiferromagnetic
fluctuations and pseudogap in high-temperature superconductors. The authors have adequately discussed these works and
positioned their own contributions within the broader context. 
I believe this paper has the potential to significantly impact the field of high-temperature superconductivity. The proposed
mechanism provides an insightful perspective on the pseudogap phenomenon and its connection to superconductivity. 
One question before be accepted is as follow; 
The study based on detecting response of the system to specific external field, such as d-wave field or Higgs field simulating
local Neel antiferromagnetic moment. Is it possible to rule out that other kinds of fields of superconductivity,
antiferromagnetism, or even charge density wave will not be able to produce similar outcomings?

Reviewer #4 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors investigate the strongly interacting hole doped Hubbard model at intermediate temperatures with two numerical
methods and discuss mechanisms for the transition from bad metal to superconductor. Both methods (as applied to the
normal state) have been introduced in previous works: The first combines exact lattice determinant quantum Monte Carlo
(DQMC) at particle hole symmetry with a first order dual fermion (DF) expansion in the next nearest neighbor hopping t’ and
the chemical potential mu (measured from the particle hole symmetric value U/2). The second method, D-TRILEX, starts
from the single site dynamical mean field theory solution (at the actual parameter point away from particle hole symmetry)
and, after a partial bosonization of the interaction, adds nonlocal correlations through self consistent equations for the
fermionic and bosonic self energies. Their main conclusion about superconductivity is that the bad metal phase of the
strongly interacting Hubbard model features local magnetic moments with strong short ranged antiferromagnetic (AFM)
correlations. In these AFM clusters (of size of about 4x4) it is energetically favorable to form pairs of two holes. Then these
hole pairs become superconducting Cooper pairs to gain kinetic energy through the next nearest neighbor hopping t’. 
I do not support publication of the manuscript in the present form because (i) the numerical results do not appear sound to
me and (ii) the interpretation (while it may or may not be true) is hardly attached to the results of the paper and rather seems

to reflect the authors’ general opinion. Below I elaborate on both of these aspects of criticism in turn: 
(i a) The lattice DQMC + DF method was introduced in Ref. [44] of the revised manuscript and abbreviated DF-QMC therein.
It starts from the exact solution at particle hole symmetry and uses the first order dual self energy diagram (a two particle
vertex closed with a dual propagator line) to get results away from particle hole symmetry. The perturbative parameters are
4t’ = -1.2 t and mu = -1.5 t. The authors claim that these parameters should be compared to the bare bandwidth W = 8t and
can thus be considered small. However it is unclear to me and not explained that 4t’/W and mu/W are the proper and only
relevant dimensionless perturbative parameters. Similarly it is unclear to me and not explained what radius of convergence
one can expect when expanding about such a special point as the AFM Mott insulator which is tied to half filling. 
While I am not convinced about the reliability of this calculation a priori (as explained above), looking at the results actually
makes me very skeptic. The left panel of Fig. 3 shows a gap in the momentum resolved spectral function around \omega=0
at k=X and k=(Gamma-M)/2. This corresponds to the exact solution at particle hole symmetry and matches with expectations.
However the right panel of Fig. 3, showing the approximate result at 15% doping, has a gap at k=(Gamma-M)/2 around a
positive frequency \omega \approx 2. An interaction driven gap at finite frequencies strikingly violates expectations. I suspect
this gap is spuriously inherited from the solution at particle hole symmetry, as the first order expansion is able to shift this gap
but apparently not to properly eliminate it, and thus an artifact of the method. 
More importantly the authors find a pseudogap in their central result, bottom panel of Fig. 1, at U/t=5.6, T/t=0.2, t’/t=-0.3, and
12% hole doping. I believe that this is an artifact of the method too and that no pseudogap exists at this point in parameter
space. The reason is that the exact diagrammatic Monte Carlo results of Ref. [43] of the revised manuscript, including
Supplemental Material, taken at this value of U and T show that the critical doping for a pseudogap is about 6% at t’=0 (or
7.5% for the onset of a pseudogap) and 5% at t’/t=-0.2 (or 8.5% for the onset of a pseudogap). I find it hard to believe that
going from t’/t=-0.2 to t’/t=-0.3 increases the critical doping from 5% (or 8.5% for the onset) to way above 12% (it should be
above 15% judging from the right panel of Fig. 3). This skepticism about the numerical results for the normal Green’s
function obviously puts the numerical results for the anomalous Green’s function, which lead to the discussion part of the
paper, into question too. 
(i b) The approximations going into the D-TRILEX method are nontrivial to understand a priori. Judging from published
numerical results of some of the authors, Harkov et al. Phys. Rev. B 103, 245123, it was argued in Fig. 7 therein that D-
TRILEX reproduces the nonlocal part of the self energy in good agreement with exact diagrammatic Monte Carlo at U/t=5.6,
T/t=0.2, t’/t=-0.3, and 4% hole doping. In the left panel of Fig. 5 of the present manuscript D-TRILEX does not produce a
pseudogap at T/t=0.1 and 16% hole doping. This is not surprising to me, given the large doping. What is surprising though is
that in this instance the D-TRILEX results are dismissed by the authors and a new ingredient h_{AFM} is introduced to make
the D-TRILEX results agree with DF-QMC. Hence I don’t think that these results can be used as an independent justification
of the DF-QMC results, on which I explained my doubts above. 
(ii) The first part (i.e. the first paragraph) of the discussion section contains the interpretation of the results. This interpretation
does not really refer to the numerical results presented in the manuscript thus far. The only superficial references are the
introductory phrase “the obtained results call for identifying…” and the last sentence “for the large doping \delta \geq 25% our
… scheme shows that the ‘bad electrons’ become much more coherent…” (this is rather trivial and entirely expected for such
large doping). The rest of the interpretation appears to be the authors’ general opinion, based on previous work of some of
the authors on 4x4 systems, and thus does not fit here. 
The discussion section then continues with (re-)explaining the D-TRILEX method and showing the D-TRILEX results. It is an
interesting observation that adding h_{AFM} induces a pseudogap in D-TRILEX. However as I argued before, this does not
convince me as an independent justification of the DF-QMC results. Also the discussion section does not seem the right
place to add another set of results. In fact the last paragraph of the “discussion” section begins with the subtlety of how to
incorporate h_{AFM} which is likely the least interesting to a general audience. 
A minor comment: 
Figures 1, 3 and 4 need color bars, and the meaning of d_w^\pm, occurring in the inset of Fig. 1, should be explained in the
caption. 
Conclusion: I do not support publication of the manuscript in the present form. If the authors wish to resubmit their paper they
need to 
(i) show explicitly that higher orders in the DF expansion are small compared to the first order or benchmark their results with
the closest available exact solution in a similar part of the phase diagram. Here this would be diagrammatic Monte Carlo at
the same U, T, \delta but at t’/t=-0.2 instead of -0.3 (as available in the Supplemental Material of Ref. [43] of the revised
manuscript). Due to its high temperature the provided benchmark in the Supplemental Material (beta=3, mu=-0.1) does not
suffice to validate the pseudogap finding in the main part. 
(ii) Further a restructuring of the manuscript is necessary so that all methods and numerical results are explained before an
interpretation which refers in detail to the results of the paper is given.

Reply to Reviewer #1
Reviewer #1: The authors investigate the spectral properties of the doped t–t′ Hubbard model, employ-
ing dual fermion lattice DQMC and D-TRILEX methodologies to explore the origins of the two-gap
phenomenon in t–t′ Hubbard model with additional parameters. Part of the results and method devel-
opment has been published in Ref. [42]. It limits the contribution of the manuscript to the theoretical
explanation of the observed result, where the current manuscript failed to convey. While the study ad-
dresses a topic of interest, the manuscript in its current form presents several critical issues that preclude
its suitability for publication in Communications Physics.
Reply: We thank Reviewer #1 for careful reading of our manuscript. Our work presents the fist DF-
QMC framework to study superconductivity and the two-gap phenomenon in t–t′ in the relative large
16× 16 Hubbard system, while our previous paper (Ref. [42]) was limited to a smaller 8 × 8 system
and investigated only normal state spectral functions. In order to do so, in this work we generalize our
DF-QMC formalism to a Nambu-Gor’kov superconducting scheme (Section: Method). Furthermore,
to elucidate the mechanism of strong-coupling superconductivity we compares these results with the
D-TRILEX methodology. The latter has also been extended to the Nambu-Gor’kov formalism for the
first time in the present work, which allowed us to draw conclusions about the role of the Higgs “local
moment” phase in the d-wave pairing. We have considered the critical comments of Reviewer #1 to
improve the presentations of our results.
Reviewer #1: Major Concerns:
1. Numerical Methodology and Parameter Justification:
- Dependence on Essential Correlations: The e ffectiveness of the dual fermion (DF) approach hinges
on the selection of essential correlations. The manuscript lacks a clear discussion on the criteria for
determining these correlations and does not justify the specific choices made in the DF-DQMC and
D-TRILEX schemes concerning high-Tc superconductors. A detailed explanation is necessary to assess
the validity of the methods employed.
Reply: The Reviewer #1 is correct that the effectiveness of the dual fermion DF-QMC approach hinges
on the selection of the reference system. Our choice of the reference system is motivated by the following
two arguments: First, one should choose the reference system as closed as possible to the target system.
Second, the reference problem should be exactly solvable numerically. In Ref. [42] we pointed out
that any set of t′/t and µ parameters (with the fixed U) is possible for reference system provided a
numerically exact QMC solution is still possible, in order to performed DF-QMC calculations for the
target system. The reality of a large 16 × 16 lattice at rather low temperatures β/t∼ 10 restricts the
parameter space of the reference system to a half-field, particle-hole symmetric case due to a severe sign
problem. Nevertheless, the most important electronic correlations are already included in this reference
system, which produce complicated four-peak structure with the Hubbard and Slater bands in the density
of states. The validity of our method is checked via a direct comparison with the DQMC scheme in the
high-temperature regime, where the sign problem is mild (see new Supplemental Materials and the Main
text)
The reference system for the D-TRILEX scheme is the standard DMFT single-site impurity problem
considered for the same model parameters as the target system. To go beyond this approximation and
use a small cluster DMFT as a reference has a problem of breaking the translational symmetry and was
also discussed in the Ref. [42].

Reviewer #1: 4. Conceptual Clarity and Physical Interpretation:
- Local Magnetic Moments (LMMs) and Superconductivity: The manuscript suggests that LMMs sup-
press superconductivity by forming plasmaron shadow bands and reducing spectral weight at the Fermi
surface, while simultaneously enhancing it by providing additional pairing “glue.” This dual role is in-

Reply: We are sorry that we did not formulate clearly enough the sentence.
Note that a bifacial effect of AFM correlations of LMMs on superconductivity is reminiscent of the
role of plasmons, which suppress superconductivity by forming plasmaron shadow bands and reducing
the spectral weight at the Fermi surface, while simultaneously enhancing it by providing additional glue
for electron pairing Ref. [71], which resulted in misunderstanding. We do not mean that LMMs lead
to ’plasmarons’. We just mean that there are twofold e ffect of any bosonic glue: additional attraction
between electrons which favors superconductivity and redistribution of spectral density which may be
unfavorable. To avoid misunderstanding we reformulated this sentence:
“Note that bifacial effect of AFM correlations of LMMs on superconductivity can be considered as
an example of a more general tendency. The e ffect of bosonic degrees of freedom on superconductiv-
ity is frequently twofold. On the one hand, they can produce additional glue between electrons (that
is, additional attractive interaction) which favors superconductivity. On the other hand, they can lead
to redistribution of spectral density decreasing the density of states at the Fermi energy which may be
harmful for superconductivity. In the weak-coupling regime, the example of this twofold behaviour is
provided by plasmonic superconductivity [71]. We consider here strong coupling situation which means
that direct analogies are not possible but this example may be make the bifacial character of the e ffects
under consideration less surprising.”

Referee Report 		The	authors	investigate	the	spectral	properties	of	the	doped	t–t'	Hubbard	model,	employing	dual	fermion	lattice	DQMC	and	D-TRILEX	methodologies	to	explore	the	origins	of	the	two-gap	phenomenon	in	t-t’	Hubbard	model	with	additional	parameters.	Part	of	the	results	and	method	development	has	 been	 published	 in	 Ref.[42].	 It	limits	the	 contribution	 of	 the	manuscript	to	 the	 theoretical	 explanation	 of	 the	 observed	 result,	 where	 the	 current	manuscript	failed	to	convey.	While	the	study	addresses	a	topic	of	interest,	the	manuscript	in	its	current	form	presents	several	critical	issues	that	preclude	its	suitability	for	publication	in	Communications	Physics.		Major	Concerns:	1. Numerical	Methodology	and	Parameter	Justification:		-	Dependence	on	Essential	Correlations:	The	effectiveness	of	the	dual	fermion	(DF)	approach	 hinges	 on	 the	 selection	 of	 essential	 correlations.	 The	 manuscript	 lacks	 a	clear	 discussion	 on	 the	 criteria	 for	 determining	 these	 correlations	 and	 does	 not	justify	the	specific	choices	made	in	the	DF-DQMC	and	D-TRILEX	schemes	concerning	high-Tc	superconductors.	A	detailed	explanation	is	necessary	to	assess	the	validity	of	the	methods	employed.			-	Chemical	 Potential	 Scaling	 (α	 Parameter):	 The	 manuscript	 introduces	 a	 scaling	factor	 α	 for	 the	 chemical	 potential	 without	 adequate	 clarification.	 It	 is	 unclear	whether	this	scaling	affects	particle	filling	or	maintains	half-filling	conditions.	Also,	the	manuscript	referring	α	=	1	to	the	“original	system”	is	questionable.	Clarification	on	these	points	is	essential.					-	Finite-Size	 Effects	and	 hyperparameter:	The	 study	 utilizes	 lattice	 sizes	 L	 =	 16	without	 providing	 a	 comparative	 analysis	 to	 assess	 finite-size	 effects.	 Such	 an	analysis	 is	 crucial	 to	 ensure	 that	 the	 results	 are	 not	 artifacts	 of	 the	 chosen	 system	size.	To	 allow	 better	 judge	 of	 the	 result,	 the	 authors	 should	 also	 give	 the	hyperparameters	 of	 the	 method	 they	 used,	including	two	 QMC	 schemes	 and	analytical	continuation.					-	Interaction	Strength	(U)	Relevance:	The	manuscript	does	not	justify	whether	the	selected	 on-site	 interaction	 strength	 U	 is	 experimentally	 relevant	 for	 cuprate	superconductors.	 Given	 that	 U	 significantly	 influences	 electronic	 correlations,	 its	selection	must	be	substantiated.
