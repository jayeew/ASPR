Reviewers' comments:
 
Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
X. Guo and colleagues report on the discovery of Sondheimer oscillations in cadmium single crystals. This phenomenon -
Sondheimer oscillations - is generally not widely known or extensively studied in the condensed matter physics community,
but it has attracted renewed interest following recent experimental developments. The present manuscript contributes
valuable novel insights by demonstrating that the observed oscillations originate from a commensurability between two
distinct quantizations. 
The manuscript is clearly and carefully written, with an excellent presentation of the experimental data and corresponding
analysis. The figures are of high quality, and the discussion is logical and coherent. All major claims are well supported by
the data, and the authors’ arguments are both careful and convincing. 
Overall, this is a high-quality and timely contribution to the research on Sondheimer oscillations. I recommend publication in
Communications Materials. 
Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author): 
The manuscript COMMSMAT-25-0957 “Scalable Sondheimer oscillations driven by commensurability between two
quantizations” presents interesting new idea and experimental results, worthing publication in Communication Materials. 
Howevwer, the manuscript does not provide a theory of the observed effect, only the idea about the direction in which the
theory should be developed to explain (if lucky) this phenomenon. The proposed theory in Sec. IIIC on page 11 is not quite
clear. Maybe this can be partially improved by stylistic revision. In particular, (1) why the right panel of Fig. 8c describes the
odd number? Maybe more depicted Landau levels and dimensional-quantization levels will make this clearer. (2) Why Eq.
(12) describes the magnetic flux times the elementary charge e? This statement is repeated after Eq. 13. (3) The comparison
in Fig. 9 shows a considerable frequency mismatch, especially upper panels. Usually, the frequency of magnetic oscillations
is described by theory quite accurately. what is the possible origin of this mismatch? 
There are other stylistic drawbacks. For example, 
(i) in the caption to Fig. 2a,b, the authors write amplitude of oscillations while the oscillations themselves are plotted. 
(ii) There are misprints. For example, before Eq. (5) the power of B should be -5/2 instead of -5. 
(iii) I don’t like the last phrase of Sec. IV Conclusions “We then showed this allows us to understand the empirical scaling as
a simple expression in which the key parameter is a dimensionless parameter set by the Fermi surface geometry.” Maybe
better start it as “This may help to understand ..”. As I understand, this dimensionless parameter is $\Omega$ given by Eq. 9,
but it is set not only by the Fermi-surface geometry. 
To summarize, I think the paper may be published in Communication Materials after a stylistic revision. 
Reviewer #3 (Remarks to the Author): 
I enjoyed reading the manuscript by Guo et al. 
The manuscript looks at a classical effect called Sondheimer oscillations (first
proposed in the 1950s) and examines the interplay with Landau oscillations. 
I think one point not sufficiently emphasized by
the authors is that in previous studies such as Ref. 17 (van Delft et al.) on WP2 there was a big separation in scale between
Landau level spacing and the confinement spacing, and as a result these could be taken as independent and additive. 
By
contrast, in the current work these energy scales are comparable, and the authors make a convincing case that when the
Landau level spacing becomes comparable to the k_z sub band spacing the two quantizations become commensurate,
leading to periodic reinforcement in conductivity. 
This provides a quantum origin to the Sondheimer oscillations. 
In my view, this is an interesting new twist on some old physics, and could potentially be used as a complementary probe of
Fermi surface topology. 
While I am supportive that this work should be published in Communication Materials, there are some concerns that the
authors should address before publication. 
Once these issues are addressed, the manuscript would make a valuable
addition to the literature. 
1. The style of presentation was a bit unusual with most of the figures showing experimental data scaled a certain way and
then judging goodness by eye e.g. Fig 3 shows that scaling by B^4 does not stabilize oscillation amplitude except in the 4th
sample beyond a critical field. 
I would prefer more quantitative fits with error bars and confidence intervals. 
2. I think the temperature data is very limited (only between 2K and 8K). 
The paper would benefit from more data below 2K,
or if the authors are unable to do this, they should explain the reason why in the main text.

Fig. 8c has been reproduced below. It sketches allowed states in a (E, 𝑘𝑧) plane. Spatial confinement 
imposes 𝑘𝑧 = 𝑛𝑘𝑑.  Each vertical line represents such a discrete 𝑘𝑧 value, which is allowed and

degeneracies is an odd integer, the incommensurability between the two is extreme. In the even case 
(left panel), the chemical potential (which is set by the electron density) is located at the intersection 
of the oblique and vertical lines, satisfying both constraints. In the odd case (right panel), chemical 
potential would be at the farthest possible distance between two neighboring intersections. We have

direct space. Multiplied by 𝐵, it represents magnetic flux, the conjugate of the electric charge. To
