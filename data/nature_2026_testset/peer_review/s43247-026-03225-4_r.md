General comment 
 
This study by Wang et al. applies a 3D glacier model to all glaciers in High Mountain Asia (HMA) 
larger than 1 km² to assess the impact of what the authors define as positive glacial regulatory processes. 
The focus lies specifically on four processes that contribute beneficially to the role of glaciers as water 
resources in HMA. 
 
The results are interesting and noteworthy, highlighting several aspects that have often been overlooked 
or not quantified until now. Overall, I find the study valuable, provided t hat some clarifications are 
added and the figures optimised. As a reader, my main difficulty lies in following the details of the mass 
balance model, which is, I believe, the key component of such a study. 
 
Main comments 
 The mass balance model appears rather simple, especially in contrast with the more advanced 
ice flow model. Why was a more sophisticated approach (e.g., a 2D balance model or a 
simplified energy balance model) not chosen to determine mass balance? 
 The study uses 28 GCMs to force the mass balance model. Was a multi-model mean calculated 
afterwards, or was a multi -model forcing applied directly to drive the model once? The first 
option would be the correct way. 
 How was the ice flow model calibrated? 
 A comparison of glacier volume with prev ious studies (some are already cited in the reference 
list) is missing. Such a comparison is essential for the credibility of the study, as it is otherwise 
difficult to judge how well the model performs.  
 It is not entirely clear to me, regarding the repor ted quantities (e.g., already in the abstract), 
whether these correspond to ice that would otherwise have melted in the absence of the 
regulatory process. For example, observations may show 30 Gt of melt, but without increased 
precipitation this would have  been 32 Gt , is this the correct interpretation? Clarifying this 
throughout the text would be useful. In addition, the filling of exposed depressions following 
glacier retreat seems to be a different type of process: it does not reduce ice volume loss but 
rather limits runoff and enhances on -land water storage. I am not fully convinced this belongs 
in the same category as the other three processes considered.” 
 There is no proper conclusion section where the main findings of the study are repeated/stated. 
 Figure 5 shows that the dynamical component is by far the largest positive GRP. This term is 
estimated by comparing results from a flow -band model with an advanced 3D model. I do not 
fully understand why this approach was chosen, as it seems prone to biased interpretations. Why 
not consistently use the 3D model, but run two simulations: (1) with ice flow velocity allowed 
to vary through time, and (2) with velocity kept constant (even as ice thickness decreases, until 
it reaches zero)? This would, in my view, provide a more robust way to isolate the dynamical 
contribution.

Model performance evaluation 256 
Comparisons with multi-source observations reveal that our model exhibits good performance. 257 
Compared our results with other studies in HMA, model performances are satisfactory (Fig. S21). 258
 
In contrast to large -scale remote sensing data 2, overall errors of simulated glacier changes under 259 
different SSPs are 4.6 %, 6.5%, 3.4% and 8.1%. In situ mass balance observations were obtained 260
 
from the World Glacier Monitoring Service  ( WGMS)54. The difference between modeled and 261 
observed annual glacier -wide annual surface mass balances is - 0.04 ± 0.52 m w.e.a -1 ( r=0.35, 262 
p<0.01), which includes 884 observations of 31 glaciers from WGMS  (Fig. S22) . To evaluate 263 
performance of the model parameterization scheme for temperature and precipitation uncertainty 264 
correction, we used elevation mass balance observations for comparison. The difference between 265 
modelled and observed elevation band annual surface mass balances is -0.05 ± 0.84 m w.e.a -1 266 
(r=0.80, p<0.01), which includes 4694 observations provided from WGMS  (Fig. S22). Notably, 267 
field observations are severely constrained by the difficult accessibility of HMA glaciers, leading 268 
to uncertainties in accuracy assessments. Since measured points within glaciers are typically 269 
distributed at lower elevations, overall glacier mass balance is affected by sample distribution 270 
heterogeneity. Consequently, the elevation band glacier mass balance is significantly better than 271 
glacier-wide mass balance.  Furthermore, a comparison of the multi -year mean and maximum  272 
glacier velocities from remote sens ing observations indicates good performance of the glacier 273 
dynamics scheme (Table. S4).  Due to simplifications in our glacier dynamics model, the complex 274 
climatic and topographic conditions within the HMA, and inherent biases in observational data, 275 
flow velocity simulations involve numerous sources of uncertainty, and significant challenges 276 
remain to be addressed. 277 
Our model also captures phenomena such as the breakage of the Glacier Hailuogou Icefall, the 278 
stationary but growing dead ice at the Glacier Dagongba terminus,  and other features of glacier 279 
evolution that are present in our field investigation  (Fig. S23, field data from eight glaciers were 280 
used as reference). Moreover, we matched the distribution of glacial lakes in 2020 with the locations 281 
of simulated potential glacial lakes and found that 70% of the large glacial lakes (with areas 282 
exceeding 0.25 km2) are already present, suggesting overall reliability in the simulation of potential 283 
glacial lakes (Fig. S24). 284 
Prospects and further aspects 285 
These results are essential for ecological maintenance and sustainable development of 286 
downstream basins, especially in endorheic regions. Projected precipitation increase is lower for 287 
endorheic regions than for exorheic regions (precipitation increase in endorheic regions is expected 288 
to be approximately one-quarter to a half of that in exorheic regions) (Fig. S4) and is accompanied 289 
by projected increases in evaporation and extreme precipitation variability. Substantial expansion 290 
of Central Asian deserts has already been observed over past decades53. Increasing and sustainable 291 
glacier runoff recharge would alleviate possible future drought stress. In addition, the application 292 
of our model on a global scale can be used to correct overestimates of glacier contributions to SLR; 293 
these results would be relevant for policymakers, governments, and stakeholders , and can be used 294 
to support rational adaptive measures to harmonize ecological impacts and socio-economic 295 
development. 296 
Our GRP simulation methodology is potentially applicable worldwide and can be used as a 297 
valuable tool for similar studies of glaciated regions worldwide to improve our understanding of 298 
the global response of glaciers to future climate warming. A full -process-based and accurate 299 
assessment of glacier mass loss on a global scale is of profound importance for regional water 300

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 19 -->

Page 10 of 21 
 
integration and allocation , as well as for mitigating global effects of SLR. Our model interface 301 
allows for expansion and integration of additional glacial elements (e.g., glacial lakes 8, light -302 
absorbing particles18,19). While the model overcomes the limitations of high temporal and spatial 303 
resolution inputs55, it can potentially be used to study transient and short -term glacier processes 304 
(e.g., basal slide, surge, and avalanche), as well as to support potential  glacial lake outburst flood 305 
assessments56-58. 306 
Furthermore, while this study provides a case for developing more comprehensive glacier 307 
evolution models, several considerations remain for subsequent extension to other glacier-covered 308 
regions worldwide and for further refinement. P artial parameter selections in this study exhibit 309 
strong regional specificity (e.g., degree-day factors, precipitation tuning factors), necessitating the 310 
selection of parameters aligned with the study region's characteristics to adjust the model structure. 311 
Simultaneously, both the mass balance and glacier dynamics components within glacier evolution 312 
models incorporate varying degrees of simplification. Since there are unresolved uncertainties in 313 
reflecting actual glaci er evolution, developing glacier evolution models based on full physical 314 
processes is essential. Despite the significant resource demands this entails, it could facilitate more 315 
accurate understanding of glacier evolution and its underlying mechanisms. Energy- mass balance 316 
model combined with full -Stokes glacier dynamics model provides the most advanced approach 317 
for realistic glacier evolution simulation . However, the model is  highly dependent on accurate 318 
physical field forcing inputs. A major challenge that urgently requires resolution is how to 319 
generalize these glacier models—which are sensitive to input parameters and physical processes—320 
across vast glacierized regions lacking field observations.  321

Uncertainty and validation 532 
Uncertainty 533 
To address the issue of uncertainties associated with complex potential fluctuations, we 534 
conducted Monte Carlo simulations of the MBG.R model to increase the randomization of variables 535 
(e.g., precipitation, debris thickness). 10 sets of random input parameters were generated for each 536 
of the 112 GCM datasets to correct model parameters, allowing 1232 modelling parameters to be 537 
applied to each modelled glacier. Relevant parameter settings can be found in Tab le. S6. Debris 538 
evolution, ice cliffs, and supraglacial lakes were difficult to quantify over the long term, and we 539 
could not meet the computational demands of the iterative updating of model initial states for time 540 
step breakpoints. A recent study compared the effects of the absence of debris and debris expansion 541 
on enhanced ablation and suggested that debris expansion in this century would contribute no more 542 
than 3% to enhanced ablation7. Moreover, ablation bias due to ice cliffs is partially included in the 543 
debris–ablation enhancement curve7. Therefore, we only accounted for current debris coverage in 544 
our model. Large-scale field observations of lake -terminating glaciers, bedrock lithology, surface 545 
fragmentation, and light -absorbing particles are missing and the relationships of these elements 546 
with glacial mass balance are unclear. Thus, these elements have not been included in our model. 547 
However, the model interface has been preset to allow expansion and inclusion of additional 548 
elements. 549 
In the glacier dynamics simulations, we repeated GCM ensemble runs for four SSPs to derive 550 
average mass balance evolution using annual time steps and omitting englacial streams because of 551 
limitations of computational and storage capacity ( Single-round simulation were conducted using 552 
a super-computing cluster and consumed substantial computational resources —over 400,000 core 553 
hours). 554 
Notably, 678 glaciers were discarded, which are not convergent. Model non- convergence 555 
stems from multiple causes, including anomalous DEM data, unreasonable ice thickness, complex 556 
geometric features, and biases arising from grid sampling. Thus, we only analysed the data from 557 
15,292 glaciers to derive results, which account for over 97% of the total glacier mass in HMA. 558 
Moreover, for the pulsation phenomena observed in regions such as the Pamir and Karakoram, 559 
these generally alter the internal mass distribution within glaciers42, without contributing to glacier 560 
accumulation. Therefore, the component of influence is excluded when quantifying positive GRP 561 
contributions. 562
