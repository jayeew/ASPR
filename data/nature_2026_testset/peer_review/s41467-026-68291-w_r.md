Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In “Spatiotemporal Regulatory Logics of Mouse Gastrulation”, Yang et al describe two resources, ST-MAGIC and ST-
MAGIC+, that project new single cell multimodal transcriptome and ATAC-seq data collected for 4 timepoints between E6.5-
E7.5 during mouse gastrulation onto the published Geo-seq spatial profiling atlas. To identify gene regulatory networks, the
authors introduce a new software package, BioCRE, to analyze multimodal transcriptome information with ATAC-seq data to
link genes with accessibility peaks. 
The authors describe several vignettes including dissection of the GRNs and enhancers
that guide mesendoderm development to showcase how these resources can be used. 
Through those vignettes, they
identify novel enhancer elements and transcription factors that may affect differentiation. 
The manuscript is a tour-de-force work integrating many published data sets and tools. 
Indeed, most figures both in the main
text and supplementary had a very large number of panels. 
The manuscript was reasonably well written so that I could follow
the logic through analysis of this complex data set and I commend the authors for providing many data panels. 
The
schematics as the first panel for several figures was helpful. 
Moreover, I appreciated the generation of new data to follow up
new hypotheses (Pou6f1 KO and Noto KO multi-seq data) and the RNA-scope experiments for validation. 
Nonetheless, it was difficult to assess many of the claims due to a lack of quantification, statistical tests, and useful
visualizations. 
While the development of new analysis platforms like BioCRE is useful, BioCRE was not adequately
benchmarked against other methods and so it is difficult to evaluate its contribution to this manuscript.

Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, Yang and colleagues present a spatiotemporal dataset capturing mouse gastrulation, generated using
10x Genomics Multiome data. The study involves a significant amount of data integration and projection, incorporating
external datasets such as Geo-seq and ChIP-seq. To enable gene regulatory network (GRN) analysis, the authors
developed a new computational tool called BioCREs, which they claim performs better than existing pipelines. They also
adapted the Tangram algorithm—originally designed for high-resolution spatial transcriptomics—for use with their data to
align it with Geo-seq references. Through this combined approach, they aim to map spatial and temporal regulatory changes
during gastrulation. 
The scope of this work is impressive, and the integration of multiple data types is interesting. The authors also acknowledge
the limitations of current spatial resolution and mismatched cell type references, which are important caveats. I appreciate
the validation experiments included, which lend support to some of their conclusions. That said, I do have several concerns,
especially regarding the BioCREs pipeline and the modified Tangram tool. 
Major Concerns: 
1. The claim that BioCREs outperforms Signac and ArchR is based on a single performance index. This is insufficient to
justify the claim. Benchmarking against established regulatory element datasets (e.g., ENCODE) is necessary to validate
accuracy and specificity. 
2. Distal vs. Proximal regulatory region comparison: The comparison between "Distal High" and "Proximal" groups is biased
by excluding the "Distal Low" group. A more appropriate comparison would be between all distal regions and proximal ones,
with proper statistical testing (e.g., p-values) to support conclusions. 
3. Insufficient benchmarking for BioCREs and modified Tangram: The manuscript lacks technical detail and performance
benchmarking for both pipelines. More rigorous testing is needed to demonstrate their robustness and reliability. 
4. Incorporating data from high-resolution platforms such as Visium HD or Stereo-seq would significantly strengthen the
spatial claims. Even one matching experiment would add credibility. 
Minor Comments: 
• The manuscript is dense, especially in later sections discussing biological processes. Streamlining these parts would
improve accessibility. 
• Some extended data figures are poorly organized and should be revised for clarity. 
• Clarity in Extended Data Fig. 2a: The plot is overcrowded due to overlapping cells, making it difficult to interpret. A side-by-
side comparison with reference data in separate panels would improve readability. 
• Missing data in Extended Fig. 4: The claim that ST-MAGIC has higher JSD values than Geo-seq is not supported by the
figure. This should be clarified or removed.

Responses to Reviewers 
 
Summary of major new data 
We would like to thank the reviewers for their valuable input. We provide a revised version 
of the manuscript with a substantial body of new analyses and experiment data that further 
confirm and strengthen the conclusions. Please note that we used Fig. 1, 2, 3, etc. to refer 
to figures in the revised manuscript and Response Fig. R1, R2, R3, etc. to refer to figures 
in this letter. 
 
1. We systematically benchmarked the performance of BioCRE through evaluating the 
biological relevance, cell type  specificity of gene-to-peak linkages, the computational 
efficiency, and consistency of linkage composition under various sample sizes. Moreover, 
through using publicly available single -cell multiome and matched promoter -capture HiC 
datasets, we identified the reliability and accuracy of linkage prediction . These analyses 
collectively demonstrated that BioCRE outperforms Signac and BioCRE in predicting 
reliable and accurate gene-to-peak linkages. 
 
2. We compared the ST-MAGIC atlas with a high-resolution Stereo-seq dataset (SEU-3D) 
for E7.5 embryo. As demonstrated by analyzing  cell type -specific transcriptomic 
architectures, signature gene set distributions, spatial locations of cell type s and gene 
expression patterns, ST-MAGIC shows high spatial consistency with SEU-3D. Furthermore, 
in-depth evaluation highlighted the effective attributes of ST -MAGIC in detecting low -
abundance genes. 
 
3. We incorporated additional metrics (such as Pearson correlation, structural similarity 
index, P-value) and versatile plots (histogram, scatter plot, density plot) to quantitatively 
evaluate the global similarities  between ST -MAGIC and Geo -seq atlas , and 
comprehensively assessed the spatiotemporal relationships for distinct signal cascades 
and eRegulons. Overall, the original spatial claims have been substantially strengthened. 
  
4. We conducted additional gastruloid differentiation experiments to determine the roles of 
Neo_LRE element and collected more mutant embryos to summarize the phenotypic 
significances for Noto mutant and Pou6f1 mutant embryos. Consequently, we 
demonstrated the roles of Neo_LRE in regulating Lefty2 expression, and confirmed the 
axis turning defects of Noto mutant and Pou6f1 mutant embryos.

Response: Thanks for th is valuable suggestion. We fully agree that a more comprehensive

In this revision, to benchmark the performance of BioCRE, we have conducted a series of 
systematic evaluations to rigorously compare the performance of BioCRE against the state-of-
the-art methods: Signac and ArchR. 
 
1. The characteristics of predicted gene-to-peak linkages:  
Using our multi -omic dataset from the mouse gastrula, we evaluated the number, biological 
relevance, and cell type  specificity of predicted gene -to-peak linkag es. As shown, BioCRE 
identified 44,644 gene-to-peak linkages, with a median gene -to-peak distance of 127,175 bp 
(Response Fig. R 3a, b). Comparative analyses revealed that 28,472 linkages (64%) were 
specifically identified in BioCRE ( Response Fig. R 3c, d). Gene ontology analyses of genes 
associated with these BioCRE -specific linkages (28,472 linkages) revealed significant 
enrichment in embryo development, including heart development, liver development, and 
nervous system development (Response Fig. R3e). This result suggests that these BioCRE-
specific linkages could be responsible for the regulation of embryo development related genes. 
To assess the cell type specificity of predicted linkages, we analyzed the accessibility of peaks 
linked to cell type-specific genes identified by each tool (Response Fig. R3f, g). Using UMAP 
visualization and quantifying clustering coherence with the Silhouette score, we found that 
linkages predicted by BioCRE gets a higher score compared to those from Signac and ArchR, 
indicating a more effective capability for BioCRE to capture cell type -specific linkages 
(Response Fig. R3f, g).  
 
Together, these results demonstrate that BioCRE could be adept at identify cell type-specific 
linkages, which are likely to play key roles in orchestrating embryo development. 
 
2. The computational efficiency and robustness for predicting linkages:

sampling conditions. Overall, we found that the built-in deep-learning bi-orientation regression

while only 8.2% linkages (3,677 out of 44,705) for Signac and 5.7% linkages (4,479 out of
