Reviewer #3 (Remarks to the Author): 
In this manuscript, the authors investigate the amorphous-to-crystalline transition during the synthesis of BaSnO
₃
 and
BaZrO
₃
. A combination of TEM techniques, including imaging, diffraction, and electron-based pair distribution
functon(ePDF) analysis, is employed to elucidate the crystallization pathways of these two systems. The reported results are
generally consistent with previously published studies, and thus the level of conceptual novelty is somewhat limited.
Nevertheless, the authors present a solid and technically rigorous study, providing a detailed characterization of the
structural evolution and phase transformation mechanisms in both BaSnO
₃
 and BaZrO
₃
. In particular, the application of the
ePDF approach to probe local and intermediate-range order offers useful insights that may be transferable to other material
systems. However, several minor issues should be addressed before publication. In summary, considering the overall
quality of the experimental data and the thoughtful discussion, I recommend this manuscript for publication in
Communication Materials, provided that the authors adequately address the points raised below. 
Comment 1. In the Methods section, the authors state that “diffraction patterns were recorded using a camera length of 36
mm.” Please verify whether this is a typographical error, as a value of 360 mm appears more realistic. 
Comment 2. In line 239, the unit of q appears to be incorrectly written. Please correct this. 
Comment 3. The authors mention that TEM-based EDX experiments were performed; however, no corresponding spectra or
quantitative results are presented (e.g., figures or tables). Please clarify and provide supporting data. 
Comment 4. Related to Comment 3, the experimental details for the EDX measurements are insufficient. Key parameters
such as beam current and acquisition time should be specified, as well as channeling width. In addition, it would be valuable
to clarify whether any compositional variations were observed as a function of EDX acquisition time (e.g., due to beam-
induced effects).

Reviewer #1 This manuscript titled “Decoding the Evolution of Peroxo Precursors into Perovskite Oxides through Real Space Analysis” provides an investigation into the phase formation pathways and local structural evolution during the synthesis of perovskite oxides BaSnO₃ (BSO) and BaZrO₃ (BZO) from hydrogen peroxide-derived peroxo precursors. By employing an advanced multi-technique approach—including Raman spectroscopy, thermal analysis, X-ray diffraction, and, most notably, real-space pair distribution function analysis (e-PDF)—the authors elucidate the fundamental atomistic mechanisms governing precursor conversion. The BSO precursor undergoes a low-temperature topotactic transformation facilitated by preserved local order, while the BZO precursor requires high-temperature nucleation and growth from a disordered amorphous state. The article is well-written and provides a comprehensive comparison of results obtained by different methods. The justification for the findings is also well presented. The authors investigate the structural transformation from a peroxo-containing precursor to barium stannate during thermal processing using a combination of techniques and examine how the results correlate with each other. However, a significant omission is the lack of chemical composition analysis for the initial barium peroxostannate precursor. To confirm that the intended phase was successfully synthesized prior to thermal treatment, elemental analysis (for Ba and Sn) and quantification of the active oxygen/peroxide content are essential. The use of the e-PDF method is undoubtedly interesting, and the authors employ it professionally in conjunction with other methods. Undoubtedly, the results are interesting from the perspective of peroxo-compound transformations. However, they concern a very specific reaction—the decomposition of cubic peroxostannate—and are unlikely to be of interest to a broad research audience. Therefore, I recommend that the authors consider submitting their technically sound study to a reputable journal with a more focused scope on solid-state chemistry, advanced characterization, or inorganic materials synthesis, where it would undoubtedly be of significant interest to a specialist audience. Response:  We acknowledge that the fundamental differences between amorphous and crystalline structures typically result in distinct PDF patterns. It is important to note that at the nanoscale, where the proportion of surface atoms is elevated, PDF peaks are often strongly attenuated or may even vanish, as atomic positions become less correlated in a manner that accurately reflects the average structure.
