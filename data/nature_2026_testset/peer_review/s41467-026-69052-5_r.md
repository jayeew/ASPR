Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript systematically demonstrates that bubble dynamics - rather than the conventionally emphasized
electrochemical surface area - constitutes the performance-determining factor for anion exchange membrane water
electrolyzers under industrially relevant current densities. Through modulation of pore architecture and wettability in
stainless steel anodes, particularly via designed gradient square-pore electrodes, the authors present significantly enhanced
bubble management capabilities and improved electrochemical performance. The work features clear logic, robust
methodology, and provides a compelling challenge to existing AEMWE electrode design paradigms, demonstrating both
academic significance and practical potential. 
However, the authors should address the following comments for improvement: 
Major comments: 
1. The introduction fails to adequately highlight the reason for investigating how bubble behavior affects mass transport and
electrochemical performance in stainless steel electrode. The literature review between Lines 72-92 requires further
refinement to be more comprehensive. Furthermore, the authors should explicitly state whether this represents the first
implementation of a systematic “gradient structure + bubble control” strategy in AEMWE technology. An expanded literature
review or dedicated comparative discussion section should be added to clearly establish this work’s scientific positioning
and breakthrough contributions. 
2. Regarding the conclusion that 10 μm pores facilitate bubble release more effectively than 5 μm pores in Figure 2d, a
single snapshot may not provide sufficient evidence as it lacks statistical significance. To strengthen this claim, we
recommend either: (a) Including supplementary video recordings at the same frame rate as supporting evidence, or (b)
Performing quantitative analysis of bubble dynamics (e.g., average detachment size, residence time, and detachment
frequency), following the methodology presented in Figure 3 of Article: ACS Appl. Mater. Interfaces 2025, 17, 7109−7118. 
3. In Figure 2f, it would be beneficial to: (a) Clearly label each peak position with arrows, and (b) Provide explicit explanation
of the methodology used for identifying the four peaks. 
4. The manuscript currently lacks corresponding discussion of Fig. S9. 
5. Lines 193-194: How was surface oxidation of the stainless steel prevented? Additionally, how were potential impacts of
this additional variable on the study’s conclusions mitigated? 
6. Lines 204-205: The statement “...which results in a little change in activation overpotentials...” requires revision. As clearly
demonstrated in Fig. 3f, the acid-washed felt exhibits significantly higher activation overpotentials across all current
densities compared to the alcohol-washed counterpart. This substantial difference should be properly acknowledged.
Furthermore, pleasr add a brief discussion to explain the potential causes of these overpotential differences between the two
treatment methods. 
7. Lines 216-217: “The exact reason for the above findings can be explained by the fact that the key processes of water
electrolyzer are affected...” is too vague. Clarify: (1) what exact reason, and (2) which key processes. The statement should
be made clear. 
8. Please mark the contact angle values in Figures 4a-b. Explain the statistical methods for bubble quantity and detachment
size in Figure 4d in the METHODS section. Additionally, format “Daverage” as “Daverage” (with “average” as subscript) in
Figs. 4d and 6d. 
9. Citations of current bubble dynamics studies would significantly strengthen the interpretation and impact of these results.
Specifically, I recommend referencing: (a) Insights into bubble dynamics in Water Splitting in “INTRODUCTION Section”:

Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, the authors experimentally demonstrated that bubble dynamics, which has been an overlooked factor in
the performance degradation of AEMWE, plays a critical role under high current density conditions (> 1.0 A cm-2). Based on
this insight, they proposed a new electrode design strategy. The study shows that even with a high ECSA, performance can
degrade if bubble removal is inefficient, whereas effective bubble management can lead to superior performance even with
a lower ECSA. The authors designed a gradient-structured stainless steel square hole mesh electrode to facilitate rapid
bubble detachment and promote water diffusion. 
While a more quantitative analysis of catalytic activity and mass transport—such as the impact of ECSA reduction—is
somewhat lacking, the study aligns well with the scope of Nature Communications by presenting a novel electrode structure
and supporting it with diverse experimental evidence focused on bubble behavior. 
Comments: 
1. The pore size of the stainless steel felt appears to be a critical variable in this study. However, the basis and method used
to determine the pore size are not clearly stated. To enhance the reliability of the results, it would be beneficial to include a
pore size characterization or provide additional explanation on how the values were obtained. 
2. The authors claim that smaller pore sizes lead to improved interfacial contact, but the experimental or theoretical basis for
this is not clearly presented. It would be helpful to clarify whether this trend is supported by direct evidence in the current
study or by previous literature that discusses the effect of pore size on interfacial contact. 
3. The ECSA values vary significantly depending on pore size, with the 5 μm sample showing the highest ECSA. However,
as shown in Figure 2c, the activation overpotential of the 5 μm sample is higher than those of the 10 μm and 30 μm samples
across the entire current density range. The authors attribute this to better bubble removal in the 10 μm sample, but it
remains unclear whether this effect is strong enough to outweigh the benefits of higher ECSA. Additionally, if the influence of
bubbles is minimal in the low current density region (e.g., < 0.5 A cm-2), it would be worth discussing whether the trends in
this region reflect the ECSA-based expectations. 
4. To clearly demonstrate the effects of acid-washed and alcohol-washed treatments, it is essential to include a comparison
with untreated samples. If data showing differences in electrochemical performance or ECSA before and after treatment are
available, including them would help better convey the effectiveness of the surface treatments. 
5. Figure 5f presents an impressive durability result, showing stable operation over approximately 400 hours. If the voltage
decay rate (mV h-1) during this period is quantified and compared with values reported in the literature, it could serve as a
compelling metric to highlight the superior long-term performance demonstrated in this study.

(Remarks to the Author) 
An et al. have implemented a multiscale visualization cell to explore how pore size and the hydrophilicity of a stainless-steel
electrode impact the behavior of gas bubbles produced by water electrolysis. The authors find that bubbles cover anode
active sites and hinder the transport of water to the membrane and cathode, such that maximizing the electrochemically
active surface area does not necessarily lead to improved performance. These problems were then addressed in an
engineered stainless-steel mesh leading to an improved anion exchange membrane water electrolyzer for operation at high
(5 A cm-2) current densities. 
Scientifically, the experiments and findings in this work are not particularly original and they do not differ much from recent
papers from the same authors published in the last years (e.g. Wu & An et al. 2025 Adv. Science), except for a new “acid
washing” treatment. Practically, the performance of the AEM electrolyzers in this work are not exceptional—many other
works have been published (e.g. Yanagi & Peng et al. 2025 Joule) with improved performance at both low and high current
densities, with more detailed characterization of transport layers and interfaces. I do not see the novelty or practical
importance of this work that justifies its rapid communication in Nature Communications. 
1. There is very little data or discussion on the changes caused by the acid treatment. At early times, it would be helpful to
know how the surface chemistry and < µm scale texture influences the gas release properties. For practical relevance, it
would be helpful to know how long the difference between the two meshes persist, since the structure and composition of
the anode will evolve during the break in period and much longer break in periods are used for industrial electrolysis
systems. 
2. Despite the focus on transport properties, only a single anode flow rate appears to have been used (5 mL per min). How
do these results change when the flow rate is increased/decreased. Obviously flow rate is an important design parameter for
transport and some of the limitations addressed here might be equally addressed by increasing the flow rate of 1 M KOH.
The authors should compare the sensitivity of their results at an increased flow rate (>20 mL min-1 as well as a reduced flow
rate, e.g. 1 mL min-1 where the effects of gas evolution may be more evident). 
3. I doubt that the comparisons of IrO2, stainless steel, and Ni mesh—which are based on small scale suppliers for R&D
research—are applicable to the costs of these materials at scale. Notably, the difference in the cost of Ni felt and IrO2 are not
that much… How sensitive are the estimated costs to simple changes in catalyst loading or mesh thickness?

We agree with the Reviewer and have provided the clearer statement. The exact reason for the 
performance improvement of AEMWE caused by acid -washed felt can be attributed to the 
improved bubble dynamics, which optimizes its key processes (Figure 1): (1) Reduced active site 
coverage mitigates anode activation and mass transport polarization; 2) Promoted water across the 
membrane improves ohmic polarization; 3) Increased transmembrane water alleviates cathode 
water shortage-induced activation.  
Specifically, microscale visualization characterization is employed to elucidate the relationship 
between electrode structure, electrochemical performance, and bubble behaviors. SEM imaging 
(Figures 4a -b) reveals acid -washing-induced surface roughening, while contact angle 
measurements confirm 38% hydrophilicity improvement (wetting time reduction from 56.6 ms to 
41.1 ms), promoting faster bubble evolution. And in-situ bubble tracking (Figures 4a-d) quantifies 
optimized bubble management (also see video S 2). Acid -washed felts generate 20% smaller 
bubbles (56.4 to 44.9 μm diameter) with 33% higher number density (126 to 168 counts).  
Crucially, the acid-washed stainless steel felt also consistently presents better OER performance 
under the constant-current test (Figure 4c), proving that the above key process 1. Direct monitoring 
of cathode-collected water (Figure 4e) substantiates the above key processes 2 and 3.  
Finally, this improved bubble dynamics is ultimately identified as improving performance through 
above three key processes (Figure 4f).

Response:  
We appreciate the reviewer’s further comments. However, we believe there may have been a 
misunderstanding regarding the primary scientific focus and the core contribution of our work. 
We would like to clarify that the central theme of this study is not the optimization of surface 
treatments (acid vs. alcohol washing) or the use of commercial meshes.  
Instead, the major scientific insight we aim to communicate is the fundamental shift in performance-
limiting factors at high current densities. Specifically: 
1. Bubble dynamics vs. ECSA: We demonstrate that at industrially relevant current densities (e.g., 
> 1.0 A cm-2), the bubble dynamics become s the dominant factor governing performance, 
outweighing the influence of the electrochemically active surface area (ECSA). This challenges 
the conventional emphasis on maximizing ECSA through complex catalyst nanostructuring. 
2. Decoupling of factors: While the reviewer noted "marginal" differences in Figures 4f and 6b, 
we would like to highlight that this is precisely part of our key finding: despite similar ECSA 
or initial surface conditions, the performance diverges significantly under high -current 
operations due to mass transport limitations that are often overlooked in standard lab -scale 
characterizations. 
3. Significance for practitioners: Our work reveals that for large -scale AEM electrolysis, 
engineering the physical structure for efficient bubble removal is more critical than incremental 
improvements in intrinsic catalytic activity. This provides a clear, actionable direction for the 
field to bridge the gap between lab-scale discovery and industrial-scale application. 
We have revised the Introduction and Conclusion sections to more explicitly emphasize that the 
"bubble dynamics at high current density" is our primary discovery, ensuring it is not overshadowed 
by the description of the experimental preparation methods.
