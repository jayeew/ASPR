Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this work, Chen’s group elucidate a cascade immobilization methodology that synergistically integrates in-situ
immobilization processes with dynamic exchange immobilization techniques for the fabrication of hierarchically-structured
multicomponent biopharmaceutical systems. The investigation employs zeolitic pyrimidine frameworks as versatile and
reversible matrices, facilitating the immobilization of biomacromolecules with precise spatial orientation and distribution.
This strategic approach demonstrates significant implications for the advancement of complex biopharmaceutical
formulations where spatial control of bioactive components is important for therapeutic efficacy and functional performance.
Thus, I highly recommend it be accepted after a minor revision. 
Comments 1: Obviously, this cascade immobilization approach shows higher advantages over the core-shell method. The
comparison should be emphasized in the introduction part. 
Comments 2: A comparative analysis of the enzymatic activities between GOx⊂ZPF-2 and materials from conventional
defect generation strategy should be incorporated into the manuscript. In the event that materials from conventional defect
generation strategy exhibit meaningless enzymatic activity due to insufficient loading capacity, authors should add the
corresponding description in the corresponding part. 
Comments 3: For recyclability of GOx@CAT⊂ZPF-2, the authors should clarify what the initial activity is. 
Comments 4: During the enzyme immobilization, the pH of the enzyme solution should also be considered as to whether it
affects the structural transformation and defect generation of the MOFs 
Comments 5: Some figures are too small, e.g., Supplementary Figure 47. The authors should make some adjustments. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The current manuscript presents a strategy for achieving reversible encapsulation of enzymes and proteins by exploiting the
dynamic conformational response of ZPF-2 to various solvents, including acetone, THF, DMSO, 1,4-dioxane, and ethyl
acetate. By combining an in-situ encapsulation approach for inner-layer enzymes with a dynamic exchange method for
outer-layer enzymes, the authors developed several systems: a hierarchical cascade enzyme system (GOx@CAT⊂ZPF-2),
multi-component anti-tumor agents (GOx@Tf⊂ZPF-2), and antibacterial formulations (Tps@Nisin⊂ZPF-2). Among these,
only the first qualifies as a cascade system, whereas the latter two consist of independently functioning proteins that do not
engage in interdependent interactions. The manuscript has several critical shortcomings. Most notably, it fails to clearly
demonstrate the innovation, necessity, and uniqueness of the proposed reversible encapsulation strategy when compared
with existing methods (Nature Catalysis, 2018, 1(9): 689–695; Angew. Chem. Int. Ed., 2023, 62, e202302436). The
preparation of GOx@ZPF-2 via an in-situ method followed by organic solvent-induced outer-layer enzyme replacement does

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 2 -->

not appear to offer substantial advantages over established techniques. The current data do not provide convincing
evidence to demonstrate the spatial distribution of the different components within the hierarchical cascade enzyme system.
The manuscript also suffers from a lack of narrative coherence. The first half focuses on a cascade enzyme system
(GOx@CAT⊂ZPF-2), while the second half abruptly transition to multi-component systems (GOx@Tf⊂ZPF-2 and
Tps@Nisin⊂ZPF-2) that are not cascade-related, in which the relevance of these systems to the initial design rationale
remains unclear. Moreover, the necessity of employing the complex reversible encapsulation strategy in the latter systems is
not sufficiently justified, further weakening the overall conceptual continuity. In conclusion, I do not recommend this
manuscript for publication in its current form. While it explores the use of ZPF-2 for encapsulating cascade enzymes and
multicomponent protein systems, it does not convincingly demonstrate the benefits or uniqueness of the proposed dynamic
encapsulation strategy. Instead, it adds unnecessary complexity and leaves key design rationales insufficiently justified. A
transfer to a more specialized journal may be more appropriate. 
Other important issues should also be considered and addressed: 
(1) While the authors point out the limited spatial control in traditional enzyme loading strategies using MOFs—such as in
situ encapsulation and covalent attachment—the nanoparticles prepared in this study exhibit significant heterogeneity in
both size and morphology. This raises concerns about whether the proposed approach truly offers improved precision in
enzyme distribution and loading as claimed. 
(2) During the construction of the GOx@CAT⊂ZPF-2 system, GOx@ZPF-2 undergoes a conformational transformation into
GOx@ZPF-8 upon exposure to organic solvents such as acetone, THF, DMSO, 1,4-dioxane, or ethyl acetate. It is unclear
whether this solvent treatment affects the catalytic activity of GOx. Given the known sensitivity of enzymes to external
environment, this warrants further clarification and possibly experimental validation. 
(3) For the cascade enzyme system (GOx@CAT⊂ZPF-2), as well as the multi-component anti-tumor agent (GOx@Tf⊂ZPF-
2) and antimicrobial agent (Tps@Nisin⊂ZPF-2), what is the loading capacity of each enzyme per nanoparticle? How was
this determined, and was the quantification accurately performed using fluorescence quantification methods? Considering
that the fluorescent molecules are not covalently linked to the enzyme molecules, questions arise regarding the precision
and reliability of this approach. Was this fluorescence quantification cross-validated against thermogravimetric analysis
(TGA) or other independent methods? The quantification process should be thoroughly detailed, including the specific
protocols and controls used. 
(4) Some data analysis lacks depth and is insufficient to fully support the conclusions; for instance, FTIR characterization did
not reveal significant differences, and the relationship between enzyme fluorescence quantification data and
thermogravimetric data was not thoroughly investigated. In Figure S7, both samples (GOx@ZPF-2 and ZPF-2) show
essentially identical peaks with only minor shifts. This similarity undermines the claim that GOx is exclusively present in one
sample. Likewise, the interpretations based on FTIR spectra (Figures S21, S30b, and S31b) are not sufficiently supported by
the data and may not be accurate. 
(5) For the cascade enzyme system (GOx@CAT⊂ZPF-2), as well as the multi-component anti-tumor agent (GOx@Tf⊂ZPF-
2) and the antimicrobial agent (Tps@Nisin⊂ZPF-2), apart from SIM images of GOx@Tf⊂ZPF-2 in Fig 3c, there is no direct
evidence provided regarding the spatial distribution of other enzymes. The authors are advised to provide more visually
intuitive characterization results to substantiate their claims of controlled enzyme localization. 
(6) The manuscript highlights limitations of conventional immobilization strategies, including difficulties in achieving ordered
enzyme distribution and precise spatial control. However, prior studies, particularly those involving MOFs and DNA origami
have demonstrated the feasibility of spatially organized, multi-enzyme assemblies. In this context, the manuscript does not
convincingly demonstrate how the proposed strategy offers a clear advantage over these existing approaches, especially in
terms of spatial accuracy, individual enzyme activity, or cascade reaction efficiency. 
(7) In the stability comparison shown in Figure S30, the enzyme activities of free GOx (5%), GOx⊂ZPF-2 (55%), and
GOx@ZPF (79%) were reported. While the GOx⊂ZPF-2 system immobilizes GOx on the outer surface of ZPF crystals using
a dynamic exchange method, a notably higher reduction in GOx activity was observed under trypsin treatment. This
observation contradicts the authors’ claim regarding the superior stability of this system. 
(8) I understand that GOx@ZPF-2 could exhibit improved environmental stability compared to free GOx. However, the
GOx⊂ZPF-2 system, prepared via dynamic exchange and thus not tightly encapsulated within the MOF framework, still
retains most of its initial activity under various harsh conditions, which is somewhat unexpected and deserves further
clarification. 
(9) The reported enhancement of the MOF-confined biocascade system is intriguing. However, considering the relatively
small aperture size of ZPF-2, it is expected that substrate and intermediate diffusion would be restricted, especially given the
spatially separated enzyme arrangement. This apparent improvement in catalytic performance requires further explanation. 
(10) In comparing bioactivity across different materials, how to ensure the loadings of each enzyme or protein were
consistent among different groups, particularly in co-encapsulation system. the manuscript does not clarify how this
consistency was achieved, and the reported enzyme/protein loadings appear to vary across different materials, which may
confound the interpretation of bioactivity differences. 
(11) For the multi-component anti-tumor agent (GOx@Tf⊂ZPF-2) and the antimicrobial agent (Tps@Nisin⊂ZPF-2), the term
"cascading" is not accurate; it would be more appropriate to describe their mechanism as a "multi-component synergistic
effect". 
(12) This strategy does not provide clear advantages over cancer treatment methods involving cascade enzymes,
photothermal therapy, and drug targeting. Since the manuscript discusses the preparation of GOx@CAT⊂ZPF-2, why not
utilize GOx@CAT⊂Tf⊂ZPF-2 for cancer therapy? 
(13) For GOx@Tf⊂ZPF-2 with an average particle size of 150 nm based on DLS measurements (Figure S37), particularly
TEM images reveal significant particle size heterogeneity. Considering this, can the Super-Resolution Imaging System
(SIM) reliably distinguish between GOx and Tf? 
(14) Many cascade catalytic systems have been reported previously. The authors should cite relevant studies and provide a
quantitative comparison of their results with the existing literature. Such a comparison is essential to justify the necessity and
advantages of the hierarchical spatial arrangement strategy proposed in this work.

2 group were almost entirely eradicated, with a significant reduction in tumor burden, which validates the therapeutic effect.
To provide a more comprehensive illustration of the treatment process, the authors are advised to include digital images

correct all SI figure citations to ensure accuracy and clarity.
