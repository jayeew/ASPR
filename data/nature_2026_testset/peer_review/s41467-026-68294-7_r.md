NCOMMS-25-66272-A 
 
We thank all the Reviewers for the rigor of their reviews and their insightful comments. 
Below is our point-by-point response to their outstanding comments: 
 
-In F5F the authors label the panel with “mechanical adaptation” and describe this in the 
text P10 as “However, upon prolonged compression, intracellular calcium gradually 
decreased, indicative of cellular adaptation to sustained mechanical stress.”. The data 
show a very sharp decrease in GCamp signal, basically within 1 time point of imaging. 
This is inconsistent with a gradual decrease. Also I do not understand how the sharp 
decrease can be perfectly synchronized between different cells. Could that be due to a 
movement of the sample? 
We thank the Reviewer for this careful observation. Upon re-examining the original 
datasets, we agree that the apparent abrupt drop in GCaMP6s signal seen in the 
previous version of Fig. 5F likely reflects a technical artifact caused by transient sample 
movement during intravital imaging. Such motion artifacts are common in live mouse 
imaging, where changes in respiration depth or body position can lead to brief focal 
plane shifts in the x y or z axes, resulting in an apparent loss of fluorescence signal. To 
address this issue, we reprocessed the raw image stacks by manually correcting for 
motion across all spatial axes and normalizing fluorescence intensity across time points. 
Although these steps cannot completely eliminate motion artifacts, they substantially 
improve signal quality and measurement accuracy. We therefore repeated the analysis 
across three independent datasets, all of which consistently support our original 
conclusion that intracellular calcium dynamics show clear differences between wild-type 
and Piezo1 cKO skin, during sustained mechanical stress. We have revised Fig. 5e-f 
according to the new analysis. We have also removed references to “mechanical 
adaptation” and “cellular adaptation” from the text and figure, as these terms were 
speculative and could be misleading to the reader. The revised manuscript now focuses 
strictly on the experimentally supported observation of persistent calcium elevation 
during prolonged mechanical stress in the Piezo1 cKO. 
-Issue with sentence P15 “Taken together our findings suggest that stress vesicle 
biogenesis may be heterogeneous, with macropinocytosis likely serving as the 
predominant pathway, to a lesser degree.” 
We have removed this sentence from the revised text for clarity.  
-F1I replace “ with s for time stamp as in other figures 
We have made the suggested change to the figure.  
-F3I “NO of vesicles/area” could easily be changed to “number of vesicles/area” 
We have made the suggested change to the figure.
