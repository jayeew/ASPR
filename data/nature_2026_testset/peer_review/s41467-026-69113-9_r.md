Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors have done an exceptional job addressing the substantial concerns that I raised in the previous round of review,
and those of the other reviewer as well. Most importantly, the interpretation of the body mass and size ratio key findings in
the first discussion paragraph is both biologically plausible and quite interesting. In particular, pointing out that the larger
species in the dyad tends to “lose” the temporal response race turns the complex, mind-bending interactions involving ratios
into a single, consequential take-home message. I congratulate the reviewers on convincing this fairly skeptical reviewer
that these findings are strong, coherent, and advance our understanding of “temporal ecology” in a meaningful way, even if
the mechanisms cannot be identified from this dataset. In a final read through, I found a few very minor issues that should be
easy to address. 
The abstract would be more effective if that main take home identified in the first discussion paragraph (mentioned above)
was highlighted there. I realize the length limits are tight but it seems pretty important. The last sentence of the abstract is
quite general and I think this paper would be more impactful if that sentence was replaced with one giving the take home of
the larger species losing the temporal response race. 
L47-48 Wording here is too strong – better to say “CAN stabilize interactions…” Not all species interactions will be
influenced by temporal partitioning (E.g., exploitation competition) 
Fig 4 – unit for mass in A and B is needed in x axis labels – looks like it is grams, so it should be “(log g)” 
L206 – simplify wording to “Body mass did not influence how subordinates responded to humans” 
L326-331 – I appreciate the additional explanation for the contrast with findings from Gaynor’s prior meta-analysis, but the
first explanation does not make sense to me. It appears to be saying that the species in this analysis had to respond to their
predators or prey in addition to humans, whereas the species in Gaynor’s analysis only had to deal with humans. But the
species in Gaynor’s analysis presumably were embedded in multi-species communities – it was just the analyses that
focused on single species responses, just like the sub-analysis in this paper. So I don’t really understand the logic behind
that argument and it would be good to either clarify, remove, or replace it. 
For the sensitivity analyses r.e. “strong evidence”, it would be good somewhere to indicate the sample size of these tests
relative to the full dataset (i.e., what % of studies were excluded). This could be in the main text, methods, or supplement. If it
was reported and I missed it, then disregard.
