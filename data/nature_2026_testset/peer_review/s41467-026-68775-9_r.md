Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Polyoxometalate (POM) clusters contain numerous ionic and polar groups, making them versatile multifunctional materials. It
is well established that incorporating POM clusters into polymer matrices can enhance dynamic crosslinking, thereby
improving the mechanical properties of the resulting polymers. In this manuscript, the authors synthesized hybrid polymer
matrices and investigated the enhancement of their physical properties. However, to interpret their results, the authors relied
on schematic illustrations suggesting the formation of nanowires and bundles within the polymer matrices—without
providing any supporting experimental evidence or theoretical justification. As it stands, this manuscript is not suitable for
publication. The current version of the manuscript requires a “Major Revise” before getting further consideration for this
journal. The following critical issues must be clearly addressed, and adequate supporting data must be provided. 
1. As shown in Figure 1(a) and (b), these schematic illustrations depict the self-assembly of Ca²

the directional aggregation of monomer/POM hybrid clusters that leads to the formation of nanowires and bundles. What
specific interactions or anisotropic forces govern this structural alignment? Is it electrostatic, coordination-induced, or driven
by solvent-mediated effects? Furthermore, the authors must provide direct experimental evidence to support this structural
evolution—such as SEM, TEM or AFM imaging, SAXS patterns, or other relevant characterization techniques. 
2. Based on Figure 1(d), the polymerization results in a random polymer matrix; however, the presence of nanowires and
bundles within this matrix is suggested without any accompanying experimental validation or theoretical rationale. The
manuscript lacks clear evidence—either structural or morphological—to justify the formation and stability of these
nanostructures post-polymerization. A detailed discussion or mechanistic explanation, supported by appropriate data, is
essential to substantiate this claim. 
3. The schematic illustration in Figure 1(f) suggests that polymer chains become entangled, with POM clusters aligning via
interactions with amine groups on the polymer side chains. However, the use of TEM imaging alone is insufficient to
conclusively demonstrate the formation of nanowires and bundles. TEM may show contrast variations, but without high-
resolution, selective staining or complementary structural characterization (e.g., cryo-TEM, AFM, or electron tomography), it
is difficult to distinguish between genuine nanowire structures and random aggregates. The manuscript should include
additional, more definitive evidence to support this claim.

4. POM clusters inherently possess a distributed ionic field due to their multivalent and symmetrical nature, resulting in
dynamic and often non-directional interactions with monomers or polymers. The interactions between POM clusters and
amine-functionalized monomers or polymer chains are expected to be transient and stochastic. Moreover, Ca²
⁺
 ions can
also coordinate with amine groups, adding further complexity to the system. Given this high degree of interactional flexibility,
it is theoretically unlikely for these hybrid clusters to undergo controlled, unidirectional growth without an external templating
mechanism. The manuscript fails to address what specific force or guiding factor—chemical, physical, or templating—would
promote the linear alignment of hybrid clusters into nanowires and bundles. A clear mechanistic explanation, supported by
both experimental and theoretical evidence, is necessary to validate this hypothesis. 
5. Contrary to the mechanism proposed in Figure 1, it is more plausible that during polymerization, POM clusters interact
dynamically with the amine groups on the polymer side chains as they form. This interaction would occur concurrently with
polymer growth, resulting in a randomly entangled polymer matrix rather than a well-defined nanowire structure. This
interpretation is consistent with the schematic shown in Figure 1(f), and the manuscript should reconcile these two
conflicting depictions with experimental evidence. 
6. Although it is theoretically plausible that incorporating POM clusters could enhance mechanical properties through
dynamic ionic crosslinking and polymer chain entanglement, the formation of POM cluster nanowires and bundles, as
shown in Figure 1, remains challenging. The current manuscript lacks sufficient experimental validation—such as SEM or
TEM images—to substantiate this claim. 
7. According to the experimental section, the polymeric nanowires (PNWs) were synthesized and purified. However, direct
morphological evidence—such as SEM images of isolated PNWs and their aggregated bundles—is essential to support
their claimed formation. Without such data, the structural assignments remain speculative. 
8. The integrated areas of several NMR peaks do not correspond to the expected proton counts based on the proposed
molecular structures. This discrepancy raises concerns about the accuracy of structural assignments. If the compound was
not synthesized as intended, the foundational assumptions of the manuscript are undermined, and the subsequent
conclusions are not scientifically valid. The authors must provide corrected NMR data or additional structural confirmation
(e.g., HRMS, elemental analysis, or 2D NMR) to verify the identity and purity of the synthesized compounds.

In this study, certain aspects require further clarification, particularly concerning the interpretation of specific characterization
results and the underlying mechanism of gelation. 
1. In Fig. 2a and Extended Date Fig. 3k, although the storage modulus of the gel increases significantly after polymerization,

on the conclusions drawn from prior studies. However, the current work introduced polymer-based products that differ
significantly from the previously reported systems. For example, the driving force of gelation is likely dominated by extensive
van der Waals forces between polymer carbon chains and the long alkyl chains of oleylamine, rather than solely by
electrostatic interactions between amino groups and the inorganic core parts of Ca-POM. Therefore, it is recommended to
include additional experimental characterizations or theoretical calculations to more clearly elucidate the gelation
mechanism.

found to require further clarification, particularly concerning the interpretation of specific characterization results and the

Reply: We thank the reviewer very much for th ese critical comments. We agree  with the

Science 2022, 377, 100-104; J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2024, 146, 26587-26602). During the formation 
of nanowires, ionic species in the solution first aggregate into clusters or nuclei via coordination 
interactions. These nuclei are then capped by long -chain alkyl ligands such as oleylamine and

only when ligands desorb from the nuclei, exposing the cluster cores. The simultaneous

detachment of ligands from different crystal faces is significantly more improbable than their 
removal from one set of crystal faces, which kinetically favors 1D oriented growth of nanowires.

electrostatic interactions to form nanowires, with oleylamine and protonated oleylamine 
attaching to the nanowires through coordination and electrostatic interactions. Therefore, the 
synergistic effect of electrostatic interactions and co ordination is the driving force prompting 
Ca-POM to self-assemble into well-defined nanowire structures.
