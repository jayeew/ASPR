Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript reports intrinsically stretchable MoS2-flake transistors with high n-type performance. The work is technically
solid and well-organized; however, several key issues must be addressed before the manuscript can meet the impact and
rigor expected for Nature Communications. Therefore, I recommend major revision. 
1. Please provide extended mechanical cycling tests (≫50 cycles) to demonstrate long-term stretchability and durability. 
2. Please clarify the mechanism of strain-enhanced mobility with quantitative evidence such as TLM measurements or
channel-length-dependent analysis. 
3. More mechanical characterization is needed to support the “intrinsically stretchable” claim, including strain distribution or
interface delamination analysis. 
4. The manuscript should provide the dielectric constant, leakage characteristics, and strain-dependent COX of the
SEBS/NBR gate dielectric. 
5. A simple circuit-level demonstration (e.g., an inverter) or a toned-down claim regarding CMOS relevance is necessary. 
6. Please analyze how contact resistance evolves under strain to clarify the origin of strain-dependent electrical
characteristics. 
7. The authors should include hysteresis, and subthreshold swing data to evaluate device reliability more completely. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript reports the stretchable thin-film transistors using 2D MoS2 semiconducting flakes. The semiconducting
channel is constructed with stacked MoS2 nanosheets produced by a solution-based method. Under a strain of up to 20%,
the average field-effect mobility is 8 cm2V-1s-1, and the on/off ratio is 107. The stretchability is attributed to the interflake
sliding between MoS2 building blocks, which can maintain the flake-to-flake contact and electrical behavior during
mechanical deformation. Although the authors have presented some new results, the major claims in this manuscript may
not be fully supported by the experimental data. Because the key points claimed in this manuscript have already been
reported in a previous paper (detailed in the following paragraphs), it is difficult and also questionable to conclude that the
current work presents major advancements over existing literature reports. 
First, the stretchability of thin-film transistors based on MoS2 2D flakes was previously reported (Science 375, 852-859
(2022)). In that paper, the MoS2 film can be stretched for up to 50% strain with an average field-effect mobility of ~10 cm2V-
1s-1. The current manuscript used the same material production method (i.e., both are from electrochemical exfoliation and
MoS2 nanosheet ink) and showed thin-film transistors of a similar mobility value (i.e., both 8-10 cm2V-1s-1), while with a
lower stretchability (i.e., current 20% is lower than the previous 50%). I do not see a higher performance or a new application
in this work. It is questionable if the claimed key advancements in stretchable n-type 2D semiconductors are supported by
the experimental data shown in this manuscript. 
On the other hand, I do not see a breakthrough in fundamental science or understanding of mechanism in this work either.
Since the mechanism of stretchable MoS2 film is also the same as that reported in the previous literature, which is the
interlayer sliding of nanosheets. It seems that the authors are not trying to propose a new mechanism for the stretchable
MoS2 film. 
Together, the current work used the same material/chemistry and demonstrated similar transistor performance compared
with the previous work. So, it is kind of confusing to conclude that major advancements have been made in this work.
Although the authors fabricated transistors on a plastic substrate, it may not support the claim of “addresses the critical lack
of high-performance stretchable n-type materials”. Because the stretchable n-type MoS2 thin film has already been reported

literature (Yan et al., Science 2022). We agree that the material system (MoS2 nanosheets) and 
the fundamental concept of interflake sliding share similarities. However, we respectfully 
disagree with the assessment that our work does not present major advancements. 
The cited Science paper focuses on “bio-interfacing” using electrolyte -gating, whereas our

work in terms of device architecture, switching efficiency (On/ off ratio), and mechanistic

1. Fundamental Difference in Device Architecture: Bio-Gated vs. Solid-State 
The reviewer compared our mobility and stretchability to the Science paper. However, it is 
crucial to note that the transistors reported in Yan et al. (2022) are electrolyte-gated transistors 
(specifically leaf-gated and skin-gated, as shown in Figs. 3 and 4 of their paper). 
* Yan et al. (2022): Utilizes ions within biological tissues (leaves/skin) as a gate. This relies 
on electric double-layer capacitance (EDLC) or electrochemical doping. While this is excellent 
for bio-sensing, it is not a standalone electronic component. 
* Our Work: Demonstrates intrinsic stretchability in a fully integrated solid -state thin-film 
transistor (TFT) structure (Gate/Dielectric/Channel/Contacts). Achieving stable performance 
in a solid -state stack under strain is significantly more challenging than in electrolyte -gated 
systems due to interface delamination and dielectric failure issues, which we have successfully 
overcome. 
 
2. Critical Advancement in Switching Performance (On/off Ratio) 
A major limitation of the referenced Science paper is the switching capability. 
* Yan et al. (2022): As evidenced in Fig. 3H and Fig. 4K of Yan et al. (2022), their devices 
show an On/off ratio of approximately ~102 with high off-currents (10-7 A). This high leakage 
current renders them unsuitable for practical logic circuits or power-efficient electronics. 
* Our work: Our devices exhibit an On/off ratio of ~107, which is 5 orders of magnitude higher 
than the referenced work. We attribute this drastic improvement to our water-mediated transfer 
process. Our experiments revealed that the KOH-based transfer damages the nanosheet surface 
and degrades the interface, leading to high off-currents (Figure R1). Our damage-free transfer 
method is a key te chnological advancement that enables high -performance logic operations, 
addressing a critical gap in the field.

3. Clarification on Reviewer’s Misinterpretation of Reference Data (Mobility & Stretchability) 
We respectfully point out that the reviewer’s comment – “In that paper, the MoS2 film can be 
stretched for up to 50% strain with an average field-effect mobility of ~10 cm2V-1s-1.” – appears 
to conflate two separate datasets from the reference (Yan et al., 2022). 
The reference does not report a transistor that maintains a mobility of ~10 cm2V-1s-1 under 50% 
strain. The mobility value of ~10 cm 2V-1s-1 shown in  Fig. S7 of the reference re flects the 
intrinsic material quality measured on a rigid substrate (before and after transfer), rather than 
the performance of a stretchable transistor under applied strain. Importantly, the reference does 
not discuss Fig. S7 in the context of stretchable transistor operation, nor does it claim such 
mobility values for devices under mechanical deformation. 
Moreover, the claim of 50% stretchability reported in the reference is based on the strain level 
at which the sheet resistance of the film begins to increase sharply. This metric is fundamentally 
different from the field-effect mobility of a fully operational TFT.

The bio-gated transistors presented in the reference (Fig. 3 and 4) do not exhibit high mobility 
under large strain. Therefore, it is not accurate to assert that the reference demonstrates high 
mobility transistor operation at 50% strain. 
 
4. Direct Evidence of Mechanism via Systematic Raman Spectroscopy 
The reviewer noted that the “interlayer sliding” mechanism is the same. While Yan et al. (2022) 
proposed this mechanism to explain the malleability, they primarily relied on 
phenomenological observations and sche matic models (Fig. 1) without direct microscopic 
evidence under strain. 
* Our Contribution : We provide direct spectroscopic evidence of interflake sliding. By 
conducting systematic in -situ Raman mapping under tensile strain, we observed the strain -
induced evolution of vibrational modes. 
* New Discovery : Furthermore, we observed a “strain-induced mobility enhancement ” 
phenomenon, which was not reported in the Science paper. We successfully correlated this 
enhancement with the reorientation and densification of the nanosheet network via our Raman 
analysis. This moves the field from “hypothesizing” the mechanism to “proving and utilizing” 
it. 
 
5. Reliability and Resilience 
The referenced work focuses heavily on the “conformability” to skin textures but lacks detailed 
characterization of electrical resilience (e.g., hysteresis, threshold voltage stability) in a 
transistor setup during repetitive strain cycles. Our work demonstrates that the solid -state n-
type TFTs maintain their high  mobility and high On/Off ratio (10 7) even after mechanical 
deformation, proving true resilience suitable for reliable stretchable electronics. 
 
Summary of Advancements 
In conclusion, while the material platform shares a common origin, our work advances the field 
from “breathable bio-sensors with poor switching ” (Yan et al., 2022) to “high-performance 
solid-state stretchable electronics” with logic-level reliability (10 7 On/off ratio) and provides 
the first systematic spectroscopic proof of the sliding mechanism.
