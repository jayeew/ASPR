Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Evaluation of a single-atom effect in the core skeleton of drugs is of great significance in drug-development campaigns.
However, this process often be hampered by their synthetic accessibility because a totally different synthetic strategy has
been required to edit the core, typically requiring rebuilding multistep sequences from scratch, which is cost- and time-
consuming. A strategy that could directly convert already-existing or easy-to-make molecules into other challenging
candidates is an appealing approach to producing new analogues. This work by Lan, Zhang, and Wang deals with the
transformation of readily available isoquinolines into synthetically challenging naphthalenes through a formal CN to CC
replacement. This transformation was initiated by an uncommon polarity-mismatched Diels-Alder cycloaddition. Guided by
DFT calculations, the key to this cycloaddition was utilizing the isoquinolinium carbonate as an EDA complex to harness
photonic energy and achieve polarity inversion for the isoquinolinium cation. This photosensitizer-free synthetic strategy
features high compatibility and functional-group tolerance, particularly, two commercial drugs are also good candidates for
late-stage functionalization. Additionally, this transformation provides access to valuable PAHs, ligands and drug analogies.
After an evaluation of the novelty as well as the broad applicability of this synthetic strategy, this reviewer considers this
work represents a significant advancement in the field of drug development and synthetic chemistry, and thus
recommending publication of it in Nature Communications after the following issues to be addressed.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Dear authors, 
In the manuscript, Zhang and Wang have reported a photocatalytic strategy for converting isoquinolines into naphthalene
derivatives. Supported by DFT calculations, they have suggested the formation of an isoquinoline radical, obtained by an
excitation of an isoquinolinium carbonate salt EDA complex, which undergoes a (4+2) cycloaddition reaction with an alkyne
partner, followed by an extrusion of an imine to provide the target naphthalene. 
The strategy reported in this manuscript is elegant and efficient to a large diversity of substrates, including highly
functionalized ones. The manuscript is organized well and the experimental data relating to the precursors and target
compounds are convincing and well-documented. I certainly agree with the authors that there is genuine novelty in this
isosteric replacement strategy, however the mechanism and experimental results obtained during the optimization need to
be further investigated to make this article suitable for publication in Nature Communication. 
Comments 
As I am not an expert in TD-DFT, I can only briefly assess the computational aspects. That said, I have two synthetic
concerns that warrant clarification: 
- It would be interesting to know the energy of the intermediate formed by the (4+2) cycloaddition with the other
regioselectivity (TS1’ and Int2’). 
- In page 7, the authors have written: “Density functional theory (DFT) calculations lend support to our hypothesis and reveal
an electron-donor-acceptor (EDA) mechanism […].” It would be helpful to more clearly explain how DFT calculations “reveal”
an EDA mechanism. 
In page 7, the authors have written: “Due to the better light absorption characteristics of CO32− than those of Br−, the
isoquinolinium carbonate salt works as the EDA complex, which can be excited by irradiation at 462 nm.” The underlying
concept is not obvious to me, so the authors should provide a more precise explanation to improve readability. UV
absorption spectra of the two complexes (CO32−, Br−) would be greatly appreciated. 
According to Fig. 2C, when the sodium carbonate was replaced by NEt3, DABCO or DBU in DMSO, relatively good
efficiencies were maintained (38% or 47% compared to 64%). What is the nature of the supposed EDA complex? 
According to the proposed mechanism, a hydrogen atom transfer would occur between ethanol and carbonic radical anion.
How can you explain the formation of the target product when the reaction is performed in DMF, CH3CN or DCE? In ethanol,
did you observe/isolate the imine formed during the rearomatisation of the naphthalene product (or the corresponding
benzylamine obtained by hydrolysis of the latter). 
According to Fig. 2C, the authors obtained better yield in the presence of 5A MS, a rationalization of this experimental result
would be appreciated. 
To confirm the involvement of an isoquinoline radical in the reaction, have you tried to trap it with an electrophile? 
Have you investigated the reactivity of 1-substituted isoquinolines? Do they work?

Q4 
The mechanism proposes that CO₃•⁻ undergoes hydrogen atom transfer with EtOH to generate EtO•, 
which then couples with the intermediate (Int ₃ → Int₄). Currently, labeling experiments/trapping

Re: Thank you very much for your professional suggestions. In light of comments from you and 
other reviewers, a more reasonable skeletal editing mechanism were calculated, where the explicit 
solvent molecule EtOH is not involved in the reaction process. As illustrate in the revised Fig. 2B, 
a single electron transfer (SET) process , instead of the hydrogen atom transfer with EtOH , occurs 
between intermediate Int3 and the carbonic radical anion (CO 3−), affording the iminium cation 
intermediate Int4. Then the given CO32− is added back to Int4, yielding a much more stable 
intermediate Int5. The cascade SET and nucleophilic addition facilitates continuous energy release 
by totally 61.4 kcal/mol. Subsequen tly, the bridged structure is disrupted through the C −C bond

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 19 -->

cleavage via transition state TS3, with CO2 being released concurrently. The activation energy for 
this step  is 2 3.8 kcal/mol, a reasonable value for the experimental conditions. Finally, the 
rearomatization proceeding via TS4 promotes release of the naphthalene product and elimination of 
a N-benzylformamide anion. It is noteworthy that the N-benzylformamide was detected via GC-MS 
analysis (Fig. S5), offering experimental support for the rationality of the proposed mechanism. All 
these results have been incorporated into the revised manuscript.

Q5

Re: Thank you very much for your professional question. As stated for Q4 from Review 2, a more 
reasonable skeletal editing mechanism were calculated, in light of comments from you and other 
reviewers. As illustrate in the revised Fig. 2B,  a single electron transfer (SET) process , instead of 
the hydrogen atom transfer with the explicit solvent molecule EtOH, occurs between intermediate 
Int3 and the carbonic radical anion (CO 3−), affording the iminium cation intermediate Int4. Then 
the given CO32− is added back to Int4, yielding a much more stable intermediate Int5. The cascade 
SET and nucleophilic addition facilitates continuous energy release by totally 61.4 kcal/mol. 
Subsequently, the bridged structure is disrupted through the C−C bond cleavage via transition state 
TS3, with CO2 being released concurrently. The activation energy for this step is 23.8 kcal/mol, a 
reasonable value for the experimental conditions. Finally, the rearomatization proceeding via TS4 
promotes release of the naphthalene product with the simultaneous elimination of a N-
benzylformamide anion. It is noteworthy that the N-benzylformamide was detected via GC -MS 
analysis (Figure S5), offering experimental support for the rationality of the proposed mechanism. 
All these results have been incorporated into the revised Fig.2 and the main text.
