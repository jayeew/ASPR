Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors have attempted to optimize the compatibility between the macroscopic geometric and microscopic band
structures of the heterojunction components to address the kinetic and thermodynamic limitations of photo-induced charge
carriers. However, several critical issues remain unresolved. The resulting materials do not exhibit a well-ordered structure,
and electron microscopy images fail to reveal any remarkable or novel features. Furthermore, the performance data
presented do not demonstrate any significant improvement over previously reported results, raising concerns about the
novelty and impact of the work. Given these limitations, I am unable to recommend this manuscript for publication in its
current form. The work may be more suitable for submission to a more specialized journal rather than a broad scientific
journal like Nature Communications.

However, the observation of well-defined ordering in 2D/2D heterostructures
remainsawell-knownandintrinsicchallenge,notspecifictooursystem.Inourstudy,
both PNs and BS possess characteristic 2D features and exhibit comparable lateral
dimensions. The inherently limited morphological contrast between the two
components makes it intrinsically difficult to resolve ordered interfaces at the
nanoscale. More importantly, physical perturbations during material preparation

process for PNs production inevitably leads to fragmentation and edge irregularities;
and during the assembly of PNs and BS, random stacking orientations—such as

compositestructure.

features are widely reported across the field: even in seminal works, electron
microscopy images typically show coexisting nanosheets without consistent stacking

27. In comment 33, do other parameters such as solvent viscosity and polarity also

Response: We appreciate the reviewer’s comment. Indeed, other solvent 
parameters can also influence the exfoliation process. Taking polarity, as you 
mentioned, as an example, it can affect the interactions between 2D materials and 
exfoliating mediums. As described in Science, 2011, 331: 568 -571, the Hansen 
solubility parameters provide an approach to quantify how exfoliating medium 
influences the exfoliation process. These parameters incorporate three contributions to 
the cohesive energy density: dispersion, polar, and hydrogen -bonding interactions. In 
practice, effective exfoliation of a specific 2D material can only be achieved when all 
three parameters (dispersion, polar, and hydrogen bond) of the solvent closely match 
those of the 2D material. As can be seen, polarity is not an independent parameter, 
rather, it is inherently embedded within the Hansen parameter set. That is to say, 
although polarity is one of the parameters influencing exfoliation, its effect cannot be 
clearly isolated from other intermolecular interactions, making it methodologically 
challenging to use polarity as an independent screening criterion. In other words, 
while polarity does play a role, it is not appropriate to treat it as a standalone indicator 
for solvent selection. 
In contrast, the surface energy is an independent parameter that has been widely 
and successfully used for initial solvent selection. Although other parameters may 
influence the exfoliation process, their existence does not undermine the validity of 
using surface energy as a reliable descriptor, which is well supported by the 
experimental results in this work. Also, many classical studies have employed surface 
energy as a guiding parameter to identify suitable exfoliating mediums for various 2D 
materials (J. Am. Chem. Soc, 2013, 135: 18-21; ACS Nano, 2016, 10: 2745-2751; Nat.
