(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, the authors present an asymmetric interrupted CuAAC reaction with cycle diaryliodoniums to synthesis of
atropisomeric biaryl triazoles with good yields and enantioselectivities. The idea of using CuAAC reaction using cycle
diaryliodoniums is not first (Org. Lett. 2012, 14, 3332–3335). The authors propose an alternative reaction pathway
envisioned to preferentially generate the 1,4-substituted triazolyl copper intermediate, which is then interrupted by cycle
diaryliodoniums. As claimed by the authors, the reaction system has a variety of active intermediates, which may generate
the target products through multiple reaction pathways. The authors excluded the [3+2] cycloaddition as well as C–H
activation followed by coupling reaction pathways with aryl-Cu(III) intermediates by controlled experiments. The results of
reaction progress kinetic analysis indicate that the terminal alkynes reveal unusual secondary kinetic isotope effects, and
0.4th-order for cyclic diaryliodonium and first-order for both the alkyne and the azide. These results provide indirect evidence
for this reaction pathway design. Overall, this work provides valuable insights into the future development of enantioselective
interrupted CuAAC reaction. It also provides an opportunity for the application of atropisomeric biaryl triazoles. Thus, I
recommend its publication in Nature Communication, however, after reconsidering the following points. 
1.Page 2, line 43, the authors states that “After the pioneer work of Wu et al.,13 a variety of electrophiles, such as allyl halide,
acid chloride, RS(O)2 SR, molecular S, Se etc., can be used to terminate the triazolyl copper species, a process typically
referred to as the interrupted CuAAC reaction.14-26” No references using allyl halide were found in the references cited by

Comments to the Author 
I have carefully reviewed the manuscript titled "Atroposelective Interrupted CuAAC Reaction Using Cyclic Diaryliodoniums."
The study reports the first asymmetric interrupted copper-catalyzed azide–alkyne cycloaddition (CuAAC) reaction using
cyclic diaryliodonium reagents, enabling the enantioselective synthesis of atropisomeric biaryl triazoles—something not
previously reported, which expands the CuAAC reaction framework into asymmetric synthesis. The manuscript is well-
written, and the experimental design is robust. The findings are clearly presented, and sufficient analytical data support the
results. Experimental kinetics, isotope effects, and control reactions corroborate mechanistic claims. The work is of high
quality and relevance to the journal’s readership. While the manuscript is generally sound, I have identified a few minor
issues that should be addressed before publication. These revisions will help improve the clarity and completeness of the
work. Given the overall merit of the study, I recommend acceptance with minor revisions. I will provide specific comments
and suggestions in the attached review report. 
1. The authors are encouraged to incorporate these two references to provide more current and relevant sources on the
subject matter: J. Org. Chem. 2023, 88, 10434 and Org. Lett. 2024,26,2233. 
2. Line 99, DMSO-d6, toluene-d8, d6 and d8 should be in italics 
3. Figure 3, The authors should include the caption for Figure 3d.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript by Li et al. describes an interrupted CuAAC reaction using aryliodonium salts to obtain asymmetric
atropisomeric triazoles. The reaction design is elegant and integrates the authors’ previously established copper-catalyzed
stereoselective ring opening of cyclic diaryliodoniums (Chem 2018, 4, 599; Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 2021, 60, 5788) with a
strategy to capture 1,4-disubstituted triazolyl copper species. 
The authors identify flame-dried K
₂
CO
₃
 as a critical factor for the reaction outcome. Considering its importance, a more
detailed description of the drying protocol (e.g. time, temperature, apparatus) in the manuscript would be beneficial for the
reproducibility and clarity. 
One of the main limitations of the developed reaction is the relatively narrow substrate scope, regarding the alkyne and
azide components. A more detailed discussion in particular of attempts (if any) to extend the reaction to other alkynes would
be beneficial (the authors stated that the reaction is restricted to alpha-acid azide acetate derivatives). As it stands, the utility
of the method appears to be limited to products 4 in Table 2. 
The mechanistic part is currently the most problematic aspect of the manuscript. While the authors perform several
experiments, including stoichiometric reactions with the proposed intermediates and kinetic analyzes with RPKA, the
mechanistic proposal remains incomplete. 
Specifically, the reaction between 2a-Cu and 1a led to 5a (69%), but the reaction of 5a is not investigated further. It should
be investigated whether 5a can subsequently react with the azide under standard conditions: the reaction in which the
alkyne is added to the reaction mixture solely as 2a-Cu under standard reaction conditions. This would shed light on
whether the reaction may proceed in this manner, so that ring opening occurs first and then the CuAAC reaction of the
formed 5a with the azide component. 
The reaction order was determined for the alkyne and the azide, but not for the copper catalyst - this should be analyzed to
provide further clarity. 
Finally, computational support (e.g. DFT calculations) evaluating the plausibility of the proposed reaction pathway would
strengthen the mechanistic claims. 
In summary, the study is well designed and conducted and clearly presented. However, I feel that it falls somewhat short in
terms of substrate (product) scope and mechanistic rationale to be of interest to a wide readership of this multidisciplinary
journal, and perhaps would be more appropriate for more chemistry journal.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors have submitted a substantially improved manuscript, addressing the reviewers' concerns in detail, including
through several additional experiments. The experiments related to the reaction pathway further support the authors'
proposal. 
At this stage, and considering the extensive data already provided, I agree that adding DFT analysis may be excessive.

transition states have been successfully studied in the literature. Although identifying and optimizing numerous conformers

mechanism. 
In my opinion the manuscript is suitable for publication.
