Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This study provides compelling and novel evidence that FUNDC1in vascular endothelial cells plays a significant role in
obesity and insulin resistance. The findings are potentially groundbreaking and suggest that FUNDC1 may serve as a future
therapeutic target for obesity and diabetes. However, several concerns remain. 
Although the authors propose an FUNDC1–GATA2–ET-1 signaling mechanism, the majority of their data are derived from in
vitro experiments, with insufficient in vivo validation. This raises important questions regarding the physiological relevance
of the findings. Notably, the authors suggest that the knockdown of FUNDC1 leads to increased degradation of GATA2 in
HUVEC. However, this does not fully explain the observed increase in GATA2 mRNA levels in obese patients, where
FUNDC1expression is also elevated. This discrepancy highlights the need for additional in vivo studies to clarify the role of
FUNDC1 in regulating GATA2 and SIRT3-L under obese conditions. 
Specifically, the following points should be addressed, at a minimum, in mouse models to substantiate the proposed
mechanism: 
a) The expression of GATA2 and SIRT3-L in endhothelial cells should be examined in obese mouse and Fundc1f/YCdh5
+mice models to determine whether they reflect the patterns observed in HUVECs. 
b) It should be demonstrated whether GATA2 and SIRT3-L can account for the phenotype observed in Fundc1f/YCdh5
+mice mice. This should be addressed using approaches such as recombinant protein supplementation or genetic deletion
model 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This study investigates the role of endothelial FUN14 domain-containing 1(FUNDC1), a mitochondrial outer membrane
protein, in endothelial dysfunction associated to obesity and diabetes. The authors use a genetic mouse model lacking
FUNDC1 specifically in EC on high fat diet (HFD), combined with cellular and molecular approaches to dissect the
mechanisms underlying the observed phenotypes. 
Mice ECKO for FUNDC1 gain much less weight (ca. 27%) and present less insulin resistance, which is expected
considering the dramatic reduction in body weight on HFD. 
The authors found BP in obese mice significantly reduced by endothelial FUNDC1 deletion and vascular functions,
particularly endothelial-mediated vasodilation improved. The authors claim that endothelial-derived ET1 production is the
mediator of all these effects. 
That ET1 is implicated in insulin resistance and lipogenesis is not novel. This has been long reported. FUNDC1 has been
reported to play many important functions, including mitophagy via LC3, regulating MAMs integrity, fission via DRP1, Ca++
homeostasis…etc. 
The novelty here is in the role of FUNDC1 in regulating ET1, with vascular and metabolic impact in HFD, which is
interesting. However, the study present major drawbacks in the data presented not supporting the conclusions of the
authors. Vascular studies and blood pressure are questionable. Whether endothelial FUNDC1 plays a role in this context
via ET-1 or other biological functions already described has not been investigated and/or discussed. For instance, Ca+_
release is important in the activation of eNOS, mediator of Ach-induced vasorelaxation in aortic rings, just to mention one.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
General Comments: 
The manuscript presents a novel mechanism by which endothelial FUNDC1 regulates metabolic reprogramming in obesity-

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 3 -->

induced diabetes via the SIRT3/GATA2/ET-1 axis. The study integrates in vivo models (endothelial-specific Fundc1 KO
mice) and human clinical samples, addressing an important topic in metabolic vascular biology. While the concept in this
study holds translational potential, further mechanistic validation, clinical correlation, and methodological gaps that
undermine the proposed pathway are required. This study should be reconsidered after revision: 
Specific Comments: 
1. There is a lack of mechanistic specificity of FUNDC1-SIRT3 interaction. Although the authors claim that FUNDC1 acts as
a "mitochondrial receptor" for SIRT3-L, the study lacks direct evidence (e.g., co-IP, FRET, or proximity ligation assays). The
authors shall provide additional data demonstrating the close proximity of the FUNDC1-SIRT3 interaction, and validate the
functional impact of this interaction on SIRT3-L nuclear-mitochondrial shuttling. 
2. There is potentially inadequate validation of endothelial-specific knockout model. The authors shall confirm Fundc1
deletion efficiency in other relevant vascular beds (e.g., adipose tissue, liver, skeletal muscle) beyond lung endothelial cells.
Include qPCR/WB of isolated ECs from multiple organs to rule out any compensatory adaptations. 
3. Concerning the temporal discrepancies in FUNDC1 expression, the biphasic FUNDC1 response (downregulation at 2
months, upregulation at 6 months in HFD-fed mice) conflicts with the sustained protective phenotype of KO mice. Please
clarify how an early FUNDC1 reduction contributes to later metabolic protection if its deletion is consistently beneficial. 
4. The clinical correlations might be underdeveloped. The human data (positive FUNDC1/ET-1 correlation in T2DM) mainly
rely on small samples without mechanistic links. If possible, please expand cohorts to also include non-obese T2DM
patients, and measure FUNDC1/SIRT3/GATA2/ET-1 in human endothelial cells to better establish causality. 
5. The roles of SIRT3 isoforms remain elusive. The study focuses on SIRT3-L but omits data on the short isoform (SIRT3-S).
Please specify how each isoform contributes to GATA2 acetylation/degradation and whether FUNDC1 selectively regulates
SIRT3-L. 
6. Incomplete vascular dysfunction data was noted. The protective effect of Fundc1 KO on vasodilation is mainly attributed to
reduced ET-1, but NO bioavailability, oxidative stress markers (e.g., eNOS coupling), and ET-1 receptor expression were not
assessed. Address these gaps to further solidify vascular conclusions. 
7. In the Discussion, the authors might contrast their findings with prior work on FUNDC1 in mitophagy and SIRT3 in
endothelial glycolysis. Discuss why FUNDC1-mediated SIRT3 regulation is distinct from its known roles. 
8. The abstract emphasizes the "obesity-diabetes transition," but data focus on HFD-induced phenotypes without tracking
transition dynamics. The authors should reframe to match results or add longitudinal assessments.

 Comment 5: Did the author compare their data with published studies? Studies show 
glucose intolerance on HFD as early as 2 weeks. How the authors explain that? 
 
Response: We thank the reviewer for raising this important point. The timing of 
dysglycemia after initiation of a HFD is highly dependent on multiple experimental 
factors (diet composition and energy density, mouse strain/substrain and age, 
husbandry/microbiota, and the precise GTT/ITT protocol). Some studies report very early 
impairments in glucose tolerance (days) with certain obesogenic diets, but others report 
preserved glucose tolerance over the first weeks of HFD. For example, Williams et al. 
found an early peak in glucose intolerance at day 3 that subsequently improved and 
remained relatively stable between 1 and 12 weeks on HFD. [Ref: Williams, Lynda M., et 
al. "The development of diet-induced obesity and glucose intolerance in C57BL/6 mice on 
a high-fat diet consists of distinct phases." PloS one 9.8 (2014): e106159.] However, a 
recent study comparing HFD and HFD combined with fructose (HFD+FR) found that at 
21 weeks, the OGTT AUC was significantly elevated in the HFD+FR group compared 
with normal chow and HFD, whereas there was no difference between the HFD and 
chow-fed mice, highlighting how dietary composition critically shapes the onset and 
magnitude of metabolic impairment. [Ref: Ruppert, Zsófia, et al. "Characterization of 
obesity-related diseases and inflammation using single cell immunophenotyping in two 
different diet-induced obesity models." International Journal of Obesity 48.11 (2024): 
1568-1576.] In addition, longitudinal analyses suggest that short- versus long-term HFD 
exposure may engage distinct mechanisms (ectopic lipid accumulation dominating early
