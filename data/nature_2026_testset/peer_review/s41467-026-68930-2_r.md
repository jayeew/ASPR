Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
his manuscript reported report a bioresorbable, flexible piezoelectric composite composed of Rochelle salt (RS) crystals
embedded within poly(L-lactic acid) (PLLA) nanofibers. 
The author used a method combining electrospinning and uniaxial compression to prepare this biodegradable nanofiber

dysfunction. Overall, this research is interesting and the data is well presented. However, the writing needs to be improved.
To improve the quality of the manuscript, the authors need to address the following issues. 
1. Regarding the application of piezoelectric materials in tissue repair, biodegradable piezoelectric materials have their
unique advantages. However, there is a contradiction between their degradability and piezoelectric performance. In this
work, the biodegradable piezoelectric materials constructed with RS crystals have excellent piezoelectric performance.

• Comment #1:  “The authors describe the ratio of PLLA:RS, however, the authors 
should describe after electrospinning the loading efficiency of the RS in the fiber. The

Our response: We thank the reviewer for this valuable suggestion. To evaluate the 
loading efficiency in PLLA/RS (4:1) fibers, we have supplemented thermogravimetric 
analysis for PRSC, pure PLLA fibers and pure RS crystals (Supplementary Figure 2f). 
The RS content in PRSC was estimated from the distinct thermal degradation profiles

50–120 ° C (Optik 127, 4 (2016): 1804–1808), whereas PLLA shows negligible mass 
loss in this range. At temperatures above 300 ° C, PLLA undergoes nearly complete 
degradation, while RS crystals retain 50–55% of their mass (Optik 127, 4 (2016): 1804–
1808). By integrating these characteristics, the RS content in PRSC was determined to 
be 19.5% ±  0.6%, closely matching the initial feeding ratio of PLLA to RS (4:1). This

corresponds to a loading efficiency of ~97.5%, indicating that the RS content remained 
consistent after electrospinning and post-processing.

• Comment #3:  “The bulk measurements of d 33 and voltage output will be highly 
dependent upon the porosity of the electrospun mats. Please provide more detail in the

Supplemental Figure 2 of 0.2% and less than 0.1% do no t appear to be accurate

Our response: We thank the reviewer for the comment. We agree that porosity 
measurements based on image analysis may introduce considerable errors. To obtain a 
more accurate value for the PRSC film, we employed the density bottle method as 
reported in the literature (Biomaterials, 27(2006): 4923-4933). Specifically, the density 
bottle was first filled with ethanol (density ρe) and weighed (W 1). Subsequently, the

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 8 -->

electrospun nanofiber sample (weight W s; total volume V s, c alculated as the area 
multiplied by the thickness) was immersed into the bottle. The bottle was then refilled 
with ethanol and weighed again (W 2). The scaffold volume of the nanofiber film is 
equal to the volume of displaced ethanol, i.e., (W 1-W2+Ws)/ρe. The porosity ( φ) was 
thus calculated using the formula: 
φ = 1-(W1-W2+Ws)/(Vs×ρe) 
The porosity for the uncompressed PLLA/RS film and compressed PRSC film was 
calculated to be 77.4 ± 6.4% and 21.8 ± 4.6%, respectively.
