Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript proposes a novel approach to reveal the non-trivial fusion rules by coupling fermionic modes with Majorana
zero modes (MZMs). The core innovation lies in simplifying the experimental design: only requiring the regulation of the
energy levels of fermionic modes and their coupling strength with MZMs, the non-trivial fusion rules can be directly reflected
through charge pumping signals (half-integer or integer charge transfer). Theoretical analysis shows that the charge transfer
is achieved via electron or hole tunneling channels, and is related to the number of zero-energy crossings in the parameter
space (odd counts corresponding to non-trivial pumping). In numerical simulations of nanowire-superconductor systems,
MZMs demonstrate robust integer charge pumping, while Andreev bound states exhibit similar behavior only under specific
conditions, validating the universality of the scheme. This approach only requires gate voltage to regulate the parameters of
fermionic modes, avoiding direct manipulation of topological superconductors, thus providing a feasible path for
experimental verification of the non-Abelian statistical properties of MZMs. Therefore, this manuscript holds significant
scientific value and is timely. The writing is also very clear. So I recommend its publication. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
I read this manuscript with great interest. The authors present a novel theoretical proposal to demonstrate the nontrivial
fusion rules of Majorana zero modes (MZMs) by coupling a single MZM to a fermionic mode (FM). By tuning the FM energy
level and its coupling strength with the MZM, they construct various fusion loops that result in quantized charge pumping,
serving as a clear physical manifestation of non-Abelian fusion. A particularly appealing aspect of the proposal is that it
avoids direct manipulation of the topological superconductor itself, relying instead on gate-controlled parameters, which
greatly enhances experimental feasibility. The charge transfer analysis provides an accessible experimental observable,
and the concise criterion—namely, that charge pumping becomes nontrivial when the FM energy level and coupling
intersect an odd number of times at zero energy—offers an elegant and practical guide for experimental implementation. 
The authors further support their proposal through numerical simulations in a realistic nanowire–superconductor setup,
elucidating two distinct charge transfer processes associated with MZMs and Andreev bound states (ABSs). This distinction
provides valuable insights into the interplay between topological and trivial bound states, deepening our understanding of
MZM physics. Overall, the manuscript offers a timely and conceptually clear contribution to the study of Majorana fusion and
topological 
computing. I strongly recommend the manuscript for publication in Communications Physics, provided the
authors address the following suggestions to further improve the clarity and completeness of the work. 
1. The authors should elaborate on the experimental detectability of charge transfer. A more detailed discussion of possible
measurement schemes (e.g., charge sensing via 
dots or single-electron transistors) would strengthen the connection
between theory and feasible experimental setups. 
2. It would be helpful to sharpen the distinction between MZMs and ABSs by analyzing how the pumped charge behaves
under repeated fusion cycles or varying magnetic fields. Such analysis could provide a clearer criterion for differentiating
between topological and trivial origins of charge pumping. 
3. The manuscript mentions that the feasibility of the fusion protocol depends on a specific time window (1/Δ ≪ T ≪

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this paper, the authors propose a method to demonstrate the nontrivial fusion rules of Majorana zero modes (MZMs) by
coupling a single MZM to a fermionic mode (FM), such as a 
dot. The key idea is that the FM acts as two pre-fused MZMs,
and by modulating the FM's energy level and its coupling to the MZM in specific sequences, different fusion and splitting
processes can be simulated. 
They show that these processes, arranged in "nontrivial loops," result in a quantized charge transfer (either zero or an
integer charge) between the FM and the topological superconductor, which is detectable via charge sensing. This pumping
effect is robust for true MZMs and depends on the number of times the system parameters cross zero energy. The proposal
is tested in a realistic nanowire model, showing that while near-zero Andreev bound states can mimic this effect, true MZMs
provide more robust and initial-state-independent quantized pumping. This offers a feasible and less intrusive experimental
pathway to confirm the non-Abelian nature of MZMs. 
I believe the key novelty is as follows. Prior “fusion-rule” discussions usually manipulate multiple wire segments or island
couplings. Here, one attaches a single tunable fermionic level to one MZM and drives a 2-parameter loop—lowering
experimental complexity while still testing non-Abelian fusion order dependence via a charge pump observable. This is a
compelling, experimentally approachable fusion rule witness, distinct from (but complementary to) braiding. 
I recommend the publication of the paper. But the authors should be careful about the typos and mistakes in the draft. 
If any sentence equates your pumped-charge loop directly with non-Abelian statistics in full generality, tighten to “fusion-rule
witness/diagnostic”. Your protocol compellingly witnesses fusion order (odd/even zero-crossing parity) but is not a braiding
demonstration. 
You note ABS can sometimes mimic pumping features. Anywhere you imply the loop is sufficient to exclude ABS, soften to:
“We identify practical discriminants”. 
There are many mistakes in journal, volume, page, author, or typos. I only list two of them, and I believe there are more than
10 such typos. 
Replace: “Lutchyn, R. M., Sau, J. D. & Das Sarma, S., … Phys. Rev. B 105, 077001 (2010)” With: “Lutchyn, R. M., Sau, J. D.
& Das Sarma, S. Phys. Rev. Lett. 105, 077001 (2010).” 
Replace: “Önder, G. et al., Appl. Phys. Lett. 13, 192 (2018)” With: “Gül, Ö., et al., Nat. Nanotechnol. 13, 192–197 (2018).” 
In addition, FM (fermionic mode) and QD ( dot) both appear. If you intend them to be the same physical element, define once
(e.g., “a single-level 
dot (QD, hereafter the fermionic mode, FM)”) and use one acronym thereafter to avoid confusion.
