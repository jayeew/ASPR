Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript is a timely, high-quality summary of a critical area in catalysis and is well-suited for publication as a
Perspective/Review in CommsChem. 
This Perspective/Review provides a valuable and timely summary of synthetic advancements in the challenging area of
deuterated alcohol synthesis using heterogeneous catalysis. The core aim of the work is twofold: first, to systematically
consolidate the fragmented literature on using solid catalysts for alcohol deuteration; and second, to critically contrast the
performance (activity, chemoselectivity, and site selectivity) of heterogeneous systems with conventional homogeneous
methods. The paper successfully argues that heterogeneous catalysis offers a robust, recyclable, and scalable alternative
that has evolved to overcome the limitations of homogeneous systems, particularly their dependence on costly ligands and
challenging catalyst recovery. The manuscript clearly tracks the field's development from early non-activated systems (Pd/C)
to highly controlled modern techniques, including ligand-modified catalysts and Single-Atom Catalysts (SACs). 
More Major revisions: 
• The mechanistic understanding evolved over time needs some clarity. The redox pathway for Pd/C, Ru/C, a proposed C-H
activation pathway for Ru/C study on sugars, and two distinct pathways including C-H activation for Pd SAC makes it
confusing. Integrating a discussion/comparison of how the definitive experimental validation of direct C-H activation
provided by the Pd-SAC work helps resolve or shed light on the mechanistic ambiguities encountered in the earlier
supported nanoparticle systems. This integration would provide a powerful conclusion about the progression of surface
mechanism understanding in this field. 
• The Outlook section is correctly oriented toward continued mechanistic study. To strengthen its impact, the authors should
explicitly define 2-3 specific, high-priority synthetic challenges that remain difficult even with the current state-of-the-art SACs
and ligand modification. 
• To fully convey the practical impact of the latest systems, the authors should briefly and explicitly introduce the impressive
quantitative metrics associated with the advanced catalysts (e.g., the Pd SAC achieving up to 95% deuterium incorporation
at the α-position) earlier in the main text when discussing these innovations. This small addition will effectively showcase
the leap in performance achieved by these advanced heterogeneous designs. 
Minor revisions: 
• The readability could be improved by providing cited references in the figure captions in addition to the text. Given the
discussion of different structures and pathways, and figure placement relative to where they are discussed in the text, it
would be easier to follow with the cited reference in the caption. More details can be added to the captions. 
• Page 1 lines 36. Sentence “Notably, around…..group” Needs a reference 
• Fig 8 caption panel (a) give a bit more detail on the catalyst/recycling

Response: We thank the reviewer for this very insightful comment. Actually, p rior to our Pd single -atom–
catalyzed deuteration, the mechanistic picture of heterogeneous alcohol H/D exchange was largely inferred rather 
than firmly established. To the best of our knowledge, only one experimental observation—the fully racemization 
of a chiral alcohol under Ru/C -catalyzed conditions —supported the operation of a borrowing -hydrogen 
mechanism, whereas the previous proposed direct C –H activation pathway (e.g., in the Ru/ C study on sugars) 
remained purely hypothetical, without direct experimental confirmation. In our Pd single-atom system, however,

the racemization observed for enantioenriched benzylic alcohols provided the first clear experimental evidence 
that, in addition to the borrowing -hydrogen pathway, a genuine direct C –H activation process also operates on 
the heterogeneous catalyst surface. This finding fills a critical gap in mechanistic understanding and supplies 
concrete evidence that was previously lacking for supported nanoparticle systems. 
In the revised manuscript, we have therefore strengthened the mechanistic discussion. We now summarize 
the key ambiguities in assigning mechanisms for heterogeneous alcohol H/D exchange, outline our experimental 
strategy fo r distinguishing between the two pathways, and discuss the implications of the racemization 
experiments more explicitly in the revised manuscript.  
We have added the following discussion in the revised manuscript to address this point:  
“It is worth noting  that, the frequently proposed direct C –H activation pathways for Pd/C and Ru/C, as 
exemplified in this sugar-deuteration study, remain largely hypothetical and, to the best of our knowledge, have 
not yet been corroborated by direct experimental evidence.” (Ref Page 6, paragraph 3) 
“Next, we investigated the reaction mechanism by examining the racemization behavior of a chiral alcohol 
(Fig. 8d). Based on the conventional understanding that direct C –H bond activation can, in principle, preserve 
chirality (Fig. 8 e), whereas the borrowing -hydrogen pathway typically leads to complete racemization, we 
selected (S) -33 as a model substrate. After 10 h of reaction, 81% D α-incorporation was obtained and the 
deuterated product was isolated with an enantiomeric rati o (er) of 72:28. This outcome indicates that 
approximately 37.2% of the (S)-33-d product is formed via a direct C–H activation pathway, while the remainder 
arises from a borrowing -hydrogen process. In addition, to the best of our knowledge, all previously reported 
catalytic methods for the synthesis of deuterated chiral alcohols result in complete racemization, presumably 
because they proceed exclusively through borrowing -hydrogen pathways. In contrast, our results reveal a 
significant contribution from direct C–H activation in the Pd SACs system and thereby demonstrate the distinctive 
selectivity of this catalyst in H/D exchange.” (Ref Page 9, paragraph 3)

hydrogen pathways that inevitably ind uce racemization;  (2) achieving true site -selective deuteration of

carbohydrate scaffolds, particularly at challenging positions such as bridgehead carbons, which remain 
inaccessible with existing Ru/C systems;  (3) enabling α -selective deuteration of non-benzylic aryl-substituted 
aliphatic alcohols, where current heterogeneous catalysts cannot discriminate among electronically unbiased

We have added the following discussion in the revised manuscript to address this point:  
“Looking forward, however, several important challenges remain unresolved and present valuable 
opportunities for future research. 
1) Stereoretentive H/D exchange for chiral secondary alcohols. 
Although Pd SACs provide the first definitive evidence that direct C –H activation can occur on isolated 
metal sites, the simultaneous operation of a borrowing -hydrogen pathway inevitably leads to racemization of 
chiral secondary alcohols. To date, no heterogeneous or homogeneous catalyst can achieve stereoretentive H/D 
exchange at stereogenic alcohol centers. The development of catalysts capable of suppressing racemization while 
enabling efficient α-deuteration represents a high-priority goal. 
2) Site-selective deuteration of carbohydrate scaffolds, especially bridgehead positions. 
While Ru/C systems have enabled α-deuteration adjacent to hydroxyl groups in sugars, achieving true site-
selective deuteration at more challenging positions —such as bridgehead carbons in glucose or related 
saccharides—remains beyond reach. The ability to access such isotopologues would greatly expand the utility of 
deuterated carbohydrates in metabolic and mechanistic studies. 
3) Selective deuteration of non-benzylic aliphatic alcohols at the hydroxyl-adjacent site. 
For aryl-substituted aliphatic alcohols in which the hydroxyl group is remote from the aromatic ring (i.e., 
non-benzylic alcohols), current heterogeneous catalysts cannot achieve α -site-selective deuteration. Methods 
capable of differentiating between electronically unbiased aliphatic C–H bonds are urgently needed to extend the 
reach of heterogeneous H/D exchange. 
4) Harnessing enol intermediates for α,β-bis-deuteration of non-benzylic alcohols. 
Given that borrowing-hydrogen pathways necessarily generate transient enol or enolate-like intermediates, 
leveraging these species to achieve controlled α,β-multisite deuteration of non-benzylic substrates represents an 
unexplored but promising direction. Designing catalysts and reaction environments that can selectively intercept 
these intermediates would open new synthetic possibilities. 
Addressing these challenges will req uire deeper mechanistic understanding of surface -mediated hydrogen 
transfer, improved control over the balance between borrowing -hydrogen and direct C–H activation pathways,

Reviewer #2: This manuscript describes several deuteration methods of alcohols using transition metal catalysts. 
Recently, many efforts have been devoted to developing a new method for the incorporation of deuterium (D+ or 
D-) into saturated compounds, or the direct substitution via C-H activation, because of the significant utilities of 
the deuterium-incorporated compounds in the research areas of analytical chemistry and drug discovery. 
This manuscript aims to inform the history and recent advances of deuteration focused on supported 
nanoparticles and single -atom catalysts for the synthesis of synthetical ly and industrially useful deuterium -
labelled alcohols. The reviewer recognizes that this chemistry is important. However, this manuscript covers only 
several references (refs. 40-45). Most of them were reported more than 15 years ago. Others consist of ge neral 
information on the characteristic aspects of deuterium and deuterated compounds. It seems to me that the review 
of this research topic is premature. 
Response: We sincerely thank the reviewer for recognizing the importance of this topic and for the constructive 
comments. We have carefully revised the manuscript in accordance with the reviewer’s suggestions. 
 First, we acknowledge that the works of reported nanoparticle-catalyzed H/D exchange reactions of alcohols 
is still relatively limited. In the revised manuscript, we have added 3 additional works (ref. 21, 48 and 51) of 
nanoparticle-catalyzed deuteration and expanded the corresponding discussion in the main text. The 
manuscript now includes 9 such studies  (ref. 41-48 and 51) , which provides a more comprehensive and 
informative overview of the current state of heterogeneous, catalyzed alcohol deuteration. 
We have added the following discussion in the revised manuscript to address this point:  “The use of 
heterogeneous catalysts for the deuteration of alcohols can be traced back to 200247. At that time, Sajiki and co-
workers showed that a Pd/C–D2O–H2 system could promote benzylic H/D exchange at room temperature. Under 
these mild conditions, deuteration was confined to benzylic positions and no α -deuteration of the hydroxyl -
adjacent C –H bonds were observed. For example, in the case of 3 -phenylpropan-1-ol (1), deuterium 
incorporation occurred exclusively at the benzylic position, while the α -C–H bond next to the hydroxyl group 
remained untouched (Fig. 3).” (Ref Page 3, paragraph 1) 
“Although the authors recognized that hydrogen gas is essential for the Pd/C –D2O system, its specific role 
was not clarified in the initial reports. To address this question, Sajiki and co-workers subsequently examined the 
function of H2 more carefully. They found that when Pd/C was pretreated with H2 in D2O and the H/D exchange 
reaction was then performed under an argon atmosphere, deuteration still occurred only with reduced efficiency. 
This result suggests that hydrogen gas is not strictly required for the H/D exchange itself and may instead serve 
mainly to activate the catalyst. Based on these observations, the authors proposed that a small amount of H 2 
functions primarily to acti vate Pd0 on carbon, most likely through the formation of surface Pd –H species that 
facilitate oxidative insertion into C–H bonds and promote catalytic turnover.48” (Ref Page3 paragraph 2)

“A subsequent 2012 study by Sajiki  and co -workers further advanced this Ru/C –H2–D2O platform by 
demonstrating that the α-deuteration of carbohydrates can proceed with high levels of stereocontrol. Building on 
their earlier protocol for glycosides and six -membered sugars, they showed that a  wide range of mono - and 
disaccharide derivatives undergo efficient H/D exchange at the positions adjacent to hydroxyl groups while 
largely preserving the original stereochemistry at the anomeric and neighboring centers. This work therefore 
established Ru/C as a powerful and practical heterogeneous catalyst for regio - and stereoselective deuteration 
of complex carbohydrate frameworks, and it significantly broadened the synthetic utility of heterogeneous sugar 
deuteration.51” (Ref Page 6, paragraph 3) 
 Second, we have deepened the mechanistic discussion of nanoparticle- catalyzed pathways, especially where 
ambiguities previously remained. For example, we now provide more detailed analysis of the redox-mediated 
Pd/C-catalyzed H/D exchange of secondary alcohols (Fig. 4), the Ru/C-catalyzed site-selective α-deuteration 
of alkyl alcohols (Fig. 5), the NHC -modified Ru/C systems for α -selective deuteration (Fig. 7), and the Pd 
SAC-catalyzed site -selective deuteration of benzylic alcohols (Fig. 8). In each case,  we have added 
explanatory text and refined the corresponding figures so that the mechanistic insights are clearer and go 
beyond purely descriptive coverage. 
 Third, we have expanded the Outlook section to more explicitly define the remaining challenges and  future 
opportunities in heterogeneous deuteration chemistry, including unmet needs in site -selective labeling of 
complex alcohols and carbohydrates (please see Summary and Outlook Part). 
 Finally, we would like to respectfully clarify that this manuscript is intended as a Perspective rather than a 
comprehensive Review. A Review seeks to provide exhaustive coverage across an entire field, whereas a 
Perspective focuses on a more selective, author-informed discussion of emerging themes, conceptual insights, 
and possible future directions. In revising the manuscript, we have made additional efforts to better reflect 
this intended scope by contextualizing how a small number of representative heterogeneous studies have 
contributed to the development of catalytic methods for alcohol deuteration. 
Taken together, we believe these revisions and clarifications directly address the reviewer’s concerns and 
support the appropriateness and timeliness of the current Perspective.
