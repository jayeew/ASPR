Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript of Bao Ma et al reports on the cryo-EM structure of the calcium-activated SK2 channel in complex with four
pharmacological modulators, the outer pore blockers apamin and UCL1684, the negative gating modulator AP30663, and
the activator CAD-1883. Taken together, this is a highly significant and impactful study, since SK2 channels are important
drug targets for neurological and cardiovascular disease. The field urgently needs structures that can enable structure-based
drug design and assist with a deeper understanding of the channel’s gating mechanisms and its pharmacological
modulation. The study significantly adds to the field. Especially, the apamin structure and its description are superb. The
authors provide a large amount of high-quality structural, electrophysiological and mutagenesis data and should be
applauded for their success in obtaining structures for human SK2, which in contrast to rat SK2 (Nam et al. Nat
Communications 2025) and a chimeric human SK2/SK4 construct (Cassell et al. eLife 2025), has proven difficult to query in
structural studies. However, the authors should clarify a few points and discuss differences between their structures and
previously published structures in more detail since there are some very interesting differences and similarities that are likely
to provide deeper insights into the mechanisms of pharmacological modulation. 
Major comments: 
1. In contrast to the other two structural studies using rat SK2 or a human SK2/SK4 chimera the density of the channel
associated CaM in three of the four structures is weak and it is not clear from the provided figures, the text or the methods,
how much calcium was present during the purification process and whether the apamin, UCL1684 and AP30663 structures
contain any calcium ions in the EF hands of the four CaMs. Figure 1, which shows human SK2 in complex with CaM is
showing the open structure in the presence of the activator CAD-1883. Was there any calcium visible in the CaM C-lobes in
the apamin, UCL1684 and AP30663 structures? This question is of interest because the Cassell and the Nam studies
captured SK2 with a wide-open inner gate when they were complexed with apamin or UCL1684. So, it is very interesting
that authors obtained structures with a closed inner gate. These differences in channel state should be commented on by the
authors. As a reviewer, I do not think acknowledging previous structures will distract from the significance and the impact of
the authors’ work. But the authors really need to clarify how much calcium was used and whether the experiments were
designed to capture open or closed channels, especially since their study does not contain an Apo structure. 
2. Did the authors also attempt to capture an Apo structure in the absence and presence of calcium? Was the resolution
maybe too low? This goes towards answering the previous point. If the authors expressed and isolated SK2 protein and
associated CaM at normal intracellular calcium concentrations, that could explain why only the CAD-1883 bound structure
contains calcium. Like the authors correctly state, CAD-1883 increases the calcium sensitivity of SK2 and “glues” the CaM-N
lobe to the S4-S5 linker. The presence and absence of calcium should be discussed. 
3. The authors should clarify if the cholesterol density shown in Figure 1 was present in all four structures. 
4. The authors might want to consider removing Supplementary Fig 9 showing a comparison of the CAD-1883 binding site
at the S4-S5 linker to the “infamous” C-terminal crystal of CaM in complex with the isolated HA-HB helices. Ever since the
SK4 structure was published by the MacKinnon lab, it has been clear that this C-terminal crystal is simply an artifact and this
comparison might confuse younger readers. It would be much more interesting to see an overlay of the closed SK2/apamin
and the closed SK2/UCL1684 structures from the authors’ work with the previously published open structures. How

hypothesis that prolonged opening of the inner gate by CAD-1883 might eventually induce channel inactivation by altering

Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this study, Ma et al. present cryo-EM structures of human SK2/calmodulin in complex with three pore blockers (apamin,
UCL1684, and AP30663) and a positive gating modulator (CAD-1883). Together with electrophysiological analyses, these
data reveal structural mechanisms underlying the inhibition and modulation of SK2 channels by different drugs. This is a
solid structural investigation that provides valuable insights into the pharmacological properties of SK2. 
While I appreciate the scientific significance of the findings, my main concern lies with the writing quality. The manuscript
contains numerous grammatical errors, awkward phrasing, and imprecise statements. It therefore requires substantial
editorial revision before it can be considered for publication. Additionally, several interpretations appear overstated or
unclear and require correction. Below are my specific comments: 
1. Abstract, line 33: Please clarify what “these disorders” refers to. 
2. Lines 53–54: The sentence “However, the detailed structural information of SK1-3 that discriminates them from SK4 has
remained unclear” is vague. Are you referring to specific structural features distinguishing SK1–3 from SK4? Please be
explicit.

⁺

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, Ma et. al. investigated the regulation of human small-conductance calcium-activated potassium channel
(SK) by four distinct modulators. SK channels are potassium-selective channels activated by intracellular Ca binding to
calmodulin, which is constitutively associated with the channel. The SK channel family comprises four isoforms (SK1, SK2,
SK3, and SK4) and this study focuses on SK2. Structural information of SK channel in complex with modulators is limited
and 
the authors addressed this gap by determining cryoEM structures of calmodulin-bound SK2 in complex with bee venom
peptide apamin and other small molecule modulators i.e. UCL1684, AP30663, and CAD-1883. These structures revealed
the binding sites and regulatory mechanisms of each modulator. Apamin, UCL1684, and AP30663 act as inhibitors of SK
channels while CAD-1883 is an activator. Their results show that both apamin and UCL1684 bind the outer vestibule
occupying an overlapping binding site and blocks the ion permeation pathway from the extracellular side while AP30663
binds to a distinct site in the central cavity of SK2 channel blocking the pore. CAD-1883 binds the calmodulin N-lobe and the

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 3 -->

S45A helix of SK2, stabilizing the open conformation of the intracellular gate. Further, they carried out mutagenesis and
electrophysiology studies to validate these structural findings. 
Overall, the study is well designed and clearly presented, providing mechanistic insights into the Ca-dependent activation
and modulation of SK2 channel activity by inhibitors and activators. The findings will help further guide rational development
of drugs targeting SK channels. The manuscript is suitable for publication. Some of the concerns that should be addressed
before acceptance are as follows: 
1. Two recent studies reported SK channel structures with overlapping findings to those presented in the current manuscript.
Nam et al. (Nature Communications, 2025) resolved cryo-EM structures of the SK2 channel in complex with calmodulin and
Ca, as well as with the inhibitor UCL1684. Cassell et al. (eLife, 2025) reported the cryo-EM structure of a chimeric SK
channel bound to apamin, generated by fusing the transmembrane and extracellular domains of human SK2 with the
intracellular domains of human SK4. The authors should cite both studies and discuss whether their findings align with or
diverge from these published results. 
2. The text between lines 167–183 is somewhat difficult to follow, as it brings together multiple interactions in a way that
interrupts the logical flow of the structural description. For instance, in lines 174–176, the interactions of R240 with
neighboring subunit residues are linked to stabilization of the S3–S4 linker, though this was not experimentally tested. This
makes the statement feel less convincing. 
A clearer way to present this section could be to first highlight that the S3–S4 linker is ordered in the apamin-bound
structure. The authors might then propose that H336, N367, A242, and Y245 contribute to stabilizing the linker based on
structural evidence. Building on this, they could emphasize how mutating these residues reduces apamin inhibition, thereby
supporting their role in stabilizing the loop conformation and enhancing apamin binding. Finally, the narrative could be
strengthened by referencing similar findings from other groups. 
3. In lines 168–169, the authors report that 300 nM apamin inhibits the H336N and N367H mutants by only ~20% and ~60%,
respectively. It would be helpful if the authors could clarify the rationale for choosing these specific substitutions—why H336
was mutated to Asn and N367 to His. Providing this context would make the functional data more informative and easier to
interpret. 
4. For Fig. 4f, the author should provide representative current trace for all the mutants. Why did they mutate the interacting
residues to bulkier ones only and not to Ala to abolish inhibitory effect of AP30663? Also, A383F shows reduced inhibition
compared to A383L while it is reversed in case of T386 where Phe mutant shows stronger inhibition compared to the Leu
mutant. What is the basis of this difference? 
5. Y245 is not labelled in Fig. 2e. 
6. In line 234, L32 is mentioned but not shown in the Fig. 3c, d. 
7. Lines 241-242 states “L291W and L291A/N292A mutants display reduced currents” However, from the data it seems that
all the mutants show reduced current and not only the two mutants mentioned here. 
8. In Supplementary Fig. 2b, the Y-axis reads excitation (%). It is not clear what it means. 
9. Line 263, ‘involving of HC helix’ should be ‘involvement of HC helix’. 
10. Line 367, ‘pocket that apamin does’ should be ‘pocket than apamin does’. 
11. In the method section, it should be mentioned how different modulators were applied during electrophysiology
recordings.
