Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript presents a high-performance RuO2@GCE system for electrocatalytic CH4 chlorination, achieving high
CH3Cl production rates, operational stability, and selectivity. The study is well-structured and provides rich experimental
data, supported by both molecular-level and system-level investigations. However, there are several critical issues that need
to be addressed to strengthen the work and demonstrate its broader industrial applicability. Therefore, I recommend major
revision before the manuscript can be considered for publication. 
1. The authors use 3 M NaCl electrolyte to simulate high-salinity wastewater. However, actual high-salinity wastewater
typically contains various other compounds and impurities. It is important to investigate whether these impurities affect the
catalytic activity of RuO2 to substantiate the potential for industrial application. 
2. Lines 202-204, some of the data presented in the main text appear to be inconsistent with those in Supplementary Table
1. The authors should provide a clear explanation of the calculation methods. 
3. Supplementary Video 1 could not be accessed or located. The authors should check and ensure that all supplementary
materials are available and properly referenced. 
4. Does the optimized hierarchical porous gas convection electrode (GCE) effectively suppress the chlorine evolution
reaction (CER)? The manuscript should include experimental or computational evidence to clarify this point. 
5. In line 293, it is mentioned that the liquid film thickness is a key factor. Can the thickness of the liquid film be effectively
controlled in practical operation? 
6. The optimal catalyst loading for GCE is reported to be five times higher than that of GDE. Is there a trade-off between
catalytic activity and material cost? 
7. In Fig. 4c, at higher current densities, the performance ratio between GCE and GDE becomes closer. Would GDE
outperform GCE at even higher current densities (>80 mA∙mg-1)? The authors should discuss or experimentally explore this
possibility. 
8. The DFT results suggest a tendency for Cl2 formation; however, Fig. 4e does not consider this product. It is recommended
to include an analysis of Cl2 production to complete the discussion. 
9. The claimed “exceptional operation stability” (e.g., maintaining 104.25 ± 4.89 mmol cm
