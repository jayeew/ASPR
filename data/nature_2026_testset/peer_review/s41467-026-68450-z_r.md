Reviewer 1 
 
This reviewer does not recommend publishing the current perovskite CO 2 reduction study in a 
high-impact journal (e.g., a prestigious Nature sister journal) for the following reasons: 
We hope our additional experiments and explanations (described below) will resolve any 
confusion. 
 
 
1. the concept here using NIR to reduce CO2 is not new, and does not represent any breakthrough. 
Using perovskite as the photocatalyst does not necessarily guarantee the unique outcome. Here 
in this work, there has been little novelty from a fundamental point of view to understand CO2 
photocatalytic chemistry. 
We agree with the reviewer's viewpoint that NIR -driven CO2 reduction has been previously 
reported in literature . However, most existing  NIR systems led to the production of C 1 
compounds, such as CO, CH 4, and HCOOH ( Chem. Soc. Rev ., 2025, 54, 7174-7215). In 
addition, it is often necessary to employ multi -component heterojunction photocatalysts (e.g. 
Z-scheme) to achieve NIR-driven CO2 photocatalysis (Nat. Commun. 2024, 15, 7825). Up-to-
date, no single-component NIR photocatalyst has been report ed to selectively afford C 2 
products. Therefore, discovering the first single -component NIR photocatalyst to 
produce C 2 hydrocarbons is of sufficient novelty in CO 2 photocatalysis chemistry. We 
hope our explanations in detail in detail will resolve any confusion. 
 Single-component photocatalysts offer distinct advantages over multi-component 
systems in terms of structural simplicity and long -term operational stability. By 
integrating both light -harvesting units and catalytically active s ites within a unified 
framework, single -component systems eliminate the need for complex interfacial 
engineering, thereby minimizing charge recombination losses commonly observed at 
heterojunctions. In addition, they typically exhibit higher synthetic reproducibility and 
scalability, making them more appealing for practical applications. Therefore, 
developing a single -component narrow-bandgap photocatalysts has emerged as the 
most direct strategy to achieve large-scale NIR photocatalysis. However, a significant 
challenge for NIR -driven photocatalysis is that the low energy of NIR light leads to 
inadequate photon absorption and insufficient carrier accumulation, which hinder the 
multi-electron CO 2 reduction kinetics for C 2+ product formation. Thus, designing a 
single-component photocatalyst capable of efficiently utilizing NIR light for CO2–to–
C2 conversion remains a major challenge in the field of CO2 photocatalysis, and is the 
central focus of this study.  
 To address this challenge, we explore lead halide h ybrids (not limited to perovskite 
and our structure s are completely different from perovskites ) for single -
component NIR -driven photocatalysis. This selection is motivated by the well -
established, excellent photophysical properties of hybrid lead halides. Nevertheless, 
lead halide hybrids face three critical limitations in CO 2 photocatalysis: 1) 
Instability: The inherently ionic nature of  hybrid metal halides lead s to structural 
instability, especially under highly polar conditions commonly encountered during

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 5 -->

photocatalysis. 2) Symmetric active sites: Most lead halide hybrids feature symmetric 
PbX6 octahedra coordination environments without charge polarization, which  
disfavor C –C coupling pat hways. 3) NIR inactive : These halide hybrids generally 
exhibit limited absorption in NIR region and suppressed charge accumulation to drive 
multi-electron reduction upon NIR irradiation. In our study, we have employed the 
coordination-driven assembly synth esis to produce a series of M(tpy) 2 
(M=Fe2+/Co2+/Ni2+) antenna functionalized lead halide hybrids, which overcome all 
three critical issues. 1) High stability: The covalent Pb2+-carboxylate linkages within 
these coordination networks overcoming the inherent instability of conventional lead 
halide perovskites. 2) Active site engineering: Compared to the symmetric Pb2Cl4 and 
Pb2Br4 building units, the additional coordination of DMF molecules in Pb 2I4(DMF) 
clusters induces intrinsic charge polarization at the local Pb2+ catalytic sites to promote 
C-C coupling. 3) NIR active:  The coordination -driven assembly of M(tpy) 2 
photosensitizers with lead halide units enables inorganic-organic orbital hybridization, 
affording broad  UV-visible-NIR absorption (up to 1150 nm ) and efficient 
photoinduced charge transfer to [Pb2I4(DMF)] catalytic sites. 
 To conclude, this work presents the first examples of single-component photocatalysts 
capable of NIR -driven C 2 hydrocarbon production from CO 2. By employing 
coordination-driven assembly with a benchmark M(tpy) 2 photosensitizer, this is a 
breakthrough in lead halide photocatalysis that  not only overcomes the  inherent 
stability limitations of conventional lead halides, but also induces  intrinsic charge 
polarization at the active si tes and promotes NIR-active carrier accumulation. This 
synthetic strategy establishes a new paradigm for designing structurally tunable and 
ultrastable lead halide photocatalysts.

We sincerely appreci ate the reviewer’s insightful questions regarding the fundamental 
principles underlying the excellent photocatalytic performance observed in our system. We 
agree that a mechanistic understanding is essential to support the significance of our work. 
Below, we provide a detailed explanation that highlights the structural, electronic, and 
mechanistic novelty of our photocatalyst, which hopefully resolve any confusion. 
 Advancing both stability and NIR absorption for hybrid lead halides by 
establishing coordination-driven synthesis. Conventional lead halide hybrids are one 
of the benchmark components in photovoltaic due to their excellent structural tunability 
and outstanding photophysical properties, such as broad visible -light absorption, high 
extinction coefficient, and efficient charge carrier transfer. However, their application 
in photocatalysis has been largely limited by inherent instability, particularly under 
high-polarity protic conditions. This instability primarily originates from their soft,

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 6 -->

ionically bound lattice.  To overcome this limitation, we have previously employed a 
coordination-driven assembly strategy to construct lead halide frameworks with Pb2+-
carboxylate coordination motifs, resulting in substantially enhanced structural integrity 
and chemical robustness (Acc. Chem. Res.  2023, 56, 452 ; Nat. Catal. 2020, 3, 1027; 
Angew. Chem. Int. Ed . 2024, 63, e202316080; Nat. Commun. 2022, 13, 4592 ). 
However, these chemically stable frameworks often have a narrow visible -light 
absorption. In this work, we successfully construct a series of ultrastable lead halide 
framework with extended light absorption to NIR region, by integrating benchmark 
M(tpy)2-based photosensitizers. Therefore, we report for the first time a class of 
single-component lead halide frameworks that combine high structural stability 
with strong NIR absorption, distinguishing them from previously reported lead halide 
photocatalysts or multi-component NIR photocatalysts.  
 Intrinsic charge polarization in asymmetric Pb 2+ sites for C 2 product formation. 
A critical challenge in CO2–to–C2 photocatalysis is to suppress the strong dipole-dipole 
repulsion between adjacent C 1 intermediates, which often prevents C -C coupling 
(Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 2025, 64, e202418708; Acc. Chem. Res. 2023, 56, 2500). The 
Pb2+ active sites in conventional lead halides typically reside in centrosymmetric PbX6 
octahedra, resulting in uniform charge distribution that disfavors C –C coupling. In 
contrast, our coordination -driven synthesis allows for the  rational modulation of the 
local Pb2+ environment. While the Pb 2Cl4 and Pb2Br4 units retain high symmetry, the 
Pb2I4(DMF) clusters induces intrinsic charge polarization at the local Pb 2+ catalytic 
sites due to the axial coordination of DMF molecules. This asymmetric Pb2 sites breaks 
the uniform charge distribution, lowering the activation barrier for C–C bond formation 
(Figure 5d) and enabling selective CO 2 photoreduction to C 2 products. Overall, this 
study represents a new insightful phenomenon to precise ly modulate charge 
polarization of local Pb 2 sites in lead halides to modulate CO 2 photoreduction 
products, which has not been realized in other single-component NIR photocatalysts. 
 Photoinduced charge -transport pathway from benchmark M(tpy) 2 
photosensitizer to lead halide units. In the field of hybrid lead halides functionalized 
with benchmark photosensitizers, researchers have typically employed surface 
modification strategies to graft photoactive moieties, such as  ferrocene, metal -
porphyrin and metal-pyridine groups (PNAS. 2024, 121, e2315956121; Angew. Chem. 
Int. Ed.  2024, 63, e202401344) , onto lead halide quantum dots ( e.g. CsPbBr3). 
However, the inevitable structural disorder at heterojunction interfaces hinders precise 
regulation of the charge transfer pathway and limits in -depth understanding of 
structure-activity relationships. In this work, our single-component photocatalysts, 
with well -defined single -crystal X-ray structures, offer an ideal  platform for 
probing charge-transfer processes between benchmark photosensitizers and lead 
halide components.  Extensive photophysical spectroscopy combined with 
computational calculations clear ly reveal an efficient photoinduced electron transfer 
from the M(tpy) 2 antenna to the charge -polarized Pb2I4(DMF) catalytic sites, thereby 
promoting multi-electron CO2 reduction kinetics.  
 Overall, this study offers three key contributions to advancing the  new insightful 
understanding of CO 2 photocatalysis: 1) Both enhanced stability and NIR absorption 
of hybrid lead halides to achieve single -component NIR -driven photocatalysis. 2)

Precise modulation of local dimeric Pb 2 site charge polarization to tune CO 2 
photoreduction selectivity. 3) Use of X-ray structurally well-defined single-component 
photocatalysts to elucidate charge -transfer processes  between two photoactive 
components (benchmark M(tpy)2 photosensitizers and lead halide sublattices). 
 
3. why selectivity is so high? The calculation and the whole manuscript here do not mention 
anything insightful regarding selectivity for C2 product? 
We sincerely appreciate the reviewer’s insightful comment regarding the high selectivity 
toward C 2 products. We a gree that elucidating the selectivity mechanism is critical for this 
work. In our study, among the nine lead halide framework, only TJU -60(I) featuring 
unique Pb 2I4(DMF) clusters exhibited remarkable selectivity ( 64-86%) toward C 2 
products in CO 2 photoreduction. In contrast, the other six TJU -59(Cl/Br) materials, 
constructed from highly symmetric Pb2Cl4 and Pb2Br4 units, showed negligible selectivity for 
C2 product formation. Moreover, the control experiment further confirmed that the M(tpy) 2 
ligand alone (without incorporating lead halide framework) only produces C1 products. Based 
on these experimental observations, we have performed thorough experimental mechanistic 
studies as well as DFT calculations to investigate the selectivity issue. We hope our additional 
experiments and explanations (see below) will resolve any confusion. 
 Asymmetric local active sites: A key factor contributing to the high C 2 selectivity is 
the intrinsic charge distribution of Pb 2 active sites in three TJU -60(I) photocatalysts. 
Unlike the symmetric Pb 2Cl4 and Pb 2Br4 units found in TJU -59(Cl/Br), the axial 
coordination of DMF in the Pb 2I4(DMF) clusters induces asymmetric charge 
polarization (Bader charge difference: ~0.09 e -; Supplementary Fig. 33). This 
polarization creates a more favorable environment for stabilizing C2 intermediates and 
facilitates C–C coupling, which suppresses the strong dipole-dipole repulsion between 
adjacent C1 intermediates (Chem. Soc. Rev., 2024, 53, 9964-9975; Angew. Chem. Int. 
Ed. 2025, 64, e202421353; Acc. Chem. Res . 2023, 18, 2500 -2513). Experimentally, 
this structural difference directly correlates with product distribution: while TJU -
59(Cl/Br) yield only C1 products, TJU-60(I) selectively produces C2 hydrocarbons (Fig. 
4d). This stark contrast highlights the critical role of charge polarization at local active 
sites in directing product selectivity. 
 Control experiments to elucidate selectivity: We have also investigated the influence 
of the M(tpy) 2 photosensitizers for photocatalysis. The Fe(tpy) 2 ligand alone (before 
incorporation into the lead halide framework) produced only C1 products (CO and CH4). 
Under UV-Vis-NIR irradiation, both active sites in TJU -60(I) afford the overall C 2 
electron selectivity to 79%. Meanwhile, under NIR irradiation, Fe(tpy) 2 is no longer 
active based on its HOMO/LUMO positions, and the charge-polarized Pb2 sites become 
the only active sites. This largely promote the C 2 electron selectivity to 86% (Figure 
4c). These cont rol experiments suggest that in TJU -60(I), the Pb 2I4(DMF) sites 
functions as the primary active site and the Fe(tpy)2 are the secondary active site. 
 In-situ DRIFTS evidence of C2 intermediates: In situ diffuse reflectance infrared 
Fourier transform spectroscopy (DRIFTS) provides direct evidence supporting the C–
C coupling in TJU-60(I)-Fe(tpy)2. The in-situ spectroscopy shows that the Pb2 sites in

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 8 -->

TJU-60(I)-Fe(tpy)2 stabilizes *CO and *C OH intermediates, and promote their 
coupling to form the key intermediate *COCOH. A distinct vibrational absorption band 
at 1256 cm-1 is assigned to the *COCOH intermediate (Adv. Mater. 2023, 35, 2208132), 
along with stronger intensities than those of *CO and *COH, indicating rapid formation 
and accumulation of the C –C coupling intermediate (Figure 5b). This is important 
experimental evidence to support the charge polarized Pb2 sites in TJU-60(I) favors the 
C-C coupling to afford CO2-to-C2 photoconversion. 
 DFT calculations of C–C coupling to form *COCOH : DFT calculations further 
support the experimental observations. For TJU-60(I), the Gibbs free energy diagram 
shows that *CO and *COH intermediates preferentially undergo C–C coupling to form 
*COCOH intermediate, with a low energy barrier of 0.49 eV. In contrast, formation of 
the C1 intermediate (*CHO) is energetically less favorable, requiring a higher barrier 
of 0.59 eV. These computational findings are in excellent agreement with our 
experimental observations of *COCOH intermediates. 
 In summary, the high C2 selectivity arises from the asymmetric, charge-polarized Pb2+ 
sites in the TJU -60(I), which effectively stabilize C –C coupling intermediates and 
facilitate their conversion into C 2 hydrocarbons. This is supported by experimental 
product distribution, in situ spectroscopic evidence, and theoretical calculations. 
 
4. perovskite CO2 reduction itself in an organic solvent system is of significant concern/debate in 
the catalytic community. Unfortunately, this group of authors does not have the necessary 
background to interpret the chemistry involved here. 
We sincerely appreciate the re viewer’s critical perspective regarding CO 2 photoreduction in 
organic solvents. We fully recognize that some photocatalytic studies have questioned whether 
organic solvents (e.g., DCM, DMF, ethyl acetate ) may act as unintended carbon sources or 
degrade per ovskites under photocatalytic conditions  (Angew. Chem. Int. Ed.  2022, 61, 
e202205572; ACS Energy Lett. 2021, 6, 9, 3270). However, our work directly addresses these 
concerns through the careful studies and rigorous experimental validation: 
 Structural robustness of our lead halide frameworks: Unlike traditional perovskite-
type lead halide hybrids with ionically bound lattice, our coordination-driven assembly 
of lead halide frameworks feature high intrinsic structural integrity and chemical 
robustness. T his series of M(tpy) 2-functionalized frameworks are stable in aqueous 
conditions and highly polar organic solvents, as confirmed by PXRD, XPS and SEM 
(Supplementary Figure S40-S42). These results demonstrate that the structural integrity 
of our photocatalysts is well-preserved throughout the photocatalytic process. 
 Rationale for using acetonitrile (MeCN) as the solvent: MeCN was chosen as the 
reaction medium owing to its high CO 2 solubility and high stability under 
photocatalytic conditions. In contrast, solvents such as DMF  and ethyl acetate readily 
undergo decomposition under strong reducing conditions, producing CO and 
(CH3)2NH2+ as the main byproducts. MeCN exhibits superior resistance to such 
degradation, especially in O2-free environments. Previous studies have systematically 
demonstrated that O2 plays an important role in the oxidative decomposition of organic 
solvents during CO 2 photoreduction (Angew. Chem. Int. Ed . 2022, 61, e202205572).

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 9 -->

When ultra-pure CO2 gas sparging is used to afford O2-free condition (<1 ppm O2), no 
meaningful CO and CH 4 production was observed. Therefore, MeCN has become a 
widely adopted solvent in CO2 photoreduction research involving crystalline materials 
(J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2025, 147, 16481; J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2023, 145, 8261; ACS Energy 
Lett. 2024, 9, 2739; Adv. Funct. Mater . 2025, 35, 2422348). In our study, solvent 
oxidation was completely avoided by conducting all photocatalysis experiments 
under rigorously oxygen -free conditions. The reaction system was thoroughly 
vacuum-degassed and subsequently purged with high -purity CO 2 (99.9%) until 
saturation, ensuring complete isolation from O 2 and thereby eliminating any potential 
interference from solvent photo-oxidation reactions in the experimental results. This is 
further confirmed by the blank control experiments (conducted in the absence of CO2) 
produced no detectable products (Supplementary Fig. 38), ruling out solvent -derived 
carbon. 
 Isotope labeling (13CO2) confirms CO2 as the carbon source: To rigorously rule out 
any contribution from solvent decomposition, we have re-performed additional 13CO2 
isotope labeling experiments: 20 mg of photocatalyst and 5 mL of reaction solution 
(0.5 mL TEOA, 0.1 mL H2O and 5 mL MeCN) are sonicated for 15 minutes to obtain 
a uniformly dispersed suspension. The reaction system was vacuum-treated and refilled 
with high-purity 13CO2 gas (99 atom% 13C, Aldrich) to achieve the removal of any air 
or oxygen and ensure that the final reactor is filled with 13CO2. All other reaction 
conditions were exactly the same as conventional CO 2 reduction experiments, and 
gaseous products were collected after reaction for 6 h. The products were analyzed by 
GC-MS (Agilent, 7890B and 5977B). This unambiguously confirmed that the detected 
products ( 13C2H4 and 13C2H6) were derived exclusively from CO 2 instead of other 
carbon sources (Fig. 4 g). Moreover, blank control experiments (conducted in  the 
absence of CO 2, light, or photocatalyst) produced no detectable products, further 
confirming that the products do not originate from the solvent or from the degradation 
of the photocatalyst (Supplementary Fig. 38), ruling out solvent-derived carbon.

 Fe(tpy)2 primarily functions as a photosensitizer , transferring photogenerated 
electrons to the Pb2 sites of the Pb2I4(DMF) clusters via Pb2+-carboxylate linkages. This 
charge-transfer pathway is fully supported by pDOS analysis, showing that VBM is 
dominated by Fe(tpy)2 while the CBM is mainly contributed by the lead iodide nodes 
(Figure 2b). In addition, the lower LUMO level of TJU-60(I)-Fe(tpy)2 relative to free 
Fe(tpy)2 allows for spontaneous transfer of photoelectrons (Figure 2g). These results 
confirm that the prima ry role of Fe(tpy) 2 is to act as the photosensitizer (“electron 
pump”) that transfer photoelectrons to Pb2 centers for CO2 activation and C–C coupling.  
 At the same time, Fe(tpy)2 can also serve as independent catalytic sites under UV-
Vis irradiation (but not NIR irradiation) . Free Fe(tpy)2 ligands exhibit moderate 
CO2 reduction activity, producing  CO and CH 4 but not generating C2 products, 
consistent with its ~2.2 eV bandgap that restricts activity to the UV -Vis region.  
Consequently, in TJU-60(I)-Fe(tpy)2, dual catalytic pathway exists under UV-Vis light: 
Fe(tpy)2 contributes both as a photosensitizer and as a catalytic site, lowering the C 2 
selectivity to ~ 79%. In contrast, under NIR irradiation, Fe(tpy) 2 sites are no longer 
catalytically active, thus its sole role is photosensitization. This explains the enhanced 
C2 selectivity up to 86% observed under NIR light.

We sincerely appreciate the reviewer’s constructive comment. Here, we have systematically 
compared the structural differences between our lead halide -based MOFs and conventional 
MOFs. 
 Crystallographically distinct from conventional MOFs.  First, conventional metal-
organic frameworks (MOFs) are predominantly constructed from highly symmetric 
metal-oxo clusters (e.g., Zr6O8, Zn4O) as secondary building units (SBUs). These hard 
oxygen-bridged SBUs typically result in insulating behavior and suppressed charge 
transport. In contrast, our framework incorporates lead halide components that actively 
participate in frontier orbitals, affording a rare class of semiconductive MOFs. 
Moreover, the bandgap of these semiconductive frameworks can be primarily governed 
by the 3d metal centers in M(tpy)2 moieties, and halide substitution provides additional

fine-tuning. The ability to precisely modulate semiconductive bandgaps for our 
lead halide frameworks disting uish them from conventional MOFs.  Second, in 
conventional MOFs, the high symmetry of metal active sites often leads to  identical 
coordination environment without intrinsic charge polarization, making C–C coupling 
for CO 2 photoreduction challenging. Our  systems overcome this limitation by 
employing soft, structural deformable  lead halide units, which generates 
asymmetric coordination environments  and intrinsic charge polarization at  
neighboring Pb2+ centers.  This structural feature significantly contributes to the 
enhanced C2 selectivity for CO2 photoreduction. 
 Synergistic combination of M(tpy)2 sensitizer, Pb2+ metal center, I⁻ counterion and 
coordinating DMF molecules. We thank the reviewer for raising the important point 
in terms of the combination of sensitizer, metal center, counterion, and coordinating 
solvent in MOFs. Through systematic literature survey, we confirm that  our study is 
the first example to realize the synergistic combination of M(tpy)2 sensitizer, Pb2+ metal 
center, I⁻ counterion and coordinating DMF molecules. Although M(tpy)2 is a classic 
photosensitizing ligand in MOF, the vast majority of them form Zr -oxo MOFs with 
high symmetric SBUs and charg e-balanced Zr4+ sites. Therefore, their photocatalytic

Soc. 2019, 141, 7115; Inorg. Chem. 2023, 62, 19015; Inorg. Chem. 2022, 61, 1590). In

the active site. Therefore, our unprecedented combination of M(tpy)2, lead halide SBUs

NIR-driven CO2-to-C2 photoconversion.
