Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author): 
Reviews by Sébastien Verkercke: A Space-Forged Super-Thermal Insulating Material—Lunar Agglutinates 
The authors present a comprehensive study of the thermal properties of different types of lunar grains, including
agglutinates, rock fragments, and glass beads, based on both measurements from lunar samples and on multi-scale
numerical simulations. The study is very thorough as it combines a very detailed experimental analysis of the samples’
structures, and an impressive multi-scale description of the grain composition and micro-structure constrained by the
measurements. The authors found that agglutinates exhibit thermal conductivity as low as ~ 8 mW m-1 K-1, surpassing any
known natural materials or aerogels. The authors attribute this extremely low value to the combination of nano-pores in the
agglutinates, limiting the cross-sectional contact area, and the multi-phase interfaces present in the agglutinates, both
resulting from space-weathering. This result is really novel as the thermal insulation of regolith grains was previously solely
attributed to the porosity. 
The results appear significant to planetary science applications where regolith heat diffusion is important. The authors
provide good experimental data to support their theoretical findings, while also using their models to illuminate key gaps in
the thermal description of agglutinate structure with complex mineralogy. My main comment is that the atomic scale
description using Molecular Dynamics simulations is not detailed enough than to be reproducible. The authors could further
expand on the possible perspectives and potential implications of this work. I would recommend this manuscript for
publication with minor revisions.

Reviewer #3 (Remarks to the Author): 
The paper presents some new interesting developments concerning properties of lunar regolith. The authors study particles
of lunar agglutinates from the Chang’E-5 mission and show that they display very low thermal conductivity that allows them
to surpass all known natural materials and to rival with synthetic super-insulating aerogels even with limited porosity. The
authors analyze the mechanism of this superior insulation and show that it stems from the joint effect of multiscale voids and
multiphase interfaces formed by space weathering, which effectively block heat transfer by phonons. They discuss
approaches of designing ultra-thermal-insulating materials rooted in the recognition that space weathering generates the
Moon’s exceptionally insulating regolith. The paper will be of interest for scientists studying lunar exploration, materials
science, thermal physics, etc. 
Although the paper is written in a good manner, there are some items which should be taken into account in its new version. 
The authors study lunar regolith particles which were brought to the Earth by Chang’E-5 spacecraft. The question (important
especially for scientists studying lunar exploration) arises as to how closely the conditions under which the lunar regolith
particles were studied correspond to the conditions on the Moon. Indeed, the temperatures at the illuminated part of the
Moon are high (about 400 K), and solar-wind protons are absorbed in particles of the lunar regolith resulting in some
chemical processes [A. Yu. Dubinskii and S. I. Popel, Water Formation in the Lunar Regolith, Cosmic Research, 2019, Vol.
57, No. 2, pp. 79-84]. Correspondingly, thermal properties of the lunar regolith at the Moon can be different from those under
the laboratory conditions on the Earth. Furthermore, charging of regolith particles (A. V. Zakharov, S. I. Popel, I. A.
Kuznetsov, N. D. Borisov, E. V. Rosenfeld, Yu. Skorov, and L. M. Zelenyi, Physical Processes Leading to Surface Erosion
and Dust Particles Dynamics of Airless Bodies, Physics of Plasmas 29, No. 11 (2022) 110501, 28 pages) due to the
influence of the photoelectric effect can affect the thermal properties of the lunar regolith at the Moon. This should be
mentioned in the paper. 
The authors have to take the above comments into account and to revise the manuscript. I am ready to consider the revised
version of the manuscript.
