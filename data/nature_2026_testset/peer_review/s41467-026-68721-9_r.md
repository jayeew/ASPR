Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors report a CuCl
₂
-based binary ionic thermoelectric (i-TE) system in which Cl
⁻
 ions are proposed to regulate the
coupling between interfacial redox reactions and bulk thermodiffusion, leading to a reported thermopower of −30.6 mV K
⁻
¹.
The work combines experimental thermopower characterization with molecular dynamics (MD) simulations to rationalize the
distribution of Cu–Cl–H
₂
O complexes. The topic is timely and relevant to the expanding field of ionic thermoelectrics, and
the data appear internally consistent. 
However, the mechanistic interpretation and novelty require substantial clarification. The reported transient current profiles,
limited thermogalvanic (TG) quantification, and incomplete benchmarking against the state of the art raise significant
questions about the claimed TG-dominated mechanism. 
Substantial revision, including direct quantification of the TG
contribution, clarified novelty, and improved benchmarking, would be necessary for to justify publication in this journal. 
1. Novelty and Relation to Previous Work: The claimed TG-in-TD mechanism is conceptually similar to prior reports: eg. Han
et al., Science 2020, 368, 1091–1098 and Zeng et al., Adv. Energy Mater. 2023, 13, 2204357. Both studies describe
synergistic thermodiffusion and thermogalvanic contributions producing large thermopower. Although the current materials
differ in composition (n-type), the mechanistic framework and observed behavior appear analogous. The authors should
clearly delineate what is genuinely new in this work—whether in chemical design, redox kinetics, or thermodynamic
coupling—and avoid implying a fundamentally new mechanism without quantitative differentiation. 
2. Transient versus Steady-State Current Behavior: The long-term current–voltage data (Supporting Fig. 34) display sharp
transient spikes that rapidly decay to near zero, rather than steady currents under ΔT. This response is typical of
thermodiffusion-dominated systems, not TG-driven ones. If a true thermogalvanic process were dominant, a sustained redox-
driven current would persist as long as the temperature gradient remains, as in the TG + TD system of Han et al. (Science
2020, 368, 1091–1098). The combination of transient decay and low open-circuit voltage relative to Cu²
⁺
/Cu
⁺
 redox
potentials weakens the mechanistic conclusion. The authors should provide quantitative TG validation e.g., steady-state
current monitoring, or equivalent circuit separation of ionic vs. redox contributions. 
3. Interpretation of “Simultaneous High Current and Voltage”: The claim of achieving both large current output and high
voltage is misleading. The CuCl
₂
-based gel cannot transport electrons internally; current appears only when an external
load provides an electronic pathway. The authors should correct this statement to specify that current flow arises from
external-circuit conduction, not from intrinsic electronic transport in the gel. 
4. Quantitative Estimation of TG Contribution: While the XPS results suggest Cu²
⁺
/Cu
⁺
 redox involvement, peak-area ratios
cannot be directly correlated with current density or Seebeck enhancement. Without quantitative estimation of the TG
fraction, the mechanistic claim remains speculative. Given the non-sustained current profile, the system appears
thermodiffusion-dominated with a limited or transient TG component. The authors should revisit the data interpretation and
possibly reframe the paper around TD-enhanced ionic thermopower rather than TG dominance. 
5. Comparison with the State of the Art: Figure 1f and Supporting Table 1 and 2 omit key recent references. For example, a
recent report in Energy & Environmental Science (Energy Environ. Sci., 2025,18, 1294-1309) achieved thermopower
exceeding −40 mV K
⁻
¹ at ΔT = 5.3 K. Accordingly, the statement “Binary PVA–CuCl
₂
 gels exhibit remarkable advantages in
thermopower and output performance compared to other reported multicomponent n-type i-TE systems” is overstated and
should be revised. Comprehensive benchmarking with the latest literature is required for an accurate contextual comparison.
6. Power Density Discrepancy: The reported power density of this work (binary PVA–CuCl
₂
 gel) 
is orders of magnitude
lower than comparable systems: For eg. 
Adv. Energy Mater. 2023 , 13, 2204357. The authors should discuss the physical
origin of this low performance—e.g., internal resistance, poor interfacial kinetics, or limited ionic–electronic coupling—and
provide quantitative reasoning for why the binary PVA–CuCl
₂
 gel performs so modestly despite the large thermopower.

speciation/transport-tuned balance between TD-like and redox contributions

Comment 1: 
The " competitive-coupling" mechanism proposed by the authors in the article is 
essentially a linear superposition of the Soret and thermogalvanic effects, weighted by 
ionic concentration. It may be more accurate to characterize this as a synergistic effect 
modulated by ion concentration. 
 
Response:  
We appreciate the reviewer’s valuable comment. While we agree with the reviewer 
that concentration is the tuning parameter, we need to emphasize that the underlying 
physics is a competition between two effects mediated by speciation, not a synergy.  
Therefore, w e proposed  this “competitive-coupling” i-TE mechanism to 
distinguish from the reported “synergistic-coupling” i-TE effect  [Science, 368, 
1091-1908, (2020)]. In this work, we did lots of v erifications on the competitive-
coupling relationship between the Soret effect and the thermogalvanic redox reaction 
of Cu 2+/Cu+ stabilized by Cl -, by combining operando-SHINERS, DFT calculations, 
and MD simulations.  
We first explain the competitive relationship by comparing with the p -type 
gelatin-KCl-FeCN4-/3- systems based on a synergistic mechanism [Science, 368, 
1091-1908, (2020)]. In gelatin-KCl-FeCN4-/3- i-TE gels, the KCl and FeCN 4-/3- were 
employed as thermodiffusion ions and thermogalvanic redox couples, respectively. 
Therefore, these two i-TE processes show a synergistic effect in parallel, i.e., one plus 
one equals two (Figure R1a). However, in our work based on PV A-CuCl2 gels, Cl⁻ 
acts both as the primary charge carrier for the Soret effect  and as an essential

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 6 -->

3 
 
ligand for the Cu2+/Cu+ redox couple in the thermogalvanic effect. Increasing the 
concentration does not merely "weight" two independent processes; it actively 
shifts the system's speciation, diverting Cl ⁻ from its role in thermodiffusion to its 
role in coordination and redox chemistry  (Figure R1b). Correspondingly, the i-TE 
performance data clearly demonstrate an antagonistic relationship : as the CuCl ₂ 
concentration increases, the thermogalvanic effect is enhanced (leading to higher output 
short-circuit current, Isc), but this occurs  at the direct ex pense of the thermodiffusion 
effect (leading to a decrease in open -circuit thermal voltage, Voc) (Figure R1c). This 
opposite trend between the thermal voltage and output current is unusual, 
compared with the reported p-type gelatin-KCl-FeCN4-/3- systems. In gelatin-KCl-
FeCN4-/3- cells based on “synergistic-coupling” i-TE effect, higher thermal voltage 
results in a higher output current and power. 
Next, we want to illustrate the understanding of “coupling”. Although the 
competitive relationship, thermopower values of the two i-TE effects could also be 
a numerical superposition. We used an isothermal three-electrode system to determine 
the temperature coefficient (αR) of PV A-x CuCl2 gels, which showed a positive value , 
indicating the n-type thermogalvanic property of Cu2+/Cu+ redox couple. Therefore, a 
negative thermodiffusive thermopower and a positive temperature coefficient 
(equal to negative thermogalvanic thermopower) could be numerically added. The 
competitive-coupling” effect leads to a non-monotonic trend in thermopower Si: at low 
concentration (0.01 M ≤ x ≤ 0.07 M),  the thermopower increased from -19.5 to -28.7 
mV K -1; at intermediate  concentration (0.1  to 0.7 M),  the thermopower  achieved a 
maximum value of -30.6 mV K-1 at x = 0.1 M and then gradually decreased; and at high 
concentration ( x ≥ 1M), suppressed thermodiffusion and dominant thermogalvanic 
effects collapse the thermopower to -5.9 mV K-1 (x = 2.0 M) (Figure R1d). These two 
parts' contributions show a competitive relationship but could be combined. 
As a result, we called this mechanism “competitive-coupling”, in which the 
two i-TE effects show a competitive relationship, but the values of each part could 
be numerically superposition. This new coupling effect indicates a more complex 
competitive relationship between thermodissusion and thermogalvanic  effects
