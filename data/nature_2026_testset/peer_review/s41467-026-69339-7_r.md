Responses to the Reviewer 1: 
1. Why did authors use Poly IC LMW instead of HMW? 
Response: 
We sincerely appreciate the questions raised by the reviewer regarding the selection of 
the Poly(I:C) formulation. Poly(I:C) LMW is a low molecular weight poly(cytidine) 
acid, while Poly(I:C) HMW is a high molecular weight poly(cytidine) acid. The 
difference between the two lies in the length and molecular weight of their double-
stranded RNA. The reasons for our selection of Poly(I:C) LMW (low molecular weight) 
in this study are as follows: (1) In this study, we transfected cells with DNA sensor 
cGAS/STING plasmid, RNA sensor RIG-I plasmid, and CD38 plasmid, each with 
different expression levels, and detected the luciferase reporter gene driven by the IFN-
β promoter. We found that CD38 specifically inhibits RNA sensor RIG-I–mediated type 
I IFN activation in a dose-dependent manner, whereas CD38 does not participate in the 
activation of type I IFN mediated by the DNA sensor cGAS/STING. Previous research 
has shown that Poly(I:C) acts on Toll-like receptor 3 (TLR3) and RIG-I-like receptor 
(RLR) (such as RIG-I and MDA5), transmitting signals through mitochondrial antiviral 
signaling proteins (MAVS) that can induce NFκB and IRF3 transcription factors, which 
in turn upregulate the production of type I interferon (IFN-I). Poly(I:C) LMW is more 
inclined to activate RIG-I, while Poly(I:C) HMW is more inclined to activate MDA5

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 5 -->

(PMID: 33892139). Therefore, we chose Poly(I:C) LMW for the experiment. (2) In 
addition, we consulted relevant literature and found that Jin S et al. used Poly(I:C) 
LMW to activate the type I IFN signaling pathway in their study published in Mol Cell 
in October 2017 (PMID: 28965816). Similar findings were reported in other studies 
(PMID: 24366338, PMID: 34016972, PMID: 33490069).

4. The 48-hour killing assay is problematic. Cytotoxicity typically occurs within 
hours, and the assay duration should be adjusted accordingly. 
Response: 
We sincerely thank the reviewers for their valuable suggestions. In response to 
your question regarding the rationality of the 48-hour duration for the killing 
experiment, we conducted gradient time point experiments (6h, 12h, 24h, 48h) using 
human primary CD8 + T cells co-cultured with nasopharyngeal carcinoma cells at 
various effector-to-target ratios (no T cells, 5:1, 10:1). Using crystal violet staining 
(quantitative ratio of live cells) and CellTiter-Glo assay (detection of metabolic activity), 
we found that the cytotoxic effect of CD8+ T cells was most pronounced after 48 hours 
of co-culture (Figure R2.2A–2C). Additionally, flow cytometry analysis of PI + dead 
tumor cells in co-cultures incubated for 24 hours and 48 hours further confirmed that 
the killing effect was more pronounced at the 48-hour time point (Figure R2.2D) . 
Moreover, our literature review revealed that several studies, including those published 
in Cell (PMID: 37548590) and Cancer Cell (PMID: 35216678), also used a 48-hour 
co-culture duration to evaluate T cell cytotoxicity. Based on these findings and our 
experimental data, we selected the 48-hour time point for our killing assays.  The 
relevant content has been added to the Results section titled "Tumor-intrinsic 
CD38 inhibits CD8 + T cell-mediated anti-tumor immunity," and the results are 
presented in Supplementary Figure S2A-S2C. Corresponding figure legends have 
also been updated.
