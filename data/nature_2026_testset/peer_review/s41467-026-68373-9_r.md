Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Xu et al. developed a core-shell structured probe for detecting free-phase organic contaminants (FPOCs) in groundwater.
This probe consists of a carbon black core stabilized by two organic ligands, in which Nile Red (NR) was incorporated as a
reporter. Concentrations of FPOCs exhibited a linear relationship with the loss of NR reporter in groundwater, which has
been validated using laboratory-based column experiments and field demonstrations. This study was well conceived and
conducted. Therefore, I think this manuscript can be accepted for publication by Nature Communications after minor
revision. 
Specific comments: 
1. The role of the molecule ACVA should be better described. How do ACVA, PVA, and NR bind to the carbon black core? 
2. In line 142, fCB only lost 3% weight within a temperature between 300-500 ◦C. Why does fCB possess such great thermal
stability? 
3. Typo: Line 159: “report” should be “reporter”. 
4. For a specific contaminated site with an unknown FPOC concentration, how to determine the dose of nano probes? 
5. Will different types of FPOCs affect the quantitative performance of this method? 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The study builds on a long series of chemical probes designed to detect subsurface conditions as they flow through an
aquifer. 
The strongest evidence that the probes can be used to detect free phase organic contaminants comes from the
small-scale micro models and column experiments conducted with sand, glass, etc. 
These idealized materials and
geometries were presumably carefully prepared to contain simple or convenient FPOC compounds. 
The results are
suggestive but do not support the interpretation that these probes can provide accurate estimates of free phase contaminants
under field conditions. 
The results from the field experiment do not support the author's conclusion that the probes "underline
the effectiveness and robustness of the nano reporter for practical application under complex geological settings". 
Not sure
why the Field-scale demonstration" section is in blue font ? 
Since the quantity of FPOC in the subsurface sediments at the
field site are unknown or at least only approximately known with large uncertainty, the summary in Figure 6 e is misleading. 
I
am doubtful if the "actual FPOC contents" from coring at a few locations actually represents the "actual FPOC content"
across the site or a long groundwater flow paths. 
The results are interesting and the paper should be published but the description of the results of the field experiment should
be more realistic and accurate
