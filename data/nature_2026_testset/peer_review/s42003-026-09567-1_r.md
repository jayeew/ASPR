Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript by Guo and coworkers investigates the role of High-mobiliy group box-containing protein 1, HBP1 in
endometrial stroma differentiation. They demonstrate that HPB1 is differentially expressed in the human endometrium during
the menstrual cycle and down regulated in recurrent pregnancy failure patient endometrium. Knockdown and transcriptomic
analysis demonstrates that HPB1 is critical for decidualization in vitro by regulating the transcription of several genes,
especially progesterone responsive genes while not impacting the level of expression of the progesterone receptor (PGR).
They also show that FOXO1 signaling is altered and AKT phosphorylation is increased. This study identifies a new regulator
of PGR action and uterine stroma decidualization that may have clinical implications. This proposal must address several
issues. 
1. Although PGR target genes are altered with out altering PGR expression , does HBP1 regulate the occupancy of PGR at
target gene enhancers. This is especially true since FOXO1 signaling is altered and it has been shown to regulate PGR
occupancy. 
2. Since pAKT is altered does it regulate FOXO1 nuclear localization which would impact PGR signaling. Also in Figure 3B
the LY294002 
alone 
control is missing. The decrease in pAkt observed with the inhibitor and HPB1 knockdown may be
independent. 
3. To give a full picture of the role of HPB1 in regulating the transcription of PGR, an overlap of the DEGs with HPB1
knockdown 
and Cistromic analysis of HPB1 should be compared to the published data of 
similar analysis of PGR and
FOXO1. This would give a complete analysis of the PGR dependent and independent impact of HPB1. 
Finally the references are a total mess. This manuscript site the wrong papers and in fact some of the citations have incorrect
authors with the journal article. This should be completely redone. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript identifies HBP1 as a critical regulator of human endometrial stromal cell (HESC) decidualization, a key
process for embryo implantation and successful pregnancy. Knockdown of HBP1 significantly reduces the expression of
decidualization markers IGFBP1 and FOXO1, despite unchanged PGR (progesterone receptor) expression. However, PGR
target genes (e.g., FKBP5, FOSL2, FKBP4) are downregulated, suggesting impaired PGR activity. Through ChIP-Seq and
RNA-Seq, the authors show that HBP1 directly regulates IGFBP1 transcription and enhances PGR transcriptional activity by
promoting H3K4me3 enrichment at target gene promoters. Importantly, clinical samples from patients with recurrent
implantation failure (RIF) show reduced HBP1 and PGR target gene expression, linking HBP1 deficiency to impaired
endometrial function. 
However, there are some issues that need to be addressed before acceptance. 
1. The more official name of HBP1 is high mobility group box transcription factor 1. The author may think about changing it in
the manuscript. 
2. It's better to provide the negative control for the IHC results.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript presents a comprehensive study that identifies HBP1 as a novel and critical regulator of human endometrial
stromal cell (HESC) decidualization. By integrating transcriptomic and epigenetic data, the authors provide strong evidence
that HBP1 not only directly regulates IGFBP1 expression but also enhances progesterone receptor (PGR) transcriptional
activity via H3K4me3 histone modification. The findings are further corroborated by clinical data from patients with recurrent
implantation failure (RIF), adding translational relevance. This is a well-designed and significant study that provides novel
insight into the transcriptional and epigenetic regulation of human endometrial decidualization via HBP1. The linkage to
clinical outcomes such as RIF further underscores the translational value. 
Although PGR levels remain unchanged, the study claims enhanced PGR activity. A luciferase-based PGR response
element (PRE) assay would provide direct evidence of HBP1’s effect on PGR-mediated transcription. 
The authors should include the RNA-seq data file (e.g., as an Excel or supplementary file). I found it difficult to access the
genomic data using the information provided in the manuscript's Data availability section (line 456). 
Maintain consistent use of gene/protein nomenclature (e.g., FRBP5 is a likely typo for FKBP5) (line 274, 277).

Conclusions 
PGR regulates the expression of HBP1, while HBP1 does not affect the 
transcription of PGR, but can improve its transcriptional activity, increase the 
occupancy of PGR on the enhancer of target genes (FOXO1, HOXA10), and 
enhance its transcription. 
HBP1 affects FOXO1 transcription, protein expression, and subcellular 
localization. However, the current data analysis does not show that the two 
factors have a direct effect, and whether there may be indirect regulation or the 
influence of cofactors needs to be further study.
