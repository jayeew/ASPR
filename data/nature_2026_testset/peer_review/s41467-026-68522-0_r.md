Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this study the authors have tested if the addition of low-intensity vibrations (LIV) to zoledronic acid would prevent the bone
and muscle loss and adiposity that comes with complete estrogen deprivation. This is clinically relevant considering how
common complete estrogen deprivation is in women undergoing breast cancer treatment. 
In young estrogen deprived mice not treated with zoledronic acid there seems to be a potent effect of LIV to improve bone,
adipose and muscle parameters. In older mice however this effect is blunted and the addition of LIV to zoledronic acid did
not significantly improve any or few of the measured parameters. However, many of the diagrams and tables are to tiny or
blurry to interpret and the statistics is not clearly described, se comments below. 
The techniques are relevant and improving the presentation of the data would make it easier for the reader to interpret the
importance of the results. 
Specific comments:

the authors and a more reliable indicator of E2-depletion would be uterus weight, please provide if possible.

-The number of animals (n) 
-The statistics used (if for example 2-way ANOVA, specify the factors tested and between which groups post-hoc test has
been done). 
-Make graphs larger where needed, for example figure 2c and explain where the p-value comes from, is it comparison
between the two groups at a certain time-point or something else? 
-Remove all personal comments on whether the data is increased or decreased especially if this is not supported by a
significant difference and even if it is, I think these type of comments should be kept to a minimum in the figure legends. 
3.In the text it says that the p-value for figure 1A is 0.047 but in table 2 it says 0.048? 
4. Please refer to a figure or table when data is mentioned in the text or write data not shown (if this is accepted by the
journal otherwise remove these sentences and only report data that is presented). 
5. The scores plot and the heat map in supplemental figure 1 and 2 is identical?

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This research builds upon a large mass of already previously published data showing that Low Magnitude Mechanical
Signals (LIV) improve bone and muscle including in osteoporosis caused by low estrogen
(https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8734256/). This hypothesis is embedded to the extent that LIV is offered as a
standard of care treatment to postmenopausal women to prevent bone loss and muscle weakness. The first part of the
current study, shown in figure 1 confirms these previous data. 
It is also well established that zoledronic acid inhibits osteoporosis induced by loss of estrogen in humans and mouse
models, therefore the hypothesis tested in the current study that combining zoledronic acid with LIV may add further benefit
does warrant testing. Unfortunately, however the data presented do not appear to support the title of the manuscript. Authors
state that LIV combined with zoledronic acid reduce musculoskeletal weakness and adiposity in estrogen deprived mice,
whilst this is not untrue the data presented show that giving zoledronic acid alone to estrogen deprived mice has the same
effect as giving zoledronic acid and LIV. Given that the majority of osteoporotic patients will receive an anti-resorptive drug
such as zoledronic acid as standard of care, this study suggests that there is no need to add LIV into their treatment
schedule. I am therefore unsure how the data presented will lead to improved outcome for patients. For example: 
Figure 2 shows no difference in BMD, microarchitecture, mechanical or structural properties between estragen depleted
mice treated with Zoledronic acid and those treated with zoledronic acid and LIV 
Figure 3 suggest no additional benefit of adding zoledronic acid to LIV. Figure 3B is impossible to interpret as there is no
indication as to which groups are significant compared with which other groups. It would help to provide more clarity around
how these analyses were performed and what the starts pertain to given that the lines appear to overlap. 
Figure 4 shows no additional benefit from adding zoledronic acid to LIV on fat metabolism and 4A has no legend making it
impossible to interpret. 
There are some experimental issues that the authors should consider: 
C57BL/6 mice gain bone mass much more rapidly following zoledronic acid treatment than other models. To ensure that
data generated are not model specific experiments should be be performed in more than one model system, ideally in a
second model from a different genetic background. 
Authors should provide ELISA data showing estradiol levels in these mice to provide confidence in the model system and
enable readers to interpret the data in relation to estardiol levels observed in women who have recieved endocrine
treatments. 
The way statistical significance is indicated in the various graphs are very difficult to interpret, please provide more detail in
the figure legends.
