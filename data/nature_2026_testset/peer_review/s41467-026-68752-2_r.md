Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
F-box helicase 1 (FBH1) contains two functions: it is the E3 ubiquitin ligase subunit of a SCF complex, whose activity
reduces RAD51 association with chromatin, and it is a DNA helicase that promotes fork reversal. Greer and colleagues
demonstrate that SCF(FBH1) engages the lagging strand template preferentially and this function stimulates the DNA
translocase activity required for fork reversal. They present the cryo-EM structure of SCF(FBH1) on a three-way DNA fork
and describe the interaction with the fork junction. Amino acid changes altering fork junction engagement impair fork
reversal in a test tube and replication in cells. The authors derive a parental DNA annealing mechanism different from other
known fork remodellers. In addition, a translocation and ubiquitination model for RAD51 disassembly implies that RAD51 at
replication fork engages the lagging strand. 
This is a thorough and insightful study that addresses the molecular mechanism of an important yet poorly understood
protein. In my opinion, the work deserves to be published in Nature Communications after addressing the issues listed
below. 
1. The authors state at the start of the results section that all experiments were conducted using the intact SCF(FBH1)
complex. Nonetheless, throughout the text and in the figures FBH1 and SCF(FBH1) appear to be used interchangeably. It is
important to clarify whether some experiments were conducted with FBH1 alone or else check the entire manuscript for
consistency. 
2. Depiction of the 3D variability analysis results should be improved. Currently, only overlapping volumes are shown to
depict the movement of the CUL1/BRX1head and the CUL1 neck. There is no color coding of the various subunits which
makes immediate interpretation challenging. Also, overlapping volumes do not allow evaluating map quality. It seems
appropriate to include a molecular movie to depict the structural flexibility, in addition to the current panels shown in Figure
S6. Furthermore, I am curious to ask whether similar movements can be observed in 2D averages? Such analysis could be
performed by aligning 2D averages via the CUL1/RBX1 head. Including these data would help validate the 3D variability
analysis results, which is notoriously a challenging task. 
3. The authors state that the major and minor grooves could be inferred from an alternating pattern of bulges and
indentations in each cylinder density. I must admit I recognise these features. Could the authors include a supplementary
panel, perhaps showing the DNA density at different threshold to make major and minor grooves more evident?
Alternatively, could the authors attempt postprocessing the map using EMReady, which might help improve DNA density? 
4. The authors find that a FBH1 variant targeting two Zn-coordinating cysteines has a detrimental effect for both helicase as
well as ATP hydrolysis function. The authors suggest that this might reflect a dependency on the zinc ribbon for the
mechanical motion of the helicase core. Can the authors explain why they do not entertain the possibility that disrupting the
ZnF could instead be negatively effect global structure stability? 
5. The authors should better explain the rationale for the design of the DNA substrate used for cryo-EM studies and how it
differs from the substrate used for functional assays. 
6. Line 305 – “1A” should be “2A”.

(Remarks to the Author) 
Summary 
Greer et al. present a comprehensive biochemical, single-molecule, and structural analysis of FBH1, an F-box superfamily 1
helicase that acts within the SCF^FBH1 ubiquitin ligase complex. Building on prior genetic and cell-based studies that
identified FBH1 as a fork remodeler and anti-recombinase, the authors delineate how SCF^FBH1 recognizes and remodels
stalled replication forks and how its architecture is suited to regulate RAD51 filaments. 
Using purified SCF^FBH1, they show that the complex preferentially binds replication fork structures via the lagging-strand
template, which strongly stimulates helicase activity and is essential for fork reversal in vitro. Single-molecule magnetic
tweezers reveal that FBH1 is a processive 3′–5′ translocase on ssDNA and can unwind duplex DNA but lacks intrinsic
annealing activity, implying that fork reversal is driven primarily by ssDNA translocation rather than classical branch-
migration mechanisms. 
The centerpiece of the work is a high-resolution cryo-EM structure of SCF^FBH1 bound to a three-way fork DNA substrate,
which reveals an intimate interface between the FBH1 helicase core and the fork junction. Mutational analysis identifies a
“junction binding” surface within subdomain 1B (notably R447/K448) that is crucial for fork reversal but has only modest
effects on helicase and ssDNA translocation activities. Complementary DNA combing experiments show that these junction-
binding mutations impair FBH1-dependent fork slowing in cells, linking the structural and biochemical findings to replication
dynamics. 
Finally, by modeling the SCF^FBH1 structure together with a known CRL1^β-TRCP/IκBα E3–substrate complex, the
authors propose a coordinated model for RAD51 filament disassembly, in which FBH1-driven translocation along the
lagging strand positions the E2 Ub–RBX1 catalytic module to ubiquitylate RAD51, thereby limiting RAD51’s chromatin
association during replication stress. 
Overall, the study provides a compelling mechanistic framework for how FBH1 integrates fork reversal with RAD51
regulation. 
This is an excellent and timely contribution that will be of broad interest to the DNA replication, repair, and genome stability
communities. The work is technically impressive, conceptually clear, and builds a coherent bridge between previous cell-
based observations and a detailed mechanistic picture of SCF^FBH1 function. The manuscript is well within the scope and
quality expectations of the journal. 
Points for clarification / minor suggestions 
These are mostly clarifications or small textual refinements; I do not view them as prerequisites for publication. 
1. Clarify the status of the RAD51 lagging-strand model 
The structural modeling strongly implies that RAD51 filaments at stalled forks are configured on the lagging strand, and that
this geometry is optimal for SCF^FBH1-mediated ubiquitylation. 
It would be helpful if the authors could more explicitly distinguish in the text between what is directly demonstrated here (the
position of the fork DNA, the SCF architecture, and proximity of the E2 Ub module to the lagging arm), and what is more
inferred or hypothesized (preferential lagging-strand association of RAD51 filaments in cells). 
A brief sentence in the Results or Discussion clearly labeling the lagging-strand RAD51 placement as a
structural/mechanistic model (albeit consistent with existing data) would improve interpretability. 
2. Isoform choice and physiological context 
All experiments use FBH1 isoform 4, which lacks the N-terminal 125 residues of isoform 1, with the rationale that helicase
activity is equivalent. 
A short clarification on: 
1) whether isoform 4 is a major physiological isoform, and 
2) whether any known regulatory motifs (e.g., regulatory phosphorylation sites) reside in the missing N-terminal segment, 
would reassure readers that the mechanistic insights are broadly representative of full-length FBH1. 
3. Wording regarding mechanistic differences from SNF2 remodelers 
The manuscript nicely contrasts FBH1 with HLTF. 
To reviewers’ understanding, RAD51 mediated strand invasion before
fork remodelers to form a new junction behind CMG to accommodate fork reversal enzymes (Liu and Cortez 2023 Science),
the model fits the SNF2 family very well. In the case of FBH1, it seems FBH1 helicase activity can unwind the lagging strand
to form a flap. This flap invades the leading strand to initiate fork reversal. If authors could comment on how FBH1 differs in
reconciling RAD51 requirements, that would be very helpful.

On lines 108-109, the reviewer does not understand how “immediately” is used.

Please explain what the different line colors mean in figure S3G. 
In summary, this is a strong, carefully executed study that provides important mechanistic insight into FBH1-mediated fork
reversal and RAD51 regulation, and nicely rationalizes prior cellular observations at a structural and single-molecule level. I
