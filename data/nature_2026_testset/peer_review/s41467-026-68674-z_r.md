(Remarks to the Author) 
Li, Tang, Yu and coworkers present a construction of N-N bond via photoredox nitrene transfer of a bespoke sulfilimine.
Even though sulfilimines have been exploited as a nitrene source (Ref. 36-38), the acceptors of nitrenes are primarily
restricted to unsaturated systems or intramolecular reaction mode. In the manuscript, the authors develop a novel
intermolecular nitrene transfer reaction which allows the formation of hydrazine bearing N-N bond while overcoming the
generation of urea via homodimerization of nitrenes. For the scope of nitrene acceptors (Figure 2), the chemoselectivity is
generally modest to poor, but much improved chemoselectivity has been observed while the nitrene donors have been
investigated (Figure 3). The synthetic utility of this transformation was showcased in natural products modification and
downstream derivatization. A combined experimental and computational study of mechanism was conducted to shed light
on the details of the reaction mechanism. In general, even though hydrazine derivatives have been prepared via nitrene
transfer of amines (Nat. Chem. 2021, 13, 378 (ref. 19); ACIE, 2025, doi.org/10.1002/anie.202509056; Org. Lett. 2023,25, 36,
6671-6676; 2025,27.7,1626-1630) the novelty and significance of this work can warrant its acceptancy by Nat. Comm., but
the following issues should be addressed first: 
1. In the optimization part, control experiments should be provided, such as standard conditions without light, or under the
atmosphere of air instead of N2. 
2. The phororedox protocol is very sensitive to the setup, so the authors should provide the details of their setup, ideally with
a photo. 
3. Why the chemoselectivity indicated in Figure 2 or 3 cannot be accurately determined? It is weird to see something like “>
3.5 : 1”. 
4. For the mechanism part, Figure 4g, since the reaction was performed in deuterium solvent, where is the source of
hydrogen? In another word, what part of 1a-1 was deteriorate? This information is particularly important to support the
mechanistic proposal by DFT, perhaps 31a-1? 
5. From the computational study (Figure 5A), the carbonyl group on nitrogen of 1a-1 is very important, in agreement of the
previous report (J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2023, 145, 6310). Did the authors try DBT sulfilimine bearing a non-coordinating group
on nitrogen, for example, phenyl? Is the chemistry still proceeding smoothly? 
6. Three very related papers (ACIE, 2025, doi.org/10.1002/anie.202509056; Org. Lett. 2023,25, 36, 6671-6676;
2025,27.7,1626-1630) should be cited in the manuscript. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The paper reports a photo-selective N–N bond formation protocol employing sulfilimines as nitrene precursors, providing
access to a range of hydrazide derivatives. This approach demonstrates commendable control over reaction selectivity
through structural modification of the sulfilimine scaffold. The method shows broad functional-group tolerance. However,
while the authors present their approach as highly novel, they appear to overstate the significance of their contribution,
particularly in comparison to existing N–N bond-forming methodologies (see references 17–20). These prior works already
provide powerful and versatile solutions for N–N bond construction, and this paper should more carefully contextualize the
current method within this established landscape. Furthermore, although the authors emphasize the use of structurally
modified sulfilimines, it should be noted that these species have already been employed in related C–N bond-forming
reactions (e.g., Chem. Eur. J. 2020, 26, 8235; Nat. Chem. 2022, 14, 898–904). The authors are encouraged to acknowledge
this prior work more explicitly. Overall, the framing of the work does not convincingly demonstrate the level of originality or

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 2 -->

urgency. The discussion of N–N bond formation is limited to energy configuration data, which is unsatisfying. Additionally,
the selectivity with respect to urea byproducts appears rather poor. In its current form, the manuscript falls short of the
standards for Nature Communications. 
Questions and concerns. 
-in Figure 2, it is unclear whether primary (aryl) amines participate in the reaction. If reactivity is restricted to secondary
amines, the mechanistic basis should be discussed. 
-In Page 8, evidence for intermolecular hydrogen bonding should be provided. In DFT studies, this could be supported by
bond distances from optimized structures or NCI plots. Without such evidence, it is questionable for hydrogen bonding. 
-In Figure 4j: The Stern–Volmer plot appears cropped. The slope and linearity should be clearly displayed. Data related to
Stern–Volmer quenching studies and CV measurements are missing in SI and should be included. 
-In Figure 1b, the description of the N–N bond as weak and nonpolar is unclear with respect to hydrazides. To substantiate
this statement, bond dissociation energies of hydrazide N–N bonds should be provided. 
-In Figure 3, it would be informative to test sulfilimines with unencumbered, longer aliphatic chains to determine whether the
reaction proceeds in the absence of steric protection. 
-Organic azides are known to undergo photodecomposition to triplet nitrenes (J. Am. Chem. Soc. 1965, 87, 1947; 87, 5491).
The authors should examine whether their protocol also operates with azides or other precursors. If so, the limitation posed
by equivalent recovery of dibenzothiophene as waste should be acknowledged. 
-Sulfilimine 1a-1 exhibits a high triplet energy (61.6 kcal/mol, Fig. 5) yet delivers 32% yield under blue LED irradiation
without catalyst (Table 1, entry 14). However, no absorbance is observed near this wavelength (Fig. 4i), and the UV–vis
baseline appears incorrectly measured. A clear rationale should be provided. UV–vis data for both 1a-16 and 1a-1 are
necessary. 
-The proposed HAT pathway via triplet nitrene should be further validated. In particular, sulfilimines containing cyclopropane
substituents should be tested for ring-opened olefin products. Additionally, experiments with added triplet sensitizers under
blue LEDs could help correlate yields with sensitizer triplet energies. 
-In Figure 5a, the nucleophilic attack step is incorrectly placed; the amine should add only after the nitrene state 1csB1. 
-In Figure 5a, for the 1CSE1 intermediate, evidence of its detection by crude NMR or LC–MS would strengthen the
mechanistic proposal. 
-In ON/OFF light experiment, product yield continues to increase (~5%) in the dark phase (4–8 h). A plausible explanation or
additional controls are needed. 
- Many spectra are of poor quality. Impurities appear to have been obscured by decreasing peak intensities, which
compromises reproducibility. High-quality spectra from purified samples should be provided. Numerous typographical errors
and solvent misannotations (e.g., Fig. S96) are present. The SI should be carefully proofread and corrected. 
Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript by Tang and Li et al. discloses an N–N bond formation via photoinduced generation of nitrenes from
sulfilimine precursors. The acylnitrenes were produced under 365 nm LED irradiation from sulfilimines bearing a
dibenzo[b,d]thiophene (DBT) moiety, which showed moderate to good selectivity for N–N formation with aromatic and
aliphatic amine coupling partners. The mechanism of this reaction was probed using experimental and computational
methods and shown to proceed at least partially through radical intermediates. I will focus my comments primarily on the
computational investigations. 
- In Figure 5, the singlet (Fig 5a) and triplet (Fig 5b) pathways appear to be calculated at two completely different levels of
theory. This is very confusing and contradicts the computational methods section in the Supporting Information. The authors
should clarify what level of theory was actually used. 
- In addition, Figure 5’s caption also implies that the singlet pathway (Fig 5a) showes the free energies while the triplet
pathway (Fig 5b) shows the electronic energies. This again contradicts the figure itself, which shows G (Gibbs free energy)
on both Fig 5a and Fig 5b. What energies are these two diagrams actually showing? This is very important to clarify before
any conclusions about the mechanism can be drawn. 
- Furthermore, if the mechanism in Fig 5a is indeed calculated at the B3LYP/def2-SVP level of theory (as stated in the
caption), that would imply that these energies are not calculated with a triple-zeta quality basis set or with the incorporation

of dispersion effects. DFT energies should generally be calculated with triple-zeta basis sets and with at least empirical
dispersion incorporated. 
- While the computational methods section in the Supporting Information states that empirical dispersion was included, it is
not clear how dispersion was included, as neither B3LYP nor BP86 includes dispersion. Please clarify how dispersion was
actually included in the calculations. 
- In the SI, imaginary frequencies should be provided for transition states. 
Reviewer #4 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Selective N-N bond construction remains a significant challenge in organic synthesis. Li, Tang, Yu, and their coworkers
have reported a robust and efficient method for synthesizing substituted hydrazines via a photochemical nitrene strategy.
Specifically, they employed a sulfilimine-based photoinduced approach to achieve controlled nitrene release and
intermolecular N-N coupling under metal-free conditions. This method enables precise tuning of nitrene reactivity through
structural modification of sulfilimines, which effectively suppresses the side reactions associated with free nitrenes. This
transformation demonstrates broad functional group compatibility that overcomes the substrate limitations of conventional
metal-catalyzed and photochemical methods. Additionally, it addresses critical gaps in nitrene chemistry by integrating
photoactivation and controlled nitrene transfer, thereby expanding the synthetic toolkit for constructing nitrogen-rich
molecular scaffolds. 
Based on its scientific merit, I recommend this work for publication in Nature Communications pending the resolution of the
following minor revisions: 
Manuscript corrections: 
1 In the condition table (Entries 1–6), chlorinated solvents are listed prior to other solvents. The authors should clarify
whether chlorine-containing additives contribute to improved yield and selectivity of the reaction. 
2 In the condition table (Entries 10–12), only trace amounts of the target product are obtained. The authors should specify if
any side products were observed (e.g., products derived from nitrene insertion into the sulfur atom of DMSO) and discuss
their formation. 
3 For the amine substrate scope: Even if certain amine types are incompatible, the authors should discuss and present data
for a broader range of amines (e.g., primary amines and diarylamines) to provide a more comprehensive assessment of
substrate applicability. 
4 The substrate scope of sulfonyl nitrene precursors is also critical, yet only four examples are reported in the manuscript. It
is recommended that the authors investigate and include additional types of sulfonyl nitrene precursors to validate the
generality of the method. 
5 Further mechanistic studies are needed to clarify the reaction mechanism. For instance, internal alkenes should be added
as reactants and tested with the sulfilimine nitrene precursor under standard reaction conditions to probe potential nitrene
trapping pathways. 
Supporting Information Corrections: 
1 In the "General Information" section, the description of the "commercially available LED light (50 W, 365 nm)" must be
supplemented with the manufacturer’s name and product model for reproducibility. 
2 In Figure S189 (¹H NMR spectrum of compound 53), some peaks are not labeled, and the peak at 1.25 ppm is incorrectly
assigned. These issues should be corrected. 
3 Several ¹H NMR spectra exhibit non-standard formatting: In Figures S223, S219, and S215, the integral values for key
peaks are missing and should be added to ensure data completeness.
