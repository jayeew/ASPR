Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript reports measurements of microwave-irradiated Al/WTe
₂
 Josephson junctions and claims that the missing
first Shapiro step—often regarded as evidence of 4π-periodic supercurrents induced by Majorana bound states—can arise
from intrinsic non-linearity of the current–voltage characteristics in low-to-moderate transparency junctions. 
To support this claim, the authors introduce a modified resistively and capacitively shunted junction model (NRCSJ)
incorporating a voltage-dependent normal resistance, and they show that the model reproduces several experimental
observations, including the suppression of the first Shapiro step and zigzag transition lines at intermediate frequencies. 
The topic is of interest for the Josephson and topological superconductivity communities, and the work addresses an
ongoing controversy concerning the interpretation of missing Shapiro steps. However, in my view, the current manuscript
lacks the quantitative rigor and transparency required for publication in Communication Physics. The overall impact is
moderate, and several critical points in both the data analysis and theoretical justification remain unclear. 
[Major Comments] 
1. Small-β Regime Claim and Lack of Experimental Justification: The central claim of the paper is that the missing first
Shapiro step and switching jumps can occur even in the small-β (overdamped) regime, due to intrinsic non-linearity of the
junction. However, β = 0.59 is not experimentally extracted—it appears only as a theoretical fitting parameter in Fig. 3d. The
authors provide no independent estimation of capacitance or hysteresis-based analysis to justify that the actual junction
indeed lies in the overdamped regime. Given that β = 2.0 reproduces the same data within the conventional RCSJ model,
the small-β conclusion currently rests only on a fitting assumption. Quantitative evidence such as I–V hysteresis comparison,
retrapping vs. switching current, or capacitance estimation would be essential to substantiate this key claim. 
2. Inconsistency for β between Main Text and Supplementary Simulations: The main text emphasizes that the NRCSJ model
with β ≈ 0.6 reproduces all key features, including the zigzag transitions. However, according to the Supplementary Material,
several theoretical spectra of Fig.S4, which is one of the main result of this manuscript supporting the frequency-depedent
zigzag IVC, were generated using β = 2, which is inconsistent with the “small-β regime” narrative. This discrepancy
undermines the main conclusion and raises doubts about whether the claimed effects indeed persist in the overdamped
limit. 
A consistent set of parameters across main and supplementary simulations must be provided. 
3. Physical Justification of the Nonlinear Normal Current: The proposed nonlinear normal current reproduces the
experimental I–V curves well, but its physical justification is weak. The authors refer to single-channel BdG calculations of
SNS junctions to motivate this nonlinearity. Yet, their actual devices are clearly multi-channel (width ≈ 1 µm, R
∼
65Ω), and
the corresponding transport regime is far from the single-channel limit which introduced in their physical validation. The
analogy therefore remains phenomenological rather than microscopic. A more quantitative discussion—e.g., comparison
with multi-mode BTK or Usadel-type models, or an experimental extraction of the nonlinear term under suppressed
superconductivity—would be necessary to claim genuine physical justification. 
4. Missing Parameter: α
₁
 in Fig.3d is not specified in the text, figure captions, or Supplementary Notes. Without these
numbers, the NRCSJ fits in Fig. 3d cannot be independently reproduced.

Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The article presents experimental results on microwave irradiation of an Al/WTe2-based SNS-type Josephson junction,
along with an analysis of the obtained results using a modified RSCJ model. I would highlight two key findings: the absence
of the first Shapiro step at low RF radiation frequencies, and the presence of a zigzag structure at intermediate frequencies.
The first result is not novel, having been observed in several works cited by the authors. The second result appears to be a
novel observation to my knowledge. 
After reading the article, I have several questions about the phenomenological model used. The authors added two new
factors to the RSCJ model. How critical is including the i_e(v) term for obtaining the zigzag-like result? Could it be sufficient
to account only for the voltage-dependent resistance? I believe the authors should add a discussion of this issue in the
Supplementary Material, accompanied by figures for VIC and the impact of microwave irradiation both with and without the
i_e(v) term (particularly at frequencies where the model produces zigzag-like structures). In my view, the zigzag structure
represents the most significant and interesting finding in this work. Identifying the factors influencing its emergence — even
within a phenomenological model — is crucial. 
Based on the authors' explanation on page 9, accounting for R(V) results in a steeper IV-curve (dV/dI) near Ic at beta~1. The
absence of the first Shapiro step in this case is a natural consequence, as discussed in Ref. [16], where a large derivative
dV/dI near Ic was achieved by other means – either through appropriate selection of beta or heating, while simultaneously
ensuring the absence (or minimal magnitude) of hysteresis. The proposed model introduces an additional parameter that
controls the steepness of the IV-characteristic, but leads to a qualitatively similar result: the absence of the first Shapiro step
due to a large dV/dI value. I believe this point needs to be discussed in the manuscript. 
Comments on the presentation of results: 
1. In my opinion, panels a, b, and c in Figure 3 are redundant. The behavior of i_n(v) is entirely clear from Eq. (2), so plotting
this function for different n and vc is unnecessary. I believe that VIC should be added to panel d) in the model with absence
of i_e(v) term. 
2. The value of the parameter α1 used in the simulations is not specified. 
Overall, I support the publication of the article in Communications Physics. However, the revised version of the manuscript
must address the points I have raised in my review. 
Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript by L. Xu et al. reports on the study of the transport properties of Al/WTe2 Josephson junctions. Specifically,
the authors report on the measurements of IV characteristics under microwave bias for the study of Shapiro steps, as a
function of the power and frequency of the external bias. These results are analyzed in the framework of the non-linear
resistively and capacitively shunted junction model, which reproduces the experimental findings. In particular, the missing
first Shapiro step is ascribed to the intrinsic nonlinearity of the Josephson junctions under study, with an impact on the
interpretation of similar results in the context of topological superconductivity and Majorana bound states. 
In my opinion, the topic of the manuscript is quite interesting, the experimental data along with the corresponding analysis
are well supported and the paper is well written. Therefore, I recommend publication of the manuscript in Communications
Physics, after a few remarks have been considered. 
Specifically:

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 3 -->

1) The presence or the absence of the first Shapiro step depends on the frequency of the external microwave bias. While the
non-linear resistively and capacitively shunted junction model is able to capture the experimental outcomes, in the
manuscript a physical explanation of the phenomenon is missing. Also in the Supplementary, there is only a technical
discussion of numerical and experimental findings. For the reader it is hard to understand why the first Shapiro step is visible
at higher frequencies and disappears at low frequencies. 
2) At page 9, the discussion on the effective beta factor near the switching to the resistive state is misleading. According to
several works reported in literature, for instance those related to Nobel Prize Martinis, Devoret and Clarke, it is well known
that the quality factor at the switching form the superconducting to the resistive state is related to dissipation from the
environment and external circuit. Therefore, the beta factor is lower with respect to the beta factor in the running state. This
part of the paper should be modified according to the mentioned literature, along with a general consistency within the
manuscript. 
3) Finally, it is interesting that the manuscript reports on simple and “old” arguments to explain some anomalies in the
context of hybrid Josephson junctions, which are quite common in recent years. At the same time, it is quite strange that
appropriate references have not been acknowledged. For instance, the Likharev review, Barone and Paterno’s book, the
papers by Martinis, Devoret, Clarke (PRL 1984, PRL 1985, Science 1988), or by Kautz and Martinis (PRL 1989, PRB 1990)
have not been cited. The nonlinear resistively and capacitively shunted junction model is well-known form the ‘80s.

We thank the reviewer for raising this important point regarding the physical origin of the 
nonlinear normal current. We fully agree that our device is far from the single-channel limit, 
and that the single -channel BdG results discussed in the manuscript should not be 
interpreted as a direct microscopic description of the present junction. Rather, they are used 
to illustrate a generic mechanism by which intrinsic nonlinearity can arise in SNS junctions. 
We have carefully examined whether the same nonlinear behavior emerges naturally in a 
multichannel description obtained by integrating single-channel results over the 
transparency distribution of a diffusive metal or a disordered interface. In the absence of 
additional dephasing, such multichannel averaging indeed leads to current –voltage 
characteristics without large resistance at low vo ltage and  does not reproduce the 
pronounced nonlinearity required to explain the experimental observations. 
Importantly, we note that a nonlinear normal current consistent with our phenomenological 
description does emerge in Usadel-based calculations when a finite dephasing is taken into
