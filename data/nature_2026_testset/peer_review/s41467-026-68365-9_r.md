Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The paper reports the performance of a bionic photothermal evaporation platform (BPEP) containing polypyrrole-coated
bacterial cellulose hydrogel (BCBH/PPy), which could extract low-concentration nitrate from contaminated urban rivers. The
system could reduce the nitrate concentration from 23.7 to 2.2 mg/L and remain stable after 10 cycles. Moreover, the
absorbed nitrate could be converted to ammonia to promote plant growth. 
This is an interesting system showing a promising
solution for the sustainable recovery of nitrates especially in the urban environment. However, the writing/presentation of the
paper particularly the results and discussion are not well-organized. The detailed information of the construction of BPEP
should be provided. The results are plainly listed without correlated interpretation. 
More explanation of the calculation
process should be added, e.g., the enthalpy obtained from the differential scanning calorimeter curves. Major revision is
required before publication. 
Some concerns are noted below: 
1) The use of abbreviation should be defined when it first appears. 
What is PS in Fig 1d; APS/Py in Fig. 2a? 
2) The clarity of figures should be improved. For instance, the fonts in the figure Fig. 1b are not clearly shown. 
3) The figure captions should explicitly describe the content. More explanations are needed in the captions of manuscript as
well as in the supplementary. What are ‘C’ and ‘T’ representing in Fig. 1d? 
4) Line 85, “BCBH/PPy can adsorb nitrates through electrostatic adsorption and chemical action even without light”. Does
author mean the ‘chemical action’ as “chemical reaction”? Nitrate adsorption is an important issue. The correlated chemical
reaction should be more specifically explained here. 
5) The author use ‘sun’ as the unit of solar flux. It is quite obscure to the reader. The scientific value or explanation of one
sun should be provided for the better understanding. 
6) Figure 3 contains many subfigures. The organization or presentation could be improved. For example, Fig. 3e and 3f
could be combined. Fig. 3h, I, m look not presented in a general scientific way in my view. 
7) In the supplementary, ‘3D’ system is introduced which the author states is better than ‘2D’. In the view, the difference lies
in the angle of sun irradiation to increase the light absorption of BCBH/PPy. 
Instead of giving an obscure description of ‘3D’,
precise measurement of the absorption increase is more pertinent. It is also interesting to compare the efficiency of nitrate
absorption for 3D structure with those of 2D in the main context. 
8) English improvement.

Reviewer #4 
(Remarks to the Author) 
My summary of the manuscript is that the authors used an existing evaporation scheme to remove water from a nitrate
solution and thereby recovered nitrate. 
The rate of nitrate recovery increased because of concentration of nitrate and
perhaps a decrease of viscosity due to the temperature increase. All this is automatic if you heat and remove water. I do not
find this to be novel in and there were no interesting mechanistic aspects. In addition, there was no particular selectivity for
nitrate and the new results show that the polypyrrole is not special, other adsorbing materials work too. 
This new work
simply confirmed for me that by removing water, one obtains better adsorption/precipitation. Therefore, this manuscript falls
far short of the standard usually considered for this journal. The best aspect of the manuscript is technological, in that the
authors used an existing scheme to separate water from nitrate. The new mechanism section mainly describes the
adsorption of nitrate to polypyrrole. 
This was reasonable, but it is difficult for me to envisage a mechanism where
concentration of the nitrate would not enhance adsorption and, indeed, the authors explain it with Le Chatillier’s principle. 
Overall, I find the results sound, but not that scientifically novel compared to the author’s prior work on the same system
where enhanced water evaporation was shown. The explanations for results are very long, and there are many sentences
with a posteriori explanations without a deep improvement in knowledge. In particular, the new “Mechanism for enhanced
nitrate recovery” section did not reach the level of explaining new phenomena, which is unsurprising since the authors show
that other materials work well too.

Section “Mechanism for enhanced nitrate recovery” (Nitrate recovery mechanism 
related): 
The enhanced nitrate recovery performance of BPEP mainly resulted from the selective 
adsorption properties of the PPy coating and the multi -field effects generated by the 
interfacial evaporation process, with the underlying mechanisms as follows: 
(1) Nitrate-selective adsorption mechanism of PPy coating: The adsorption process was 
mainly governed by electrostatic interaction and hydrogen bonding. Density functional 
theory (DFT) calculations demonstrated favorable adsorption energies of -0.34 eV for 
nitrate, confirming the thermodynamic spontaneity of the adsorption process (Fig. 4a). 
Note that PPy existed as a three-dimensional structure in the DFT calculation, allowing 
for the spontaneous adsorption of nitrate ions at multiple accessible sites  
(Supplementary Fig. 22). XPS spectra further confirmed the adsorption mechanism .

Before adsorption, the N1s peak at 399.8 eV (Fig. 4b) was associated with benzenoid 
amine (-NH-)51. After nitrate adsorption, the novel N1s peaks at 406.7 eV  (Fig. 4c), 
corresponding to the nitrate 52. Moreover, the decrease and shift in the binding energy 
of benzenoid amine suggested that this group was responsible for nitrate adsorption. 
The adsorption mechanism likely involve d electrostatic attraction or coordination

hydrogen bonds were formed during the adsorption process 38. The dual adsorption 
mechanism of PPy coating synergistically enhances nitrate selective recovery efficiency. 
(2) Evaporation-induced flowing field. The interfacial evaporation process significantly 
enhances ion transport within the BPEP system, increasing the collision frequency 
between target nitrate ions and active adsorption sites. To quantify this effect, we 
performed COMSOL simulations comparing the spatial distribution of nitrate 
concentrations in the BPEP absorber under 1 -sun illumination with dark conditions 
(simulation details are provided in the Supplementary Information). As demonstrated 
in Figure 4 d-I, solar-driven evaporation significantly enhances BPEP ’s nitrate 
adsorption capacity, with a maximum increase of 5.2 times at the same sampled location 
compared to the dark conditions (Figure 4d-II). From a kinetic perspective, interfacial 
evaporation generates localized temperature and concentration gradients at the gas -
liquid interface, triggering the Marangoni effect. This phenomenon induces 
microvortices that disrupt the static diffusion boundar y layer, allowing for the 
continuous delivery of nitrate ion s to adsorption sites while minimizing mass transfer 
resistance. Consequently, the nitrate concentration within BPEP exhibits a distinct 
gradient along the flow direction ( Figure 4d-II), reflecting the synergistic interplay 
between evaporation-driven convection and adsorption kinetics.

the preferential escape of water molecules at the gas -liquid interface, leading to a 
significant accumulation of target ions (nitrate  ions) in the evaporation region.

(nitrate ions) between the solution phase and the adsorbent interface, governed by the 
mass action  law. Consistent with Le Chatelier’s principle, an increase in bulk ion

enhancing adsorption capacity  to push the equilibrium toward adsorption . Our

adsorption capacity by ~8 times (Fig. 4e). Kinetically, the adsorption rate is governed 
by ion mass transfer from the bulk phase to the interface.  The evaporation-sustained
