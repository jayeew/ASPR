Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript of Zheng, Zhu, and collaborators presents a highly efficient method for the direct construction of bicyclic
compounds including bicyclo[2.2.1]heptanones and bicyclo[3.2.1]octanones, which are achieved through an unprecedented
photoinduced decatungstate-catalyzed selective bicyclization between internal alkynes and aldehydes. A highlight of this
work is the exceptional diastereoselectivity observed, which the authors rationalize via an uncommon HAT-mediated
dynamic epimerization. I found the preparative part to be very robust, as it not only demonstrated good compatibility with
around 50 examples, but also included limitations, such as the tether length requirements, incompatibility of terminal
alkynes, and necessity of the R3 group. The detailed mechanistic investigation is also commendable, through a combination
of dynamic interconversion studies, judicious control experiments, and comprehensive DFT computations. Additionally, the
usefulness of this method was well confirmed by fast assembly of an organic catalyst, a new norbornene scaffold, a
functional material molecule, and a more potent analogue of WAY-100635. Considering the significant novelty of this in situ
skeletal and stereochemical editing strategy and its expediency for rapidly accessing valuable 3D frameworks, I felt this
manuscript is well suitable for publication in Nature Communications. 
Minor revisions are listed below. 
1. The control experiments revealed that the unfavorable SN2 cyclization proceeded at 100 °C. Therefore, it is
recommended that the authors investigate temperatures above 50 °C to determine whether the reaction can be further
accelerated while maintaining the diastereoselectivity. 
2. Halogen atoms are widely recognized as valuable synthetic handles. Examples 5-7, 16, and 17 demonstrated
compatibility with F, Cl, Br, and I on the aromatic ring. However, variation of the R2 group to halogens other than Cl remains
limited. The authors are suggested to investigate the compatibility of Br at the R2 position, given the prevalence of cross-
coupling of C-Br bonds. 
3. As for the photoinduced bicyclization of ynones (Fig. 6), given the facile decarbonylation of COBn radical, can the
corresponding benzylated bicyclo[2.2.1]heptanones be obtained? If so, it would further enhance the scope and value of this
method. 
4. Computational method should be included in the caption of Fig. 4. 
5. The abbreviations DT and DTH should be clarified throughout the text.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Zhu and Zheng et al. reported an efficient and highly diastereoselective synthesis of bicyclo[2.2.1]heptanones and
bicyclo[3.2.1]octanones via a photoinduced decatungstate-catalyzed bicyclization of internal alkynes with aldehydes. This
approach enables the concurrent in situ editing of molecular skeleton and stereochemistry under mild conditions. By
integrating experimental studies with density functional theory (DFT) calculations, the authors provide mechanistic insights
into the C–H epimerization process and the underlying dynamic kinetic resolution, which is mediated by hydrogen atom
transfer. The reaction pathway—featuring a radical cascade involving HAT, radical addition, 1,5-HAT, cyclization, and
stereoselective HAT-mediated epimerization—is systematically elucidated, offering robust experimental validation and a
clear theoretical framework for the observed diastereocontrol. 
Overall, this work represents a significant advance in synthetic methodology and reaction mechanism understanding, and is
therefore strongly recommended for publication. 
It is suggested that the following contents be modified and improved as necessary before acceptance. 
1. It is recommended that Figure 4d be separated from Figure 4 and exchanged with Figure 5. Presenting the reaction
mechanism first, followed by analysis using the free energy profile, would establish a more logical and coherent narrative,
facilitating reader comprehension of the reaction pathway evolution. 
2. The authors should explicitly address the roles of both ground-state and photoexcited decatungstate (DT) throughout the
manuscript, particularly in the theoretical section, to provide a complete mechanistic picture and strengthen the rationale for
the catalytic cycle. 
3. In Supporting Information, modify the table on page 46 to ensure consistency in the language of the article.

Comments: (a) Table 1, entry 8, the result shows that base is only beneficial, but not essential to

provide a mechanistic rationale explaining both how the reaction proceeds without K₂CO₃ and how

Answer: Thanks for the good questions. To address these concerns, some additional experiments 
and computations were added. In the absence of K 2CO3, anti-4 (>20:1 dr) underwent an 
intramolecular cyclization to furnish 3a in 94% yield at 50 °C (Figure R2a). Given that it can 
undergo a facile keto-enol tautomerization at ambient temperature (25% enol isomer according to 
the 1H NMR spectrum, Figure R1), we proposed that the SN2-cyclization of anti-4 may proceed via 
an enol intermediate, which has been corroborated by DFT calculations ( Figure R2b ). The 
computed Gibbs free energy profile shows that the tautomerization of anti-4 to enol Int1ea is 
endergonic by 4.1 kcal/mol. Subsequent SN2 cyclization via TS2ea has an activation free energy of 
27.5 kcal/mol with respect to anti-4 (blue pathway), which is kinetically accessible at the reaction

temperature of 50 oC. In contrast, cyclization of Int1es via TS2es has a higher activation free energy 
of 32.5 kcal/mol (purple pathway), which is consistent with the experimental results that syn-4 
requires an elevated temperature (100 °C) to achieve the SN2 cyclization.  
Our calculations indicate that the HAT-mediated epimerization remains operative in the absence 
of K2CO3 (Figure R2b). HAT between the benzylic C-H bond of syn-4 and excited triplet DT (3DT) 
via TS1k requires an activation energy of 18.8 kcal/mol (gray pathway), while that  between the 
benzylic/allylic C-H bond of Int1es and *DT via TS1e requires a 1.8 kcal/mol lower activation 
energy of 17.0 kcal/mol (black pathway). Back hydrogen atom donation from the other side of the 
resulting benzyl radical finishes the C-H epimerization.  
Collectively, these data indicate that the inherent keto-enol tautomerization is able to trigger the 
epimerization and following S N2 cyclization of 4 under base -free conditions, whereas both 
processes occurred with higher barriers as compared with the results shown in Fig 5. The mechanism 
of reaction without K2CO3 was briefly discussed in footnote 57 and the comprehensive Gibbs free 
energy profile has been included in Fig S5 in the Supporting Information.
