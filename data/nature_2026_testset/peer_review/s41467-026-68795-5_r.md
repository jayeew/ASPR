Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, Nan et al. report the discovery of a SARS-CoV-2 PLpro’s orally bioavailable inhibitor, YL1004, which
features a novel chemical scaffold and exhibits potent in vitro inhibitory activity, low cellular cytotoxicity, robust in vivo
antiviral efficacy, and the ability to restore host antiviral immune responses antagonized by SARS-CoV-2 infection. 
The study began with high-throughput screening that identified compound 1a. The authors subsequently optimized 1a by
merging it with the classical PLpro inhibitor GRL0617, yielding compounds 2a, 2b, and 2c, which displayed improved IC
₅₀
values and ΔTm. Structure–activity relationship studies were then performed by systematically modifying the R1, R2, and R3
groups of compound 2b. Through these efforts, YL1004 was identified as a lead compound. In both in vitro and in vivo
assays, YL1004 outperformed the current best-in-class inhibitor Jun12682. Furthermore, the co-crystal structure of the
PLpro–YL1004 complex was determined, providing mechanistic insights into its mode of action. 
Overall, this manuscript reports the discovery of a novel PLpro inhibitor with clear improvements over current leading
compounds, including Jun12682 and PF-07957472. The study is well-designed, integrates multiple complementary
approaches, and provides both mechanistic and therapeutic insights. Nevertheless, there remain several issues that should
be addressed before the manuscript can be considered for publication. 
Major Comments: 
1. In Fig. 1a and lines 100–103, the authors describe analog 2c as a derivative of GRL0617 by replacing the phenylmethyl
group with a phenylamino group. However, another key difference is the N-methylpiperazinyl group present in 2c but absent
in GRL0617. A more rigorous side-by-side comparison with GRL0617 is necessary to clarify the contributions of these
modifications. 
2. The manuscript introduces the terms “fragment fusion” and “fragment assembly” (Fig. 1a). In medicinal chemistry, the
concept of “fragment” is more commonly associated with fragment-based drug discovery (FBDD), where “fragment merging”
is a standard term. The authors should reconsider the terminology for consistency with established usage. 
3. The data presented in Fig. 2 and Extended Data Figs. 1–3 are dense and difficult to follow. Reorganizing these results
into well-structured tables would significantly improve clarity. Moreover, the structure–activity relationship (SAR) analysis
could be expanded and presented in a format more commonly seen in medicinal chemistry studies. 
4. In the Results and Methods sections, more details about the in-house chemical library screening are required. How was
the screening performed, and what were the overall outcomes beyond the top five compounds? Providing this information
will enhance reproducibility. 
Minor Comments: 
1. The abbreviation “Nsp1-3” (Page 3, Line 55) should be written out in full at its first occurrence. 
2. In Fig. 2, the unit of AUC is shown as µg/L·h. Please ensure consistent notation across the manuscript, including
Supplementary Table 11. 
3. On Page 7, Lines 156–157, the authors state that “The SARS-CoV-2 PLpro comprises four domains: an N-terminal
ubiquitin-like (Ubl) domain, followed by thumb, palm, and finger domains.” In fact, thumb, palm, and finger represent three
subdomains within the larger ubiquitin-specific protease (USP) domain. This should be corrected. 
4. In Fig. 3c, the electron density should be shown using the 2Fo–Fc map after Refmac refinement, rather than the Fo–Fc

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript presents a well-executed study describing the discovery and characterization of YL1004, a novel SARS-
CoV-2 PLpro oral antiviral drug candidate. The authors employed an innovative approach that combined high-throughput
screening (HTS) with fragment fusion using GRL0617, followed by systematic chemical optimization. The work
demonstrates strong translational potential, with YL1004 showing high potency in enzymatic assays. The mechanism of
action is confirmed by an X-ray co-crystal structure, and the compound exhibits favorable pharmacological properties,
efficacy in cell-based assays, activity against nirmatrelvir-resistant viruses, and strong protection in a SARS-CoV-2-infected
mouse model. 
The manuscript will be suitable for publication in Nature Communications upon minor modifications, once the authors have
addressed the following comments: 
1. While recent advances with orally active PLpro inhibitors in murine models such as Jun12682, PF-07957472, and GZNL-
P36 are reported, we would suggest including other recent such as WEHI-P8 (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-57905-4) and
Jun13296 (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-56902-x). Note that many of them have either a naphthalene or quinoline moiety.
Some comparison in terms of superposition of the co-crystal structures would be of interest to readers, even if it is just in the
Supporting Materials. 
2. Enzymatic Assay Methodology - Tight Binding Kinetics: The enzymatic assay method does not refer to any previous
article. Is this a new method? Or is it based on a previous one? If tight-binding kinetics were not explicitly considered, this
should be addressed, as it may impact the accuracy of reported IC50 values. The Morrison equation may be more
appropriate than standard IC50 calculations for tight-binding inhibitors, especially YL1004, both for the protease and
deubiquitanse/de-ISGlase activities. Related to this, the concentration of enzyme utilized (200 nM) may also be a bit too high
for tight-binding inhibitors, with reports going as low as 50 nM PLpro for enzymatic inhibition assays (see, for example, DOI:
10.1038/s41467-025-56902-x). Note that Morrison equation fitting might be possible to perform even with the same
experimental raw data (see https://www.graphpad.com/guides/prism/latest/curve-fitting/reg_morrison.htm). 
3. Rationale for the naphthalene 4-position Chemistry Exploration: While the manuscript describes the chemical optimization
process, the strategic rationale for exploring the naphthalene 4-position modifications is not clearly articulated (for instance,
the Pfizer inhibitor or Jun13296 have a quinoline 3-position substituent instead). Adding a brief explanation would be
interesting. Was this guided by computational modeling, initial SAR data, analysis of the GRL0617 co-crystal structure, or
other considerations?

Reviewer #4 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Reviewer report 
Manuscript: YL1004 is a SARS CoV 2 papain like protease inhibitor with immunomodulatory and antiviral activity in mice 
Key results 
Nan et al. introduce YL1004, a rationally designed, orally active inhibitor of the SARS CoV 2 papain like protease (PLpro).
Starting from a fragment fusion concept that blends an in house molecular library hit of a hetrotricyclic with features of
GRL0617, the authors work through clear SAR to a lead that engages PLpro structurally, strongly inhibits PLpro activity,
restores antiviral signaling in cells, suppresses replication across several SARS CoV 2 variants, and improves outcomes in
a stringent mouse model (full survival of mouse sample population. This study represents a rational and comprehensive
design and characterization of a novel PLpro inhibitor having very promising activity in mouse models. 
Validity 
Overall, the evidence thoughtful and presented well: medicinal chemistry rationale → on target engagement → cellular
mechanism → antiviral efficacy → in vivo benefit. The structural data provide a persuasive on target anchor, a 2.7
 
Å co
crystal structure (PDB 9U7D) that locates YL1004 in the BL2/S3–S4 pocket; the IC50 and EC50 values of a series of lead
compounds support grat promise as PLpro inhibitors, sub micromolar antiviral activity across wildtype, Delta, and Omicron
sublineages including JN.1 and KP.3; and full survival protection in a Delta challenge where vehicle survival is 10%; and
the cell based deubiquitination/deISGylation rescue, together with innate immune reporter recovery, tie the biochemical
activity to cellular consequences. Antiviral data align with the target story and extend to a nirmatrelvir resistant virus (NSP5
E166V), which is a thoughtful orthogonal test of relevance. 
Significance 
PLpro remains an important, underexploited antiviral target because it couples viral polyprotein processing with antagonism
of innate immunity. A well characterized, selective, orally bioavailable inhibitor that shows cross variant antiviral activity and
survival benefit in vivo is notable. The manuscript’s integrated view—chemistry, mechanism, virology, and animal efficacy—
will be useful to both the PLpro community and the broader antiviral field, especially with a deposited structures, data, and
assay results not focused on in this manuscript, as well as the clear and transparent design narrative. 
Data and methodology 
The work is generally well designed and well reported. The discovery path is easy to follow (screen → fragment fusion →
regional optimization → lead selection → biochemical/biological assays), with enough intermediate disclosure to be
instructive without overwhelming the reader. The structural biology and the cellular mechanism assays are appropriate and
mutually reinforcing, and the in vivo studies use a recognized lethal model with virology, histology/IHC, body weight, and
survival endpoints. Where a little more context would help: linking on study exposure (infected mice) to in cell potency
targets, and presenting selectivity indices (CC50/EC50) in the same cellular backgrounds used for antiviral testing. 
Analytical approach 
As a non-expert in statistical methodology, I find the analyses chosen for the biochemical, cellular, and animal studies
broadly appropriate for the study designs and presented with sufficient transparency to follow the results. The manuscript
and figure legends indicate standard group-comparison and survival analyses and summarize data with means and
variability, which is adequate for interpreting the findings. 
To further aid reproducibility, please state consistently what constitutes biological vs. technical replicates across where
appropriate, including all relevant figures and in the Supplemental Information. For example, early figure captions reference
“three independent experiments” and “three technical replicates,” whereas later legends specify that “each data point
represents one biological repeat” and that data come from “two or three independent experiments.” Unifying this terminology
in the figure captions and adding a brief definition in the Supplement would make replication straightforward. 
Suggested improvements 
1. Figure 4d could use some clarification, along with the Human DUB assay against YL1004. There could also be additional
discussion in the text on why there is selectivity. Is there a rational for this selectivity of YL1004 for PLpro vs. DUB structural
homologs? Is there a structural basis for this, residue interactions? 
The figure is unclear, is the raw DUB inhibition assays only at one YL1004 concentration of many, could they be reported as
IC50s? 
This is not essential to publishing but would help with impact as a major challenge of PLpro drug design is selectivity
against human DUB enzyme homologs, accordingly, this warrants some more discussion/rationale. 
2. The term “broad spectrum” is used, consider “cross variant SARS CoV 2 activity,” since non SARS CoV 2 coronaviruses
were not tested.

Clarity and context 
The narrative is clear and accessible with figures adequately supporting the text. The head to head benchmarking with
Jun12682 is useful; ensuring the matched conditions for those comparisons are fully described in the Supplemental
Information will make those claims easier to evaluate as YL1004 is competing for a new benchmark PLpro inhibitor. 
References 
Coverage is broad and current, spanning PLpro biology, earlier chemotypes, recent mouse efficacious PLpro series, and
nirmatrelvir resistance. The manuscript is well situated in the literature. 
Your expertise 
My expertise is in chemistry and biochemistry with recent experience in medicinal chemistry, specifically anti-viral drug
design and characterization of PLpro inhibitors. 
Arguments for and against publication 
For: Coherent design to efficacy story with structural validation, cellular mechanism rescue, cross variant antiviral activity
