Reviewer #3 (Remarks to the Author): 
The authors attempt to integrate assays from different modalities, specifically spot/grid based spatial transcriptomics with
metabolites based MSI-MALDI. This workflow seems to be highly specific to grid/spot-based assays. 
1) The authors utilized simple pearson correlation to match similar spots across modalities (both grid/spot based). Then use
CCA transfer to have some latent space representing or integrating both modalities. These approaches are fairly simple,
with the interesting approaches probably involving the integration of spatial alignment with integration. 
2) This workflow looks to be aligning discrete entities (grids, spots etc) across modalities and won’t be applicable to image
base modalities. As such the authors need to be clear about the applicable modalities and be clear about the resolution of
the spatial transcriptomics utilized in this study. They appear to be using 10x visium spot based spatial transcriptomics. 
3) The simulation appears to be circular as they generated maldi data from ST using the workflow, then use the generated

Reviewer #4 (Remarks to the Author): 
This manuscript presents haCCA, a new computational workflow designed to integrate two different spatial omics
modalities: spatial transcriptomics data and spatial metabolomics data (specifically MALDI mass spectrometry imaging,
MALDI-MSI). The main challenge in data integration is these two modalities do not share common features (genes vs
metabolites) nor spatial coordinates, since they are typically measured on adjacent tissue sections. The authors propose
their method haCCA, which operates in two stages: first, a modified spatial registration aligns the tissue sections
morphologically, then a shared latent space is constructed by applying canonical correlation analysis (CCA) to highly
correlated feature pairs between the transcriptome and metabolome datasets. This way haCCA can identify genes and
metabolites with strongly correlated spatial patterns across the sections, uses CCA to project them into a joint latent space,
and then refines the alignment of spots based on this shared representation. 
Generally I found the work to be scientifically sound and presents meaningful, if incremental, advances in methodology with
a clear biological application. The current weaknesses are primarily in communication (method clarity, contextual framing,
and some details of analysis) rather than in the data or conclusions. In its present form, the manuscript needs more revision
to improve clarity and completeness, including explanation of methods, context, and minor presentation issues. I recommend
this manuscript for publication only after the authors have addressed these issues I higlighted: 
1. The paper would benefit from a clearer step-by-step explanation of the haCCA workflow. In particular, the description of
for alignment is a bit hard to follow. Please clarify each step in the integration process. For example, define how you initially
identify anchor spot pairs between the two spatial datasets? What constitutes an outlier spot that needs second-stage
alignment? Please explain exactly how highly correlated feature pairs are identified, presumably you compute correlations
between every gene's spatial pattern and every metabolite's spatial pattern after an initial alignment? Providing a concise
pseudocode or flowchart in the text (complementing Figure 1) could help readers grasp the method without ambiguity. In
summary, please improve the clarity of the Methods section, ensuring that a reader could in principle reproduce the workflow
step by step. This is especially important for those who may want to apply haCCA to their own data. 
2. Key implementation details are missing. For example, the threshold used to define 'highly correlated' pairs, the procedure
for selecting anchor spot pairs, and the hyper-parameters of ICP/FGW in the gross alignment step are mentioned only in
passing. And how exactly is the psueodo-maldi data constructed? It's not really clear either. Reproducibility would improve if
all parameters were reported in a concise table together with runtime and memory benchmarks on the datasets
analysed.Critical implementation details are missing. 
3. The integration performance results (Figures 2 and 3) are key to showing improvements of haCCA over other methods,
but I found the figures too crowded. Consider cutting some results to supplementary, and perhaps higlight the key results
more prominently. For instance, figure 2D is key, and yet I have to zoom in to read it. Could you please revise figure clarity
so it's easier to get the main message? 
4. Scalability and robustness are not addressed. Running time, peak memory use and behaviour under varying spot
densities should be reported. The manuscript never addresses computational complexity. haCCA involves iterative ICP-like
registration followed by optimal transport on thousands of spots. Reporting some runtime characteristics will be very useful
to demonstrate to the reader that the method is practical. 
5. I put this last because this doesn't affect the scientific soundness of the manuscript, but maybe this should be the first
point. The presentation would benefit from thorough editing. The manuscript contains many grammatical errors (“marix-
assisted”, “muti-modal”, “benckmark”, etc.). The writing at the beginning of the manuscript is quite awkward, for e.g. "such
integration may be challenge". The abstract is also too short and does not fully convey the significance of the work. In this
age of LLM, I would recommend using a tool like ChatGPT to help you polish the writing. The text is not bad, but the poor
writing definitely distracts from the scientific content.
