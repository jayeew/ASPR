Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript by Liu and co-workers (manuscript ID: NCOMMS-25-58664) describe a homogenized heterogeneous
catalys, based on single-atom Pd(II)-N
₄
 sites anchored on graphitic carbon nitride (g-C
₃
N
₄
), engineered to the selective
photocatalytic semihydrogenation of alkynes. In their design strategy, Liu and co-workers were inspired by pincer-type metal
complexes, that frequently exhibit metal-ligand cooperativity to activate E-Y bonds (e.g., H2). Using these fundamental
concepts from homogeneous catalysis, the authors were able to demonstrate that under light irradiation, the Pd-N
₄
 moieties
transform into H–Pd···N–H dual-atom-like sites that synergistically mediate alkyne activation and intramolecular proton
transfer. This metal–ligand cooperativity, combined with the steric hindrance of the carbon nitride support, affords complete
Z-selectivity across a series substrate scope. Limited mechanistic investigations, supported by in situ spectroscopy and DFT
calculations, confirm that Pd–H species are key intermediates and that the system exhibits strong preference for alkyne
adsorption over alkene absorption, suppressing over-hydrogenation 
Structural characterization confirms the isolated Pd(II) single atoms coordinated to four nitrogen atoms. The catalyst exhibits
high stability and photocatalytic activity under mild, aqueous conditions using visible light. Control experiments exclude
radical pathways and validate the involvement of metal-hydride intermediates and adjacent N–H units. The protocol allows
for sustainable and stereoselective hydrogenation using water or silanes as hydrogen sources. 
Overall, the experimental work appears to be conducted carefully, and the data interpretation is generally sound. However,
the manuscript suffers from significant issues in clarity and presentation, with numerous unclear or awkwardly phrased
statements throughout the text. The title, in particular, is overly long, difficult to follow, and contains grammatical errors. More
importantly, the study lacks rigorous mechanistic investigation, offers a relatively narrow substrate scope, and does not
present a level of conceptual or methodological novelty that would meet the publication standards of Nature
Communications. For these reasons, I do not recommend the manuscript for publication in its current form. Detailed
comments and concerns are provided below. 
1. Lack of novelty. I do not believe that the current manuscript meets the novelty threshold required for publication in Nature
Communications. As the authors themselves note in the introduction, numerous homogeneous systems have already
achieved exclusive or near-exclusive Z-selective semihydrogenation of alkynes. More critically, the use of Ni–N
₄
 sites on
carbon nitride, while allowing minor formation of E-isomers, follows a remarkably similar mechanistic paradigm (Angew.
Chem. Int. Ed. 2023, 62, e202216511). Specifically: (i) both systems involve a two-electron reduction of the metal center
upon photoirradiation; (ii) both employ water as the proton source; (iii) both enable the formation of deuterated alkenes; (iv)
in both studies, the formation of an N–H bond is essential for catalytic turnover; and (v) in both, steric effects at the active site
suppress formation of the E-isomer. Taken together, these mechanistic parallels significantly diminish the conceptual
novelty of the present study. While the work is competently executed and may offer some incremental value, it is better
suited for a specialized journal such as ACS Catalysis. 
2. Limited Mechanistic Investigations. Although the authors present some mechanistic data that support the involvement of
Pd–H and N–H functionalities in the catalytic cycle, further mechanistic investigations would significantly enhance the depth
and rigor of the study. For example, kinetic studies, such as initial rate measurements under varying substrate or catalyst
concentrations could help identify the rate-determining step and differentiate between alternative mechanistic pathways.

3. Limited Substrate scope. Given the strong mechanistic resemblance to previously reported systems (as outlined in point

Communications. The current study focuses primarily on aromatic and aliphatic internal alkynes, with minimal exploration of
substrates bearing diverse or sensitive functional groups. In contrast, many homogeneous systems in the literature have
demonstrated excellent functional group tolerance, including successful semihydrogenation in the presence of aldehydes,
ketones, esters, amides, and halides. Exploring such substrates would not only highlight the robustness and selectivity of
the catalyst but also help differentiate this system from prior work. In its current form, the scope feels more proof-of-concept
than comprehensive, and does not convincingly establish the generality or practical utility of the method. A broader and
more challenging substrate panel—particularly involving reducible or coordination-sensitive functionalities—would be
necessary to elevate the significance of this work to the level expected for Nature Communications.

Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, the authors report that the Pd(II)-N4 sites anchored on carbon nitride (Pd-N4/CN) successfully achieved
alkyne semihydrogenation with complete Z-selectivity. While the semihydrogenation reactions have been extensively
studied on SACs (e.g., Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 2025, 64, e202500389; Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 2024, 63: e202410394;
Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 2023, 62, e202216511), this work revealed that the ligand N-atoms in Pd-N4 SACs directly
participate in the catalytic cycle, going beyond merely electronic modulation. Specifically, the study highlights the metal-
ligand cooperativity mechanism involving H-Pd-N-H sites. The authors successfully elucidate the indispensable role of
ligating N-atoms in the catalytic cycle through a combined spectroscopic analysis, DFT calculations, and control
experiments. These findings provide important insights into the structure-activity relationships in single-atom catalysts and
pave the way for further exploration of coordination-environment engineering. I recommend this work for publication in
Nature Communications, pending the resolution of the following issues: 
1. Additional characterizations should be included to better demonstrate the formation of Pd-H species, such as solid-state
NMR. 
2. Was E→Z isomerization ruled out during the transformation? Photochemical isomerization warrants explicit exclusion. 
3. Why were in-situ IR studies conducted under H
₂
(g) rather than aqueous-phase reaction conditions? 
4. What motivated the use of prop-1-yn-1-ylbenzene instead of diphenylacetylene for TPD experiments? 
5. This manuscript would benefit from a more comprehensive discussion of previous studies on semihydrogenation
reactions performed on SACs. More previous reports should be included.

1. Comparison relative to state-of-the-art 
Although the authors demonstrate in their response to the reviewers that their method offers advantages over recent state-of-
the-art approaches (for example Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 2023, 62, e202216511), the manuscript would benefit from a
dedicated paragraph clearly articulating how the present system differs mechanistically and conceptually from existing
methods. A comparison that goes beyond E/Z selectivity – for instance, detailing how the proposed PdH–NH dual reactivity
contributes to the enhanced performance – would significantly strengthen the broader impact and mechanistic positioning of
the work. 
2. Mechanistic discussion 
The authors have carried out substantial kinetic and mechanistic experiments, yet the corresponding discussion in the main
text remains relatively brief. In particular, the conclusion that Pd–H formation is rate-limiting is stated rather quickly and is not
fully integrated with the DFT results. At present, the mechanistic experiments and the computed reaction profile are
described somewhat independently, without a unifying narrative. The manuscript would clearly benefit from a better
integration between the two sections. 
Importantly, an alternative interpretation should be acknowledged: proton transfer from the NH moiety to the Pd-alkenyl
intermediate (int2a → int3a) could also constitute the rate-determining step. This pathway is compatible with the observed
first-order dependencies on H
₂
O, TEOA, and Pd if the buildup of the NH species originates from H2O or TEOA. Moreover,
the DFT calculations identify the protonolysis transition state as the highest barrier in the cycle. 
I therefore suggest that the authors either (i) include additional calculations evaluating the barrier for Pd–H formation to
support the mechanistic assignment, or (ii) briefly discuss this alternative NH-transfer-limited pathway in the main text,
indicating that both interpretations are plausible on the basis of the current data. 
3. Manuscript readability

difficult to follow. A light editorial revision focused on clarity and flow would greatly improve readability and ensure
