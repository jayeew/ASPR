Noteworthy Results: The study by Chetterjee, et al. identifies inositol polyphosphate multikinase (IPMK) and inositol
hexakisphosphate (InsP6 or phytic acid),) as activators of HDAC3 leading to maintenance of intestinal barrier integrity. It
establishes a mechanistic link between IPMK, HDAC3 enzymatic activation, and histone deacetylation via the recruitment of
the DAD domain of corepressor proteins. Loss of IPMK leads to increased histone acetylation, upregulation of matrix
metalloproteinases (MMPs), and compromised intestinal permeability. The study demonstrates the therapeutic potential of
InsP6 in mitigating loss of barrier integrity and implications for inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) pathogenesis (since there
is frequent loss of IPMK in IBD patients) using cell-based assays and a DSS-induced IBD mouse model. It provides strong
evidence for InsP6 as a dietary factor that modulates intestinal homeostasis through epigenetic regulation. 
Significance: The work introduces an epigenetic regulatory pathway linking dietary InsP6 to HDAC3 activity, with potential
applications in intestinal barrier function maintenance for IBD treatment. Given that pan-HDAC inhibitors cause significant
gastrointestinal toxicity, the identification of InsP6 as a physiological HDAC3 activator offers a targeted therapeutic approach
for IBD in the context of intestinal permeability. 
The use of the “the leaky pathway” in the Results section, again is very colloquial and isn’t totally clear what the pathway is,
since it is a complex interaction beyond only what is addressed in this manuscript. 
The authors note that transcriptomic analysis of IBD patients showed a significant increase in MMPs (Extended Figure 4 A),
this analysis was done specifically in Crohn’s disease patients and then the mouse model they employ is a DSS-colitis
model which mimics Ulcerative Colitis and impacts mainly the colon in terms of inflammation. The authors note this in the
Results section (Figure 6) and also analyzed the KO mice small intestine for molecular analysis of MMP3 which were
consistent with their findings. But, it is hard to reconcile the relationship to IBD as an umbrella since Crohns and Ulcerative
Colitis have unique pathophysiology. Are these genes involved in Ulcerative Colitis 
The manuscript builds on previous studies identifying HDAC3’s role in intestinal epithelial homeostasis, and IBD (Crohn’s
animal models). It addresses an important knowledge gap regarding the direct biochemical and physiological role of higher-
order inositol phosphates in epigenetic regulation, and uncovers the role of InsP6 in HDAC3 activation, which had not been

intestinal barrier integrity. While Ins(1,4,5,6)P4 has been visualized in HDAC3 crystal structure, biochemical assay showed
the more phosphorylated 
InsP5 and InsP6 have been higher affinity to HDAC3. In this manuscript, the author found that
IPMK, the enzyme synthesizes InsP4 and InsP5, is required for HDAC3 activity in cells. IMPK physically interacts with
HDAC3 and the locally produced InsPs are required for interaction of HDAC3 and its corepressors, and therefore HDAC3
deacetylation activity. HDAC3 was previously reported to regulate the expression of matrix remodeling enzymes, and indeed
the authors found loss of IPMK resulted in increased MMP gene transcription, compromising intestinal barrier integrity.
Overall, the experiments were well designed, and the manuscript was clearly written. 
The major concern of the study is which InsP is the physiological molecular regulating HDAC3 in vivo, although the authors
