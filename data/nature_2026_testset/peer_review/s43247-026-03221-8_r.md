REVIEWER COMMENTS: 
Reviewer #3 (Remarks to the Author): 
Thank you for providing the opportunity to review the manuscript by Hu et al., titled "Machine Learning Uncovers Dominant
Fractions of Heavy Metal(loid)s in Global Soils." The paper develops a framework for predicting the dominant fractions of
heavy metals (loid) in soils using a large global dataset and interpretable machine learning techniques. The study’s
application to mercury results in a global high-mobility mercury map and quantifies exposure levels for both populations and
croplands. Here are the major comments and suggestions to further improve the quality of the paper and make it more
suitable for publication in Nature Communications Earth & Environment. 
1.Scope beyond mercury: The study and its application are restricted to Hg, although the dataset covers multiple metals.
Please discuss in greater depth how the framework can be extended to other HMs (Pb, Cd, As, Cu), and why Hg was
prioritized, or needs to amend the title of the paper. 
2.Dataset imbalance and uncertainty: The dominance of residual fractions (F4) is a clear limitation. While you employed
class weighting and F1-based evaluation, please highlight more explicitly how this imbalance might bias predictions,
especially for non-residual fractions. Similarly, geographic gaps (e.g., northern Asia, central Africa, northern North America)
are acknowledged but not fully discussed. Consider adding examples of how these gaps may influence hotspot prediction
reliability. 
3.Study Timeframe: This study includes 9,489 data points collected from 219 sources. However, there is no clearly defined
methodological framework for the timeframe. Additionally, the timeframe for the explanatory variables and the temporal
dynamics map should be explained and clearly stated in the manuscript. 
4.Abstract: Please clarify that the global application is demonstrated only for Hg, to avoid the impression that multiple metals
were mapped. 
5.Figures: Uncertainty maps (currently in supplementary material) provide critical context. Consider integrating at least one
into the main text. 
6.Terminology: When discussing “non-residual fractions,” specify (F1–F3) consistently for clarity. 
Reviewer #4 (Remarks to the Author): 
This manuscript addresses a globally relevant issue by combining a large dataset of heavy metal(loid) (HM) fractions with
machine learning to map mercury mobility in soils. The approach has clear potential to advance large-scale environmental
risk assessment. However, the current version requires major revision to enhance methodological transparency, robustness
of validation, and clarity in linking data-driven results to underlying geochemical mechanisms and policy implications. 
1) Methodological Transparency: 
Provide a detailed explanation of data preprocessing steps, including handling of missing values, normalization, spatial
autocorrelation mitigation, and class imbalance treatment, to ensure reproducibility of the workflow. 
2) Feature Interpretation and Mechanistic Linkage: 
Strengthen the interpretation of model-derived feature importance (e.g., pH, organic carbon) by linking them to established
geochemical principles governing HM fraction transformations, supported by relevant literature. 
3) Uncertainty and Spatial Validation: 
Incorporate uncertainty quantification (e.g., confidence intervals, ensemble variability) and spatial cross-validation to validate
the robustness of global mercury fraction predictions and minimize overfitting from spatial dependence. 
4) Comparative and Statistical Validation: 
Present details on XGBoost hyperparameter tuning and include statistical significance testing when comparing model
performance with other algorithms. Test the model’s generalizability across different geographic or data-scarce regions. 
5) Relevant AI works: The authors should consider citing the article titled: "Isochronous Temporal Metric for Neighbourhood
Analysis in Classification Tasks”. This work introduces a refined classification metric for spatially correlated datasets. Its
conceptual framework for neighbourhood-based class separability will strengthen the methodological foundation for
modeling soil descriptors and heavy metal fraction interactions. 
6) Practical and Policy Relevance: 
Expand the discussion on how the predicted high-mobility regions can guide targeted soil remediation or monitoring
programs, particularly in heavily impacted regions like Asia, to demonstrate the broader environmental applicability of the
work.

Revised Statement:  136 
A global dataset of soil HM concentrat ions was compiled from publicly available 137 
sources (Supplementary Fig 12). To ensure data quality and representativeness, a 138 
systematic literature search was conducted across multiple databases, including Web of 139 
Science and Google Scholar , to retrieve studies published between January 1975 and 140 
January 2025 that reported HM fractions in soils. This period was selected to maximize 141 
data coverage while acknowledging the limited accessibility of literature published 142 
before 1975. The search combined the keywords "soil," "heavy metals," and ("fraction" 143 
OR "distribution" OR "classification" OR "sequential extraction"). After screening and 144 
validation, the final dataset comprised 9,489 data points from 56 countries or territories 145 
across five continents, covering 52 HMs, various soil textures and types, and all major 146 
land cover groups (Supplementary Fig 13 and Supplementary Table 2). 147 
 148 
Ultimately, following standardization of the input data, a two -step feature selection 149 
process was conducted, resulting in the retention of 12 significant features with low 150 
collinearity (Supplementary Table 3). 151
