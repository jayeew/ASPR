Reviewers' comments:
 
Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
This review manuscript provides a comprehensive overview of the development, structural characteristics, preparation strategies, and
biomedical applications of silk fibroin nanoparticles. Overall, the manuscript is well-structured and logically organized, effectively summarizing
the recent progress in SFNP fabrication and their biomedical potential. However, several issues should be addressed as below. 
1. The review mainly summarizes the existing literature but lacks sufficient critical evaluation and comparative discussion of different
preparation methods and application strategies. A deeper analysis highlighting the advantages and limitations of each approach would
strengthen the scientific insight of this review. 
2. Several figures appear conceptually simplistic and low in resolution. The authors are encouraged to redesign them using clearer graphical
abstracts or schematic illustrations to enhance visual clarity and scientific presentation. 
3. Many of the cited references are outdated (before 2021). To improve the timeliness and relevance of the review, the authors should
incorporate recent publications related to SFNPs. 
4. Antibacterial applications of SFNPs have been actively reported in recent years, yet this aspect is not covered in the current review. It is
suggested that the authors include relevant discussions and references. 
5. The degradation behavior of SFNPs is a fundamental parameter for biomedical applications and should be introduced and discussed in the
manuscript. 
6. The Enhanced Permeability and Retention (EPR) effect plays a key role in tumor-targeted drug delivery. Numerous studies have
demonstrated that SFNPs can exploit the EPR effect for passive tumor accumulation. Therefore, this important aspect should be included in the
review. 
7. The author should add some reports on protein-based nanoparticle drug delivery systems, including recent reviews and studies. The
following several references can be used for reference: Targeted protein delivery based on stimuli-triggered nanomedicine. Exploration. 2023.
doi: 10.1002/EXP.20230025. Advances in oral treatment of inflammatory bowel disease using protein-based nanoparticle drug delivery
systems. Drug Deliv. 2025. doi: 10.1080/10717544. Development of an 'off-the-shelf' gene therapeutic nanoparticle formulation for incorporation
into biomaterials for regenerative medicine applications. European cells & materials. 2024. https://doi.org/10.22203/eCM.v047a11. 
Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author): 
The review by Shengfei Bao and colleagues, titled “Recent advances in silk fibroin nanoparticles: fabrication, characteristics, and applications
in drug delivery”, is a useful addition to the existing literature. The following comments may help to further improve this work: 
Major Comments: 
1. Throughout the manuscript, a number of strong statements are made without sufficient supporting evidence or appropriate scholarly
references. This represents a missed opportunity. A more rigorous and selective discussion of the most relevant primary literature is required.
For example, the first primary paper describing the manufacture of silk nanoparticles has not been cited. Additional examples are noted below;
however, this list is not exhaustive. I estimate that at least 25 similar instances occur in the manuscript. 
2. Abstract: The abstract should be more precise in its claims. For example, the term “programmable biodegradability” is used prominently, yet
this concept is not adequately developed or supported in the main text. 
3. Abstract: The description of the manufacturing techniques for silk nanoparticles should be more explicit. These methods should also be
discussed and compared in more depth within the manuscript. 
Other Comments: 
4. A considerable number of the cited references are outdated, particularly the review articles (e.g., references 2–7 and several others). More
recent, high-quality reviews are available and should be incorporated. Furthermore, reviews are cited in the context of silk but they are on other
proteins (and not silk!) 
5. Line 52: The mechanism of drug accumulation described here is unclear and should be explained more precisely. 
6. Line 58 onwards: PLGA is a widely used polymer for nanoparticle/microparticle formulations and are clinically approved or in human clinical
trials. By contrast, many of the listed biopolymers have only been investigated at the research stage. The current wording is therefore not
sufficiently rigorous and should be revised. 
7. Line 61: The statement *“Unfortunately, most of these materials…” lacks clarity and specificity. Which materials, and which adverse effects,
are being referred to? Supporting primary literature should be cited. (See Major Comment 1) 
8. Line 63: The references cited here are weak in relevance and impact. Relevant primary refertences must be selected. (See Major Comment 1)
9. Line 70: The text implies that silk fibroin nanoparticles are clinically approved. This is misleading and should be revised. 
10. Line 75: The phrase “Compared with the nonspecific biodistribution characteristics of conventional drug administration…” is overly general
and should be made more precise and balanced. 
11. Line 82: The term “silk fibroin” is used here to refer to spider silks. This is incorrect. Spider silks are known as spidroins. Please revise. 
12. Line 84: The materials described here are not natural spider silks but engineered or “designer” silks inspired by spider silk sequences.
Please revise.

Reviewer #3 (Remarks to the Author): 
In this work, the authors summarized recent advances of silk fibroin nanoparticles in fabrication, characteristics, and applications in drug
delivery. However, to ensure this paper meets the acceptance criteria of Communications Materials, major revisions are required: 
1. A schematic diagram should be included to summarize the entire content of the manuscript. 
2. I recommend that the author summarize a roadmap of major advances in the preparation and processing of silk nanoparticles in recent years
to highlight significant progress in the field. 
3. In abstract, the author mentioned “Finally, we address the current challenges in the large-scale production and clinical translation of SFNPs.”
However, limited content was found in the manuscript. The author should discuss this in detail. 
4. More tables need to be included to better summarize the relationship between the processing method and physicochemical properties of the
nanoparticle. 
5. As drug delivery vehicles, understanding the interactions between nanoparticles and specific cells or tissues is crucial for disease treatment. I
recommend that the author further summarize this section to make the review more comprehensive. 
6. I suggest that the author highlight existing issues that need to be overcome and suggest future research directions in this field. The current
discussion in “Conclusions and future perspectives” lacks specificity. 
The author also needs to carefully check the manuscript for readability and quality. 
a. In abstract, the sentence “the obtained silk fibroin nanoparticles (SFNPs) have been widely applied in the field of drug delivery due to field
because of their desirable drug loading capacity and natural stimulus-responsive properties” is confusing. The authors should revise the
representation to better illustrate this point. 
b. In page 23, “The tunable β-sheet crystalline structure in SFNPs confers controlled drug release behavior, while the abundance of surface
functional groups (-NH2, COOH, and -OH) facilitates ligand conjugation and stimuli-responsive modification.” The amino group should be
abbreviated as “-NH2”. 
Reviewer #4 (Remarks to the Author): 
This review aims to summarize recent advances in silk fibroin nanoparticles (SFNPs), covering their fabrication, characteristics, and
applications in drug delivery. Specifically, according to the abstract, the authors intend to describe the extraction methods and structural features
of silk fibroin, outline the conventional strategies for fabricating and modifying SFNPs, introduce the physicochemical properties, characteristics,
and biomedical applications of SFNPs, and finally address the current challenges in their large-scale production and clinical translation, while
also discuss potential strategies to bridge the gap between laboratory research and clinical applications. 
Although the topic is timely and relevant, the review provides only a broad and descriptive overview, lacking in-depth analysis of the literature or
original interpretation. It mainly reiterates established knowledge without offering novel insights, critical discussion, or expert perspective, thus
adding limited value compared to previous published reviews. 
Specifically, the manuscript fails to meet many of the stated objectives for the following reasons. 
1. Despite claiming to review recent advances in the title, the manuscript fails to provide an up-to-date overview. For example, out of 105 cited
papers, only 24 were published from 2020 (excluded) onward, which significantly limits the review’s timeliness. Several significant recent
developments, such as microfluidic-assisted SFNP fabrication, continuous-flow synthesis, and green, scalable production methods, are either
missing or discussed too briefly to be informative. The most promising section of the paper is the forward-looking paragraph: “Looking ahead,
synthesis strategies have to focus on the development of green, scalable, and controllable processes for SFNP production”; however, this
statement is condensed into only a few lines, without elaboration or supporting analysis. The authors therefore miss an opportunity to
demonstrate scientific leadership by expanding on this vision and identifying the specific challenges and opportunities in these emerging
directions. Similarly, the section on microfluidics, one of the most dynamic and impactful directions in SFNP research, is treated in a highly
superficial manner. 
2. While claiming to address “current challenges in large-scale production and clinical translation,” the authors fail to connect materials science,
pharmaceutical technology, and regulatory science into a coherent discussion of these challenges. The text omits essential regulatory and
manufacturing concepts, such as Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP), Quality by Design (QbD), Critical Process Parameters (CPPs), and
Critical Quality Attributes (CQAs), all of which are fundamental to the industrial and regulatory development of nanomedicines. Furthermore,
there is no mention of pharmacopoeial standards, ICH Q8/Q9/Q10 principles, or Common Technical Document (CTD) structure, which are
essential for the translational pathway from bench to bedside. Similarly, there is no discussion of raw material traceability, purity, or sterilization
methods, all of which are critical for reproducibility and safety of SFNPs. The biological performance of SFNPs is also treated superficially: the

manuscript does not discuss pharmacokinetics, biodistribution, metabolism, or toxicity testing, nor does it reference ISO 10993 biocompatibility
standards, which are indeed significant challenges for clinical translation. In this regard, if a researcher were trying to understand how to bring
SFNPs toward clinical application or GMP pharmaceutical production and turned to this review (whose abstract promises to address these
aspects), they would likely come away disappointed and without a practical understanding of how to proceed. 
Minor Comments 
3. While it is true that silk fibroin, as a raw biomaterial, is FDA-approved, its use in nanoparticle formulations requires new regulatory
assessment for each specific product and intended use. This important clarification is missing, and its absence may create the misleading
impression that SFNPs are already close to regulatory acceptance. 
4. Page 5, lines 07/08: In this paragraph, the initial steps of the nanoparticle preparation procedures are explained well, but dialysis and
lyophilization are only mentioned. It would be appropriate to dedicate a few lines to briefly explaining these two crucial steps in the nanoparticle
production process. 
5. Page 14, line 329: It is stated that the surface charge of the nanoparticles significantly influences their stability. I think it would be best to
include a citation to a paper that explores this topic in greater depth.
