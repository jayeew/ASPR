Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This study correlates the structural distortion with the STEs emission in 2D lead bromide perovskites (R-NH3)2PbBr4
(where R is the cyclic carbon group) using temperature dependent PL spectroscopy, ultrafast transient absorption (TA)
spectroscopy and density functional theoretical (DFT) calculations. The authors have found that the increasing ring size of
cyclic cation leads to an enhanced steric hindrance that amplifies the Pb off-centering displacement and lone pair activity,
resulting in a stronger short-range Holstein electron-phonon interaction and thereby an enhanced STEs emission. They then
have concluded that lattice softness is not a prerequisite for strong electron-phonon coupling strength in 2D lead bromide
perovskites. This finding lays the foundation for the fundamental understanding of the structural distortion-property
relationship in 2D lead bromide perovskites. It could be acceptable after some revisions. 
1. “2D hybrid lead halide perovskites with a chemical formula of A2PbX6 (where A is the organic cation and X is the halide
ion) …” The chemical formula for 2D lead based perovskite should be changed to “A2PbX4”. It is necessary to characterize
the morphology of the crystalline samples, together with phase purity. Please provide the detailed information of the
experimentally measured single crystal structures. Mark the scale bar in Figure 1b. 
2. Please double check the Pb eccentric displacement parameter in Table S1. The value increases from 1.6 × 10
⁻
⁸ for C3 to
174.8 for C6 – a change of ≈10¹⁰ magnitudes that seems implausible. 
3. Please refine the calculation method for “Out-plane tilting distortion”. 
4. “As the ring size increases, the amino group will sink into the octahedral pockets, which will decrease the distance
between the N and halogen atoms (both terminal and bridging).” Please quantify the distance between N atoms and halogen
atoms. In addition, it necessary to further check the conditions of C7 ring cation and non-ring C2-C4 cations (ethyl,iso-propyl,
tert-butyl) to solidify the claim. Hydrogen bonding interactions between the c ations and inorganic parts and the conformation
of the cations should be analyzed to solidify the correlation study. 
5. “In this scenario, the increasing ring size of cyclic cation increases the 6s2 lone pair activity of the PbBr6 octahedra,
resulting in a stronger short-range Holstein interaction and an easier formation of small polarons.” Please explain in detail
how the increasing ring size of cyclic cation affects the 6s2 lone pair activity of PbBr6 octahedra. 
6. How about the PLQY values of the series of 2D halide perovskites? Are they dependent on grain morphology and size? 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This work presents a case study for modulating narrow versus broadband emission in 2D lead bromide perovskites by
controlling the extents of structural distortion using cyclic organic spacer cations. The authors compare a series of four n = 1
(R-NH3)2PbBr4 structures with cyclic alkylammonium cations (3, 4, 5, and 6-membered rings) that exhibit differing ratios of
narrow to broadband PL emission. Increasing the ring size is found to increase the metal off-centering displacement and
enable self-trapped/broadband emission more favorably. The authors further investigate the impacts of the off-centering
using transient absorption, temperature-dependent PL and Raman, and DFT which each help to correlate the nature of the
self-trapped emission with the electron-phonon coupling and specific phonon modes. While this series of perovskite
structures have been previously reported and broadband emission in 2D lead bromide perovskites has been well-studied,
the work offers a systematic comparison and helps to further unveil structure-property relationships. Even though I believe

this systematic structure-property relationship study of halide perovskite could be valuable, the current work is very lopsided
with a lot of spectroscopic measurements and very little structural discussion (or even support, see details below), making
the work quite difficult to evaluate. The main appeal of the work should be further strengthened. I do not find the current form
of the manuscript suitable for publication in Nat. Commun., however it could potentially be considered after the following
significant improvements could be made. 
Major revisions: 
1. The source of the four n = 1 (R-NH3)2PbBr4 structures that were analyzed for their distortions is never discussed. The
(powder?) XRD data mentioned is also not shown anywhere, but of course PXRD could not yield the information needed,
only confirmation of phase identification and purity… Were single crystal structures solved in this work, or calculated? Or
were they just adapted from previous literature? It looks like each of these structures have been reported previously in the
ref. 59 (10.1039/B819455F) but this is also not acknowledged. These key facts are not clear, so at present, I do not even
know if the structure aspects of the work are supported by facts or fiction. 
2. More importantly, it is concerning that the crystal structures and structural parameters are not displayed or well-discussed
when the work strongly emphasizes the importance of structural distortions, off-centering in particular. Please significantly
revise Figure 1 to show the crystal structures and analyze the structural distortions (NH3 penetration depth, Pb-Br-Pb bond
angles, θtilt and σ22) for each more comprehensively. This will allow the readers to see clearly what spacer cation
characteristics give rise to the off-centering that ultimately lead to the broadband emission… For instance, do the C5 and C6
spacers penetrate more deeply into the 2D pocket, or is it simply from greater steric hinderance? Are the ring protons closer
to the terminal iodides in the C5 and C6 versus the smaller ring cations? Further, the SEM/EDS mapping in Figure 1b are
not relevant for the structure-property discussions posed in Figure 1 and should be relocated in the SI to make room for
these more important comparisons. 
3. Page 9, lines 205-209: Related to comments 1 and 2 above, please select crystal structure views that demonstrate
whether the layers exhibit staggered versus eclipsed geometries. While the ref. 59 reports their structures as eclipsed, each
of them actually exhibit staggered layering typical for Ruddlesden-Popper perovskites. Please revise your hypothesis here
for the C3/C4 versus C5/C6 phonon modes in terms of the more rigorous structural analysis you perform around Figure 1. 
4. The concept of lone-pair expression and metal off-centering seems central to the arguments in this work but they are not
extensively introduced for less-familiar readers to understand and appreciate. I would suggest expanding the introduction of
lone-pair expression on page 3 on how they can be directly tied to the quality of the photophysics of 2D perovskites;
10.1038/s41570-021-00335-9, 10.1002/anie.202304515, 10.1039/D3DT01627G, 10.1021/acsmaterialsau.4c00101 would
support these discussions well. (In fact, the cited ref. 34, 35 discussed Jahn-Teller distortion but not lone pair expression).
Similarly, the importance of the main structural parameters θtilt and σ22 used are not well-introduced or sufficiently
discussed. Why focus on these particular parameters over others, and what has previous literature shown about the
relevance of these? Is there a reason why the intraoctahedral bond angles (Pb-Br-Pb) that are tied to bandgap and
photophysics are not displayed? Please include such reasonings in the related paragraphs. 
5. Consistent with the above, a key structural parameter table, such as the current (mislabeled) Table S2, should be
presented in the main text. Additional structural parameters could be included if they are important to the structure-property
discussion (see the points above). Furthermore, the values for σ22 in this table seem suspiciously small compared to what is
shown in Figure 1c (though the y axis units are also unlabeled here). Table S1 is not correctly labeled in the SI (currently
there are two Table S2). 
6. The intensity comparisons in Figure 1e are insightful, but I assume these were determined from the ratio of peak areas
given the values? Please carry out PLQY measurements (either relative or absolute) to better quantify the differences
between free and self-trapped emission in the four compositions. This would better frame the novelty of these phases
compared to previously reported broadband emitters (for instance, is this C6 phase a very good emitter with high PLQY?),
but more importantly would better quantify the STE and improve your structure-property relationships. It would be insightful
to know [1] whether there is some threshold that determines when STE will turn on or become more dominant over FE, and
[2] what exactly makes a better broadband emitter, does STE PLQY simply scale with off-centering, or is it more
complicated? 
7. Lines 230-236: The claim that only the latter (anharmonicity of the inorganic lattice) plays a dominant role in electron-
phonon coupling is incorrect. This has been studied quite thoroughly and compared for diverse cation structures (see
10.1038/s41563-018-0081-x and 1002/adma.202208875 as examples). If anything, your results suggest that the similarly
rigid cyclic character of the spacer cations reveals more of the effects of the metal off-centering and lattice anharmonicity
towards electron-phonon coupling, but not necessarily that the spacer cations themselves do not contribute… Please revise
the claims and discussion here and onward to be more globally accurate with the broader literature.

8. Page 2, line 52: The formula for 2D perovskites seems to have a typo and should read A2PbX4. 
9. Page 4, line 82: Is CnHn-1 the correct formula for the rings? Should it not be CnH2n-1?

how minor differences in their qualities (NH3 depth, steric hinderance, etc.) can lead to dramatically different structures and
properties. 
11. Related to the above point, the use of parentheses in page 5 line 116 is somewhat confusing and should be reworked.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The paper by Y. Zhang discusses how Pb off-centering impacts white-light emission in 2D lead bromide perovskites. The
reviewer finds that while the topic is important, the paper is not suitable for publication in its present form. The reviewer has
several comments listed below that must be addressed before a recommendation can be made. 
In Lines 114-117, the author argued that as the ring size increases, the octahedral tilting is larger, but Line 117-118 said the
opposite. The author should address this discrepancy. 
Most of the figures have issues, which significantly lowered the Reviewer’s enthusiasm in supporting its publication in
Nature Communications. – the reviewer will list a few (non-exhaustive). The PL spectra in Fig. 1 do not have a labeled X
axis (impossible to read). Fig. 1 has no panel labels (this is an issue for all the figures indeed) – hard to correspond these to
the caption. The atoms in Fig. 1a are not indicated. The y axis of the Pb off-centering is not labeled with values. The EDS
mapping does not even show what the red, cyan, and green color correspond to. In Fig. 2a, the color bar indicates that the
PL has a minimal count of 5000 photons everywhere – indicating that no background subtraction was used and hence the
data being unreliable. 
In Lines 203-205 the author assigned the activated phonon mode during TAS in C3 and C4 to mode M3. Could the author
explain why the lower-energy M2 mode was not activated during TAS in C3 and C4, even though these two modes have
comparable strength with M3? Also, different phonon branches could have distinct contributions towards polaron formation.
Does the sole excitation of a single phonon mode during TAS imply that the excitons exclusively couple to this specific
mode? 
How does the long pair activity relate to the formation of Holstein electron-phonon interactions? This connection has been
referred to several times in the manuscript, but it is not a straightforward effect. 
Most perovskites show prominent bandgap renormalization with temperature, while the samples involved in this work show
only minimal emission shifts with temperature. Could the author explain this. 
The author used lattice anharmonicity as a measurement of the lattice softness to exclude its influence on the electron-
phonon coupling strength. However, anharmonicity is an indirect probe of lattice softness. The author should provide more
straightforward evidence on the stiffer lattice in samples with stronger e-p coupling, such as elastic modulus measurements. 
The author argued that increasing ring size leads to stronger electron-phonon coupling, where C6 showed only broad
emission and no free-exciton peaks. This argument does not agree with the fact that (PEA)2PbBr4, (PEA =
phenylethylammonium) and (PMA)2PbBr4, (PMA = phenylmethylammonium) with similar-sized ring spacers showed sharp
excitonic emissions and negligible STE emissions. How could this discrepancy be reconciled?

Comment 2) Please double check the Pb eccentric displacement parameter in Table S1. The value increases 
from 1.6 × 10⁻⁸ for C3 to 174.8 for C6 – a change of ≈10¹⁰ magnitudes that seems implausible. 
Response: We thank the reviewer for this concern. We would like to point out that the Pb off -center 
displacement is governed by the steric hindrance effect of cyclic cations. This is because the steric hindrance 
effect of cyclic cations determines the penetration depth of -NH₃⁺ into the pockets of inorganic octahedra, 
which in turn affects the hydrogen bonding strength between the N and Br atoms, thereby the octahedral 
tilting and the Pb off-center displacement. For smaller cyclic cations (such as C3 and C4), the penetration 
depths are smaller due to their compact ring structure.  This results in a stronger hydrogen bonding  that 
would induce a significant out-of-plane octahedral tilting but with  a trivial Pb off-center displacement 
parameter. In comparison, when the cyclic cation becomes larger (i.e., C5 and C6), the amnio group will 
be further penetrated into the octahedral pockets, leading to a weak hydrogen bonding and significant Pb 
off-center displacement. And the much larger Pb off-center displacement of C6 perovskite may be because 
the much larger-sized C6 cyclic organic cation induces additional structural distortion that enhances the Pb 
off-center displacement. Such extra structural distortion can be verified from its unique orthorhombic phase 
in contrast to the monoclinic phase found in the other three (C3-C5) perovskites.
