Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
Bao et al. fabricated a well-structured (SrRuO
3

2

using high-resolution STEM. For nanostructure analysis, atomic-resolution EDS was a powerful analytical technique,
providing direct insight into the Ru/Ti intermixing, which is the primary focus of this study. Although this study presents
interesting content, there are some questions and insufficient points regarding the data. I hope that the manuscript will
improve after reviewing the following points. 
1) The authors have fabricated a clean superlattice thin film, but it is necessary to confirm whether the sample is uniform over
a wider area. In other words, it should be verified that uniform crystals are observed in lower-magnification images and that
no lattice defects are present. In the case of epitaxial thin films, it should be confirmed whether there is a coherent structure
at the atomic level near the interface with the substrate and at the surface of the thin film, where strain relaxation is often
observed. 
2) All EDS data in Fig. 2a, S1, and S2 have been Wiener-filtered. Since the EDS intensity significantly varies with the
strength of the filtering, raw data without filtering should be used when analyzing small differences in the EDS intensity in
Fig. 2b-e. Additionally, O-K mapping data should also be included in each EDS dataset. 
3) The EDS mapping of the Ru element in Fig. 2a shows more Ru diffusion into the Ti layer on the left half compared to the

Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author): 
Bao et al. study the structural and magnetic properties of (2,2) superlattices (SLs), grown by pulsed laser deposition (PLD).
The SLs are formed by two perovskite oxides, the ferromagnetic metal SrRuO3 (SRO) and the insulator SrTiO3 (STO). They
focus in particular on the asymmetric Ru/Ti interdiffusion at the two different kinds of interface, and how this impacts the
measured properties. The authors have used a wide range of state-of-the-art techniques to examine the samples, including
magnetisation and resistivity measurements, scanning transmission electron microscopy (STEM, with energy dispersive,
EDS, mapping), as well as x-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) and circular dichroism (XMCD). The authors have also
carried out some density functional theory (DFT) calculations to show the tendency of these SLs to interdiffuse, as well as
making some estimate of the change in the magnetic moments on the various key elements (Ru and Ti). A key claim in this
paper is that the interdiffusion is driven by differences in the PLD repetition frequency, and that this difference between the
various samples directly impacts the anomalous Hall effect (AHE) and magnetic properties via tuning of the Berry phase
curvature. 
Overall I find the quality of the experimental work high in this paper. The authors have clearly thought carefully about the
results and have tried to present as complete a picture of the system as they are able to at this stage. There are however a
number of issues I would like to have some clarification on before I can recommend this work for publication – further review
is needed. 
Below I list various Major and Minor questions I have. 
Major issues: 
1. Line 80 onwards, and Methods: at no point do the authors state the number of SL repeats created in their structures, nor
whether each sample has the same number of repeats. This is critical, since it is quite likely that the degree of intermixing is
depending on the position within the larger SL: in other words interfaces nearer the substrate may be quite different to those
further away. It is important that the authors discuss this point, and state at what position the STEM data are taken. Clearly
many of the other measurements are volume averaging, but still it is important to clarify that all of the samples are of the
same total number of SL repeats. 
2. Line 91 onwards: at this point the Berry curvature is *stated* to change, rather than suggesting this as a *possible* reason
for the observed changes. However the authors have no clear evidence of this, or any modelling to back this up. Is this an
intrinsic change the bandstructure? Or due to extrinsic effects? This was not clear at all and should be made more explicit
(see also point 6). 
3. Line 111: the authors state that the resistivity of the samples increases at lower temperatures due to “weak-localization”. Is
this 2D or 3D localization? How has this been established? Is there previous work that the authors can refer to? If not, then I
am not convinced that their interpretation is true (although it might not be that surprising) based on the data they have shown.
4. The EDS data were normalised by the Sr concentration. In the growth conditions used it is very likely that the STO layers
have Sr vacancies in them. It is not so clear that the SRO layer will have the same concentration of Sr vacancies when
compared to the STO. In my understanding the authors’ analysis has removed any possible evidence of this kind of issue,

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 4 -->

and I am concerned that this impacts the interpretation of the data, since possible Sr gradients are excluded by design. It is
important for the authors to show the raw EDS data for a typical sample before this normalisation is done. 
5. Lines 151-2: the authors state that “… Ru atoms of the RuA layer are more likely to diffuse to the TiB layer…”. Is such a
simple interpretation enough to explain the phenomena? Surely the TiA layer is also more likely to diffuse into the RuB layer
for similar reasons. I do not agree with this simple argument to explain the observed asymmetry – I think the authors should
provide more details here about the microscopic mechanism. 
6. A major issue regards the interpretation of the AHE data. The authors claim that this due to a direct tuning of the Berry
curvature. However they do not discuss the difference between intrinsic tuning of the band structure, and extrinsic effects
due to disorder. The former needs some DFT modelling, which has not been carried out. I did not find the discussion here
particularly useful, and I would prefer more discussion here, and less on the DFT studies of the interdiffusion (see next
point). 
7. I am concerned by the use of DFT to justify the instability of the heterointerface to diffusion. Since the calculations are at T
= 0 K, and the PLD process highly kinetic, I do not see how this work is useful at all in helping understand what is a highly
complex process. Personally I would remove this section. 
8. Finally on the XMCD data – I am not an expert here, but I note that the reversal of the magnetic field (in addition to the
reversal of the helicity of the light polarisation) was not carried out. My understanding is that this gives the risk of introducing
errors in the measurement if the degree of circular polarisation is not perfect. This is especially important given the very
small signal detected on the Ti site – this seems to be close to the resolution of XMCD – some more detailed discussion are
needed. 
Minor issues: Broadly there are various minor issues with the language in parts of the paper. Although not complete, some
that I spotted were as follows. While I would not expect the authors to agree with every one of the comments below, and
would not want to use correcting these as a requirement for accepting the manuscript, I hope the authors are willing to adjust
some of their language in response to the comments below. 
Lines 18-22: These read more like the first lines of an introduction rather than an abstract, so I would remove them from this
part. 
Line 28: I do not agree with the authors that the asymmetry is “completely tunable”. To be strict, the asymmetry cannot be
switched off in these SLs. I would modify this phrase. 
Line 44: I would not class “complex oxide(s)” as a unique family of materials – these types of phrases are common but not
especially helpful or precise. I am also not sure I agree with the idea of symmetry being the “fundamental” tuning knobs. 
Line 64: remove “analogous” – I do not think this word fits here. 
Line 88: enhances   increases. 
Line 96: “relatively heavy”. 
Line 100: “the Berry phase”. 
Lines 113-114 and later: it is important to use “lower” and “higher” when referring to frequencies, temperatures etc rather than
the absolutes “low” and “high”. 
Line 133-4: these are *averaged* line profiles over the areas shown. This should be stated more clearly (I think referring to
them as line profiles is unhelpful in that regard). 
Line 165 onwards: I think the phrase “spin flip” can be misinterpreted when discussing ferromagnet hysteresis loops – I think
the term “magnetisation reversal” is more appropriate. 
Line 245: I would remove the word “impossible” and change it to “too computationally time consuming”. 
Reviewer #3 (Remarks to the Author): 
Dear Editor, the manuscript by Bao et al hypothesizes on the possibility to change the inversion symmetry at interfaces in
SRO/STO super lattices and its effect on magnetic properties. While I find the idea intriguing and the overall clarity of
presentation quite in line what one would expect to see. I have one major objection and one remark. These need to be
addressed before the manuscript can be deemed suitable for (any) publication with current narrative: 
1)The overall hypothesis hinges on the idea of the possibility to manipulate the inversion symmetry at interfaces. The
objection I have is that in the manuscript, there is no evidence that such changes are effective. There needs to be some
(structural) evidence that the films that exhibit a systematic change in the (magneto) transport properties, also show this in
terms of the symmetry at the interfaces. Without it, the trends might very well be just a change in overall (dis)order. 
2) The presented XRD data does not show any trends whereas there are significant changes to be seen in the peak widths.
Also, it is not very clear what Bragg peak we see here (at least not for me). 
Finally, very generally, while the authors do present a very interesting idea and case, the style of the manuscript is rather
tendentious. I would recommend the authors to focus a bit more on the falsification of other possible hypothesis for the
observed phenomena.

Reviewer #3 (Remarks to the Author): 
Dear Editor, I have reviewed the comments by the referees of the previous round as well as the responses of the authors.
For the most part I believe the responses are adequate and the proposed changes do improve the overall quality of the
manuscript. The only thing I think is still missing is a, as referee 1 already alluded to, good measure of the uniformity of the

needed insight and convincing to draw the conclusions suggested by the authors. 
I suggest the authors make an attempt to add this analysis to the manuscript, without it publishing would be premature in my
view.

suggestion, we have performed the X -ray reflectivity (XRR) measurements which 
cover a large area and provide a large area check to the STEM data.  As shown in the 
following Figure RR1, the 2 Hz grown sample has a much stronger superlattice peak 
than the 0.5 Hz sample near 2.6 deg, which corresponds to the (001) superlattice peak. 
It agrees to the STEM results that the higher frequency film has more distinct 
superlattice structure, while the lower frequency film tends to have stronger level of 
intermixing between SRO and STO.  
 
We also performed model fitting to the data using the software attached to the XRD 
instrument (Panalytical Co.). In order to simplify the fitting, we assign two layers on 
top of the substrate, the superlattice of 2SrTiO3-2SrRuO3 (approximately 1 4 to 15  
repeats) and a top covering layer of 2SrTiO3. The fitted parameters are the roughness 
of each layer. Overall, the fittings in both data sets are similar, which give a relatively 
large roughness (0.4 nm) near the substrate , a negligible roughness within the 
superlattice (0.02 nm), and a small roughness in the top layer (0.05 nm). Nevertheless, 
neither of the fits are perfect, which indicate that these results are only good estimates. 
In addition, we have also tried more complicated multilayer models but the fitted results 
are not as good as the simple two-layer model.  
 
Therefore, we conclude that the XRR data reveal more distinct superlattice structure in 
the 2 Hz sample than in the 0.5 Hz sample, agree ing to the STEM measurements. We 
have added XRR data to SI Fig. S6 and added a short discussion of the data in page 8, 
paragraph 1 of the main text. 
 
With the new data and semi-quantitative analysis, we believe our manuscript is ready 
to be published in Communications Materials. We appreciate the reviewer ’s valuable 
suggestions and comments very much.
