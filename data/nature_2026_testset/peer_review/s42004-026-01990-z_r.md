Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this study, the authors identified CBDP acts as a novel NAM of CB1R by in vitro assay, a naturally homolog of CBD
isolated from FM2 chemovar of C.sativa. Through computational modeling combined with single-point site-directed
mutagenesis, they demonstrated that CBDP interacts with two distinct allosteric sites. Molecular dynamics simulations were
further employed to elucidate the underlying allosteric mechanism of CBDP. The authors used tetrad assay to evaluate the
pharmacological effects of CBDP. 
Nevertheless, the authors should provide some new analyses to support their arguments more rigorous, my main concerns
are as follows: 
1
、
 This study examined the NAM effect of CBDP that inhibit CP55940-induced CB1R activation. In the previous studies,
several allosteric ligands can modulate not only the potency or efficacy but also the affinity of orthosteric ligands. Does the
CBDP affects the binding affinity of CP55940? I recommend referring to the methodological approach described in the work
of Xiao P et al (PMID: 33571432) for assessing the effects of allosteric ligands on orthosteric ligand affinity, and providing
corresponding experimental data and discussion. 
2
、
 In the [3H]-SR141716A radioligand competition binding assay, the concentration of [3H]-SR141716A was set at 1 nM.
Did the authors evaluate the saturation binding of the [3H]-SR141716A among WT and different mutants? How can it be
ensured that the binding baselines are comparable between WT and mutant conditions at 1 nM [3H]-SR141716A? Please
provide the corresponding data for both WT and mutant receptors. The validation approach described in the author’s
reference 21 (PMID: 33571432) may serve as a useful reference. 
3
、
 For the intracellular allosteric site, the single-point site-directed mutagenesis data presented only S4018.47 and
D4038.49. However, Fig. 2(c) and subsequent MD interaction analyses indicate interactions between CBDP and several
other residues within this binding site, particularly S1523.39, L3416.33, T3446.36, and R4008.46. Could the authors clarify
whether these additional residues that constitute the allosteric pocket also influence the affinity of CBDP? Please provide
the corresponding mutagenesis data for intracellular allosteric site. 
4
、
 This manuscript explored the binding modes of CBDP at two allosteric sites through MD simulations. However, it did not
provide complementary in vitro experimental validation to assess how these sites influence CBDP-induced allosteric
modulation of CB1R downstream signaling. It is recommended that the authors refer to the approach described in the work
of Yang X et al (PMID: 35637350), where the effects of ORG27569 allosteric site mutants on allosteric modulation were
experimentally evaluated. Please present corresponding data addressing the impact of the MD-identified residues on
CBDP’s allosteric effects. In addition, the MD analysis indicated that CBDP exhibits different binding affinities at the two
allosteric sites. It remains unclear whether such differences in binding affinity correspond to distinct allosteric effects. The
authors are encouraged to provide relevant data to clarify whether CBDP exerts differential allosteric modulation depending
on the binding site. 
5
、
 Both the MD simulations and in vivo experiments employed THC as the orthosteric ligand to investigate the properties of
CBDP. However, the characterization of CBDP’s allosteric features in this study was performed using CP55940 as the
orthosteric ligand. Furthermore, in vivo studies assessing analgesia, catalepsy, and core body temperature, co-
administration of CBDP with THC did not alter the pharmacological effects of THC. This raises the question of why THC was
selected as the orthosteric ligand in the MD simulations and in vivo experiments, rather than maintaining consistency with
CP55940. More importantly, does CBDP’s allosteric modulation exhibit probe dependence? The authors are requested to
provide supporting data to address this issue.
