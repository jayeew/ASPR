Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript introduces a novel and exciting approach to implementing Schrödinger dynamics in circuit metamaterials.
Based on this, the authors present compelling experimental evidence for topological braiding and dynamical phase
transitions in time reflection and refraction. The concept of topological braiding was proposed very recently and has never
before been experimentally demonstrated, making this work a significant contribution to the field. The experimental work is
clearly well-executed, and the results are promising. Before I make my final recommendation, I would encourage the authors
to address a few points to further strengthen the manuscript. 
1. The theoretical work (Ref. 55) that proposes one of the core physics of this work (topological braiding in time reflection
and refraction) should be well introduced in the introduction; In addition, the dynamical phase transition, another core
physics, has been theoretically and experimentally investigated in other physical systems [Ref. 56-59, and Phys. Rev. B
100, 024310 (2019); Phys. Rev. Lett. 124, 043001 (2020); Phys. Rev. Lett. 126, 200602 (2021)]. The author should point out
the theoretical and experimental progress of the circuit experiment compared to previous research. It is necessary for people
to understand the research background and accurately evaluate the contribution of this work. 
2. In the introduction, the authors state that ‘However, realizing Schrodinger dynamics in circuit metamaterials remains a
significant experimental challenge’. It seems that topological circuits are also governed by the Schrodinger-like equation
(Ref. [51-54]) and the temporal dynamics can be found in the work: Nat Commun 16, 198 (2025). In contrast to these works
that do not need additional nodes, the implementation in this work uses additional circuit nodes to separately encode the
real and imaginary parts of the wavefunctions, which is obviously more complex than others. Could the authors explain the
advantage of this new setup compared with the formers? 
3. On page 5, the authors state: ‘The initial state |ψi−(k)
⟩
 is prepared by making a Fourier transformation and modulating it
with a Gaussian envelope, such that…’. 
Do the authors mean that initial state is prepared by making a Fourier transformation

5. Fig. 1c is not mentioned in the main text. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors experimentally demonstrate that temporal scattering processes exhibit two distinct topological features:
eigenstate topological braiding and dynamical topological phase transitions. Expanding on their earlier theoretical work
(Ref. [50]), they implement various temporal scattering processes in circuit metamaterials and observe diverse topological
braiding and linking structures, establishing their utility as probes of bulk topology. Notably, the linking numbers of these
braiding structures are determined by the difference in topological winding numbers across the temporal boundary. Given
the novelty and soundness of the experimental results, I’d like to recommend this work for publication if the authors fully
address the following comments: 
1.Is the temporal boundary necessary? I mean, the authors could simply initialize the system H_f in the eigenstate
|\psi^{i}\rangle of H_i and observe its evolution under H_f to determine r_{+} and r_{-} via Eq.(2). It is unclear why introducing
a topological system first, followed by a temporal boundary to scatter the wavefunction, is necessary for this analysis.

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 2 -->

2.Is necessary for H_f to have two eigenstates (i.e., two bands)? If H_f is instead a single-site lattice system (with only one
band), forward and backward waves may still exist at the temporal boundary. However, would the resulting dynamics
reproduce the key findings shown in Fig. 1? 
3.Since knots are a primary braiding structure, is it possible to have knot-like structures in this system? If so, could the
authors discuss their potential formations? I suspect they might emerge when H_i is chosen to be a gapless system, though I
am uncertain. 
4.Dynamical topological phase transitions are really interesting, as they demonstrate a potential method to identify the
topological difference between H_i and H_f through temporal scattering. I have two questions: (1) The dynamical topological
invariant in Eq. (7) appears to take multiple values (e.g, |v_d|>1). Could the authors show some examples? (2) In Fig. 5(e,f),
the dynamics for the braiding case with v=−1 closely resemble those for the braiding with v=1. Why do these topologically
distinct braiding structures share nearly identical rate functions and dynamical topological invariants? 
5.Could this framework be generalized to four-strand systems (because of chiral symmetry) for realizing non-Abelian
braiding? For instance, by implementing a four-band Hamiltonian as H_f, one would obtain four eigenstates in Eq.(2).
However, the connection between this non-Abelian braiding topology and the Abelian (integer-valued) topological invariants
(e.g., winding number) remains unclear. This may be beyond the scope of the current work, but I would appreciate it if the
authors could give some discussions about how these distinct topological properties might be reconciled within their
theoretical framework. 
Minor comments: 
1.It looks Fig.1(c) does not appear to be referred in the main text. Please refer it in the proper place. 
2.The supplementary material is quite informative. I suggest the authors consider integrating some of its key parts into the
Methods section of the main text. Currently, the Methods section only focuses on sample fabrication and temporal boundary
realizations. Important details, such as the formation of dynamic topological phase transition, the mathematical definition of
the Pancharatnam geometrical phase in Eq. (7), and measurement scheme, would be more appropriately placed there.

Our reply: We greatly thank the reviewer for this insightful question. We emphasize 
that the results in this work demonstrate the Abelian topology. However, the framework 
can be straightforwardly applied to multi-band non-Abelian Hamiltonians. Then, there 
emerges multiple scattering channels [Eq. (2)], giving rise to an intricate multi -strand 
link. In such cases, the braiding of the scattering coefficients is not simply connected 
to the topological invariants or the Berry phases associated with the bands, which are 
not integer times of π for the non -Abelian topology. Therefore, the exact 
correspondence between them [Eq. (3)] remains unclear and deserves to be further 
explored. Nevertheless, we point out that distinct topological phases before and after 
the temporal boundary can be characterized through dynamical topological phase 
transitions. 
To be specific, in the temporal scattering, one can extract the temporal scattering 
coefficients, which are directly related to the Loschmidt amplitude with Eq. (6) in the 
main text. When the system evolves across different topological phases, the Loschmidt 
amplitude exhibits nonanalytic points at critical times, indicating the occurrence of a 
dynamical to pological phase transition. These singularities are accompanied by 
quantized jumps in the dynamical topological order parameters, thereby providing a 
clear dynamical signature for distinguishing the topological phases before and after the 
temporal scattering. 
In new Supplementary Note 9, we have included a brief discussion on the 
extension to non-Abelian topology and the corresponding dynamical topological phase 
transitions.
