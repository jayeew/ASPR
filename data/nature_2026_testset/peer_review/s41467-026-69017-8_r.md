Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript, NCOMMS-25-12523-T, on “Engineered Internal Architecture of Core-Shell Lipid Nanoparticles Promotes
Efficient mRNA Endosomal Release”, submitted to Nat. Com., reports on a new strategy in mRNA-LNP structure
engineering/design that shows a marked increase in mRNA delivery efficiency. The work presented is comprehensive,
covering structural and stability characterization of the novel mRNA-loaded gold-lipid nanoparticles (Au-LNP), in vitro
evaluation of mRNA expression efficiency in two cell types, in vivo testing in mouse models, biocompatibility and biosafety
evaluation. Of note are the higher mRNA expression both in in vitro and in vivo models of the Au-LNPs compared to the
benchmarking MC3-LNP and their superior efficacy in two mRNA therapeutic applications. 
The work will be of notable significance in the field as mRNA endosomal escape into the cytosol has been identified as the
bottleneck in LNP-mediated therapeutics (Chatterjee et al., PNAS, 2024), while organ-specific mRNA accumulation and
translation is a persistent challenge for targeted delivery of mRNA drugs (Su et al., nature communications, 2024). 
Most of the conclusions and claims are supported, however I would like the authors to address the following aspects: 
- The molecular exchange of PEG on AuNPs by IL MC3, identified by the disappearance of the PEG peak at 2875 cm-1 in
the FTIR spectra is not correct. The Au-PEG is in fact flat in the range of 3000-2000 cm-1. 
- The authors claim that membrane disruption by Au-LNPs “decreased as pH rose from below 4.5, then increased—peaking
at pH 5.5—before declining again at more neutral pH values” while that “of traditional LNPs consistently decreased with
increasing pH”. The trends are the same, but the absolute values are systematically lower for LNPs. Also figure 2 g has no
measurement at physiological pH of 7.4 and the disruption looks like it’s around zero for both formulations, questioning the
claim that “Notably, at physiological pH, Au-LNPs induced greater membrane disruption than conventional LNPs”. 
- The authors claim that “The enhanced membrane destabilization near pH 5.5 may be attributed to significant charge
variations that promote hexagonal-phase lipid structures, which are more disruptive to membranes.” Is this based on
unpublished data or on literature? 
- The structural characterization based on TEM and DLS from Figure 2 presents some inconsistencies. While the authors
claim that Au-LNPs have an average diameter of 80 nm, the inset in Figure 2d is more around 45-50 nm in diameter. Is the
value mentioned based on image analysis? And if so, what is the size distribution and how many TEM images were
collected and analysed? Also, why was there no TEM of the traditional LNP? Finally, why haven’t the authors used cryo-EM
instead of TEM as the negative-staining and then drying of LNPs might affect their original morphology. 
- For the DLS data, the authors do not mention the polydispersity index, which is a critical quality attribute for NA-based
therapeutics applications. 
- How did the authors come to the conclusion that “Across various mRNA doses and time points, Au-LNPs significantly
reduced endosomal acidification compared to traditional LNPs (Figures 4a–c, Figure S3)” simply by tracking “Cy5-labeled
mRNA and lysotracker-labeled lysosomes following transfection to determine whether Au-LNPs alter endosomal
maturation”? 
- In the Methods section the authors mention that “The lipid molar ratio of LNPs has not been adjusted”. Can the authors
explain what is meant by that and why they did that, and whether the same is true also for the Au-LNPs? How can the
authors be sure of the mechanistic details of the endosomal escape they present in Figure 1 if they don’t control the molar
ratio in the LNPs? 
- The Methods section is poorly written and in some parts it is more like a set of instructions or a protocol for a student to
follow. It is also not detailed enough for the work to be reproduced.

Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript proposes advances in the technology of LNP for RNA delivery by including a core of AuNPs. In particular,
the authors propose a structural optimization of AuNP core for enhancement of RNA delivery. 
Following a physical-chemical characterization, the AuLNPs have been studied in terms of in vitro transfection by measuring
luciferase activity HeLa and 293 cells and mRNA delivery has been investigated by confocal microscopy. The authors
showed that AuLNPs have a transfection efficiency significantly superior to that achieved by LNP. TEM analysis of cells
incubated with Au-LNP showed enlarged vesicles attributed to a stronger membrane disruption capability of Au-LNP. More
in details, the authors hypothesize that positively charged core of AuNPs attracts negatively charged mRNA, “drawing it
closer to the core region, while simultaneously repelling positively charged ionizable lipids and pushing them toward the
endosomal membrane”. The superiority of the AuLNP has also been demonstrated in vivo by two different administration
routes. Enhanced immunogenicity of SARS-CoV-2 Spike-mRNA Vaccines has been demonstrated; on the other hand, a
significant reduction in tumor volume and extension in survival rate has been obtained. 
First of all, the rationale for using AuNP as core for LNP is not clear. Actually, the authors motivate the choice of the AuNP
with the high electron density together with its biocompatibility. However, the electron density cannot be considered an
advantage for drug delivery purposes. Moreover, according to the literature, the biocompatibility of gold cannot be
considered superior to that of other biomaterials, such as lipids or other inorganic materials. Moreover, the authors affirm that
a further advantage of AuNP is their versatile surface modifications. However, in this system the AuNP are the core of the
core/shell particles, thus not requiring surface modifications. 
The authors affirm that AuNP are “ideal” marker for localization of LNP by TEM; however, while AuNP are useful to study NP
localization and structure, the choice of the core for drug delivery purpose in not presented. Moreover, also when AuNP are
used for visualization into the cell, the visualization of AuNP does not correspond to the visualization of the LNP that, once
into the cells, should lose their integrity. 
The authors affirm that “To achieve efficient endosomal escape and mRNA capture, we require Au core that responds well to
changes in pH.” 
However, while for ionizable lipids the mechanism of endosomal escape is well-know, any mechanism of
endosomal escape is clearly described here for the AuNPs. 
The preparation protocol of the core-shell LNP arises many concerns. The authors prepared Au-PEG-NP by reaction of
PEG-SH with the preformed AuNPs. Then, they perform a ligand exchange demonstrated by change of the solubility of
AuNPs from the aqueous to organic solvent. It is not clear what is the mechanism for PEGylation. Have the authors
performed a chemical conjugation? If the case, mechanism of “ligand exchange” is not clear once mixed with the lipids.
Finally, in the case the PEGylation is required for the core-shell NP in which lipids for the outer layer, PEGylated lipids can
be used thus avoiding a previous conjugation of PEG to the AuNPs.

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 3 -->

The authors prepared the IL-AuNPs by mixing the AU-PEG NPs with cationic (DOTAP) or ionizable (SM102) lipids. The Au-
LNP are then prepared by mixing the IL-Au NP with the RNA, that should provide lipoplexes. Thus, it is not clear how this
preparation protocol should provide core-shell LNP encapsulating RNA; on the other hand, the mechanism seems to
provide lipoplexes for DOTAP, while in the case of SM102 any pH change (typically used in the PLN preparation) is used to
modify the charge of the ionizable lipid thus inducing the efficient RNA encapsulation. In line with this, it is not clear what
kind of interaction, hydrophobic or electrostatic, takes place among the AuNP and the lipids. Finally, MC3 is not mentioned
in the preparation method. 
In Figure 2, a core-shell structure is showed; however, it is not reported if the different lipids used, especially cationic and
ionizable, provide a different assembling with the AU-NPs and, consequently different core/shell structure. 
The authors speculate on the lipid assembly to justify results of luciferase expression. In particular, at lines 146-150 they
affirm that AuNP with higher size “interferes with the optimal assembly of the lipid components”. However, they did not
demonstrate what kind of assembly arrive by mixing AuNP and the lipids, as well as when also mixing the RNA. 
The methods of the self-quenching sulforhodamine B (SRB) to study endosomal escape is questionable because suitable to
study the liposomes stability, but not the stability or ensosomal escape of Au-LNP. 
The speculation for the highest transfection efficiency of MC3-AuNP is not convincing because the authors affirm “favorable
zeta potential shift of Au-MC3 under acidic 
Conditions”; actually, it is well known that MC3 switchs to positive zeta potential in acidic condition of the endosomes while
the contribution of AuNP is not clear. Moreover, at line 218, the authors affirm that the Au-LNP reduces the endosomal
acidification favoring RNA release, while the acid pH is known to be responsible of RNA release when using LNP. This
explanation is too superficial and not suitable to justify the observed results. 
Although the manuscript reports positive finding, important concerns on rationale and methodologies make very difficult a
deep evaluation of the proposed technology. The manuscript is, in my opinion, far to be suitable for publication in Nature
Communication. 
Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript the authors incorporated gold into lipid nanoparticles to form a core-shell LNP system for mRNA delivery.
The authors then tested this system for delivery of mRNA in vitro & in vivo, finding that incorporation of gold into the
nanoparticles results in higher expression of mRNA. Due to the incorporation of gold into the LNPs, microscopy-based
evaluation of endosomal escape was performed, and the authors found that the increased mRNA expression may be due to
increased endosomal escape as compared to traditional LNP systems. The authors then show this system provides benefits
in vaccine and cancer therapeutic applications. 
Overall, the results are of high interest as they shed light on methods to increase endosomal escape of LNP systems, which
is a key bottleneck to efficient delivery. However, there are some minor comments that should be addressed to suggest
accepting the manuscript for publication. 
Strengths: 
1. Research is well designed and executed. 
2. Presentation of methods will permit replication. 
3. Data are unambiguous and properly analyzed. 
4. Conclusions are supported by data. 
Below are minor comments: 
1. In figure 2g, the authors state the Au-LNPs disrupt liposomal membranes greater than traditional LNP systems. The graph
seems to show single points at each pH tested, were replicates tested & is there associated statistical analysis to suggest
there is a difference between the systems? 
2. On line 195-196, the authors state “in the Au-LNPs group, we did not observe a peak within our tested dosage range”. It
would be insightful to include further datapoints to assess if/at what dose the protein expression peaks following Au-LNP
delivery. The authors claim the increased expression efficiency of mRNA may be attributed to improving endosomal escape,
but how does this explain the lack of a protein expression peak? 
3. The authors state as a conclusion “making Au-LNPs a viable option for clinical use”. Inclusion of gold nanoparticles into
this system doesn’t seem to affect the safety of the formulation, but there are concerns about the use of gold nanoparticles in
the clinic due to accumulation in clearance organs. Are the gold nanoparticles in this LNP system cleared from the animal?
Do the authors expect the Au-LNP systems would be used with repeat dosage? In this case, further safety testing should be
done.

3- The authors claim that “The enhanced membrane destabilization near pH 5.5 may 
be attributed to significant charge variations that promote hexagonal-phase lipid 
structures, which are more disruptive to membranes.” Is this based on unpublished 
data or on literature? 
 
Response: 
 
     We thank the reviewer for seeking clarification on this mechanistic interpretation 
(Results, page 5). This claim is not based on unpublished data but represents a 
hypothesis drawn from our observations  and interpreted in light of well -established 
literature on ionizable lipids. Specifically, our SRB  release assay showed a biphasic 
disruption pattern with a clear peak at pH5.5 for Au-LNPs, in contrast to the monotonic 
trend observed for conventional LNPs. We interpret this in the context of published 
studies showing that protonation of ionizable lipids at endosomal pH (~5.0–6.5) drives 
charge variations that favor the formation of non -lamellar hexagonal (H _II) phases, 
which destabilize membranes and facilitate cargo release.  
     Foundational studies such as Cullis & Hope (Nature, 1978) and Semple et al. (Nat. 
Biotechnol., 2010) demonstrated that ionizable lipid protonation promotes H _II 
structures responsible for endosomal escape1,2. More recent reports have reinforced this
