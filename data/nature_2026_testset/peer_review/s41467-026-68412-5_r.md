Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this work, Yuan et al. discovered a novel type of PT modification system involving BrxP and BrxC, validated its function,
identified the modification sites, and quantified the frequency. As the only known backbone modification in DNA, PT
modifications are widespread in bacteria and not only have unique chemical properties but also play roles in host defense.
In addition, PT modifications have been developed into new biotechniques such as nucleic acid detection and site-directed
RNA editing. The authors and collaborators elucidated the chemical structure of PT modifications in previous studies,
opening the window to a new epigenetic era. They subsequently uncovered the ssp system, a PT modification-based
bacterial defense system, and the antioxidation mechanism. PT modifications can be specifically recognized by sulfur-
binding domains, with some able to recognize all sequence patterns, even those not discovered in nature at that time. The
discovery of the third type of PT modification system in this study significantly expands the range of PT consensus
sequences, offering a perfect explanation for the recognition issue of PT modifications. 
This work expands our understanding of epigenetic diversity and provides a foundation for epigenetics in gut microbiota and
its association with health and disease. 
Overall it is very well written manuscript and the experiments appear to have been conducted properly. I would recommend
acceptance. Here are some minor issues that I recommend the authors to address: 
1. Line 353, For the 122 bacterial strains where PT dinucleotides were not detected, the authors suggest that this could be
due to the majority of strains lacking the essential dndCD, sspBCD, or brxPC gene clusters, and errors in the reference
genomes cannot be ruled out. Is it possible that gut microbiota can achieve PT modification in a symbiotic environment but
lose this ability when cultured independently? 
2. Figure 2B, in the figure legend, the authors claimed that an edge (lines connecting nodes) is drawn between two nodes
that have a BLAST E-value cutoff of ≥10–100. To my knowledge, a lower E value indicates higher homology, so is there any
mistake in the “cutoff of ≥10–100”? 
3. Line 276, “13,663”. Line 283, “humane” should be “human” 
(Remarks on code availability) 
There is a README file, and the code seems to work well. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors explore the distribution of phosphorothioation genes in a large number of bacteria present in the human gut
microbiome, finding that 6.3% of these organisms possess the genes necessary to produce PT modification in their
genomes. They demonstrate PT modification is indeed present in these strains and in total DNA isolated from human gut
microbiome samples. They identify several putative new PT synthesis systems through gene neighborhood analyses and
characterize PT modification in one of these, a Type 4 BREX system. They show PT modification requires only BrxP and
BrxC, and occurs partially at relatively frequent 3+ basepair motifs which differ for the several BREX Type 4 systems
investigated. The finding that brxP + brxC are the minimal set of genes required for PT modification in the BREX 4 system is

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 2 -->

important. This differs from other BREX Types, such as Type I, where brxA, brxB, brxC, brxX and brxZ are all required for
host modification (methylation). This work is significant as a step toward identifying what role PT epigenetics may play in the
human gut microbiome in health and disease. 
The data presented generally supports the conclusions made. The analysis on the distribution of PT modifications relative to
coding sequences is less convincing as it relies on examining 1 kb regions upstream and downstream of coding regions
(CDS) rather than actual coding and non-coding sequences. Given the dense coding in bacteria, most 1kb regions adjacent
to a CDS will themselves contain coding sequences for other gene(s), so a more precise analysis here seems warranted. 
The methodology of identifying PT systems by identifying homologs of the core PT genes using BLAST then clustering PT
systems based on neighborhood gene analysis is sound and is applied to an impressive number of human gut bacteria for
which genome sequences are available. The methodology can be readily reproduced (as the set of genome sequences
grows). 
The potential for microbes containing PT to contribute to either human health or disease through their role in the human gut
microbiome is worth investigating!

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In the work "Phosphorothioate DNA modification by BREX Type 4 systems in the human gut microbiome." Yuan et al.
searched publicly available human microbiome metagenomes to find the genomic neighborhoods surrounding homologs of
DndC and SspD, enzymes catalyzing the transfer of sulfur to the DNA backbone. In so doing they identified DndC and SspD
homologs not only in the expected context of Dnd and Ssp gene clusters, but also within Type IV BREX systems, suggesting
previously a previously unrecognized pathway of PT modification. Genetic knockouts within culturable representatives of the
hits obtained in their search, as well as heterologous expression of genes in non-PT modifying hosts confirmed that the
BrxC and BrxP of Type IV BREX systems, were sufficient to produce PT modifications of a variety of sequence contexts.
Additionally, they performed PT-seq of select strains expressing Type IV BREX systems, as well as previously
uncharacterized strains encoding Dnd and Ssp gene clusters, expanding the known repertoire of sequence motifs
containing a PT backbone modification. 
The work is significant in that it not only expands the known pathways of PT modification and the sequences targeted for
sulfuration, but reveals an entirely new role of PT modification in the host-dependent modification of self DNA in a BREX
system, functioning analogously methylation of dA at N6. I believe the evidence presented support the claims stated in the
work, with proper interpretation of the results and sufficient methods for others to reproduce the work. 
I think there is sufficiently new ground broken here to demand additional experiments, and it is sufficient for publication
pending minor revisions, but there are a few areas which, if addressed, would elevate the work. First, would be
demonstration of anti-phage activity by the Type 4 BREX systems characterized in this work, i.e. biological function. Perhaps
this function was already demonstrated by in 
earlier work or by other groups, and if it was cited, I might have missed it in the
text. Second would be some preliminary characterization of the BrxP, even a large language model predicted structure, to
account for its unexpected functions, i.e. sulfur transferase, target recognition, and protein-protein interactions. Thirdly, how
does this work square with the recent indentification of a minimal PT-synthesizing enzyme encoded in bacterial and
archaeal extremophiles: TdpC (see An T, Tan Q, Jiang L, Liu L, Jiang X, Liu L, Chang X, Tian X, Deng Z, Gao S, Wang L,
Chen S. A DNA phosphorothioation pathway via adenylated intermediate modulates Tdp machinery. Nat Chem Biol. 2025
Aug;21(8):1160-1170. doi: 10.1038/s41589-024-01832-w. Epub 2025 Jan 16. PMID: 39820821.) Are there any similarities
with this system and the systems uncovered in this work? How might what is known about TdpC affect hypotheses and
results for the minimal sets of genes needed for PT-modification revealed in this work?

(Remarks on code availability) 
I'm not very proficient in shell scripts (.sh) or Python scripts (.py) but the repository is a collection of scripts dealing with
processing the data obtained following Illumina sequencing of libraries prepared from input DNA treated by iodine resulting
in cleavage at phosphorothioate linkages followed by 3' extension and biotin tailing for enrichments. The scripts were
deposited for an earlier publication on the PT-seq method: Yuan Y, DeMott MS, Byrne SR; Global Microbiome Conservancy;
Dedon PC. PT-seq: A method for metagenomic analysis of phosphorothioate epigenetics in complex microbial communities.
bioRxiv [Preprint]. 2024 Jun 3:2024.06.03.597111. doi: 10.1101/2024.06.03.597111. PMID: 38895297; PMCID:
PMC11185561. 
The collection of scripts assumes a certain level of proficiency at the command line and does not provide information
regarding package dependencies, installation, and execution. The scripts themselves, however, do contain comment lines
that likely would be useful to an experienced bioinformatician. 
A closer reading of the Methods section also gives sufficient guidance, in my view.

method to obviate biases. We have added a comment about this in the Results section in lines
