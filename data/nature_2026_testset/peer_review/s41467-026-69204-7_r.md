Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript reports a signal-enhanced lateral flow immunoassay (SELFI) for the early detection of pancreatic ductal
adenocarcinoma (PDAC) via CA19-9 biomarker quantification. The authors fabricated silica-core, gold shell nanoparticles
(SiO
₂
@Au@Au) to generate strong plasmonic coupling “hotspots” for enhanced colorimetric signal. SELFI achieved a 27.6-
fold lower LOD than conventional LFIA and was faster than ELISA in early-stage PDAC diagnosis. Furthermore, the
successful application of this system in diagnosis of PDAC via CA19-9 quantification in serum samples using SELFI
underscores its potential for diagnostic applications. However, several aspects of the manuscript require further clarification,
justification, and improvement to enhance its overall quality and impact. 
Major comments:

instead of a smaller 20 nm? 
The introduction focuses too much on the clinical background of PDAC without closely linking it to the proposed sensing
strategy. Please condense the details of PDAC and emphasize the innovation and gaps addressed by SELFI. 
The introduction of this manuscript lacks a critical review of alternative CA19-9 detection methods, including electrochemical
sensors, Raman spectroscopy, and other enhanced LFA. Performance comparisons (LOD, time, cost) should be added,

Fig 2: relevant work on plasmonic coupling between nanoparticles on a core particle should be reviewed and discussed.

provide controlled experiments or clearly state the limitation.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors have developed a signal-enhanced colorimetric lateral flow immunoassay called SELFI, based on gold
nanoparticles assembled on a silica bead, to detect PDAC through the quantitative analysis of CA19-9 in serum samples.
The system has the potential to be utilised for the early diagnosis of PDAC, as promising results have been obtained in
early- (resectable disease) and late-stage cases (human samples=40 and 60, respectively). 
SELFI has the potential to be
faster than ELISA and more sensitive than LFIA tests. 
However, cancer cases were compared to healthy cases and not to high-risk symptomatic groups (e.g. chronic pancreatis,
diabetes, familial cases). 
Sensitivity and specificity of the system should be evaluated in these disease groups before any clinical implementation. 
Overall, the colorimetric signal intensity of SELFI was significantly higher than that of conventional lateral flow
immunoassays (LFIA) at the same CA19-9 concentration (the limit of detection (LOD) of CA19-9 in SELFI was 27.6-fold
lower than that of LFIA, and two-fold lower than that of commercially available ELISA (0.3 U mL-1). The SELFI signal
intensity saturated after 15 minutes = quick read out). 
The study included in the manuscript is of relevance to the field, but there are some limitations that should be addressed by
the authors. 
Limitations: 
• Did the authors perform any power calculation to justify why 40 and 60 PDAC samples and 50 healthy cases were
evaluated providing statistical significance? 
• Cancer vs healthy and not high-risk groups (e.g. CP, familial cases). Was the diabetes status considered in the analysis? 
• All subjects were Southeast Asians. Any access to external cohorts for validation studies? 
• Some PDAC patients can be negative for CA19-9, or show low values, similar to those of healthy patients. Are the authors
planning a combination of CA19-9 with another marker to increase the specificity of SELFI? Would a multi-analyte strategy
be possible in SELFI system?

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 3 -->

• SELFI AUC for early-stage cases versus healthy lower than 0.90 (but still higher than ELISA 0.854). 
• Future studies or the next steps are not mentioned. Any validation planned? 
• Introduction: the affirmation ‘pancreatic cancer high incident’ is not correct as the disease is characterised by an overall low
incidence, making screening the general population not feasible. 
• The authors mention the SELFI method is more accurate than conventional 20nm nanoparticles, but the reason why is not
clearly explained for a non-chemist expert community. Is this due to an increase in the signal based on size/diameter of the
construct or only due to the conjugation to SiO2? 
Positive aspects: 
• Samples in the different groups were matched for age and sex. 
• Sex balance was considered (similar number of males and females in all groups) 
• Quick and sensitive method. Good idea as the authors considered the implementation of the system into the healthcare
system (quick read out: just 15 minutes after sample loading compared to for example ELISA (approx. 4 hours) and in low-
income countries. 
• SELFI exhibited a similar signal increase pattern than the well-established ELISA test for CA19-9 detection (comparable
sensitivity), and results are obtained in just 15min. 
• Similar trends were observed not only for CA19-9 but also for prostate-specific antigen (PSA) and intercellular adhesion
molecule 1 (ICAM-1).

Reviewer #5 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Although the work includes an extensive and well-defined analytical analysis of the detection system, we cannot
recommend its publication on the journal due to a lack of novelty. The use of silica particles loaded with AuNPs has already
been reported on lateral flow assays on several occasions and with better performance than in this report. Applying the
operation principle of an existing test (or a very similar one) for a different analyte, is not enough for Nature Communications.
While reading the manuscript, the following concerns were identified: 
1. SELFI abbreviation, although sounds original, may not be the most adequate, since in bibliography lateral flow
immunoassays are often abbreviated as LFIAs. Maybe authors can use “SELFIAs”? 
2. The sentence “Consequently, recent reports have shown that not only silver nanoparticles (NPs) but also alloy-type metal
NPs—such as gold/platinum and gold/iridium—and even NPs with assembled nanostructures, in which quantum dots or
metal NPs are assembled onto silica NPs, have been utilized as probes for LFIA” feels a bit forced into the paragraph. After
explaining that AuNPs are the most common particles, we suggest first mentioning that AgNPs and the other mentioned
examples have been used exhibiting lower (?) detection limits or better sensitivity levels (?). Some values are necessary
here for comparison. Then, after this explanation and as a new sentence, we would mention examples of nanoparticles
carried on bigger structures, such as the silica particles. Authors should include examples of AuNPs on silica structures. As
a guidance, there is a review on lateral flow assays using silica particles as nanocarriers where many examples using
AuNPs are mentioned (see Bioengineering 2022, 9(7), 266.) 
3. The fact that many publications using silica particles dopped with AuNPs for lateral flow assays already exist reduces
significantly the novelty of this work. 
4. Authors report a 28-fold improvement of the detection limit, but the bibliography already reports a 100-fold improvement
following the same strategy (see Anal. Chem. 2014, 86, 7351−7359). 
5. Authors are not using a conjugate pad on their LFIA design. Why is it? Are the silica conjugates not stable? This “old”
LFIA model without conjugate pad also reduces work’s novelty compared to other reports which include it.

Reviewer #1 (PDAC biology): 
 
The authors have employed clever adaptations of conventional lateral flow immunoassays (LFIA) by 
carefully titrating small gaps between assembled gold nanoparticles to find the optimal gap to capture 
immnocomplexes with antigen, thereby creating Signal-Enhanced LFI (SELFI). I commend the authors 
for their advances. The strong positive of the paper is the advanced technology. The weak points of the 
paper are the unclear aspects of the reproducibility of the assay, how it compares  head to head with 
conventional ELISA per microliter of serum tested, and issues with the readout of the assay, as noted 
below. My sense is that the paper is better suited for a technical advance journal. 
 
1. How is the antibody set up in the SELFI system? How was the antibody titered for optimum 
sensitivity and specificity? Is more or less antibody used, per microliter serum tested, than conventional 
ELISA? 
Thank you for your detailed question regarding the use of antibody. In the SELFI system, the carboxyl 
groups of 11-mercaptoundecanoic acid introduced onto the nanoparticle surface are activated through 
an EDC/sulfo- NHS coupling reaction, followed by covalent conjugation of the antibody via amine 
groups to form stable amide bonds. As illustrated in the schematic  diagram, the nitrocellulose (NC) 
membrane contains both test and control line s. In our system, the nanoprobe was conjugated to a 
detector antibody, whereas the test line was immobilized with a capture antibody specific to CA19- 9. 
Accordingly, when CA19-9 was present in the sample, it was bound to the detector antibody of the 
nanoprobe and was subsequently captured by the capture antibody of the test line, exhibiting a 
colorimetric signal. In contrast, the control line functions independently of the analyte and serves as an 
internal control to confirm that the probe flows correctly and binds as expected; therefore, a signal must 
always appear at the control line.  Using this sandwich-type immunoassay format, our SELFI system 
was designed to detect the pancreatic cancer biomarker CA19-9 with both reliability and specificity. 
 
Fig. R1. Schematic illustration of the mechanism of CA19-9 detection in the SELFI system. 
To compare the amount of antibody used in ELISA, we asked the manufacturers and received a response 
stating that the exact amount of antibody immobilized on the probes of commercial ELISA kits is 
proprietary information that cannot be disclosed. Although a direct comparison  was impossible, we 
quantified the amount of antibody conjugated to the nanoprobes used in SELFI.

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 7 -->

The nanoparticle-antibody conjugation process used in this study involved adding 10 μL of a 1 mg mL-
1 CA19-9 detector antibody solution to 1 mL of a 0.1 mg mL -1 nanoparticle mixture. Therefore, to 
measure the amount of unbound antibodies, we analyzed the remaining supernatant and the standard 
CA19-9 detector antibody solution using LC-MS. 
The LC-MS analysis revealed that the standard CA19-9 detector antibody solution exhibited a distinct 
peak with an integrated peak area of 0.362 mAU min at a retention time (RT) of 4.67 minutes. However, 
the supernatant obtained after the conjugation process showed no integrated peak area at the same RT, 
indicating that 10 μg of antibody was fully conjugated to 0.1 mg of nanoparticles. This suggests that 50 
ng of antibody was conjugated to the 0.5 μg nanoprobe used in a single strip of the SELFI system. 
 
Fig. R2. LC-MS analysis chromatogram:  a, Standard CA19- 9 detector antibody solution. b, the 
supernatant after the antibody conjugation process. 
The CA19- 9-related antibodies used in the SELFI system were divided into capture antibodies 
immobilized on an NC membrane and detection antibodies conjugated to nanoparticles. A total of 16 
μL of a 1 mg mL-1 CA19-9 capture antibody solution is sprayed to a single NC membrane sheet using 
a dispenser; a single NC membrane sheet is cut into 70 single test strips. Considering that 1 mg of 
CA19-9 capture and detector antibody solution costs KRW 686,400, the price of the CA19- 9 capture 
antibody solution per strip was approximately KRW 157.87. Furthermore, as mentioned previously, 50 
ng of the antibody conjugated to the nanoparticles was used per strip, which cost approximately KRW 
34.32. Therefore, the cost of completing one strip using the SELFI system was KRW 192.19. 
Considering that the average price of a commercially available COVID-19 LFIA kit in Korea was KRW 
8,000 in current date (September 2025), this represents approximately 2% of the sales cost, indicating 
a significantly lower cost. Consequently, we estimated that the amount of antibody used in the SELFI 
system was not significantly higher than that used in commercial LFIA. 
Based on the reviewer's comments, we quantified the amount of CA19-9-related antibodies used in the 
SELFI system. This demonstrates that excessive quantities  of antibody were not used to enhance the 
colorimetric signal and opens the possibility of further development of the current prototype SELFI 
system by further increasing the quantity of antibody conjugated to the nanoparticle surface, and we are 
deeply hopeful of its potential for further development, considering the positive commercial feedback.

11. AuNPs can exhibit strong plasmonic effects. Why was a SiO₂ core preferred over large AuNPs? 
Discuss this design choice and justify based on signal intensity, reproducibility, or surface chemistry. 
Thank you for your insightful comment. The rationale for designing the SiO2@Au@Au11 nanostructure 
can be explained as follows: 
i) Signal intensity : As you have mentioned, AuNPs inherently exhibit plasmonic effects. However, 
single AuNPs cannot generate significant electromagnetic hotspots unless arranged in closely packed 
assemblies. In contrast, the fabricated SiO 2@Au@Au11 nanostructure, composed of multiple AuNPs 
distributed on the surface of a SiO2 NPs core with an interparticle distance of a few nanometers, supports 
numerous strong hotspots. These hotspots arise from the plasmonic coupling between neighboring 
AuNPs and are known to produce intense localized E -field enhancements. Importantly, this localized 
E-field enhancement, which in turn significantly increases both σ scat and σabs, directly contributes to 
stronger colorimetric signals, as mentioned in our responses to Comments 7 and 8. In contrast, σscat is 
enhanced but σabs is diminished rather than enhanced i n the case of single large AuNPs over a certain 
size of approximately 80–100 nm11, 25, 30. Although the enhancement of σscat can influence and enhance 
the colorimetric signal intensity by increasing the scattering ratio of incident light, it is less effective 
because of the low contrast with the white background of the LFIA system, as mentioned in our response 
to comment 8. In contrast, enhanced σabs leads to stronger light absorption, resulting in a more vivid 
color signal with higher contrast against the LFIA background. For these reasons, the fabricated 
SiO2@Au@Au11 nanoprobe exhibited stronger colorimetric signals than large single AuNPs, indicating 
its advantage in terms of signal intensity. 
ii) Surface chemistry: SiO2 NP, which are used as cores, can be easily modified using silane derivatives 
such as 3-aminopropyltriethoxysilane (APTES) or 3-mercaptopropyltrimethoxysilane (MPTS), and the 
degree of functionalization can be controlled by adjusting the reaction conditions. AuNPs, which are 
located similar to satellites, share a similar surface chemistry with conventional large -sized AuNPs 
because their outermost surface is also composed of Au. This indicates that the well-established ligand 
exchange and bioconjugation strategies for single AuNPs can still be applied to our  SiO2@Au@Au11

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 29 -->

nanoprobes. 
iii) Reproducibility: SiO 2 NPs can be fabricated with controlled sizes using Stöber or modified 
methods31, 32. Furthermore, as mentioned in section (ii), the degree of functionalization can be controlled 
in the case of SiO 2 NPs. These characteristics can increase the reproducibility of SiO 2@Au@Au11 
nanostructure fabrication. However, the attachment of AuNP satellites to large AuNP s could not be 
controlled according to a reported paper33, and which implies that the colorimetric signal intensity from 
Au-Au core-satellite nanostructures might exhibit low reproducibility  owing to the random nanogap 
distance between Au satellites. 
In summary, our SiO 2@Au@Au11 nanoprobes exhibited a higher signal intensity and reproducibility 
than conventional large-sized single AuNPs and similar surface chemistry. These advantages were the 
rationale for selecting the SiO2@Au@Au11 nanostructure as a colorimetric nanoprobe in SELFI, instead 
of the large single AuNPs. Therefore, we have added a description of the rationale for the design of our 
SiO2@Au@Au11 nanostructure, including the reason why silica nanoparticles were selected as the core

Our fabricated nanoparticle consisted of SiO 2 NP and AuNPs, where the AuNPs were located on  the 
surface of the SiO2 NP with nanogaps. The SiO2 NP core was chosen because it can be fabricated easily 
with a controlled size using St öber or its modified methods 50, 51, and the surface of SiO 2 NP can be
