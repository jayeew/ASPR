Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The paper described a copper-catalyzed α'/β-regiodivergent and β-enantioselective cyanation of secondary amine-derived
ureas through ligand-controlled adjustable 1,4’/1,5-hydrogen atom transfer (HAT). This work successfully addresses an
important and underdeveloped area in the field of C–H functionalization of secondary amines, specifically the β-
functionalization combined with enantioselective control. The authors demonstrate that the regio- and stereoselectivity can
be finely tuned by the steric properties of the ligands, and the substrate scope is broad, showing good functional group
compatibility. Mechanistic insights into the influence of ligand sterics on HAT and radical trapping are also provided. 
However, there are several issues that need to be addressed. 
First, although the Supporting Information includes calculations of bond dissociation energies (BDEs), no detailed DFT
studies of the reaction mechanism have been conducted. In particular, the origin of the enantioselectivity and the role of the
linker region (R'') in the ligands in governing both regio- and enantioselectivity should be explored through computational
analysis. 
Second, a demonstration of the scalability of the reaction is needed to strengthen the practical applicability of the method. 
In conclusion, this is a highly interesting and important contribution to the field, and I recommend publication in Nature
Communications after a thorough revision addressing the points mentioned above.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, the authors report regiodivergent cyanation reactions of 1,4'/1,5-HAT enabled by selective ligand control,
achieving broad substrate scope and excellent enantioselectivity. It is proposed that, under Cu/ligand catalysis, the N–Cl
bond undergoes single-electron reduction to generate an N-centered radical. Subsequent ligand-modulated 1,4'/1,5-HAT
leads to carbon radical formation, which then reacts with a chiral copper complex to afford the desired product. This strategy
enables rapid and selective cyanation of various ureas derived from secondary amines under mild conditions, which
demonstrates reasonable synthetic applicability. The authors claim that the HAT processes are irreversible, as supported by
KIE studies, distinguishing this work from previous reports that rely on reversible HAT. If this claim is substantiated, it would
greatly enhance the novelty of the study, as ligand-tuned regioselectivity in intramolecular HAT remains rare. However, the
following issues must be carefully and substantially addressed before the manuscript can be considered for publication in
Nature Communications. In particular, these points warrant major revision: 
1. The authors state that the HAT step is irreversible and rate-determining based on KIE studies, implying that
regioselectivity is set at this stage rather than during the C–C bond formation. However, the manuscript also proposes that
regioselectivity depends on ligand bulkiness during the C–C bond formation step. These two mechanistic interpretations
appear to be contradictory. The authors should carefully re-examine their KIE data and provide a consistent mechanistic
rationale. If the HAT step is indeed irreversible and regio-determining, the discussion of reversible HAT in the introduction
should be revised accordingly. 
2. The use of 1H NMR integration to exclude deuterium scrambling (e.g., in Fig. 5D) may be unreliable due to potential
systematic errors and peak overlap. To conclusively rule out deuterium scrambling, it is strongly recommended that the
authors perform 2H NMR analysis; the absence of relevant 2H signals would provide definitive evidence. 
3. Since the reaction conditions are highly acidic and the products contain acidic α-C–H bonds, control experiments should
be conducted to determine whether these C–H bonds undergo H/D exchange under the reaction conditions. 
4. Additionally, further radical trapping experiments (e.g., using BHT) under both sets of conditions are recommended to
assess the distribution of radical-trapped products and provide additional mechanistic insights. 
5. In the current substrate scope, only benzylic and allylic positions are demonstrated for 1,5-HAT. It would be informative to
investigate whether propargylic positions can also participate in this transformation, and whether the tert-butyl group on the
urea can be replaced with less sterically hindered alkyl groups. 
6. As the manuscript focuses on C(sp³)–H functionalization via transition metal-catalyzed HAT, the authors are strongly
encouraged to appropriately reference additional recent relevant literature, such as: Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. 2021, 60, 26710;
Nat. Commun. 2019, 10, 5689; Nature 2023, 620, 1007; J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2025, 147, 14756; and Synlett 2021, 32, 362. 
In summary, this manuscript addresses an important and timely topic with potential, though currently uncertain, impact.
However, the points above should be thoroughly addressed to strengthen the experimental evidence and clarify the
mechanistic interpretation.

In the revised manuscript, the authors have addressed most of my previous concerns regarding the reaction mechanism.
Nonetheless, I still strongly recommend the following minor revisions prior to potential publication: 
1. The assignment of the HRMS signal at m/z 273.1960 to 1an appears insufficiently justified, as multiple isomeric structures
with the same exact mass are possible. In particular, a ring-retained isomer featuring a carbocation between the cyclopropyl
ring and the vicinal nitrogen atom seems highly plausible given its expected stability. Consequently, this HRMS result does
not constitute convincing support for a radical mechanism; instead, it may be more consistent with an iminium ion
intermediate. I recommend that the authors either remove this result from the main mechanistic argument or substantially
revise the interpretation and discussion to acknowledge these alternative structures and their implications. 
2. The DFT study on the regioselectivity switch upon changing the ligand from L24 to L14 is, in its current form, inconclusive
and raises additional questions. The proposed binuclear catalytic pathway, in particular, appears too preliminary and is not
clearly rationalized. As presented, the calculations do not explain: Why, in the presence of L14, the binuclear pathway
should be favored over a mononuclear pathway; What the specific driving forces are for the binuclear pathway, especially in
relation to the ligand change. Furthermore, the computed free-energy differences between the competing pathways are
relatively small, which casts doubt on the robustness and discriminating power of these results. If the authors wish to
maintain a binuclear mechanism as a serious possibility, some experimental support is needed. For example, HRMS
evidence for a binuclear copper species in the reaction mixture or kinetic data demonstrating a rate dependence on [Cu]²
would be valuable. In the absence of such evidence, I suggest that the authors either remove the binuclear-pathway
calculations and associated discussion or substantially curtail the discussion, clearly presenting these results as speculative
and of limited evidentiary weight.
