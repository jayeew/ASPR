Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this work, authors developed a dual-network injectable hydrogel (GaCuVan&HAcHo-BSA) designed to comprehensively
address osteomyelitis through the following integrated functionalities:
​
 a) Antibacterial Action
​
: Bacterial clearance via
vancomycin (Van) release and physical adsorption of pathogens; b)
​
Immunomodulation
​
: Adsorption of pathogen-associated
molecular patterns (e.g., LTA, LPS) to activate pattern recognition receptors (TLR, NOD, and RIG-I pathways), induce
macrophage M1 polarization, and activate trained immunity through metabolic reprogramming (upregulation of HIF-1α and
COX2); c)
​
 Bone Regeneration
​
: Promotion of osteogenic differentiation in bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs)
via glycyrrhizic acid (Ga) and Cu²
⁺
 to repair bone defects. The antibacterial efficacy, immunostimulatory activity, and
osteoregenerative capacity of the hydrogel were validated through in vitro and in vivo experiments, with demonstrated
prevention of homologous/heterologous pathogen reinfection. In my opinion, it cannot meet the high level of Nature
Communications due to the following major issues: 
​
Deficiencies in Material Design and Limitations
​
 
​
1. Inadequate Biocompatibility and Safety Validation
​
: 
Long-term biocompatibility was inadequately evaluated. Hydrogel degradation products (particularly prolonged Cu²
⁺
release) may induce localized toxicity or systemic side effects.Dynamic responsiveness of the hydrogel to the infected
microenvironment (e.g., pH/enzymatic effects on material stability) remains uninvestigated. 
​
2. Suboptimal Drug Release Control
​
: 
Release profiles of Ga and Van indicate ~80% cumulative release by Day 12, yet the release kinetics were not calibrated to
match clinical demands (e.g., balancing early-stage high-concentration antibacterial action with sustained
immunomodulation).Structural stability of released agents (e.g., Van inactivation via hydrolysis/oxidation) was not verified. 
​
3. Mechanical Performance Limitations
​
: 
Despite injectability and self-healing properties, the compressive strength (~1 MPa) may be insufficient to support load-
bearing bone defects.Long-term stability under dynamic mechanical stress (e.g., joint movement) was not tested. 
​
4. Risks Associated with Metal Ions
​
: 
Although Cu²
⁺
 enhances osteogenesis, its cytotoxic threshold remains undefined. High local concentrations may suppress
cellular viability.Comparative analysis with alternative immunomodulatory/osteogenic ions (e.g., Zn²
⁺
, Mg²
⁺
) was omitted,
limiting material optimization insights. 
​
Shortcomings in Experimental Data and Mechanistic Analysis
​
 
​
1. Limitations of Animal Models
​
: 
Only rat tibial infection models were employed, failing to simulate complex clinical scenarios (e.g., diabetic osteomyelitis or
open fracture infections).Efficacy in immunocompromised or aged animals was not assessed, potentially overestimating
clinical generalizability. 
​
2. Insufficient Long-term Efficacy and Safety Data
​
: 
The maximum experimental duration (28 days) precludes validation of chronic osteomyelitis control or sustained immune
memory (e.g., ≥6-month durability).Systemic immune responses (e.g., cytokine storm risk) and organotoxicity (e.g.,
hepatic/renal dysfunction) were not monitored. 
​
3. Incomplete Control Group Design
​
: 
Comparisons with established antibacterial hydrogels (e.g., silver nanoparticle- or antibiotic-eluting systems) were absent,
hindering competitive advantage demonstration.Individual contributions of Cu²
⁺
 or Ga within the dual-network system were
not isolated, obscuring component synergy.

​
4. Superficial Mechanistic Exploration
​
: 
​
Trained Immunity
​
: Reliance on RNA-seq and ELISA for inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α) lacks direct evidence of
epigenetic regulation (e.g., histone modifications) or metabolic intermediates (e.g., glycolysis metabolites).
​
 Metabolic
Reprogramming
​
: HIF-1α/COX2 upregulation was not correlated with lactate production or ATP levels, leaving metabolic
pathway alterations incompletely resolved.
​
 Immune Cell Crosstalk
​
: Exclusive focus on macrophage polarization overlooks
roles of neutrophils, T cells, and other immune players. 
​
5. Statistical and Reproducibility Concerns
​
: 
Small sample sizes (e.g., n=3) in select experiments may compromise statistical power.Batch-to-batch consistency and
experimental replication details were omitted.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
I have reviewed this paper for the therapy of osteomyelitis via metabolic reprogramming. Authos present an injectable
GaCuVan&HACHO-BSA hydrogel that self-assembles in situ within the bone marrow cavity—the epicenter of trained
immunity—to simultaneously eradicate pathogens, induce innate immune memory, and regenerate bone. In my opinion, it is
suitable for a more specialized journal for publication due to the following major issues. 
1. Lack of Sufficient Conceptual Novelty 
The use of hydrogels for local drug delivery and immunomodulation in bone infection is an active area of research, and
several similar systems have been reported previously. 
Although the combination of glycyrrhizic acid, copper, vancomycin, and oxidized HA is interesting, the dual-network design
and the components themselves are not fundamentally new. 
The claim of inducing “trained immunity” is ambitious but is not sufficiently substantiated by epigenetic or single-cell
mechanistic data (e.g., histone modifications, chromatin accessibility, or single-cell RNA-seq). 
2. Mechanistic Depth Is Incomplete 
The study relies heavily on phenotypic outcomes (bacterial clearance, cytokine levels, osteogenic markers) but does not
provide direct evidence of trained immunity at the epigenetic or transcriptional memory level. 
The metabolic reprogramming data (succinate, lactate, ATP) are correlative and lack functional validation (e.g., through
knockdown of HIF-1α or use of metabolic inhibitors) to establish causality. 
The role of HA in targeting macrophages via CD44 is not experimentally validated in vivo. 
3. Data Presentation and Statistical Rigor 
Some figures lack sufficient quantitative analysis or statistical comparisons across all relevant groups. 
In vivo studies, while comprehensive, do not fully account for potential off-target effects or systemic immune responses. 
The diabetic and aged models are commendable additions but are not deeply integrated into the main narrative or
mechanistic exploration. 
4. Clinical Translation and Model Limitations 
The hydrogel’s efficacy in immunocompromised (diabetic) models was limited, which raises concerns about its broad
applicability.

Long-term safety, biodegradation, and immune memory persistence beyond 28 days are not adequately addressed. 
The study does not compare against current clinical standards of care (e.g., antibiotic-loaded bone cement) in a compelling
manner.

Our response: Thank you very much for your insightful comments, which have been invaluable in 
refining our manuscript. We respectfully wish to highlight the unique innovations of our work and 
summarize how we’ve addressed your concerns through expanded experiments, aiming to 
demonstrate its suitability for publication. 
Core Innovations and Key Findings: 
Our work introduces a dual-network injectable hydrogel (GaCuVan&HACHO-BSA) with a 
paradigm-shifting integration of functionalities, centered on the induction of trained immunity—the 
first reported strategy to harness trained immunity for osteomyelitis therapy and reinfection 
prevention. This innovation addresses a critical gap: conventional treatments focus solely on 
antibacterial clearance, failing to prevent reinfection, while our hydrogel achieves both acute 
control and long-term protection via trained immunity. 
Key breakthroughs include: 
Multifunctional Synergy: The hydrogel combines bacterial adsorption (via its physical 
structure and chemical adhesion) and sustained Van release for rapid antibacterial action, while 
adsorbing pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs, e.g., LTA, LPS) to induce macrophage 
M1 polarization, and drive metabolic reprogramming. This process forms a clear mechanistic chain: 
succinate accumulation and elevated ATP/lactate levels activate HIF-1α, triggering a glycolytic 
burst that promotes IL-1β and IL-17 production—ultimately reinforcing trained immunity induction 
(Fig. 9 and Supplementary Fig. 39). This dual mechanism enables protection against both 
homologous (MRSA/ S. aureus) and heterologous pathogens. 
Osteoregenerative Precision: The combination of Ga and Cu²⁺ significantly promote BMSC 
osteogenesis (via Runx-2/COL1A1 upregulation), addressing bone defect repair alongside infection 
control (Fig. 6). 
Translational Relevance: In vivo, the hydrogel eradicates acute osteomyelitis in rat tibial 
models by day 14, resists reinfection for 21 days (aligning with the temporal window of peripheral 
trained immunity), and retains efficacy in aged rats (18-month-old) by preserving trained immunity 
induction—critical for immunosenescent populations (Fig. 7–10, and Supplementary Fig. 51, and 
Fig. 52). 
 
Additionally, we have systematically addressed each concern with rigorous supplementary 
experiments, as detailed below:

3. Mechanical Performance Limitations: Despite injectability and self-healing properties, the 
compressive strength (~1 MPa) may be insufficient to support load-bearing bone defects. Long-term 
stability under dynamic mechanical stress (e.g., joint movement) was not tested. 
Our response: Thank you for raising this important point regarding mechanical performance. It is 
critical to clarify that the osteomyelitis model addressed in this study—consistent with the model 
depicted in Figure 6a of G. Lu et al.1—differs fundamentally from models of large-segment bone 
defect infections, such as the schematic shown in H. Wu et al.2, as the former is not primarily 
influenced by mechanical stress. 
While a compressive strength of ~1 MPa might seem insufficient for large load-bearing bone 
defects3, our osteomyelitis model involved a small-diameter (2 mm) osseous defect in the tibia—
one that did not compromise the rat’s weight-bearing capacity. The hydrogels were administered 
intramedullary, where they were shielded from direct mechanical loading by the intact cortical 
bone, meaning the local microenvironment of the infection site was not subject to dynamic stress 
from joint movement or weight-bearing. Notably, the core focus of this study was on the hydrogel’s 
injectability into the marrow cavity and its ability to fill the infected space completely—key 
properties for delivering therapeutic components and controlling localized infection—rather than on 
mechanical strength to withstand load-bearing. Additionally, we confirmed that the hydrogel 
maintained stability in the presence of lysozyme (a major enzyme in the infected 
microenvironment), ensuring its structural integrity for sustained therapeutic efficacy during the 
infection resolution period (Supplementary Fig. 8). 
In summary, the focus of this study was on infection control and immune modulation in
