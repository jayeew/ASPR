Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
As one of the original reviewers, I think the authors have done a great job to address my comments. Overall, this study
provides comprehensive and compelling evidence that OSCA/TMEM63 mechanosensitive channels can function as lipid
scramblases. The revised manuscript is much stronger and suitable for publication. 
There are a few minor comments which I think still need to be addressed: 
1) Figure 3H. W472K/G, for a constitutive lipid scrambling mutant, how do the authors know that these cells are not
unhealthy, and hence dying-which also leads to positive annexin-v labeling. Some evidence to show that the mutant
channels are indeed at the surface and do not make the cell unhealthy is an important control. Like they have done for
subsequent experiments. 
2) Figure 7G. The knockdown (KD) data is compelling but there is no report of percentage KD/efficiency for Tmem63a and
Tmem63b in the human oligodendromas. 
3) Figure 6J. Seems like non-red cells are positive for Annexin-V in Tmem63a. With the images provided, it is not possible to
assess whether the same cell is being visualized for the Annexin-V binding. Either larger images or better representatives
might be helpful. 
4) Line: 408: “OSC/TMEM63” missing an “A.”

Author response to reviews 
 
We thank the reviewers and editor for carefully reading our revised manuscript and the 
helpful comments they provided. We have incorporated these suggestions into a new 
revised version of the manuscript. Changes are tracked in the revised manuscript, and we 
include a list of responses and changes below.  
 
Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
 
As one of the original reviewers, I think the authors have done a great job to address my 
comments. Overall, this study provides comprehensive and compelling evidence that 
OSCA/TMEM63 mechanosensitive channels can function as lipid scramblases. The revised 
manuscript is much stronger and suitable for publication. 
Thank you for the kind assessment 
 
There are a few minor comments which I think still need to be addressed: 
1) Figure 3H. W472K/G, for a constitutive lipid scrambling mutant, how do the authors know 
that these cells are not unhealthy, and hence dying-which also leads to positive annexin-v 
labeling. Some evidence to show that the mutant channels are indeed at the surface and do 
not make the cell unhealthy is an important control. Like they have done for subsequent 
experiments. 
This of course is important however as with all scramblase studies is a hard question to answer 
because the cells are AnV positive. The PI- staining doesn’t technically tell us if the cells are 
dying only if they are in fact dead so for these mutants we don ’t think this would strengthen 
our claims. For the WT and W612N mutant we know they are at the surface because we can 
record currents from them electrophysiologically (Fig 3C). Unfortunately, these other mutants 
are not tagged and as such we cannot report on their membrane localization easily.    
 
2) Figure 7G. The knockdown (KD) data is compelling but there is no report of percentage 
KD/efficiency for Tmem63a and Tmem63b in the human oligodendromas. 
We had already validated the efficiency of siRNA using qPCR and we apologise it was not 
included. We have included this in the text.  
 
We treated HOG cells with non-targeting siRNA, TMEM63A or TMEM63B siRNA (which 
resulted in a 44.41 +/- 8.582% and 50.08 +/- 14.75% knockdown in TMEM63A and 
TMEM63B respectively) then exposed them to a high dose of αCD to induce rupture in ~20 
% of the cell population (Fig.7F-G) 
3) Figure 6J. Seems like non-red cells are positive for Annexin-V in Tmem63a. With the 
images provided, it is not possible to assess whether the same cell is being visualized for the 
Annexin-V binding. Either larger images or better representatives might be helpful. 
We replaced these images to make it easier to identify the red cells that are AnV+.  
4) Line: 408: “OSC/TMEM63” missing an “A.” 
Corrected

Minor remaining issues (numbered as in my previous report): 
 
1) An assessment of the statistical significance of the data in Fig. 7E and Fig. S9 – on 
upregulation of TMEM63 in tumor and metastatic cancer cells – should be included. 
Statistical analysis has been added. 
6) I am still not clear as to how “Surface tensions of 10 to 60 mN/m were applied in the xy-
direction using the Berendsen barostat to keep the channel open during production 
simulations”. What were the settings of the barostat (pressure tensor, compressibility, time

7) For the particle-mesh Ewald electrostatics, it would be important to list also the mesh

Barostat settings and PME grid spacing have been added to the methods section (line 678 & 
683) of the revised manuscript  
Surface tensions of 10 to 60 mN/m were applied in the xy-direction using the Berendsen 
barostat with a time constant of 5.0 ps and compressibility of 4.5 × 10−5 bar−1 to keep the 
channel open during production simulations. 
Electrostatic interactions were treated using the Particle Mesh Ewald algorithm with a grid 
spacing of 1.2 Å and a cut off of 12 Å
