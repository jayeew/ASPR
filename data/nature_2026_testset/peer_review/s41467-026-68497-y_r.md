Response to Comments from the Reviewers 
To Reviewer #3: 
I believe the current version is supported by sufficient experiments and calculations to 
support the conclusions. It seems to meet the standards of Nature Communications, 
including the high performance, in situ characterization, and mechanistic analysis. 
Moreover, the authors have resolved most of our previous issues. If I were to be 
extremely critical, I would raise two minor suggestions. 
1. The Ag content seems to have changed after the reaction. 
Response: Thank you for your suggestions. Due to the presence of carbon support, the 
XPS signal of Ag is weak after the reaction. We characterized the content of Ag in the 
metal element (Ag and Cd) for Ag0.07/CdO catalyst after the reaction via XRF (Table 
R1) and found that the content of Ag only demonstrated a small decrease compared to 
the sample  before the reaction . The possible reason for the  decrease may be the 
influence of catalyst reconstruction or electrolyte flow that causing a small amount of 
catalyst detachment.

2. The key comparison sample, Ag/CdCO 3 was evaluated at only one potential; its 
performance should be assessed across different potentials. 
Response: Thank you for your comments. We have added the performance of 
Ag/CdCO3-PM including the chronoamperometry and FEs of urea, NH4+ and NO2- at 
different potentials in Fig. R1. The FEs of byproducts (NH4+, NO2-) are verified by both 
ion chromatography (IC) and UV-vis method (Fig. R1b, 1c). The optimal potential for 
the physical mixed sample is also -0.1 V vs. RHE with a FEurea of 33.5%. A detailed 
supplement has been added to “Mechanism Study” in the revised manuscript  on Page 
12 and “Supporting Information” in Supplementary Fig. S50:  
“Its FE urea and Jurea are measured to be 33.5% and 0.33 mA cm -2 at the optimal 
applied potential (-0.1 V vs. RHE ), which are lower than those of Ag 0.07/CdCO3 
reconstructed from the Ag0.07/CdO catalyst.” 
 
Fig. R1 (a) The chronoamperometry and FE urea of Ag 0.07/CdCO3-PM at difference 
potentials in an H -cell. (b) FENH4+ and (c) FENO2- of Ag0.07/CdCO3-PM at difference 
potentials in an H-cell.

To Reviewer #6: 
I carefully read the related methods in this manuscript. The HPLC and 1H NMR method 
is ok. However, as other reviewers have mentioned, the use of colorimetry for the 
detection of urea or ammonia is difficult to accept in such a high -level journal due to 
potential interference from various factors, including electrolytes. I recommend that the 
authors supplement their analysis with additional ion chromatography data for key 
byproducts (ammonia, nitrate, and nitrite) —including the detection of ammonia after 
urea decomposition via urease method —to validate their findings. This could 
significantly improve the quality of the manuscript. 
Response: Thank you for your suggestions.  We have supplemented analysis with ion 
chromatography (IC) data for ammonia, nitrate, and nitrite on an ion chromatograph 
model 881 Compact IC pro. In IC test of anion, the column type and eluent composition
