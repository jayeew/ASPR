Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript presents a study utilizing a phosphoramidate-based self-immolative linker to expand the applicability of
payloads in antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) beyond conventional designs. Specifically, the authors claim superior in vivo
stability, pharmacokinetics (PK), and efficacy compared to existing approved ADCs. Additionally, the study explores the
versatility and tunability of various payload-linker combinations, which is a commendable effort. However, the manuscript
has several critical concerns and gaps that need to be addressed to be suitable for publication in a high-impact journal like
Nature Communications. Below are detailed comments: 
1. Lack of Comprehensive Background Information 
Comment: While the Introduction briefly mentions the importance of linker design, it lacks sufficient background to

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 2 -->

contextualize the study's significance. The impact of linker design on ADC stability and efficacy could be strengthened by
citing more recent and relevant literature. For instance, the report on Exo-linkers (J. Med. Chem. 2024, 67, 20, 18124–
18138), which shares similarities with the current study's compact and externalized linker design, should be cited.
Additionally, including references to tandem-cleavable linkers and Glu-Val-Cit peptides designed for carboxylesterase
resistance would further enhance the relevance and impact of the Introduction. 
2. Inadequate Stability Testing 
Comment: Given that the linker relies on ester hydrolysis as a trigger, stability testing against key enzymes such as
carboxylesterase and human neutrophil elastase is essential. Without this data, the reliability of the linker design in vivo
remains unsubstantiated. Comprehensive enzyme susceptibility assays should be included to support the claim of linker
stability. 
3. Additional Data for Figure 3 
Comment: While Figure 3 illustrates differences in degradation rates among various esters, additional supporting data is
required to substantiate these observations. Including time-course LC-MS data to track degradation would provide more
robust evidence for the claims made in the manuscript. 
4. Lack of Consideration for Alternative Cleavage Mechanisms 
Comment: The study focuses solely on the targeted cleavage mechanism of the linker but fails to rule out alternative
pathways. Proteases such as cathepsins (e.g., Cathepsin B, L, K, and S), known for their broad substrate specificity, could
potentially cleave the linker. Comprehensive protease susceptibility testing would help establish the specificity of the
designed linker. 
5. Unclear Intracellular Mechanism 
Comment: The manuscript does not clarify the intracellular compartment (e.g., lysosome, late endosome) where linker
cleavage and payload release occur. Imaging studies using fluorescent probes could provide insights into the timing and
location of payload release, adding depth to the mechanistic understanding of the linker. 
6. Insufficient Comparison with T-DXd 
Comment: The claim that the presented ADC is more hydrophilic than T-DXd lacks quantitative validation. Comparative
data, such as HIC retention times, would provide a clear basis for this assertion. Including such data in Figure 3d or as a
supplementary figure is recommended. 
7. Ambiguities in SI HPLC Data 
Comment: The HPLC data in the supplementary information (SI) for antibodies such as Sacituzumab and Datopotomab
contain unclear peaks, suggesting potential impurities. The extra peaks observed in Brentuximab-ADC may indicate non-
specific conjugation. Additional analyses, such as LC-MS or improved HPLC separation, are necessary to resolve these
ambiguities. 
8. Absence of Safety Evaluations 
Comment: The lack of safety evaluations is the most significant concern in this manuscript. The study does not include data
on normal cell uptake or off-target toxicity, both of which are critical for assessing the specificity and safety of the linker and
payload. To align with the standards of Nature Communications, dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) assessments using non-human
primate models (e.g., monkeys) are recommended. These data would substantiate claims of safety and tolerability. 
9. Validation of Novelty and Practicality 
Comment: While the authors emphasize the novelty of the phosphoramidate-based linker derived from ProTide technology,
its distinctiveness compared to existing technologies remains unclear. Furthermore, while the application of diverse
payloads is intriguing, more detailed evaluation of clinically relevant examples, including toxicity profiles and target
specificity, is needed to validate the practicality of this approach. 
This manuscript addresses an important topic in ADC design and demonstrates promising features of the phosphoramidate-
based linker system. However, significant gaps in stability testing, safety evaluation, and mechanistic clarity must be
addressed. Moreover, the claims of novelty and superiority require more robust validation through comprehensive
experiments and comparisons. Addressing these points will significantly enhance the scientific rigor and impact of the study,
making it more suitable for publication in a high-impact journal. 
Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript by Ochtrop and colleagues introduces a series of phosphoramidate-based self-immolative linkers for
antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs). These linkers enable stable attachment and traceless release of both aliphatic and
aromatic alcohol-containing payloads. The research team, with significant experience in ADC development, presents a
detailed study, showcasing the targeted delivery of 10 different hydroxy-containing cytotoxins with various intracellular
mechanisms of action. This excellent work highlights the potential of the phosphoramidate-based linker system to expand
the range of payloads for ADCs. The methodology used in the study is sound, with appropriate techniques employed to

assess linker stability. fully support the publication of this work, pending a few revisions and clarifications noted below: 
1. The authors are leveraging the clinically validated ProTide technology; however, they should cite recent relevant works
on ProTides, such as Jin et al., Acta Pharmaceutica Sinica B (2024) and J. Am. Chem. Soc. (2024, 146, 1, 521–531). A more
thorough literature review is recommended to strengthen the manuscript's foundation. 
2. The authors highlight two key distinctions between their design and clinically validated ProTides: 
A) Their design incorporates a conjugation handle into the core phosphorus(V) [P(V)] structure, allowing covalent
attachment to targeting moieties such as antibodies. To demonstrate the superiority of their design, the authors synthesized
multiple linker systems conjugated to antibodies and conducted thorough studies to evaluate their properties and
performance. I agree with their claim and strongly believe this innovation significantly advances the ProTide concept into the
realm of ADC development. I would like to congratulate the team on conducting such a comprehensive study to support their
claim. 
B) The authors claim that, unlike traditional nucleoside-based ProTides—which rely exclusively on esterase-mediated
cleavage following passive cytosolic uptake—their phosphoramidate-based system allows for payload release via multiple
intracellular and endosomal triggers. Specifically, their lead linker system, based on Ala-Ala for traceless release of
gemcitabine, is supported by extensive data demonstrating protease-mediated cleavage. However, it is essential for the
authors to revise their manuscript and appropriately cite J. Am. Chem. Soc. (2024, 146, 1, 521–531), which was the first
study to utilize carboxypeptidases to enhance ProTide technology. While that study employed fluorescence probes for
targeting purposes, it was the first to report the foundational use of carboxypeptidases in this context. The authors must
clarify this in their manuscript and properly acknowledge the prior contribution. 
3. Page 2- The statement “ available linker systems for alcohol attachment …….none of these functional groups combine
broad applicability, stability in serum, and efficient traceless release” requires further contextual support, particularly given
the team's expertise in ADC development. For instance, the FDA022-BB05 s-amino carbamate self-immolative spacers
used for conjugating aliphatic alcohol-based payloads have demonstrated a manageable safety profile and promising
antitumor activity in metastatic breast cancer (Breast Cancer, Metastatic, Volume 35, Supplement 4, S1420–S1421,
December 2024). Additionally, I recommend citing J. Med. Chem. (2024, 67, 21, 19852–19873). 
4. on page 6, paragraph 2, the phrase “the free carboxylic acid in dialanine linker 12” appears to be incorrect. I believe the
authors meant dialanine linker 11. 
5. In Fig. 4e, the authors compare the stability of trastuzumab deruxtecan, which uses a cleavable peptide-based linker
(GGFG), with construct 15, which utilizes a phosphorus-based linker system. Their data demonstrate that, in terms of ex vivo
stability (rat serum, Fig. 4e), Tras-15-DXd is more stable than the FDA-approved Enhertu. Just out of curiosity, is GGFG
more stable than the Valine-Citrulline (Val-Cit) linker in the presence of carboxylesterase 1C (Ces1C) in mouse and rat
plasma? If not, as carboxylesterase 1C in mouse and rat plasma is known to affect the stability of peptide-based linkers, can
this ex vivo study be considered a valid measure of stability of these two linker systems? 
Experimental section also well written and comprehensive. 
Congratulations again to the team involved in this project. I thoroughly enjoyed reading this work and am confident that it will
generate significant interest among those actively involved in the drug delivery research field.

conditions. Specifically, linker 3–SN38 demonstrated high er stability in rat serum over a 7 -day

Moreover, we performed enzyme susceptibility assays on the lead linker-payloads 3–SN38, 9–SN38, 
and 15–DXd using human neutrophil elastase. We also tested the corresponding ADCs (Sacituzumab-
3–SN38, sacituzumab-9–SN38, trastuzumab-15–DXd and trastuzumab-15-gemcitabine) for 
degradation upon exposure to neutrophil elastase. In these assays, linker–payloads 3–SN38 and 9–

concentrations where the positive control (NAcCys -MC-VC-PAB-MMAE) was efficiently cleaved to

in the neutrophil elastase susceptibility assay demonstrated markedly higher stability against esterase-
mediated cleavage  compared to the isolated linker -payloads. The approved ADC Adcetris, which

degradation, releasing ~90% of the expected NH ₂-Citrulline-PAB-MMAE fragment over  24 hours. In

trastuzumab-15-gemcitabine remained fully intact under identical conditions. Only sacituzumab-9–
SN38 showed partial susceptibility, with moderate isopropy l ester cleavage and ~25% SN38 release.  
These results suggest that while isolated linker–payloads containing cleavable esters such as in LPs 3 
or 9 may be susceptible to degradation by certain esterases, the dipeptide-based LP 15 exhibits greater 
resistance. Furthermore, the corresponding ADCs demonstrate even higher stability in the presence of 
neutrophil elastase, highlighting an improved enzymatic robustness of our phosphoramidate linker 
designs compared to conventional VC-PAB linkers. 
The additional serum stability studies and experimental setup for the human neutrophil elastase assay 
are included in Fig. S6 and in the experimental section 3.7. A paragraph that discusses the susceptibility 
towards cleavage by these enzymes has been added to the manuscript.

Response: We thank the reviewer for this constructive suggestion. To further support the observations 
presented in Figure 3, we conducted additional experiments in which the Linker-Payloads 3–SN38, 9-

Response: We thank the reviewer for carefully looking at our characterization data . We would like to

8. Comment: Absence of Safety Evaluations 
The lack of safety evaluations is the most significant concern in this manuscript. The study does not 
include data on normal cell uptake or off -target toxicity, both of which are critical for assessing the 
specificity and safety of the linker and payload. To align with the standards of Nature Communications, 
dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) assessments using non -human primate models (e.g., monkeys) are

Response:  
We agree with the reviewer and concur with the importance of safety evaluations in ADC development.  
Importantly, all Linker-Payloads in the manuscript were evaluated for non -specific cytotoxicity in vitro 
using isotype control antibodies, and these results are presented as selectivity in Figures 3c, 4d, and 
5d as well as in SI fig. S1, S2, S4 and S8. Additionally, all in vivo efficacy studies included isotype-ADC

are valuable for late -stage development, we do believe that in vivo  toxicity experiments with 
camptothecin-based ADCs extend significantly beyond the exploratory scope of the current manuscript
