Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
The authors summarized recently developed phase-shift TENGs. Fundamental to practical analysis are presented, but there
are lack of in-depth perspective from the authors. Thus, before publishing the manuscript, I would like to ask a few questions.
1. The proposed phase-shift implementation requires additional components such as diodes, which inevitably introduce
electrical losses. A brief discussion on the resulting efficiency degradation is necessary. 
2. Compared to a single-phase configuration, the proposed approach involves a more complex circuit design, which
increases implementation difficulty. 
3. Compared to DC TENG, the advantages of employing a phase-shift strategy are not clearly explained. The authors should
present what phase shifting offers practical advantages over direct DC generation. 
4. Phase-shift TENGs and conventional TENGs show different output performance. How can we directly compare their
output performance? Can you suggest an equation?

Reviewer #3 (Remarks to the Author): 
The topic of this review is timely and relevant, focusing on phase-shift engineered constant-voltage triboelectric
nanogenerators (CV-TENGs) and their role in improving energy harvesting efficiency and practical applicability. As the
authors point out, phase-shift engineering effectively addresses the intrinsic high crest factor (CF) and pulsed-output
limitations of conventional PV-TENGs, enabling continuous CV/CC output and enhanced energy transport efficiency. The
review systematically summarizes fundamental physics, design strategies, system-level optimization, and representative
applications, and includes a broad coverage of recent literature, which is informative for researchers in the field. Therefore, I
recommend the publication of this review on Communications Materials after the authors address the following concerns. 
1. Figure 1e - The description and schematic illustration of the four fundamental working modes of triboelectric
nanogenerators are inaccurate and inconsistent with the commonly accepted classifications in the literature. The authors are
advised to carefully re-examine this section and redraw the figures based on authoritative references to ensure conceptual
and physical correctness. 
2. Line 214 - The manuscript states that when the phase number is a multiple of 2, the CF can be reduced to 1.00. However,
when the phase number is a multiple of 2 but remains relatively small, can the CF still reach 1.00? The authors are advised
to re-examine the relevant literature and revise this statement accordingly.

3. The manuscript repeatedly emphasizes that the optimal phase shift is 1/2n. However, it is not clearly stated whether this
relationship is derived from theoretical analysis, or empirical/numerical observations. The authors should explicitly clarify the
theoretical basis and applicability range of this optimization rule. 
4. lines 289 - In this part of “In-plane phase-shift design”, the textual description does not appear to fully match the
corresponding figure. The authors are advised to re-check the original literature and revise the description to ensure
consistency between the text and the figures. 
5. Line 371 - In this section, the authors claim that charge excitation–coupled phase-shift designs exhibit enhanced
environmental robustness (humidity and wear resistance) compared to single-mechanism designs. However, this conclusion
is supported by only three examples, which is insufficient to substantiate such a general claim. The authors are encouraged
to strengthen this statement by providing additional representative examples and, more importantly, by explaining the
underlying physical mechanisms responsible for the improved environmental robustness. 
6. Table 1 - The table does not clearly demonstrate whether the average power is improved by phase-shift engineering, as
no direct comparison is provided. In addition, the units used for average power are not unified, and TENGs of the same
category are not grouped together for discussion. As a result, the comparative significance of the table is limited. The authors
are encouraged to reconsider the table organization and presentation strategy to enhance its clarity and comparative value. 
7. The section entitled “The application of phase-shift CV-TENG” is relatively concise compared with the technical depth of
earlier sections. The authors are encouraged to further survey the literature and expand this section to provide a more
comprehensive and balanced discussion of practical applications. 
8. Although the author has cited numerous relevant studies in the paper, in some cases the argumentation remains
underdeveloped. The authors are advised to cover more relevant progress to attract more readers of interest: Nano Energy
90 (2021) 106491; Nano Energy 143 (2025) 111289; Nano Energy 140 (2025) 111004. 
9. As this is a review article, each figure (including all subfigures) should explicitly cite the original reference in the figure
caption. The authors should revise all figure captions accordingly.
