Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The main contribution of this work is the proposal of a new dissimilarity measure for comparing networks, based on Network
Hierarchy Entropy (NHE). The authors claim that this metric captures relevant structural information of the networks under
study and propose some scenarios in which it is supposed to perform better than other dissimilarity measures. They also
show that Node and Edge Hierarchy Centrality are efficient in detecting influential spreaders among nodes, within the
context of epidemic spreading on networks. This motivates their use in the definition of the NHE dissimilarity. 
Personally, I believe that the topic of network similarity measures is interesting and useful, although many methods have
already investigated it from several point of views (for two reviews see Tantardini et al. Scientific Reports, 2019 and Wills &
Meyer, Plos One, 2020). 
The idea of considering also properties of edges instead of nodes only is conceptually interesting, even though I am not sure
that the two-nodes shrinking proposed by the author is the best way to implement it. However, in its present form I do not
consider this work as a remarkable improvement in the field of network similarity measures. First, because the definition of
the NHE metric is not intuitive to me and not fully motivated, although I recognize that this is a personal, and thus
questionable, opinion. But more importantly, the claim that the NHE dissimilarity is better than other dissimilarity measures is
not always proven by the results, that I find insufficient to motivate the choice of NHE metric over other dissimilarity
measures. I also have doubts about one of the analysis carried out by the authors and the interpretation of some of the
results that are said to demonstrate the superior performance of NHE (see comments below). 
Overall, I found the manuscript unclearly written and suggest a revision of the language to improve understandability (see
specific comments below). 
Finally, I believe that the reproducibility of the results presents several shortcomings and should be strongly improved (see
below). 
Because of these critical issues, I am not sure this is enough for a contribution to Communications Physics.

- In Supplementary Text S1, line 55: I have some doubts about the computation of node and edge hierarchy centralities. I
assume that the nodes are labelled sequentially from 0 to n-1, as in Fig. S4. The last denominator in C_v^i, that should

should be 2/(n-1-i) and the last term with numerator 1 should be 1/i! . (III) If n is odd and i=(n-1)/2 (central node), then the sum
should only contain terms with 2 at the numerator, and the last term should be 2/i!

than (n/2 -1), then the last term with numerator 2 should be 2/(n-2-i) and the last term with numerator 1 should be 1/i!. (III) if n
is even and i=(n/2)-1, the sum should only contain terms with 2 at the numerator, and the last term should be 2/i!. The
previous considerations also apply to C_v^{i+1} and C_e^{i-1,i} and influence the computation below.

- A useful benchmark to probe the effectiveness of dissimilarity measures, is to classify synthetic networks based on their
generative model and/or parameters (see for example Tantardini et al. Scientific Reports, 2019). An analysis of this kind is

Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This is a review of the article “Network Hierarchy Entropy for Quantifying Graph Dissimilarity”. The authors propose a
method for measuring similarity between networks based on node- and edge-level hierarchical information. They
demonstrate the effectiveness of the method on both synthetic LFR networks and real systems, including temporal networks
and a graph classification task. Overall, the article is well-written, and the proposed method offers an improvement over the
current state-of-the-art. Below are some comments: 
- While dk2.0 preserves the joint-degree distribution of the input network, it does not retain higher-order structures such as
the clustering spectrum. Interestingly, the NHE distance between the Proximity network and its dk2.0 counterpart is very low,
despite the Proximity network exhibiting a high clustering coefficient (0.640). This raises the question of whether the low
dissimilarity is due to the network’s size or some structural redundancy that NHE fails to detect. In contrast, other networks
like Web and Technology show larger distances to their dk2.0 versions, despite having lower clustering. For clearer
comparative visualization, it may be helpful to rescale NHE values relative to each network's baseline. 
- The proposed method relies on shortest-path computations to define node and edge hierarchies. However, in real-world
graphs that contain multiple disconnected components, shortest paths between some node pairs are undefined or infinite.
While the authors mention using only the largest connected component in their experiments, this design choice limits
applicability and raises the question of how the method would generalize to graphs with fragmented structures. A more
general formulation that explicitly handles disconnected pairs, such as assigning infinite distances, using effective
resistance, or incorporating imputed distances, would enhance robustness. 
- The NHE-based network dissimilarity is defined as the Euclidean distance between the node hierarchy entropy (NHE) and
edge hierarchy entropy (EHE) values of two graphs (Eq. 5). However, the scales of NHE and EHE may differ, as they are
computed using separate normalizations (by the number of nodes and edges, respectively) and are derived from different
centrality distributions. As a result, the dissimilarity score may be unintentionally biased toward the component with a larger
numerical range. 
- The authors state that the time complexity of the NHE computation is O(M(N+M)). However, no empirical benchmarks are
provided to assess scalability in practice. For real-world applications involving large graphs (e.g., 10,000 to 100,000 nodes),
it remains unclear how fast two such networks can be compared using NHE. 
- The authors demonstrated that NHE can be applied to a graph classification problem. Can this method be used in general
in the graph isomorphism problem? For instance, how well would it perform in the BREC dataset [1]? Additionally, can we
comment on the expressiveness of NHE in terms of the k-dimensional Weisfeiler-Lehman (k-WL) test? 
- Providing more detail in the Methods section would clarify the procedures and help readers better understand the paper. 
- To improve Figure 2, use identical X- and Y-axis scales across all panels for direct comparability. Also, switch the colorbar
from a diverging to a sequential scale so that the data gradations are represented more intuitively. 
[1] Wang, Y., & Zhang, M. (2023). An empirical study of realized GNN expressiveness. arXiv preprint arXiv:2304.07702.

hierarchy entropy (NHE) calculation. For isolated nodes, the hierarchy struc ture contains only 
the node itself at layer zero. To accurately represent the topological impact of isolation, we 
define the node hierarchy entropy (NHE) and edge hierarchy entropy (EHE) for an isolated 
node as 1.

penalties on modifications that create disconnected components than on those preserving 
connectivity. Additionally, NHE reflects that an edge alteration has a more pronounced impact
