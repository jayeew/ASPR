Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript by Subramani et al connects transcription regulation by DOT1L to the initiation of class switch
recombination (CSR) of antibody genes and “off-target” effects of Activation-induced deaminase (AID). The authors identify
DOT1L as a protein “proximal” to AID in Flp-In 293 TREx cells and then use the CH12F3 mouse cell line derived from B cell
lymphoma for most of the subsequent analyses. Several DOT1L mutant derivatives of CH12F3 have been generated and
experiments comparing WT (lymphoma) and DOT1L mutant conditions have been performed. shRNA knockdown of DOT1L
was used as an additional alternative approach. The authors report diminished CSR frequency, reduced off-target AID-
dependent mutations, and elevated Pol II pause-release upon DOT1L loss. Since Pol II pausing favors AID activity the major
conclusion of the paper is that DOT1L affects AID through its regulation of transcription elongation. 
The strength of this work is in using several DOT1L mutant alleles and revealing the effect of the mutations on CSR and off-
target AID activity leading to gene fusions. Also, the effects of DOT1L on cohesin distribution are interesting. In this regard,
does the downregulation of cohesin affect CSR? 
The weaknesses: 
1. The focus on DOT1L singled out among the numerous AID prays is not well justified. DOT1L complexes specific for cells
used in functional analyses are not investigated. 
2. Lack of direct connection between DOT1L and loci subject to CSR. Since DOT1L has a global effect on transcription, the
indirect role of DOT1L in CSR cannot be excluded. 
3. Claiming the discovery of Pol II pause-release regulation by DOT1L, despite the pioneering C. elegans study in 2013.
Notably, a study using an ESCs reprogramming system showed a functional role for DOT1L-regulated Pol II pause-release
of housekeeping genes (Wille et al. 2022, BioRxiv), like the C. elegans work. 
4. Results, such as in Figure 3 “Dot1l regulates GLTs expression fidelity” and Figure 5 “Global alteration in transcription
output caused by Dot1l loss” touch upon complex subjects that are not well integrated into the main storyline. Figure 3
results are related to the regulation of gene expression plasticity by several factors, including DOT1L, which was
comprehensively described in the Xiao et al Open Biology 2019 publication. The Figure 5 subject is complex and not limited
to the role of DOT1L in Pol II pause release. There is literature connecting DOT1L to enhancer regulation through effects on
chromatin compaction, as well as studies connecting DOT1L to antisense transcription regulation. This was not properly
referenced in the paper. 
I do not find conclusions that gene length affects the mode of transcription regulation by DOT1L (UP or DOWN) particularly
convincing, this part should be removed from the model in Figure 8 (or from the manuscript altogether). 
Data analyses based
on gene function might be more informative, especially if related to the main story of the manuscript. 
The authors relate their Pol II pause-release findings to literature about known pausing factors and their inhibitors: Spt5,
RN7SK, NELF, PAF1, etc. This is not justified by the results. Unless they do experiments and implicate these factors in
CSR, they must instead focus on the similarity of their DOT1L findings to the C. elegans system that lacks NELF and the

Wille et al. 2022, BioRxiv publication. 
5. The introduction gives no background about DOT1L and the current state of our understanding of its effect(s) on
transcription and chromatin organization. 
Specific issues: 
Figure 4A, C: How does total RNA Pol II distribution look in Dot1l +/- conditions? It is expected to change. 
Figure 4B, bottom: Why ChIP-qPCR at the P region is not shown? 
Figure 4C: Analyses using randomized groups of genes with the same # should be performed for control. 
Figure 4F: Show expression changes corresponding to each group of genes. 
Figure 6 A, C: The difference between H3K79me3 distribution in splenic B cells and CH12F3 cells is concerning and must
be addressed. How do these profiles look in unstimulated cells? 
Figure S2 B: Why does CIT lead to H3K79me reduction? 
Triplicate replicates should be shown for all RT-qPCR and ChIP-qPCR data.

(Remarks to the Author) 
Activation Induced Deaminase (AID) is an essential factor that drives somatic hypermutation and class switch recombination
in B Cells leading to antibody diversification. It is well accepted that AID mutates single strand DNA (ssDNA) sequences to
generate point mutations during SHM (V gene sequence mutations) and DNA double strand breaks (DSBs) to cause CSR.
In the process of catalyzing CSR and SHM, AID also incorporates SHM all across the B Cell genome (although at a much
lower frequency), these mutations are related with occurrence of germinal center derived B cell lymphomas. 
A puzzling and
important question is how does AID identify various DNA target sequences, at V genes and its target DNA sequence across

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 6 -->

the B cell genome. Many past studies have related AID activity with transcription states both at sense and antisense
direction, secondary DNA structures like R-loops and G4s (that form during transcription), epigenetics and genome
architecture formation, etc. In this study, the Subramani et al. proposes that the H3K79me1/2/3 inducing enzymes DOT1L
induces AID recruitment to the B cell genome. The authors also propose that DOT1L functions with a similar mechanism to
cause CSR, DOT1L deficient CH12F3 lines are also deficient in CSR. The authors show that DOT1L induces RNA polII
promoter proximal pausing to accumulate RNA polII at AID target DNA sequences to induce AID recruitment (AID binds to
RNA polII robustly and thus this is the likely mechanism). In this regard, the authors show that in absence of DOT1L
promoter proximal paused RNA polII levels decrease but S2 phosphorylated elongating RNA polII increases in the gene
body. Thus, this study also makes the argument that S2 phosphorylated RNA polII is unlikely to recruit AID. Although some
previous studies (cited in the manuscript) have investigated and shown DOT1L’s role in AID recruitment and CSR, neither of
these two studies suggested mechanisms similar to that proposed in this manuscript. Please find below some suggestions
that the authors could consider as they revise the manuscript for publication. Overall, I feel this manuscript advances the field
of AID biology and also provide insights into transcription and promoter-proximal pausing mechanism. 
1. In figure 1, authors present a nice assay with AID-BirA approach for AID interacting proteins. Some highlights of AID
interaction with the NEXT complex and MRN complex was not discussed in the text. NEXT subunits functionally interact
with AID has been shown before and with regards to AID activity regulation (Lim et al, 2017). Similarly, the interaction with
MRN is also relevant for discussion. 
2. 
The inability of AID to stimulate CSR is an important marker for functional 
aspect of its cofactors, in cells that are
deleted/hypomorphic of cofactor activity. 
In Fig. 1A versus 1K, the authors show different efficiencies of CSR in DOT1L
deleted cells (50% versus less that 30%). Some clarity will help since this assay does decide how important DOT1L is for
AID activity (at least during CSR). 
3. This maybe a confusion on my part, but it is not clear to me why in Fig. 4A the H3K79me2/3 ChIP-seq data for DOTL1-/-
cells were not shown. The parallel demonstration of the tracks will make it clear that the mutant cells are deprived of DOT1L
activity and still undergo robust transcription (RNA polII levels) and elongation (S2PRNA polII levels). 
4. The most striking observation in this study is the effect of DOT1L inhibition on AID off-targeting and IgH-myc
translocations (Fig. 7 and S6). The authors find with chemical inhibition of DOT1L (chemical inhibition is well characterized
with all proper controls), the authors report decreased translocations (fig. 7E). Myc-translocations occur at promoters and
also in gene body and the PVT1 locus downstream in humans. What translocations are the ones the authors are mapping,
are these Myc promoter translocations, some details with sequencing data of the translocation site would be helpful. Fig. 7E
could be done from two different mice (instead of B cells from one mouse). Pinemetostat treatment may decrease IgH-Myc
translocations at promoters, but does the translocations move to a new site (some bands are seen in the gel). 
5. The authors discuss that H3K79me3 modifications occur 5 Kb away from the TSS (Fig. 7A), but SHM and promoter
proximal pausing occurs in the first 1 kb and 100 bps, respectively. This could be clarified. 
6. The model in Fig. 8 and the associated discussion could be improved. Based on my understanding of this study, DOT1L
restricts PolII residence time in a promoter proximal paused state, thereby recruiting AID and increasing SHM. Previous
studies have supported the fact that bidirectionally transcribed regulatory DNA sequences (like in promoters and enhancers)
accumulate AID activity and those studies support the conclusions of this manuscript (Pefanis et al, 2014; Meng et al, 2014;
Qian et al, 2014). These DNA regulatory sequences have accumulated promoter proximal pausing and are most likely be
regulated with DOT1L activity. Authors should consider them in the discussion. The authors could also described SHM
(following DOT1L inhibition) at some of the off-target genes whose transcription was measured and shown to increase (Fig.
S6). This study does highlight that increased transcript levels at AID target sites is not always proportional to mutagenic
activity of the protein. These observations are consistent with previously published studies where it has been shown that
increase in switch region transcripts or at AID off target genes does not always lead to increase AID mediated DNA
mutagenesis. These published studies should be discussed and considered in the revision.

(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript examines the role of the H3K79 methyltransferase Dot1L in transcription, class switch recombination
(CSR), and AID mistargeting in the genome. The authors identify Dot1L and other factors including components of the super
elongation complex as AID proximity factors in 293 cells. 
As the authors summarize, it was previously known that Dot1L
contributes to efficient CSR and somatic hypermutation (SHM), but the mechanism of action was unknown. The authors also
nicely summarize previous findings linking Dot1L to defects in transcription and point out the limitations in those findings.
This study substantially extends previous work by showing that Dot1L regulates transcription by restraining Pol2 promoter
proximal pause release (PPPR). The substantial data presented here and the similarities with findings from other factors that
negatively regulate PPPR provide a strong argument that the catalytic activity of Dot1L (and likely H3K79me2/me3) help
restrict PPPR. This and the other findings related to the effects of Dot1L on transcription are the main strength of the
manuscript. 
The authors also make the claim (last sentence of abstract) that their findings have established that regulated pause release
is “a critical step for AID activity”. We do not agree. While the findings strengthen the link between Dot1L (and its catalytic
activity) and CSR and AID targeting, they do not establish mechanism. Specifically, the findings establish a correlation
between Dot1L-regulated Pol2 PPPR and CSR/AID targeting, not a causal link. One alternative explanation among others is
that methylated H3K79 is the key contribution of Dot1L, not the regulation of PPPR. We feel that the mechanistic advance
offered by this study regarding the role of Dot1L in CSR/AID targeting is significant but not definitive. This is the primary
limitation of the study. We emphasize that we find their model attractive and plausible; it simply hasn’t been established. Is
this required for publication in Nature Communications? That’s a judgement call, though overall we would support
publication without a requirement for substantial additional experiments. At minimum, the manuscript needs to be rewritten to

methylated H3K79—this would substantially strengthen the correlation and argue against a primary role for methylated

Point by point response Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): The manuscript by Subramani et al connects transcription regulation by DOT1L to the initiation of class switch recombination (CSR) of antibody genes and “off-target” effects of Activation-induced deaminase (AID). The authors identify DOT1L as a protein “proximal” to AID in Flp-In 293 TREx cells and then use the CH12F3 mouse cell line derived from B cell lymphoma for most of the subsequent analyses. Several DOT1L mutant derivatives of CH12F3 have been generated and experiments comparing WT (lymphoma) and DOT1L mutant conditions have been performed. shRNA knockdown of DOT1L was used as an additional alternative approach. The authors report diminished CSR frequency, reduced off-target AID-dependent mutations, and elevated Pol II pause-release upon DOT1L loss. Since Pol II pausing favors AID activity the major conclusion of the paper is that DOT1L affects AID through its regulation of transcription elongation. The strength of this work is in using several DOT1L mutant alleles and revealing the effect of the mutations on CSR and off-target AID activity leading to gene fusions. Also, the effects of DOT1L on cohesin distribution are interesting.  In this regard, does the downregulation of cohesin affect CSR?  The role of chromatin loop extrusion by cohesin in facilitating CSR is well established (Thomas-Claudepierre et al, 2013; Zhang et al, 2019). Transcriptional activation of Igh germline transcript units (GLTUs) in response to cytokine signaling leads to increased cohesin occupancy across the Igh locus  (Zhang et al, 2019; Costea et al, 2023). We note that new data in the revised manuscript (TT-seq and additional ChIP-seq) indicate that DOT1L regulates transcriptional elongation velocity. Given the known influence of transcriptional activity on cohesin dynamics, the moderate change in cohesin distribution in DOT1L-deficient cells is most likely an indirect consequence of transcriptional changes at the H3K79me2/3-rich GLTUs. In turn, chromatin loop extrusion alterations can explain the modest activation of internal H3K79me2/3-poor unscheduled GLTUs, as originally proposed. We make this point succinctly in Fig. 4, S5.  The weaknesses:  1. The focus on DOT1L singled out among the numerous AID prays is not well justified.  We appreciate the reviewer’s concern regarding the focus on DOT1L and have modified the text to better explain the knowledge gaps on DOT1L and AID targeting that we are addressing.  When we initially identified DOT1L as an AID-proximal protein, it had not yet been functionally implicated in antibody diversification. We chose to focus on DOT1L in part because prior studies had reported an enrichment of H3K79me2 at AID target loci (Wang et al, 2014; Álvarez-Prado et al, 2018). At the time, this modification was interpreted as a mere marker of transcriptional elongation, and the potential mechanistic relevance of DOT1L in AID activity had not been explored. While our work was ongoing, other studies were published demonstrating a requirement for DOT1L in CSR and SHM. However, these reports neither tested whether DOT1L enabled AID off-target activity, nor provided a mechanistic explanation for how DOT1L supports AID activity at the Ig loci. We sought to answer these important outstanding questions.  In the revised manuscript, we modified the Introduction to eliminate the “discovery” aspect and better justify the rationale to study DOT1L based on the gaps in knowledge.
