Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors investigated the insulating properties of Sb2O3 polycrystalline inorganic molecular film while paid particular
attentions to its grain boundaries. This kind of film attract research interests recently for its great potentials as a decent van
der Waals dielectric film in advanced 2D-semiconductor-based devices, which can be conveniently fabricated and
integrated in a scalable way. A polycrystalline film is expected to have poor insulating properties in theory since the grain
boundaries as typical crystal defects can form the leakage paths. However, in this work, the authors revealed that the grain
boundaries in Sb2O3 polycrystalline film with van der Waals structure create no defects. They provided robust evidences of
device measurements and theoretical calculations and explain a puzzling fact: why Sb2O3 polycrystalline film possesses an
excellent current-blocking property. This work is of significance for applications of this promising dielectric material in future
2D integrated circuits. I recommend it be accepted for publication in Nature Communications after my concerns as follows
are well addressed. 
1.In Fig.1a, the schematic figure depicting defect-assisted tunneling is probably incorrect. The charge carriers are supposed
to hop between defect states via emission mechanism instead of tunneling. The leakage current is temperature-dependent
only via emission mechanism in theory. The authors should reconsider the mechanism, correct the figure and the related
content in the manuscript. 
2.The standard of leakage current for a gate dielectric (DRAM for instance) should be marked in Fig.2e so that the insulating
performance of Sb2O3 film can be clearly identified. 
3.In the current mappings Fig.4 f, there are some points that show high leakage current. What has caused this leakage? 
4.As we know, the dielectric materials used in many organic devices are made of organic molecules. Is the conclusion also
applicable to organic molecular dielectric materials? I think it would be interesting if this phenomenon can be extended to
organic molecular dielectric materials and added relevant content in the part of Discussion.

(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors have answered most of my previous questions and revised the manuscript. In particular, they have added
additional DFT calculations on twin boundary structures as a representative boundary case to show that mid-gap states are
absent. However, I still have a concern regarding the spatial resolution of C-AFM measurements. 
The spatial resolution of typical C-AFM is generally larger than 10 nm in ambient conditions, and it is not correlated with the
density of data acquisition points (pixels). Furthermore, the probe tip (RNM25PT300B) used for the measurement appears to
have a tip radius < 20 nm. If the measurement has a resolution on the scale of a few nm to effectively distinguish the
transport between grain boundary (GB) and grain interior (GI) regions, clearly higher tunneling currents should have been
identified in GBs according to FNT theory. This is because there are grooves along the GBs with a smaller d than the GI,
even though there are no additional defects for leakage pathways (Fig. 4b). Demonstrating a low leakage current despite the
high density of GBs still supports the authors’ main claims. However, I believe that currents through both adjacent GB and GI
regions contribute to the total current due to current spreading, resulting in spatially averaged data, and this needs to be
correctly commented on in the manuscript.
