(Remarks to the Author) 
The bio-inspired concept of a "charge reservoir" to decouple the kinetic bottlenecks between water oxidation and CO2
reduction is not only innovative but also demonstrated with compelling experimental evidence across multiple catalytic
systems. The observed universal enhancement in performance is a significant finding that could have broad implications for
the field of artificial photosynthesis. The manuscript is generally well-written, and the data are presented clearly. I am
supportive of publication, pending satisfactory responses to the following points,s which aim to further strengthen the
mechanistic claims and contextualize the impact of the work. 
1. The authors have only considered the charge transition between W5+ and W6+. Please comment on the possibility of
further reducing the state to a W4+ state. 
2. The demonstration of catalyst performance under real-world outdoor conditions is a notable strength of this work. To fully
appreciate these results, a detailed description of the testing parameters (e.g., light intensity, duration, weather conditions,
and reactor setup) is essential. 
3. We recommend a thorough review of the supporting information to correct minor typographical errors, particularly
concerning superscripts/subscripts and capitalization, as exemplified in the caption of Supplementary Fig. 25. 
4. To substantiate the “decoupled” mechanism, time-resolved charge recombination kinetics (fs-TA decay fitting or
photoluminescence lifetime) before and after Ag/WO
₃
 coupling could be discussed quantitatively. 
5. Six-cycle tests (72 h) are promising but relatively short for a solar system. Extending to ~200 h continuous operation or
including post-reaction EXAFS would prove structural robustness of W⁵
⁺
/W⁶
⁺
 cycling. 
6. Clarify whether O
₂
 was quantified via GC-TCD or mass spectrometry, and if leakage corrections were applied, to support
the claim of stoichiometric coupling (O
₂
:CO ≈ 1:2). 
Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This study presents an original bio-inspired charge reservoir strategy in the field of solar energy conversion. The conceptual
design is elegant and logically rigorous, offering a novel approach to overcome the reliance on sacrificial agents in
photocatalytic CO2 reduction. The experimental work is particularly impressive, demonstrating the effectiveness of the
charge storage and controlled release mechanism through multi-system universality validation, a hundred-fold performance
enhancement. The experimental designs are scientifically sound, with mutually corroborating data forming a complete
evidence chain. It meets the standards for publication in Nature communications and I am pleased to recommend
acceptance after addressing the following revisions: 
1. The introduction adequately discusses the limitations of organic sacrificial agents but offers a somewhat generalized
overview of existing charge separation strategies. A more in-depth comparison with recent representative works is
recommended. 
2. The relatively high silver loading (1.68 wt%) in Ag/WO3 may raise concerns about aggregation. Further justification of its
stability is needed. 
3. XRD and Raman results do not effectively distinguish WO3 crystal phase changes. Supplementing with high-resolution
TEM or selected-area electron diffraction data would help verify crystal structure integrity. 
4. Inconsistent reporting of CO production rates may cause confusion. When comparing with literature data (Table S4), note
that most studies report μmol-level yields, whereas this work uses mmol. Units should be clearly defined. 
5. The O2:CO molar ratio close to 1:2 supports the overall reaction, but background oxygen interference should be ruled out
via dark control experiments to exclude residual air effects. 
6. Isotope labeling experiments (13CO2 → 13CO) provide solid evidence, but the MS peaks at m/z = 13, 16, and 29 lack
specific assignments. More detailed labeling in the figures is suggested. 
7. Stability tests over 6 cycles (72 hours) show good recyclability, but post-reaction surface element analysis (e.g., XPS to
track valence states) is missing. 
8. In situ FTIR shows stronger *COOH intermediate signals, supporting accelerated reaction rates, but other product
assignments should be described in detail and compared with DFT-calculated pathways. 
9. The outdoor natural light experiment is well-conceived, but insufficient description of key parameters affects
reproducibility. Additional details are recommended.

the CO2 gas through a container of deionized water, thereby delivering both CO 2 and

Supplementary note 4. DFT calculation details 
All density functional theory (DFT) computations were executed using the Vienna 
Ab Initio Simulation Package (VASP) 13. The Perdew -Burke-Ernzerhof exchange -
correlation functional (PBE-GGA) was adopted 14. Structural models were constructed 
based on experimental results from XRD, Raman, and X -ray absorption fine structure 
(XAFS) spectroscopy, while possible reaction pathways were i nitially proposed 
according to insights from in situ FTIR spectroscopy. A substantial 20 Å vacuum region 
was implemented in surface models to prevent artificial interactions between periodic 
replicas. Specific computational parameters included an energy cutoff of 520 eV and a 
Monkhorst-Pack k -point mesh of 3×3×1. For geometry optimization, convergence 
criteria required energy differences per atom below 10 -5 eV and forces less than 0.03 
eV/Å. To determine the Gibbs free energy relevant to reaction conditions , we applied 
the formula:  
𝐺 = 𝐸𝐷𝐹𝑇 + 𝐸𝑍𝑃𝐸 + 𝐸𝑒𝑛𝑡ℎ𝑎𝑙𝑝𝑦 − 𝑇𝑆 （1） 
Here, 𝐸𝐷𝐹𝑇 corresponds to the static electronic energy at absolute zero, 𝐸𝑍𝑃𝐸 to the 
zero-point energy correction, 𝐸𝑒𝑛𝑡ℎ𝑎𝑙𝑝𝑦 to the enthalpy contribution, and T*S to the
