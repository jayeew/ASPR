Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Bakhle et al. have built on an impressive body of previous work to demonstrate that epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity
activation can perpetuate an immunosuppressive tumour microenvironment in spontaneous canine mammary carcinomas,
thus complimenting previous work in murine models. Increased expression of CD109 is associated with EMP and the
authors proposed CD109 as a potential driver of the immunosuppressive tumour microenvironment. The need for functional
assays to validate this are discussed in the discussion. Publicly available datasets were effectively used throughout this
study. 
Overall, I thought this was an excellent, thoughtfully constructed, well written paper. Comparative oncology is an important
emerging field, and this report strengthens the importance of a One Health approach including animal models in which
cancer arises naturally and within the context of an intact immune system. This validates murine models and substantiates
translational application. 
Minor comments would include: 
• Lack of positive and negative controls for the IHC images in figure 1A. Although, a change in expression between grades is
evident. 
• Scale bars should be included on each image rather than just one for the whole panel. For example, grade I H&E looks a
higher magnification than II and III – appropriate scale bars embedded on each image would avoid this confusion. 
• CD109 was identified as a potential mediator of EMP immunosuppression from Fig 3L but there are other points on the
volcano blot that are not discussed. Why did you choose to focus on CD109? 
• This paper is jam-packed full of data and at points that makes it difficult to unpack. For example, a sentence to explain the
MMTV-PyMT murine model and how this links to the pB2 andpB3 cell lines.

comments and suggestions. We are pleased to read that the reviewers appreciated our 
use of naturally -occurring canine mammary carcinomas as a model to study the 
consequences of EMP and immunosuppression for human breast cancers. We have

3. CD109 was identified as a potential mediator of EMP immunosuppression from Fig 
3L but there are other points on the volcano blot that are not discussed. Why did you

We thank the reviewer for noticing this pertinent point on the lack of justification for 
pursuing CD109. We were initially interested in CD109 because it was one of the only 
genes to show a step-wise increase in expression from epithelial to heterogeneous 
tumors and from heterogeneous to quasi-mesenchymal tumors. Upon further 
exploration of the literature, we found that it was understudied compared to other EMP-
related proteins and immunosuppressive factors. Therefore, we wanted to see if the 
association with EMP replicated in other models. In canine mammary tumors, we found 
that CD109 expression associated with EMP in an independent RNA sequencing 
dataset (Kim et al, 2019, Scientific Data; Fig 4B-F). We then found that this association 
was also conserved in our mouse model and human breast cancer cell lines and patient 
samples (Wu et al., 2021, Nature Genetics). These associations across multiple species 
suggest that CD109 may have a conserved function associated with 
EMP/immunosuppression that should be studied further. These future studies could 
open new avenues for gaining mechanistic insight into CD109-mediated 
immunosuppression and may reveal novel therapeutic targets for heterogeneous and 
quasi-mesenchymal tumors.  
A line has been added at the end of this section to clarify this: “The upregulation of 
CD109 in both heterogeneous and quasi-mesenchymal CMCs was of interest, as this 
factor is not well-studied in general and particularly not in the context of EMP and 
immunosuppression.” (lines 242-244)

Specific Comments which could be addressed:  
1. The role of plasticity and EMT has also been linked to cancer stem cells. It would 
be good if the authors could address this in the discussion to outline where 
cancer stem cell theory fits within EMT.  
Thank you for pointing out the important role of epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity (EMP) 
in generating stem cell properties in cancer cells. CD109 expression has also been

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 9 -->

linked to stem cell activity. Specifically, Filppu et al. have shown that CD109 interacts 
with glycoprotein 130 are required to activate IL-6/STAT3 signaling and thereby 
maintain stemness in glioblastoma (Filppu et al., 2021 JCI Insight). Moreover, Zhou et 
al. demonstrated that CD109-mediated EGFR and AKT signaling is required for 
spheroid formation in squamous cell carcinoma cells (Zhou et al., 2022, Cancers). We 
have added a section in the discussion where we discuss how EMP and CD109 activity 
may lead formation of cancer stem cells (lines 451-455). 
“Both EMP and CD109 expression have been associated with the cancer stem cell 
phenotype. Specifically, EMP activation is sufficient to induce stem -cell properties in 
cancer cells and CD109 is required for maintaining the stem cell population 4,5,18–20,66–
68,71,72. This suggests that high CD109 expression in quasi -mesenchymal cancer cells 
may play a role in the formation of cancer stem cells.”  
2. The authors demonstrate that CD109 is up regulated and this is both conserved 
and could be linked to immunosuppression. The paradoxical expression of TGF-
b is also discussed. The drawback of this paper is the lack of mechanistic studies 
on CD109 to substantiate the hypothesis. This is an editorial decision as to 
whether it is out with the scope of this study. However, the obvious next steps 
would be to look at CD109 inhibition. The reviewer accepts that this would mean 
a considerable new set of experiments and may be the subject of a further 
manuscript.  
We thank the reviewer for this comment and whole-heartedly agree that this should be 
the immediate next step. Given the amount of data presented in this study, we do agree 
with the reviewer that such functional studies are beyond the scope of the current 
manuscript. Along these lines, we are actively working towards making CD109 function 
the subject of a future manuscript, where we can study whether this protein has a 
conserved immunosuppressive function in quasi-mesenchymal tumors across species. 
Specifically, we plan to study whether CD109 knockout in quasi-mesenchymal cells 
alters the EMP state and the recruitment of immunosuppressive cells in vivo.
