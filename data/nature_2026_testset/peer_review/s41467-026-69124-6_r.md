(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors fabricate chromium-doped cobalt spinel oxide (Cr-Co3O4) as an efficient OER electrocatalyst. The operando
measurements demonstrate the Cr doping effect on interfacial H2O dissociation kinetics. The increased hydroxyl group
coverage on the Cr site optimizes the interfacial water structure. In addition, Cr doping effectively enhances long-term
durability in acidic electrolyte system. However, the manuscript needs some additional clarifications. Therefore, publication
in the Nature Communications is recommended after major revisions. 
Comment 1 : In Figure 1i, why do the two peaks after the Cr-O around 2.5Å and 3.3Å in the EXAFS differ markedly in
intensity between Cr-Co3O4 and Co3O4? Also, aside from the decrease in Cotet-Cotet intensity, is there additional
evidence that Cr substitutes into the tetrahedral sites?

Furthermore, Cdl value of Cr-Co3O4 is smaller than that of Co3O4 (Figure S10). Please investigate the reasons for these
differences. 
Comment 3 : In the manuscript page 4 (line 113-114), authors state that ‘As shown in Figure 1d, the high resolution XPS of
Co 2p for Cr-Co3O4 and Co3O4 catalysts reveal that with the introduction of Cr, the ratio of Co2+/Co3+ is reduced, implying
the decreased valance state of Co sites in Cr-Co3O4’. If the Co2+/Co3+ is reduced, the average Co valence should increase
(a larger Co3+ fraction). Moreover, the XPS Co 2p spectra appear to indicate a higher Co3+ ratio in Cr-Co3O4 compared to
Co3O4, which seems to contradict the paper's assertion. In addition, please add a table summarizing quantitative Co2+ and
Co3+ fraction with numerical values. 
Comment 4 : In this manuscript, the long-term stability test of PEMWE device was operated with an ultrapure water
electrolyte. However, most PEMWE devices operate under acidic electrolytes, and many of the comparators listed in Table
S3 appear to evaluated under acidic conditions. Please validate the PEMWE device with Cr-Co3O4 under acidic electrolyte
condition, and update Table S3 to explicitly include the electrolyte identity and concentration. 
Comment 5 : To definitively investigate the origin of Cr-Co3O4 long-term stability, it is recommended to confirm the metal

irreversible Co4+ species in Co3O4 samples after OER behaviour. 
Comment 6 : Generally, in operando XANES, a larger increase in oxidation state upon voltage application indicates a more

whether the observed increase in oxidation state is sufficient to be termed overoxidation.

Response: Thanks for your valuable comment. It has been reported that Cr doping can optimize the 
electronic structure of noble metal oxides and enhance their catalytic performance (Nat. Commun., 
2024, 15, 7861; Nat. Commun., 2019, 10, 162; Nat. Commun., 2023, 1, 843). However, these studies 
have predominantly focused on modulating the electronic structure of the catalysts to regulate the

adsorption energy of reaction intermediates, thereby enhancing catalytic performance, while the 
relationship between the catalytic structure and the microenvironment at the catalytic interface has 
been largely overlooked.  
For the referenced paper of Cr -doped RuO2 (Nat. Commun., 2024, 15, 7861), it revealed that 
the dopant-host interaction enhances the OER  kinetics via a dynamic doping-mediated hydroxyl 
spillover mechanism. Specifically, OH* species adsorbed on Cr dopants can migrate to adjacent Ru 
sites to form OOH *, thereby increasing the OOH * coverage on the catalyst surface.  Although the 
work provides valuable insights, it foc uses on a precious metal -based system, which is often 
associated with high cost. In contrast, our study presents a noble metal -free catalyst, Cr -doped 
Co3O4, which demonstrates comparable or even superior performance for the acidic OER. Prior 
research focuses on the relationship between OH* on Cr , and OOH* on Ru sites to elucidate the 
mechanism behind the improved catalytic performance.  In contrast, our investigation targets the 
electrode-electrolyte interface microenvironment and its relationship with the oxide structure, a 
significant yet underexplored factor in catalytic performance that has not been well addressed in the 
reported literatures. 
In this work, we found the introduction of Cr into Co 3O4 lattice can create Cr-O-Co local 
environment and Cr3+-OH species modification on the surface. Operando Raman and in situ XAS 
were performed to confirm that the Cr-O-Co local environment can regulate the electronic structure 
of Co active sites through dynamic electron interaction during the acidic OER proce ss. Before the 
acidic OER, the unique coordination environment of Crtet-O-Co can significantly regulate the active 
Cooct site, without reducing the number of active sites, thereby simultaneously improving the 
activity and stability of Cr-Co3O4, since the Cooct site has been demonstrated to be more active than 
Cotet site in Co3O4 for OER. At high overpotential, the Cr dopants can donate electrons to stabilize 
activate Co site, thereby sustaining catalytic performance. In addition, Operando ATR-SEIRAS and 
in situ XPS were carried out to found that the dynamic increased Cr3+-OH species coverage on the 
Cr-Co3O4 surface can manipulate the behavior of interfacial water molecules during the acidic OER 
process, leading to the enriched free water molecules environment and promoted dissociation of 
water, thereby boosting the acidic OER performance.

Response: Thanks for your valuable comment.  The structure of interfacial water has been 
demonstrated critically determined the energy and chemical conversion processes, thereby 
significantly influencing the kinetics of electrocatalytic reactions, including hydrogen 
evolution/oxidation reactions (HER/HOR), and CO2 electroreduction.  
For HER/HOR, the alkaline conditions typically cause a significant re duction in reaction 
kinetics by ∼2-3 orders of magnitude compared to acidic conditions, even for state -of-the-art 
platinum (Pt)-based catalysts. The referenced paper (Nat. Catal., 2022, 5, 900-911) investigated the 
kinetic pH effect in HER/HOR. The authors  proposed that the significant difference in the 
connectivity of hydrogen bond network within the interfacial electrical double layer is the origin of 
the substantial kinetic pH effect. Using a Pt-Ru alloy as a model catalyst, they further revealed the 
essential role of the OH ad adsorption intermediate in improving the kinetics of alkaline HER/HOR 
by enhancing the connectivity of the hydrogen bond network in the electrical double layer, rather 
than merely affecting the surface reaction pathway and its energetics as previously believed. This is 
a pioneering work that advances our understanding of the origin of the pH effect.  In fact, factors 
affecting interfacial water structure, thus improve the electrochemical reaction kinetics, have been 
studied in the hyd rogen evolution reaction. For instance, Li and colleagues proposed that Ru 
surfaces exist in different valence states in the reaction potential range, dissociating interfacial water 
differently and generating two distinct H*, resulting in different activit ies. The local cation tuning 
effect and the large work function of high-valence Ru surfaces promote interfacial water dissociation 
(Nat. Commun. , 2023, 14, 5289). Koper and coworkers highlighted that interfacial water 
reorganization is closely related to the potential of zero (free) charge (pzfc). The strength of the 
interfacial electric field, as determined by the distance of the pzfc from the actual electrode potential, 
impacts on the rate of hydrogen adsorption by influencing the energetic barrier assoc iated with the 
reorganization of interfacial water during charge transfer ( Nat. Energy, 2017, 2, 17031). Recently, 
Zhai and colleagues identified the catalyst -water Coulomb interaction can affect the water wall at 
the catalyst/water interface and thus infl uence the ORR kinetics ( Energy Environ. Sci., 2024, 17, 
6046-6057). In comparison, the acidic OER has received considerably less attention, and

considerable development is still required in this field.

complex reaction mechanisms, research into the critical role of interfacial water is still in its early 
stages and lacks systematic investigation. The other referenced paper (J. Am. Chem. Soc., 2024, 146, 
35438-35448) is one of the few studies that investigate the critical role of hydrogen bond network 
during the acidic OER. The authors designed a series of RuO 2 catalysts incorporating Lewis acid 
(LA) species (LA -RuO2, where LA = TiO 2, ZrO 2, HfO 2). In these catalysts, the LA acts as a 
hydrophilic unit that optimizes the hydrogen bond network, while RuO 2 drives the activation of

provides a comprehensive investigation into the relationship among oxide structure, catalytic 
performance, and interfacial microenvironment, offering a promising strategy for designing non -
precious metal-based electrocatalysts with high activity and stability for acidic OER.

2. Structural and Electronic Characterization: Unresolved Ambiguities 
(1) The authors claim Cr occupies tetrahedral (Coₜₑₜ) sites in Co3O4 (Fig. 1g) based on reduced Coₜₑₜ-
Coₜₑₜ intensity in EXAFS (Fig. 1f). However, no direct evidence (e.g., aberration -corrected STEM 
imaging of atomic positions or EXAFS wavelet transform (WT) analysis of Cr -Co correlations) 
confirms Cr’s tetrahedral occupancy. The WT of Cr K-edge (Fig. 1j–l) only rules out Cr-Cr clusters 
but provides no insight into Cr-Co bonding. 
Response: Thank you very much for your valuable comment and it is useful to improve the quality 
of this work. Based on the analysis of the inductively coupled plasma atomic emission spectroscopy 
(ICP-AES), the atomic ratio of Cr to Co was determined to be 0.07. Such a low doping level of Cr 
in Cr-Co3O4 presents considerable difficulty in identifying its specific form and configuration within 
the crystal lattice.  XAS is a useful characterization technique for analyzing elemental structural 
information. Specifically, the XANES can provide electronic structure information of elements, 
including oxidation state and geometric configuration, while the  EXAFS yields atomic structural 
information of elements.  Through mathematical processing such as Fourier transform, structural 
parameters of the coordination shells can be directly obtained.  For example, Zhang and coworkers 
developed a Ru and Zn co -doped Co 3O4 spinel catalyst (RuZn -Co3O4) for the alkaline OER. 
Through XAS characterization, they confirmed that Ru and Zn atoms occupy the Co oct and Cotet 
sites, respectively ( Angew. Chem. Int. Ed. , 2024, 63, e202407509). In this manuscript, the FT -
EXAFS spectrum of Cr-Co3O4 at the Co K-edge reveals a decrease in the intensity of the Cotet-Cotet 
peak compared to that of pure Co3O4 (Figure R1). This suggests that Cr atoms occupy the Cotet sites

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 11 -->

in the Cr-Co3O4 lattice. The WT analysis at Co K-edge of Co foil, Co2O3, and Cr-Co3O4 was shown 
in Figure R2. The resolving power of WT analysis primarily hinges on its ability to discriminate 
between backscattering atoms based on their atomic numbers, with its principal advantage lying in 
distinguishing light from heavy elements. In this work, however,  WT analysis cannot effectively 
differentiate between the Cotet-Cotet and Cotet-Crtet coordination environments. This limitation arises 
because the similar atomic numbers of Cr and Co result nearly identical backscattering signatures, 
rendering their signals indistinguishable in the wavelet transform contours.  
Although aberration -corrected scanning transmission electron microscopy ( STEM) is 
unquestionably an efficient technique for identifying atomic occupancy, its capacity to resolve 
individual sites is limited by atomic number contrast. The proximity in atomic number between Cr 
and Co leads to negligible contrast, which prevents their reliable discrimination. Raman 
spectroscopy can probe the vibrational modes of a crystal lattice, which are associated with specific 
atomic bond vibrations. Consequently, the positions of Raman peaks can be directly correlated with 
different coordination environments. The Co3O4 spinel presents distinctive Raman modes, including 
Eg, F 2g, and A 1g modes, as demonstrated in Figure R3. Specially, the F 2g mode (524 cm -1) is in 
connection with the stretching of CoO4 (Cotet-O), while the A1g mode (692 cm-1) and Eg mode (484 
cm-1) are in connection with the stretching of CoO6 (Cooct-O) (J. Am. Chem. Soc., 2021, 143, 5201-
5211; J. Am. Chem. Soc., 2023, 145, 17995-18006; Phys. Status Solidi (b), 2016, 253, 2270-2282; 
Nat. Commun., 2021, 12, 3036). Compared with Co3O4, the A1g mode and Eg mode in the Cr-Co3O4 
catalyst remain unchanged, while the F 2g mode shifts. This further confirms that Cr substitutes for 
the Cotet position in the Co3O4 lattice.
