Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors reported significant and interesting work on performance and mechanism of PDS activation over a
homointerpenetrated Fe-based metal-organic framework (BUC-95) with dynamic stretching structure, which is affirmed by
MicroED, in situ characterization techniques (XRD, Raman, FT-IR and XPS) and DFT calculations. Compared with the
noninterpenetrated BUC-96, the dynamic stretchable structure of BUC-95 overcomes the limitation of weak electron transfer
ability in peroxydisulfate (PDS) activation, thus achieving excellent degradation performance of multiple pollutants.
Moreover, BUC-95 exhibits long-term water purification and detoxification capabilities. Therefore, this work can be accepted
for publication to some minor revisions. 
1. The authors mentioned the 3D printing in the Supplementary Figure 3b, however they did not mention it in the main text.
They should describe briefly the 3D printing method and parameters to illustrate how to obtain the molecular model with
homointerpenetrated structure. 
2. The authors are suggested to explain the slight inhibition with addition of Cl−. Also, to further determine the nonradical
Fe(IV)=O is as the major oxidation pathway, the influence of humic acid on OFC degradation should be supplemented. 
3. Fe-OH group in BUC-95 is an important precursor for generating the Fe(IV)=O species, so the formation process of Fe-OH
should be further explained. 
4. The authors mentioned that “However, the TEMP-1O2 signal showed a notable decrease upon the addition of D2O,
indicating that 1O2 did not contribute to OFC removal”. The reason of signal decrease should be provided. In addition, The
OFC degradation experiment in D2O should be supplemented to further confirm the contribution of 1O2. 
5. The continuous flow degradation experiment of OFC in the individual PDS system should be supplemented to exclude
the self-degradation from PDS decomposition. 
6. The authors should ensure that all supplementary figures and tables are properly referenced in the main text, with
adequate contextual information provided either in the main text or supplementary materials to facilitate interpretation. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Ths work present a homointerpenetrated Fe-MOF, BUC-95, designed for the degradation of organic pollutants through
Fenton-like reactions mediated by dynamic stretching effects. The study provides a comprehensive investigation into the
dynamic stretching mechanism and the formation of Fe(IV)=O species, supported by extensive experimental and theoretical
analyses. Additionally, the practical application of continuous-flow degradation demonstrates the material's potential for real-
world environmental remediation. The findings highlight the critical role of dynamic structural flexibility in enhancing catalytic
efficiency, offering a novel design strategy for next-generation environmental catalysts. Some minor revisions should be
further addressed: 
1.The formation of Fe(IV)=O results in electron depletion from Fe sites, primarily altering electron density and valence states
rather than directly influencing spin states. The authors should revise the abstract to clarify this distinction, emphasizing
changes in electron distribution rather than spin state modulation. 
2.The observed decrease in 1O2 ESR signal intensity in D2O (Supplementary Figure S27) contradicts the expected
prolonged lifetime effect. Either a mechanistic explanation or experimental verification should be added. 
3.The continuous-flow experiment demonstrated 100% OFC (10 mg L-1) degradation over 50 hours, suggesting that the

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This paper describes the development of homointerpenetrated BUC-95 MOF which exhibited a dynamically stretchable
structure and highlighted that this particular MOF is superior to BUC-96, an isostructural analogue of BUC-95 with
noninterpenetrated 3D framework, for heterogeneous Fenton-like reactions. The DFT simulation results provided new
insights into the role of this dynamically stretchable structure in facilitating the adsorption of peroxydisulfate, increasing the
O
‒
O bond length, thereby overcoming electron transfer issues. To demonstrate the practicality, the authors have also
assembled a continuous fixed bed reactor with good stability and high detoxification capability. 
Overall, the work is original
and significant and revealed the advantages of dynamically stretchable structure in homointerpenetrated MOF for Fenton-
like reactions and the simulation results also provided the possible mechanisms behind the observed improvements in
catalytic performance for Fenton-like reactions. Therefore, I recommend the publication of this paper after addressing the
following comments: 
1. In Characterization, the authors should specify the degassing conditions used prior to the BET tests to allow for
reproduction by others. 
2. In Supplementary Figure 7, please indicate clearly the different stages of decomposition and the weight loss for each
stage. 
3. In Supplementary Figure 21, the SEM image of the used BUC-95 is after only one cycle or after 5 cycles. This needs to be
clarified more clearly. 
4. 
In Supplementary Figure 46, some adsorption and desorption points in the N2 sorption isotherms appear to be missing. I
recommend the authors to repeat the measurements until fully connected adsorption and desorption curves are obtained. 
5. In relation to Comment 4, please also supply the corresponding pore size distribution plots for BUC-95 and BUC-96. 
6. MOFs are known for their high surface areas. However, the prepared BUC-95 and BUC-96 seem to have low specific
surface areas. What are the reasons behind this? 
7. Did the authors calibrate the XPS with reference to C 1s before deconvoluting the high-resolution spectra? If they did, this
should be specified in the Characterization section. 
8. The authors should provide SEM images of the control MOF, BUC-96, as well in the SI. 
9. The Fe 2p XPS of BUC-96 before and after the reaction can also be supplied and compared with those of BUC-95. 
10. How much are the C, Fe, N, and O contents in the prepared BUC-95? 
11. The authors proposed that "dynamic stretching" in a homointerpenetrated MOF (BUC-95) improved the Fenton-like
catalysis beyond traditional electron transfer mechanisms. Can the authors better clarify how this dynamic stretching is
fundamentally different from known flexible MOF behaviours or ligand breathing effects in other systems? 
12. The authors infer dynamic flexibility from PXRD, Raman, and XPS shifts. Can the authors provide more quantitative data
(e.g., peak shifts, time-resolved measurements) or in situ structural dynamics simulations to more rigorously define and
support the claim of “dynamic stretching”? 
13. What is the expected lifetime of the BUC-95@sponge material? Can the catalyst be regenerated? 
14. On Pages 7-8, the authors showed the BUC-95 demonstrated superior peroxydisulfate (PDS) activation capability than
the MIL-based systems. Were the loading amounts, surface areas, and Fe content normalized across all comparative
systems to ensure a fair benchmark? 
15. On Page 12, the authors mentioned that BUC-96 has better conductivity, yet BUC-95 shows better performance due to
the dynamic stretching. How do the authors rule out contributions from differences in Fe oxidation state distributions or local
Fe site environments rather than dynamic behavior? 
16. The authors highlighted that the catalytic enhancement for Fenton-like reactions is due to dynamic stretching of BUC-95.
How do the authors ensure that other factors, such as pore size, Fe dispersion, morphology, surface area, did not contribute
to the catalytic enhancement? 
17. Minor inconsistencies: In the manuscript and SI, the authors sometimes italicized symbols in texts and plots but in other
parts, the symbols are not italicized. They need to ensure all symbols are italicized in the text and figures in both the
manuscript and SI. 
18. I recommend the authors to add a schematic figure of the proposed Fe(IV)=O generation and pollutant degradation
mechanism to clarify the reaction sequence. 
19. In some OFC degradation figures in the main manuscript and SI, error bars and replicate measurements seem to be
missing.

Q 1-3. Fe-OH group in BUC-95 is an important precursor for generating the Fe(IV)=O 
species, so the formation process of Fe-OH should be further explained.
Response 1-3: We have explained the Fe-OH group formation process in the BUC-
95/PDS system for the better comprehension of readers in the revised manuscript.
Manuscript revision:
Previous literatures reported Fe sites, acting as the Lewis acid sites, can capture -OH 
from H2O to form Fe-OH species, which subsequently converted into HVIO species 33, 
35. In BUC-95, electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) spectroscopy revealed signals 
of oxygen vacancies at g = 2.003 ( Supplementary Fig. 37 ), suggesting that oxygen 
vacancies may serve as favourable sites for Fe-OH formation. Further evidence comes 
from the O 1s XPS spectra, where a new peak emerges at 533.2 eV after reaction  (Fig. 
3e), consistent with the formation of Fe-OH species. These findings support a reaction 
mechanism in which HVIO generation is initiated by the adsorption of -OH from H 2O 
onto Lewis acidic Fe(II) sites in BUC-95, forming Fe-OH intermediates that undergo a 
two-step oxygen atom transfer pathway to ultimately generate HVIO and SO42−.

Abstract: 
While extensive efforts have been devoted to enhancing electron transfer efficiency 
through metal valence cycling in heterogeneous Fenton-like reactions, the potential 
catalytic improvement induced by dynamic structural stretching remain unexplored.
Here, we introduce a homointerpenetrated Fe-based metal‒organic framework (BUC-
95) featuring a dynamic stretchable structure that significantly boosts the heterogeneous 
Fenton-like catalytic performance. BUC-95's unique stretchable structure achieved 
effective peroxydisulfate activation for degrading various micropollutants via 
Fe(IV)=O species, facilitated by a reduced energy barrier for Fe(IV)=O formation 
through modulation of the electron density at Fe sites.  DFT calculations suggest that, 
compared with the isostructural analogue with hydrogen bond-restricted stretching, the 
flexible dynamic stretching in BUC-95 overcomes the inherent electron transfer 
limitations from Fe sites to peroxydisulfate, enhancing the ofloxacin degradation 
performance. Practically, BUC-95 demonstrated effective continuous-flow degradation 
and detoxification of micropollutants. This work establishes dynamic stretching as a 
crucial design principle for advancing environmental remediation materials and 
technologies.

Q 3-6. MOFs are known for their high surface areas. However, the prepared BUC-95 
and BUC-96 seem to have low specific surface areas. What are the reasons behind this?
Response 3-6: While high surface area is a hallmark feature of many metal–organic 
frameworks (MOFs), not all MOFs process a ultra-high specific surface area. The 
specific surface area of MOFs is influenced by some key factors like synthesis method, 
solvent selection, pore accessibility, and particle size.
First, MOFs synthesized by solvent/hydrothermal method generally exhibit high 
surface area. This is attributed to the extended synthesis time, which allows sufficient 
nucleation and growth periods, resulting in small pore sizes and a homogeneous 
distribution, thereby leading large specific surface areas. While, certain rapid MOF 
synthesis methods (microwave synthesis) involve short reaction times, which restrict 
adequate crystal growth. This causes structural defects in the framework that block the 
pores, preventing N2 (the probe for BET specific surface area testing) from entering and 
thus resulting in a low specific surface area.
Additionally, if organic solvents (such as DMF or methanol) are used during MOF 
synthesis, these solvents may occupy the MOF's pores, resulting in the formation of 
small pore sizes and consequently reducing the specific surface area.
Last but not least, the size of MOF particles also significantly affects the specific 
surface area. In general, The specific surface area of MOF is related inversely to particle 
size. The smaller the particle size of MOF, the higher the specific surface area.

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 28 -->

25
Compared to nanoscale MOFs, microscale MOFs (particle size 1.0 ~ 10.0 μm) 
generally exhibit lower specific surface areas (typically 10.0% ~ 30.0% decrease). 
Given that BUC-95 and BUC-96 were synthesized via rapid microwave-assisted 
reaction in a DMF-H 2O solvent system and feature microscale particle sizes, their 
relatively low specific surface areas are consistent with the structural limitations 
outlined above.

Q 3-16. The authors highlighted that the catalytic enhancement for Fenton-like 
reactions is due to dynamic stretching of BUC-95. How do the authors ensure that other 
factors, such as pore size, Fe dispersion, morphology, surface area, did not contribute 
to the catalytic enhancement?
Response 3-16: As noted in Response 3-5, the experimentally derived average pore size 
likely reflects interparticle textural porosity rather than the intrinsic pore structure of 
the crystalline frameworks. To accurately assess the accessible pore dimensions, we 
performed DFT-based pore size distribution calculations using the crystallographic data 
(CIF files) of BUC-95 and BUC-96. The results (Supplementary Fig. 52) reveal pore 
limiting diameters of 4.4 Å for BUC-95 and 2.02 Å for BUC-96, dimensions too narrow 
to accommodate OFC molecules (13.8 Å in kinetic diameter) or facilitate their 
interaction with embedded Fe sites and co-adsorbed PDS. We therefore conclude that 
pore size effects do not contribute significantly to the observed catalytic performance.
Then, the Fe 2p XPS (Table R1) and ICP-OES (Table R2) results demonstrate that 
the Fe oxidation state and content in BUC-95 and BUC-96 are essentially identical. 
Additionally, as discussed in Response 3-14, we conducted the normalization of surface 
area and Fe content of BUC-95 and BUC-96 to exclude the contribution of Fe content 
and BET surface area. By calculation, the kS,Fe of BUC-95 is calculated to be 235.29 
min-1 m-2 mol-1, which is far higher than that of BUC-96 (27.88 min-1 m-2 mol-1), further 
exclude the contribution of Fe content and BET surface area.
