Reviewers' comments:
 
Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
This manuscript demonstrates a novel comprehensive research to produce a brand-new material made up of heterogeneous
thin film Cr-LiF as conversion-type cathode in Li-ion solid-state battery. After careful control group test and analysis through
electrochemical performance, characterization including HRTEM, STEM-EDX, EELS together with the application of DFT
calculation, the authors have succeeded in achieving a high initial capacity of 435 mAh/g and outstanding rate capability.
Besides, the identification of CrF2 as the dominant phase during delithiation process is also attractive. As a result, I agree to
recommend this manuscript for publication after the following minor revision. Some questions and opinions are listed as
follows: 
(1) The EELS characterization between charged and discharged states should be analyzed more specifically. Why does the
chemical shift suggest the existence of Cr2+? 
(2) Based on the cycling performance, the capacity of Cr-LiF rapidly dropped during the early 200 cycles, please provide the
possible attenuation mechanism analyzation in this system. 
(3) The authors claimed that a new cathode structure or phase was formed during electrochemical cycling, which is
beneficial for high rates with little extra overpotentials, the HRTEM or XRD characterization should be better provided to
confirm this conclusion. 
(4) Some recent progresses of fluoride conversion batteries also benefit from solid-state configuration and efficient LiF
splitting, e.g. Nano Energy 103, 107843, 2022; Adv. Funct. Mater., 31, 2009133, 2021; Energy Environ. Sci. 18, 923-936,
2025; Adv. Energy Mater. 15, e02645, 2025. These highly related works should be cited as references. 
(5) The STEM-EDX maps after 1564 cycles demonstrate the accumulation of Cr in the center and the segregation of F at
both sides of the edge, what should this phenomenon be attributed to? 
(6) Some abbreviations should be explained in detail, e.g. RC1 and RC2 on Page 11. 
Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author): 
The authors have demonstrated Cr-LiF composite as a cathode in solid-state batteries. As far as I know, it is the first time.
This work is well organized and shows promising electrochemical performances, including long cycle life and good rate
performance. I am in favor of its publications after address the following questions. 
(1) LiPON is a unique solid-state batteries that requires sputtering and thin cathode. Do you think this cathode can be
adapted to other types of solid-state applications? If possible, please add this perspective into the manuscript. 
(2) XPS is needed to study the valence state changes during the charging and discharging process in order to propose a
reaction mechanism.

Response to Reviewers 
We thank both reviewers for their insightful comments and concerns regarding this work. We ad-
dressed each reviewer point (in blue) with our response (in black) and the new additions to the 
main manuscript (text in italics). Please see below how we addressed each point. 
Reviewer 1 
1. The EELS characterization between charged and discharged states should be analyzed more 
specifically. Why does the chemical shift suggest the existence of Cr2+? 
We thank the referee for noting that this point was unclear in the manuscript. Below, we clarify how 
the observed shift of the EELS peak to lower energy indicates the presence of Cr²⁺ in the charged 
sample and describe the revisions made to explicitly address this in the revised manuscript. 
In general, increasing the oxidation state leads to a loss of valence electron density to more elec-
tronegative neighbors (e.g., oxygen), which reduces electronic shielding of the core levels. As a re-
sult, the core electrons experience a higher effective nuclear charge and become more tightly 
bound, requiring a higher energy to excite them. Consequently, the L₂,₃ edge onset shifts to higher 
energy loss with increasing oxidation state, a phenomenon known as the chemical shift. 
While this trend applies to many elements, transition metals with partially filled 3d shells, such as 
Cr, show more complex behavior. In these systems, the L₂,₃ edge fine structure depends strongly on 
the electronic configuration, crystal-field splitting, and spin state, which can lead to deviations from 
a strictly monotonic chemical shift. This effect is discussed in detail in Ref. [30], cited in the main 
text.

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 7 -->

As shown in Figure 1 of Ref. [29] reproduced above, the correlation plot of the Cr-L₃ peak energy 
versus the integrated L₃/L₂ intensity ratio shows that a lower L₃ peak energy combined with a 
higher L₃/L₂ ratio is characteristic of Cr²⁺. The spectral features of the charged-state cathode follow 
this trend, indicating the presence of Cr²⁺ in the sample. 
To address this, we have added the following clarification to the revised manuscript: 
"… In addition, the L₃/L₂ area ratio, calculated using the Pearson method [29], increases from 1.62 in 
the discharged state to 1.72 in the charged state. A decrease in the L₃ peak energy accompanied by 
an increase in the L₃/L₂ ratio is indicative of Cr²⁺, as reported in Ref. [30]. These observations are con-
sistent with the DFT-calculated structures, which also predict the formation of CrF₂ upon delithiation." 
(page 8)

3. The authors claimed that a new cathode structure or phase was formed during electro-
chemical cycling, which is beneficial for high rates with little extra overpotentials, the 
HRTEM or XRD characterization should be better provided to confirm this conclusion. 
It is indeed an interesting question. Due to the nature of the cell geometry, it is difficult to carry out 
deep fundamental characterization on the buried ~100 nm cathode layer. Despite this, supplemen-
tary HRTEM was carried out, and the following text has been added to the manuscript: 
"Direct evidence of this new phase is difficult to acquire as the active material layer is only 100 nm 
thick and buried under ~3 µm of other materials. Based on the STEM-EDX results in Figure 3 and HR-
TEM and FFT analysis (Figure S8) we can identify that the particles inside the cathode become larger 
and more inhomogeneous. The increase in particle size is also associated with the crystalline nature 
of the layer. However, FFT analysis suggests little changes in the predominant lattice spacings, which 
can be assigned to the expected nanocrystalline Cr and LiF (measurements taken in the discharged 
state). (S)TEM experiments as a function of state-of-charge, aimed to identify the transition phases 
are beyond the scope of this communication and are the focus of our next manuscript." (page 11)
