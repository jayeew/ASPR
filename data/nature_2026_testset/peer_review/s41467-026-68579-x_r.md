Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this study, the authors performed a large-scale investigation of serum N-glycomics in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC),
analyzing 1,074 individuals. They first used MALDI-TOF to analyze serum N-glycome profiles and detected 100–200 N-
glycan compositions. Next, they found specific N-glycan patterns associated with poor liver function. They then identified
Glycan Co-expression Modules (CCMs) that classify clinical liver conditions. To further understand the serum N-glycome
profiles, they integrated N-glycoproteomics data, inferred glyco-enzyme activities, and compared the results to mRNA data
from TCGA. Finally, they developed machine learning models for diagnostic purposes. 
Overall, this study is comprehensive, technically solid, and well-conducted. It will be of interest to the proteomics, glycomics,
and HCC communities. We therefore believe this is a strong candidate for Nature Communications. That said, we present
below some concerns and comments—mostly minor—that the authors could address or respond to improve the manuscript’s
presentation. 
Major 
1. Even without Figure 6 and Figure 7, we believe the present work is already qualified for Nature Communications.
However, the inclusion of Figures 6 and 7—especially Figure 6, which introduces the NglyBAI algorithm—complicates the
logical flow (we spent a long time reading and trying to understand this part). We therefore suggest that the authors consider
moving the NglyBAI figure to the supplementary materials (or consider publishing it in a separate paper) and downplaying
the associated results for the following reasons: a. The N-glycome and N-glycoproteomic datasets are all derived from
serum or blood, where N-glycan profiles can be very different from those in cells. Inferring glyco-enzyme activities from such
data is theoretically challenging and would require extensive validation, which goes beyond the scope of this study. b. The
manuscript already contains 8 main figures. c. It is not clear how increased/decreased mRNA expression in Figure 7
supports the results inferred in Figure 6. 
Minor

“surprising” consistency is encouraged, but the limitations should also be acknowledged. 
2. Line 88: Please briefly mention how quantification was done for MALDI. 
3. Throughout the figures, the authors should provide the number of individuals included in their analysis and plots (e.g.,
Figure 2a, Figure 3a–k, etc.). 
4. In all supplementary figure legends, the authors should provide more information and descriptions—e.g., about colors,
numbers, statistical tests, and abbreviations (e.g., Supplementary Figures 2 and 9).

Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript by Lu and colleagues presents a comprehensive study examining serum N-glycosylation dynamics during
hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) progression. Through meticulous multi-cohort analysis and sophisticated multi-omics
approaches, the authors have systematically characterized serum N-glycosylation associated with HCC development and
assessed their potential as early diagnostic biomarkers. The scale of data collection and analytical rigor demonstrated
throughout this work are impressive. 
The diagnostic machine learning models developed from N-glycome data represent a significant advancement, notably
outperforming alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), the current clinical standard. The authors' efforts to elucidate underlying mechanisms
through deconstructing glycomic changes into "carrier protein abundance-driven" versus "glycosylation process-driven"
modes demonstrates exceptional scientific depth. Furthermore, the novel NGlyBAI algorithm for enzyme activity inference
represents a methodological innovation with broad potential applications. 
While the findings are compelling, several points require clarification to strengthen the manuscript's conclusions: 
1. The 'Mean Eigenvalue' parameter used to characterize HCC subtypes in Figure 4c lacks methodological clarity. A
detailed description of its calculation methodology should be included in the Methods section to ensure reproducibility and
facilitate interpretation. 
2. Figure 5j presents an apparent contradiction requiring resolution: H5N3S1 glycan modification on A2MG peaks in the
CHB group, while total A2MG protein levels are elevated in LC and HCC groups. This observation contradicts a protein-
driven mechanism for H5N3S1 elevation in CHB and requires explicit discussion in the Figure 5 narrative. 
3. The manuscript would be strengthened by reporting whether the NGlyBAI algorithm identified glycotransferases with
significantly different activity scores specifically between LC and HCC groups. Such findings would substantially enhance
the algorithm's clinical utility by demonstrating its capacity to detect malignancy-specific enzymatic alterations beyond
general liver disease-associated dysregulation. 
4. A compelling paradox emerges between glycomic and transcriptomic findings: decreased overall galactosylation in HCC
(Fig. 3k) contrasts with the association between high B4GALT2 and B4GALT3 expression and poorer prognosis (Fig. 7d-g).
This intriguing discrepancy between gene expression and resulting glycan phenotype warrants thorough mechanistic
discussion. 
5. For completeness and methodological clarity, comprehensive survival analyses for both B4GALT2 and B4GALT3 across
all four endpoints (OS, DSS, PFI, DFI) should be provided, either in supplementary materials or with a clear rationale for the
current selective presentation. 
6. Model 3's external validation performance (AUC=0.76) for distinguishing HCC from LC, while still impressive, is lower
than other models. This reflects the well-documented clinical challenge of differentiating HCC from its cirrhotic background
and merits acknowledgment in the Discussion section. 
7. The correlation between Model 4 probability score (PP4) and disease severity raises important translational questions
that should be addressed: Could this quantitative metric enable longitudinal risk monitoring in cirrhotic patients? Moreover,
could a streamlined model using fewer N-glycan features maintain diagnostic efficacy while enhancing clinical
implementability?

In this manuscript, Fu et al. presents a large-scale, multi-omics investigation into serum N-glycosylation changes during

Enables Early Diagnosis”. I am not convinced the authors have shown that their method enables early diagnosis (which by
definition is of individual patients, not cohorts). 
Lines 367-368: The authors state that “all three models maintained comparable performance in the external validation
cohorts”. What is meant by “comparable” is not defined, but it is clear that all models perform worse (or less well) in both
external validation cohorts. This should also be stated, even though it of course is expected.
