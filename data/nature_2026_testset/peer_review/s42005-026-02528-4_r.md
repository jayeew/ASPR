Reviewer comments: 
Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors introduce a novel initialization strategy for variational quantum algorithms. Namely, the authors consider
ansatzes which are constructed via quenches of parameterized many-body Hamiltonians in their many-body localized (MBL)
phases. As (by construction) these models are nonergodic, scrambling grows slowly as the number of quenches increases,
which leads to a delay in the onset of barren plateaus when compared with an ansatz initialized in an ergodic phase. The
authors quantify this using the t-order frame potential to quantify the expressivity-depth tradeoff of these circuits as a function
of the number of quenches utilized. I find the idea novel and the author's techniques sound, and I recommend this work for
acceptance in principle. However, I have some minor questions and requests for clarification first. 
First, one potential caveat to this work is the following: while the MBL initialization strategy is trainable at a large number of
quenches M, it could be the case that it is still outperformed by the ergodic initialization strategy at shallow depths due to a
loss in expressivity when using the MBL ansatz. The authors should have some numerical experiments testing this. Indeed,
those in Appendix D go part of the way there: they show that the MBL-initialized ansatz finds good solutions at depth M=6. I
suggest comparing this ansatz with the ergodic-initialized ansatz at a depth M<6 chosen such that the associated loss
function variances between the two models match (more or less). The final relative errors can then be compared for the two
strategies. Furthermore, as these numerics are very important for showcasing the practical utility of this initialization strategy,
I recommend moving at least some of the results from Appendix D to the main text. 
Finally, one potential advantage of being able to train at larger depths using the MBL initialization strategy is that one can
potentially avoid the proliferation of poor local minima that typically occur at shallow depths (which you are otherwise
restricted with the ergodic initialization strategy). While full-fledged numerical simulations testing this are probably outside of
the scope of this work, some discussion or minor tests of this would, in my opinion, strengthen the impact of this work. 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The authors of the paper “Connecting phases of matter to the flatness of the loss landscape in 
analog variational quantum
algorithms” consider an analog variational quantum algorithm in which M sequences of quenched dynamics of a disordered
Ising chain are used to train the system. The initialization of disordered magnetic fields can be used to put the system in the
extended or the localized phases. Based on these phases the authors study two features: (i) expressivity of the system; and
(ii) the emergence of barren plateau. The authors show that extended Ising chains are more expressive in the sense that
with fewer quenches they can explore larger part of the Hilvert space. However, they are more likely to suffer from barren
plateau in comparison to the many-body localized Ising chains. Based on these observations, the authors suggest to
initialize the parameters in the localized phase and use an average quench sequence M to star the training of the system. In
this way, the system is likely, but not guaranteed, to be expressive and avoids barren plateau. 
The paper addresses an interesting problem and is very well-written and reader friendly. In particular, their analysis for
expressivity is very convincing and backed by proper analysis. Nonetheless, their claim for barren plateau requires further
clarification. My comments are as following: 
1- The authors consider the time of the evolution to be t=1/J. This is a problematic choice as one needs N (number of qubits)

Response: This is indeed a very nice suggestion! We fully agree that extending the numerical testing is essential
and truly appreciate the referee’s suggestion.
We have now implemented the numerical simulation to compare the performance between two initialization strate-
gies with similar variances on solving ground state energy and combinatorial optimization problems (see. Section III.D
and Appendix D.2). From empirical observation, when the target Hamiltonian (i.e., the long-range disordered Ising
model) more resembles the ansatz Hamiltonian, the ansatz with shallow quenches initialized in the thermalized phase
captures the solution well. The MBL initialization strategy with moderate quenches can also reach the solution, albeit
with more training iterations, due to the larger expressivity of the ansatze.
On the other hand, if the target Hamiltonian (i.e., the Heisenberg model) does not take a similar form to the
ansatze Hamiltonia–meaning the ansatz is considered to be problem-agnostic–the MBL initialization strategy in the
intermediate quench regime performs better. In addition, we also found some preliminary evidence regarding the
relation to local minima, which is further explained in the below comment.
Change: The new numerical results together with the discussion have been added to a new Section III.D and
Appendix D.2.

• On local minima of the initialized loss landscape.
Finally, one potential advantage of being able to train at larger depths using the MBL initialization strategy
is that one can potentially avoid the proliferation of poor local minima that typically occur at shallow depths
(which you are otherwise restricted with the ergodic initialization strategy). While full-fledged numerical

Response/Changes: We thanks the Referee for this insightful comment. Indeed, understanding the poor local
minimum phenomena are crucial for the scalability of VQAs.
From the new numerical simulation results (i.e., see Section III.D.2), we have observed some empirical evidence
regarding local minima where the shallow ansatze initialized in the thermalized phase sometimes converges to sub-
optimal solution. We briefly discuss how the convergence of the MBL initialized intermediate depth ansatze is
related to overparameterization phenomena. Albeit that, we admit that this is far from a complete story and further
investigation is needed. One key observation is that the assumption used in existing studies of local minimum
[International Conference on Learning Representations (2022), Nature Communications 13, 7760 (2022)] requires a
local-gate-based circuit structure. We have discussed this in a broader sense in Discussion section.
