Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
"Please see the attached file for more detailed review comments." 
Using density functional theory (DFT) and phase-field simulations, the authors proposed a strategy to enhance energy
storage properties in lead-free antiferroelectrics through a unique percolating interaction between antipolar regions and
engineered defect dipoles. Based on the experimental results, the authors claimed the increasing percolating interaction
between Li-Ta dipoles and antipolars in AgNbO3-based antiferroelectrics induced more rotated antipolar regions from the
initial AFE state. This leads to a remarkable recoverable energy storage density of 12.8 J/cm3 with 90% efficiency, stable up
to 255 °C, surpassing most lead-free ceramics. 
The manuscript is interesting and could be considered for publication until all the raised major/minor comments are
systematically addressed along with the additional experimental results. The comments are the following. 
Comments: 
1) The role of Li substitution was not very clearly discussed when Li was introduced to AgNbO3, it disrupted the long-range
antiferroelectric domain configuration and transformed the antiferroelectric state into a ferroelectric state. Theoretically, the
ferroelectric state appeared upon Li substitution also noticed experimentally in polarization hysteresis loops. However, was
it only due to the preferred Li1 to occupy the polarization region rather than the boundary or ionic radius, etc? 
2) It is understandable that the quantity of the Li was low and very challenging to atomically visualize in the STEM.
Alternatively, Raman spectroscopy can lead to verifying the disruption of the long-range antiferroelectric domains and
transformation into a ferroelectric state upon tilting the octahedra where phonon modes become softened appearing like
other studies [J. Materiomics 10, 431 (2024); Phys. Rev. Materials 3, 084405 (2019); J. Alloys Compd. 667, 310 (2016)]. 
3) In the phase field simulation results in Figure 2c, authors claimed a slim polarization-electric field (P-E) loops with an
increase in the maximum polarization (Pmax increased from 0.34 C/m2 to 0.45 C/m2) upon the increasing Ta concentration,
attributed to the due to the decreased ΔE for antiferroelectric-ferroelectric (AFE-FE) transition in the constructed rotated
antiferroelectric (RAFE) matrix. On the contrary, the Pmax of the highly substituted (AL5NT65) composition at a much higher
electric field (~750 kV/cm) was nearly comparable to the lower substituted (AL5NT5) composition at a lower electric field
(~200 kV/cm). Can the author explain the reason? 
4) Was the unipolar P-E loop in Fig. 3(a) measured in the as-sintered state (before the poling process)? The bipolar P-E loop
of AL5NT5 exhibits a hysteresis curve characteristic of ferroelectric behavior. In ferroelectric materials, if a unipolar P-E loop
is measured in a poled state, the hysteresis observed between the Pr and Pmax paths is typically small, leading to a minor
difference between the polarization value at E = 0 (Pr) and the polarization at Emax (Pmax). Additionally, the overall
hysteresis should be minimal. However, in Fig. 3(a), the unipolar P-E loop of AL5NT5 shows a substantial difference

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 2 -->

between the initial polarization at E = 0 (P = 0 μC/cm2) and the final remnant polarization (Pr = ~28 μC/cm2) after
measurement. Moreover, the loop exhibits significant hysteresis. The authors should provide a detailed explanation of the
sample state in which the measurements for Fig. 3(a) were conducted. 
5) Is the unipolar P-E loop in Fig. 3(a) measured in the as-sintered (before poling process) state? The bipolar P-E loop of
AL5NT5 shows a hysteresis curve close to the ferroelectric characteristic. In the case of a ferroelectric, if a unipolar P-E loop
is measured in a poled state, the hysteresis curve that appears between the Pr and Pmax paths is measured, so the
difference between the polarization value at the E = 0 state (Pr) and the polarization value at the Emax state (Pmax) is
expected to be small and the hysteresis will also be small. However, the hysteresis curve of AL5NT5 shown in Fig. 3(a)
shows a significant difference between the initial polarization value at E = 0 (P = 0 μC/cm2) and the final polarization value
after measuring the unipolar P-E loop (Pr = ~28 μC/cm2), and it shows a significantly large hysteresis characteristic. The
author needs to provide a detailed explanation of the sample state in which the results of Fig. 3(a) were measured. 
6) For comparison of the theoretical and experimental results, authors should provide P-E hysteresis loops of the pure AN in
Figure 3a, demonstrating AFE-FE transitions to a higher level in (Ag0.95Li0.05)(Nb1-x/100Tax/100)O3, with x values of 5,
25, 45, and 65. Also, they should provide strain loops to strengthen their arguments showing a bipolar hysteresis response
to slimmer loop characteristics. 
7) The authors claimed an ultrahigh energy storage density of 12.8 J/cm3 and an excellent efficiency of 90% for AL5NT65 at
the applied electric field of ~750 kV/cm. However, the excellent thermal stability (ranging from 5.4 J/cm3 at -70 °C to 4.9
J/cm3 at 185 °C) of the corresponding composition was presented at a moderate electric field of 450 kV/cm rather than at a
higher electric field. Authors must show the thermal stability of the AL5NT65 at the higher electric field. 
8) Based on the Rietveld refinement of the X-ray diffraction (XRD) data for the AL5NT5 to AL5NT65, it could be seen that
extra diffraction peaks around the ~23o and 46o were noticed in the XRD spectra of AL5NT45 and AL5NT65 ceramics
[Figure 4a and Supplementary Figure 11]. Also, these XRD peaks were not fitted by the provided Pbcm and R3c CIF files.
Are these secondary peaks? Or showing the existence of another phase along with the Pbcm and R3c phases which only
appeared at a higher doping level of Ta. Did authors fit XRD with other noncentrosymmetric (Pmc21) or cubic phases along
with the current Pbcm and R3c phases (Z. Lu et al., Nano energy 79, 105423 (2021)? to get a better fit like S. Ali et al.,
Chemical Engineering Journal 485, 150087 (2024). Authors must verify the origin of these peaks. 
9) This sentence is confusing and the authors need to rewrite it “The transitions from AFE M3 to AFE M2 and from AFE M2
to FE R3c gradually converge together when the composition approaches to AL5NT65, suggesting the coexistence of AFE
M3, M2 and FE R nanostructures.” 
10) The definition and explanation of 'Li-Ta dipoles' appear on line 169. However, since this term is introduced earlier in the
text, readers may be confused about its meaning. For instance, 'Li-Ta defect dipoles' is mentioned on line 117 and 'Li-Ta
dipoles' on line 119. The authors should define these terms earlier in the manuscript or consider using alternative wording in
the initial mentions. 
11) The authors represented the recoverable energy storage density as follows: 
W_rec=∫_(P_r)^(P_max) EdP 
This is an incorrect mathematical formula for Wrec. Although the positive integral of an electric field with respect to
polarization from remnant polarization (Pr) to saturated polarization (Pmax) seems to be identical to the negative integral of
an electric field with respect to polarization from Pmax to Pr mathematically, the positive integral cannot be equal to the
negative integral due to the hysteretic characteristic of ferroelectrics/piezoelectrics in reality. As the authors probably know,
the upward path in a hysteresis loop from Pr to Pmax is never identical to the downward path from Pmax to Pr. Hence, Wrec
should be represented as shown in [Ceram. Inter. 47 (2021) 23488; Adv. Funct. Mater 29 (2019) 1807321]: 
W_rec=-∫_(P_max)^(P_r) EdP 
Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript reports a recoverable energy storage density of 12.8 J/cm3 with an efficiency of 90% achieved at room
temperature in Ag0.95Li0.05Nb0.35Ta0.65O3 ceramics. This achievement is attributed by the authors to a rotated
antiferroelectric (RAFE) state related to interaction between Li-Ta defect dipole and antipolars invented by them based on
their simulation study. Therefore, the authors called their manuscript as “Ultrahigh energy storage density and efficiency in
AgNbO3-based ceramics by percolating interaction between antipolars and defect dipoles”. However, this title has several
incorrect or even misleading statements. 
First, the authors claim that the values obtained by them and shown in Fig. 3c are ultrahigh “surpassing all reported lead-free
antiferroelectric ceramics”. However, comparable values of 12.6 J/cm3 and 94% were recently reported in Chem. Eng. J. 503
(2025) 158557 for multilayer ceramic capacitors with a simpler composition of AgNb0.5Ta0.5O3. When the composition is
complicated, values up to 43.5 J/cm3 and 85% can be achieved in lead-free ceramics as reported in Adv. Energy Mater.
2024, 14, 2400821 and references therein. Therefore, if 12.6 J/cm3 is ultrahigh, how should one call 43.5 J/cm3? 
Second, these values were obtained by the authors in ceramics having just 35% Nb. Therefore, claiming that these ceramics
are based on AgNbO3 looks misleading as well. These ceramics are rather based on AgTaO3 that is not antiferroelectric but
quantum paraelectric, according to works like Appl. Phys. Lett. 95, 242904 (2009). Thus, one should not invent the rotated
antiferroelectric (RAFE) state but follow the explanations presented in the works mentioned above regarding turning the

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 3 -->

antiferroelectrics into quasi-linear dielectrics for high energy storage applications. 
Third, following the previous issue, DFT simulations done for Ta doping ions in AgNbO3 lattice do not look to be valid. One
must consider instead an opposite situation with Nb doping of AgTaO3. Moreover, even presented simulations show that
“the substitution site with lower energy, distant from the Li cations, should be occupied first”, contradicting the idea of Li-Ta
dipole formation and its interaction with antipolars. When the Ta content is low there are no Li-Ta dipoles. When Ta content
is high, the matrix is not antiferroelectric anymore. Furthermore, besides the RAFE state, the authors invented other terms
like antipolars and polars without clear definition for them. That together with a lack of definition of symbols and
abbreviations like E, FE, AN-Li, ANLi-Ta, AnLi-xTa, XRD and Tm at first mentioning or at all creates additional difficulties to
understand the manuscript. Additionally, the authors wrongly call XRD data as spectrum, despite that there is no frequency
dependence. On top of that, it is not clear why the authors call Li-Ta dipoles as defect dipoles, while no lattice defects like
vacancies or even extra charge carriers are assumed to be originated by them. Thus, the manuscript title and manuscript
itself look misleading from the beginning to the end. 
Concerning other issues, a site cannot be replaced as written in Fig. 1a or substituted as written in the corresponding text,
just ions can. Fig. 2e-f misses Ta concentration values. According to the caption of comparative Fig. 3f, it is limited by the
electric field lower than 450 kV/cm and η is higher than 85%, without explanation why these values are chosen. Based on
Fig. 4, the authors claim on “the coexistence of AFE M3, M2 and FE R nanostructures”, whereas Fig. 4b contradictory shows
not 3 but only 2 Pbcm and R3c phases. Moreover, Fig. 4c does not show any peak corresponding to the Tm point in Fig. 4f
for x = 5. Similarly, Fig. 4c-d shows 4 curves each that does not correspond to 5 points in Fig. 4e. Furthermore, Vogel-
Fulcher analysis represented in Fig. 4e must be done using angular frequency instead of ordinary one together with
imaginary part of the dielectric permittivity data, whereas Fig. 4c-d shows only its real part. Besides the freezing
temperatures, Vogel-Fulcher analysis must be also characterized by activation energies, which are not presented by the
authors. Instead, the authors suggested some “enhanced polar dynamics with increasing Ta concentration even under lower
temperatures” without any clearance what do they mean by enhanced dynamics. The density of the studied ceramics is not
indicated as well. 
There are also other examples of unclear sentences like “The further boost of Ue will greatly rely on the boost of breakdown
strength (Eb), which will increase the failure risk especially with varying temperatures” or “The schematic formation of a
rotated antiferroelectric state (RAFE) between ferroelectric structure (FE) with induced polar (P1) and normal antiferroelectric
(AFE) after introducing Li/Ta elements in AN antiferroelectric structure. The existence of RAFE form a percolating field to
divide and break the long-range order AFE and FE state, as marked in the formation of red network lines.” The manuscript is
also full of scientifically ambiguous words like “excellent”, “improved/ing” or “outstanding” as well as by words “through”
when they do not mean indeed through. 
Finally, there are many missing experimental conditions, like milling ball material and rotation rate, sintering heating and
cooling rates, XRD range, rate and step, P-E loop measurement frequency and temperature range as well as the equipment
used to control the temperature in this and dielectric measurements. Methods and equipment used to polish ceramics down
to ~80 μm thickness and to deposit electrodes are also missing. 
Thus, tis manuscript cannot be recommended for publication.

We are grateful for the reviewers’ evaluations and constructive comments on our 
manuscript entitled “Ultrahigh energy storage density and efficiency in AgNbO3-based 
ceramics by percolating interaction between antipolars and defect dipoles” 
(NCOMMS-25-08527). The point-by-point responses to the reviewers’ comments are 
summarized below and the detailed changes made in the manuscript are marked by blue 
fonts with yellow highlights. 
Responses to the comments of Reviewer # 1 
Reviewer #1: Using density functional theory (DFT) and phase-field simulations, the 
authors proposed a strategy to enhance energy storage properties in lead-free 
antiferroelectrics through a unique percolating interaction between antipolar regions 
and engineered defect dipoles. Based on the experimental results, the authors claimed 
the increasing percolating interaction between Li-Ta dipoles and antipolars in AgNbO3-
based antiferroelectrics induced more rotated antipolar regions from the initial AFE 
state. This leads to a remarkable recoverable energy storage density of 12.8 J/cm3 with 
90% efficiency, stable up to 255 °C, surpassing most lead-free ceramics. The 
manuscript is interesting and could be considered for publication until all the raised

Response: We greatly appreciate the reviewer’s positive evaluations for our work. 
To address the major/minor comments from Reviewer 1, we have provided careful 
responses and subsequently revised the manuscript accordingly. 
Comments 1: The role of Li substitution was not very clearly discussed when Li was 
introduced to AgNbO 3, it disrupted the long-range antiferroelectric domain

configuration and transformed the antiferroelectric state into a ferroelectric state. 
Theoretically, the ferroelectric state appeared upon Li substitution also noticed 
experimentally in polarization hysteresis loops. However, was it only due to the 
preferred Li1 to occupy the polarization region rather than the boundary or ionic radius,

Response: Experimental results in previous references have revealed that Li + 
cations to replace Ag + cations can stabilize the R3c ferroelectric phase of AgNbO 3 
matrix in thermal dynamics due to the A- and B-site coordinating displacements 
especially when Li→Ag substitution percentage reaches ≥5% (J. Phys. Condens. 
Matter 23, 075901 (2011); J. Eur. Ceram. Soc. 41, 1236-1243 (2021); Inorg. Chem. 59, 
17, 12595-12607 (2020)).  
In this work, we further utilized DFT calculations to make clear the in-depth 
physical reasons for the appearance of ferroelectric state in Li-doped AgNbO 3. As 
shown in Fig. R1a-b, there are two representative target sites for Li→Ag substitutions, 
i.e., Li1 (polarization region) and Li2 (antiferroelectric boundary region) sites in the 
AgNbO3 antiferroelectric superlattice. As shown in Fig. R1c, when Li is placed at the 
low-energy polarization region (Site of Li1) rather than high-energy boundary (Site of 
Li2), the higher position shift along y axis will result in a larger local polarization output. 
Therefore, it is concluded that the emergence of the ferroelectric state in 
antiferroelectric state is associated with Li-induced significant ionic displacements 
along y axis. We have added the key points of these discussions into the revised 
manuscript. 
Revisions: The discussion sentences have been inserted into the Page 6 of 
manuscript: “Supplementary Figs. 2a-c reveal that when Li+ cation is placed at the low-
energy polarization region (Site of Li1) rather than high-energy boundary (Site of Li2), 
the larger additional position shift of Nb along y axis can be achieved, resulting in a 
larger local polarization. Such a localized mechanism is well consistent with the 
experimental observations in previous references which revealed that Li dopants can 
stabilize the R3c ferroelectric phases of AgNbO3 in thermal dynamics due to the A-site 
and B-site coordinating displacements especially when Li→Ag substitution percentage

The underlying mechanism is elucidated as follows: Ta 5+ cations preferentially 
substitute for Nb 5+ cations at B-sites distant from Li⁺ positions to minimize lattice 
distortion and elastic strain energy, resulting in a lower relative energy (Fig. R12b i.e., 
Supplementary Fig. 2e). This configuration leads to minimal atomic displacement along 
the z-axis and only a minor rotation of the antiparallel polarization, thereby suppressing 
the formation of rotated antipolar regions. 
In contrast, with increasing Ta concentration, the probability rises for Ta5+ cations 
to randomly occupy sites that are nearest or next-nearest neighbors to Li-doped sites. 
Such substitutions introduce significant lattice distortion and elevate the elastic strain 
energy, leading to a substantial increase in relative energy. This energy increase 
mediates a strong local coupling between Li + and Ta5+ cations. The consequent larger 
atomic displacement along the z-axis induces a pronounced rotation of the antiparallel 
polarization, thereby facilitating the formation of a high density of rotated antipolar 
regions. These regions delay the field-induced antiferroelectric-to-ferroelectric 
transition and stabilize both antiferroelectric and ferroelectric nanodomains, enabling 
high polarization with minimal hysteresis under the applied breakdown strength. This 
mechanism is therefore critical for achieving the observed high energy storage 
performance.

enhance energy storage properties in lead -free antiferroelectrics through a unique percolating interaction 
between antipolar regions and engineered defect dipoles. Based on the experimental results, the authors 
claimed the  increasing percolating interaction between Li -Ta dipoles and antipolars in AgNbO 3-based 
antiferroelectrics induce d more rotated antipolar regions from the initial AFE state. This leads to a 
remarkable recoverable energy storage density of 12.8 J/cm 3 with 90% efficiency, stable up to 255  °C, 
surpassing most lead-free ceramics.  
The manuscript is interes ting and could be considered for publication  until all the raised  major/minor 
comments are systematically addressed along with the additional experimental results. The comments are  
the following. 
Comments: 
1) The role of Li substitution was not very clea rly discussed when Li was introduced to AgNbO 3, it 
disrupted the long -range antiferroelectric domain configuration and transformed the antiferroelectric 
state into a ferroelectric state. Theoretically, the ferroelectric state appeared upon Li substitution also  
noticed experimentally in polarization hysteresis loops. However, was it only due to the preferred Li1 
to occupy the polarization region rather than the boundary or ionic radius, etc? 
2) It is understandable that the quantity of the Li was low and very challenging to atomically visualize in 
the STEM. Alternatively, Raman spectroscopy can lead to verifying  the disruption of the long -range 
antiferroelectric domains and transformation into a ferroelectric state upon tilting the octahedra where 
phonon modes become softened appearing like other studies [J. Materiomics 10, 431 (2024); Phys. 
Rev. Materials 3, 084405 (2019); J. Alloys Compd. 667, 310 (2016)].     
3) In the phase field simulation results in Figure 2c, authors claimed a slim polarization -electric field (P-
E) loops with an increase in the maximum polarization ( Pmax increased from 0.34 C/m2 to 0.45 C/m2)

Review report 
The manuscript titled “Ultrahigh energy storage density and efficiency in AgNbO3 -based ceramics by 
percolating interaction between antipolars and defect dipoles ” reports on a chemical substitution -
based strategy to modify the perovskite structure of AgNbO3 to tailor its (anti)polar state and improve 
its energy density properties. The strategy is based on adding Li and Ta ions, respectively replacing Ag 
and Nb in AgNbO3, leading to compositions of chemical formula (Ag0.95Li0.05)(Nb1-x/100Tax/100)O3 with x=5, 
24, 45, 65. The manuscript is undoubtedly interesting; however, it is still a question if it is suitable for 
Nature Communication or a minor journal. 
I have the following comments: 
- Overall, the terminology is a bit inconsistent and perh aps slightly loose. This refers to: i) Li-Ta 
dipoles vs Li-Ta defect dipoles. Is it really appropriate using Li-Ta defect dipoles for Li-O and Ta-
O dipoles? Also, I think it should be clearly specified why these are regarded/defined as defect 
dipoles, since Li is isovale nt to Ag and Ta to Nb ; ii) antipolar regions vs antipolars vs rotated 
antipolars: I suggest sticking with antipolar regions and rotated antipolar regions). 
- There is an ongoing discussion in the literature about AgNbO3 being ferrielectric rather than 
totally antipolar (as lately reviewed in Journal of Materials Chemistry A 10 (28), 14747-14787), 
but this has not been taken into account or mentioned in the discussion.  In relation to this, 
the RAFE units that the authors mention and depict in so me of the schemes presented in the 
figures, actually resemble ferrielectric units. In this case, what is the real breakthrough of this 
paper for Nat Comm if the ferrielectric state in AN-based materials is already known? Is it that 
if these units become percolating the field -induced transition can be delayed and energy 
density can be consequently enhanced ? But what is the mechanisms by which this can 
happen? 
- In figure 1, it would be good if some additional details can be added to enhanc e the 
visualization of why the polarization experience exactly the rotation depicted in the last 
configuration of the structure. 
- In figure S1, the ions should be labelled in structure scheme of the structure and indicate the 
positions of Ta, in particular, the five positions which are energy favourable indicated in the 
plot. Also, for the plot the definition of “relative energy of Ta” should be given. Why is this a 
relative quantity?  
- Specify how the data in figure 2e and supplementary Fig.6 have been obtained. For the latter, 
how was the temperature effect simulated? 
- The sentence “If a defect dipole with designed orientation can enhance the polarization 
amplitude of initial AFE antipolars and slightly rotate the alignment of antipolars to facilitate 
the AFE -FE transitions, the polarization of the AN -based systems will be increased with 
lowered polarization hysteresis, finally achieving the simultaneous boosts in Ue and η” seems 
contradictory and unclear for the following reasons. i) It seems to hint at the fact that the AFE-
FE transition could be facilitated with increasing Ta; however, the EAFE-FE seems to increase with 
increasing Ta, as shown in Fig.3a and Fig. Suppl 8. Hence, the addition of Ta is not facilitating 
but hindering or delaying this transition,  and it is actually this  that is beneficial to improve 
energy density, along the reduction of ΔE. But it is not the reduction of ΔE alone that leads to 
the large energy density reported. Based on this, also the sentence: “In addition, the modified 
electric interaction energy near Li-Ta dipoles in AN antipolar matrix through incorporating Ta 
into the neighboring position of Li, can also decrease the switching energy barriers of AFE -FE 
transition” seems in contradiction with the results, because EAFE-FE increases with increasing Ta 
content. ii) The polarization amplitude does not seem to be always enhanced; for instance, the

- My feeling is that the  delayed field -induced transition with increasing Ta is not currently
