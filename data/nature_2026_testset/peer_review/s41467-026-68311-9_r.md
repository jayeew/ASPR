Point-by-point Response  We thank the reviewers for their thoughtful and constructive feedback, which has significantly improved the clarity and quality of our manuscript. In response, we have revised the text, figures, and methods, as well as additional new data to address all major concerns.  Notably, we have further refined our gated-state structure and now present a more conservative model that better reflects the local resolution limitations within the NT gating domain. We have also expanded structural comparisons to other connexin isoforms and gating mechanisms, clarified key interpretations and methodological details, and strengthened our discussion of pH- and lipid-mediated gating. In addition, we now explicitly acknowledge key limitations of the current study. A detailed, point-by-point response to each reviewer comment is provided below.  Reviewers #1/2:  The authors have used sophisticated cryo-EM techniques to investigate the reversible, lipid-mediated pH-gating of connexin-46/50. While they have attempted several approaches to resolve various states, there are several critical and major concerns that need to be addressed. In general, to support most of their conclusions, they need to improve the quality of the maps in certain important regions, which are central to the discussion in this manuscript. Many statements are overly speculative and not supported by map densities. The authors either need to collect more data and classify structural heterogeneity more effectively or moderate their claims accordingly.  Major Concerns: 1. MSP Belt Tightness & Nanodisc Stability o The MSP belt appears too tight, which may influence conformational changes in the N-terminal (NT) domain, particularly at low pH, where it is proposed to be stabilized by lipids. o Nanodiscs do not behave well at low pH, and MSP stability decreases under such conditions, potentially affecting lipid entry. As a result, the lipid density observed near the NT may not be physiologically relevant.  Response: We thank the reviewers for raising these concerns regarding the use of nanodiscs and the potential influence of MSP belt tightness and pH-dependent instability on our structural observations, which we address below. As for the overarching concern, that our in vitro nanodisc systems may not fully replicate the physiological membrane environments, we certainly agree and we explicitly acknowledge this limitation in the manuscript,   pg. 12: However, it is important to note that a direct role of lipids in gap junction gating remains to be validated through cellular functional studies.  pg. 15 Moreover, as noted above, the physiological relevance of lipid-mediated gating remains to be established in cellular systems, including investigations into potential lipid specificity and defining the translocation pathway(s) into and out of the pore.  Regarding MSP belt tightness: While the reviewers don’t provide their rationale for making this assertion, we respectfully note that the use of MSP1E1 in our system resulted in highly homogeneous populations of gap junction channels in the open state under neutral pH conditions. Notably, our structures of Cx46/50 in MSP1E1 nanodiscs remain, to our knowledge, the only system to provide such a well-defined and consistent open-state conformation of the NT-domain for gap junction channels under non-gating conditions. Furthermore, our 3D variability analysis (3DVA) demonstrates that even under these conditions conformational flexibility persists, especially in TM2 and other TMs, indicating that intrinsic dynamics are preserved and not overly constrained by the nanodisc scaffold. In fact, we have unpublished data showing that nanodiscs using MSP1E1 can contain more than one Cx46/50 channel, and still preserve the channels in a stabilized open-state.

Regarding MSP behavior at low pH: we understand the concern that potential destabilization of the MSP belt could, in principle, influence lipid redistribution. However, several lines of evidence strongly argue against this, or for it being a dominant contributor to the observed lipid infiltration or NT gating:  1. Reversibility: A key finding of our study is the reversible nature of the lipid-dependent gating transition. Upon restoring samples from low pH to neutral pH, >96% of channels return to the open conformation, with concomitant removal of pore-bound lipids (a key result and aspect of rigor that has been missing from other gap junction structural studies that observe lipids inside the channel pore). This reversibility strongly supports a non-artifactual mechanism and preserved integrity of our nanodisc system.  2. Detergent Controls: In parallel experiments using amphipol-reconstituted channels, we show that low pH alone was insufficient to induce pore closure, strongly supporting our conclusion that lipids are required for this gating transition.  3. Stability at Low pH: Although not shown in the original manuscript, we have additional data demonstrating that Cx46/50 nanodiscs reconstituted with MSP1E1 and DMPC are stable at pH 5.8 for extended periods (≥1 week at 4°C), as assessed by negative stain EM (image now included in our revised Extended Data Fig. 1). These findings further support the stability and suitability of our system under the experimental conditions used.  4. We would further note that this same MSP system has recently been used by our lab to capture a distinct gating mechanism in Cx46/50 in response to Ca2+, where NT movement is similarly induced but without clear evidence of lipid infiltration (Flores et al. BioRxiv 2025).  Taken together, we believe the combined biochemical, structural, and control data, and orthogonal gating studies all support the validity of our conclusions that pH-gating of Cx46/50 is reversibly facilitated by lipids when using the selected MSP-nanodisc system. Nevertheless, we have revised the text to more clearly acknowledge the limitations regarding physiological relevance.   2. NT Helix Density & Pore Radius Calculation o Despite the overall high resolution, the NT region exhibits weak density, indicating flexibility and structural heterogeneity. o Discussions regarding NT interactions lack strong map support. o The weak density in this region also calls into question the accuracy of pore radius calculations, which may lead to erroneous interpretations. To better reflect the limitations in map quality within the NT, we have revised our structural models of the gated state by pruning unresolved NT sidechains to their Cβ atoms, consistent with the lack of resolvable sidechain features in this region. Residues 2-3 also remain unresolved and are not modeled, consistent with our original models. These modeling decisions are now explicitly stated in the revised Methods section, Main text and related Figure legends.   In response to the reviewers’ valid concern regarding interpretation of NT interactions, we have removed prior descriptions of inter-subunit NT interactions from both the main text and Fig. 1, as we agree that the available map density does not support such specific conclusions. Heterogeneous Structural States & Data Processing o The manuscript discusses multiple states leading up to the "Low pH Induced an Ensemble of Gated and Open States" but later concludes that these states are mixtures of different conformations. o Since the earlier structures likely represent averaged density maps, their significance is diminished. o The study should focus on asymmetric subunit conformations rather than enforcing D6 symmetry after identifying asymmetric particles. o Structural heterogeneity is still evident, as indicated by standard deviations. Response: We thank the reviewers for raising this point. Indeed, the structural heterogeneity (particularly at low pH) is a key feature of this system and we agree that this complicates interpretation of symmetry-imposed reconstructions. In response, we have revised the manuscript to better delineate the distinct insights gained from both symmetric and asymmetric processing strategies.  The D6-symmetrized reconstructions were critical for defining the high-resolution features of the most primary conformational states, specifically the stabilized-open, destabilized-open and gated-state configurations. These idealized models allowed us to identify key structural rearrangements, including pore-lining lipids and the more nuanced changes in the NT and TM2 for the destabilized-open state, which we were not able to resolve at lower resolutions achieved by symmetry-expansion and focused refinement methods performed on single subunits. Importantly, by leveraging D6-symmetry, this approach also enabled us to detect rare gated-state and destabilized-open state populations under neutral pH conditions (transitions that were also not evident in our asymmetric processing approach).  However, we fully acknowledge that these D6-refined structures do not represent the dominant conformational state of channels under low pH conditions. Our subunit-focused classification and symmetry expansion analyses revealed that most channels exist in asymmetric, heterogeneous ensembles consisting of both open and gated subunits. This ensemble-level heterogeneity was essential for demonstrating that under cryo-EM conditions gating in Cx46/50 is not a fully concerted or cooperative process.  Moreover, we would like to emphasize that this level of conformational heterogeneity, and the use of both symmetric and asymmetric processing approaches, reflects an ongoing challenge in the field of gap junction structural biology. Similar degrees of structural heterogeneity have been reported in other connexin structures, including Cx26, Cx32, Cx36 and Cx43, where authors similarly employed symmetry-imposed reconstructions to resolve high-resolution states, alongside C1 or subunit-level analyses to reveal asymmetric features. Our approach is therefore consistent with established methodological practices used to address the inherent complexity of these high-order oligomeric membrane proteins.  We have updated the manuscript to: • Clearly state that D6 reconstructions represent idealized endpoints rather than the dominant ensemble. • Clarify how each analytical approach (D6 vs. C1) contributes complementary insights. • Reference comparable examples in the field where similar methodological strategies have been employed.

pg. 13-14 This ensemble-level heterogeneity also highlights an important methodological point. It is important to emphasize that the D6- (or C6-) symmetrized cryo-EM reconstructions presented in this work do not represent the dominant conformations present under low pH conditions. Neutral pH Data Processing o What happens when the neutral pH dataset is processed separately? o Would this yield a neutral pH open state? Why was it combined with the low pH dataset?  Response: We thank the reviewers for the opportunity to clarify these points regarding our data processing strategy.  The neutral pH datasets (collections #3 and #6) were indeed processed independently prior to employing our mixed-dataset approach for classification of rare-states, as described in our Methods section. When analyzed separately (e.g., using heterogeneous refinement, 3DVA, or symmetry expansion and signal subtraction followed by focused 3D classification) we consistently observed only the primary conformation of the stabilized open-state (see Fig. 3, Extended Data Fig. 8, Extended Data Fig. 9, and Supplemental Movie 1). These findings are consistent with our previously published structural characterization of Cx46/50 in MSP1E1 nanodiscs under similar neutral pH conditions (Flores et al., 2020).  To enhance our ability to identify and resolve minor states that we hypothesized may be present, but only poorly sampled under these conditions, we implemented a combined dataset processing strategy. Specifically, we pooled particles from both neutral and low pH datasets to leverage the dominant state captured under each condition (i.e., open at neutral pH and gated at low pH). This approach allowed us to leverage the major population from one condition to help isolate the minor population from the other. Notably, this strategy was successful and enabled us to extract a rare population of lipid-mediated gated-state particles present under neutral pH conditions, which we were unable to resolve using conventional approaches on the neutral dataset alone.  We hope this explanation resolves any confusion. We have further clarified this rationale and strategy in the revised text on pg. 9.  Rare states detected at neutral pH Previously, under neutral pH cryo-EM conditions, we have only detected the stabilized open-state of Cx46/5040,41. Consistent with this, heterogenous refinement and 3DVA performed on neutral pH nanodisc datasets (Collections #3 and #6), as well as the amphipol low-pH dataset (Collection #2), revealed exclusively open-state channels with minimal NT heterogeneity, with variability limited to a slight “wobble” of TM2 (Supplemental Movie 1, Extended Data Fig. 8 & 9). Resolution Claims & Model Interpretation o Line 133: The claim that heteromeric/-typic assembly patterns of Cx46/50 were indistinguishable is questionable. o At 2.2 Å resolution, individual residues should be distinguishable. If they are not, the reported resolution may be overestimated or your maps are still averaged density.  Response: We appreciate the reviewers’ comments and welcome the opportunity to clarify these points.  At the global resolution of 2.2 Å (now 2.3 Å in our revised gated-state map), most side chains (particularly in well-ordered regions) are clearly resolved, as shown in Extended Data Fig. 5. At positions conserved between Cx46 and Cx50, side chains are well defined. In contrast, density is weaker or ambiguous at isoform-divergent positions, consistent with averaging across two nearly identical sequences rather than a limitation of global map quality.  Our statement that heteromeric/-typic assemblies are “indistinguishable” refers not to resolution, but to the intrinsic limitations of current cryo-EM classification methods when applied to systems with high sequence identity and compositional heterogeneity. Although our prior cross-linking mass spectrometry confirmed the presence of heteromeric Cx46/50 channels (Myers et al., Nature 2018), isoform-specific features remain too subtle and spatially dispersed to support reliable classification. Cx46 and Cx50 share over 80% sequence identity across structured regions, and the few differing residues are often solvent-exposed (i.e., conformationally dynamic), and sparsely distributed – further reducing the signal available to discriminate subunit identity.  This challenge is further compounded by the combinatorially large number of possible subunit arrangements in a dodecameric assembly. Similar issues have been reported for co-expressed Cx26 and Cx32, where high-resolution AFM and Fab-labeling (Naulin et al., J. Biol. Chem. 2021) demonstrated largely stochastic subunit incorporation, underscoring the broader challenge of resolving compositional heterogeneity in such systems by cryo-EM.  Notably, these limitations persist even at higher resolution. In our prior study using datasets refined to 1.9 Å (Flores et al., Nat. Commun. 2020), we observed similar ambiguity at isoform-divergent positions, and provide detailed map-to-model comparisons to illustrate these limitations. The challenges/limitations of this system have also been described in detail in our inaugural structural characterization (see Myers et al. Nature 2018).  We are currently developing Fab-based fiducial labeling strategies to enable isoform-specific assignment in future cryo-EM studies, though these efforts are still in early stages and beyond the scope of the current work.  Given our previous detailed analysis and description of these limitations with native Cx46/50 channels, we do not feel the need to delve into such detail in this current work. We have revised the manuscript text to clarify that the inability to distinguish heteromeric/-typic assemblies arises from both the high structural similarity of the isoforms and the methodological limitations of cryo-EM, rather than an overstatement of resolution. We also display map-to-model quality at these sites of sequence similarity and difference in Extended Data Fig. 5.  pg. 4 Heteromeric/-typic assembly patterns of Cx46 and Cx50 could not be distinguished from the cryo-EM data, consistent with our prior studies40,41, reflecting the >80% sequence identity across structured regions of the two isoforms and methodological limitations of current cryo-EM classification methods.
