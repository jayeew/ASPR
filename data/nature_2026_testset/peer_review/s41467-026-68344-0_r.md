Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The paper reports on electrical control of the band gap of a suspended metallic carbon nanotube (CNT) by introducing a
potential modulation with a gate array underneath. The authors measured the transport characteristics where the data
showed an opening of the band gap in addition to the Coulomb diamonds. The authors attributed the band gap to electrically
induced potential modulation in the CNT channel; the single-electron-transistor model featuring a quantum dot with spatially
modulated potential is used to explain the experimental data. Although I find the paper proposing an interesting model with a
set of experimental data and thereby worth publication in some suitable journal, My opinion is that the paper is not
conclusive enough and would not reach the criteria for the nature communications for the following reasons. 
1) One of my big concerns about the present paper is how it is evidenced that the disorder can be washed out as a specific
gate average voltage. As show in Fig.3 of SI, the energy gap shows quite a variety of complicated behaviors as a function of
the amplitude of the gate modulation, which indicates that the CNT channel suffers from disorder. Although I find it
interesting that the result in Fig 4 can be modeled by the Mathieu equation, if the result itself is intentionally chosen to from
the data of various patterns resulting from the random disorder, it could not be a proof of the gate-modulation induced the
band gap. 
2) There are no descriptions on what kind of single-wall metallic CNT (with chirality, diameter). As very briefly mentioned in
the manuscripts, there have been many papers studying the energy gap of metallic CNTS such as ref [5] of the main text,
Mitchell J. Senger et al. PRB 97 035445 (2018), and so on, where they showed more systematic study. I would like to see
more substantial discussions on how the present work can be compared to the previous work. I would like to see a basic
characteristic such as I-Vg curves with the same voltage to 15 gates, which I think it could a standard way to check if there is
a band gap. 
3) As noted in the paper, the model featuring a modulating potential is rather simple and it does not consider the screening
effect in the metallic CNT. Therefore, I cannot judge if the interpretation of the result is correct quantitatively. I wonder if the
authors can discuss more using the density of states of the metallic CNT. 
4) Minor comment: 
I do not understand how the authors calculated the band gap for “Theory with variating alpha” in Fig. 4 c. Did the authors
introduce variating potential to the sinusoidal function in the Mathieu equation and did numerical calculations?

In this paper, the authors propose a way to open a gap in the spectrum of a 
carbon nanotube by specifically engineered externally applied gate voltages. 
The experiment described in the manuscript constitutes an impressive 
technological achievement. It is however less clear to us what precisely the 
fundamental or conceptual contribution of this work is. We propose below a list of 
points that the authors should address in order both to clarify the

We separate the comments in four groups and we provide a list of typos to be 
corrected. 
## Introduction and motivation 
- The work is motivated by the fact that being able to artificially open a gap 
is useful in many different mesoscopic systems, for various reasons. Here, 
the mechanism has been implemented in a suspended carbon nanotube. It is 
however not clear how this could be transfered to other systems. This is 
particularly hard to grasp since the physical mechanism leading to the 
opening of the gap is not clearly explained in the manuscript (see comments 
about the Mathieu equation). 
- It would help in understanding the context to cite more clearly in the 
introduction the previous theoretical works on using a static CDW to open a 
gap in the bulk and create edge states. Also a clearer connection of the 
presented work to Refs. 1 and 2 would be helpful. It feels indeed that the 
presented method is a generalization of their top-down approach to more 
gates. 
- The authors claim that the goal of this generalization is to prevent local 
perturbations from destroying the desired effect. But the interest of opening 
up a gap seem to usually be the topologically protected edge states that come with 
it, which is not discussed here. Therefore, the references to the 
topological features related to gap opening in condensed matter systems seem 
a bit misleading. 
- An analogy with Peierls systems is proposed in the introduction. It would 
therefore be nice to add a sentence or a reference to say in which sense 
this analogy is justified and what are its limits. 
## Physical mechanism and theory 
- The information about the master equation approach is very detailed (perhaps 
a bit unnecessarily detailed). At the same time it does not become clear why 
the system can be modeled as a large metallic island, instead of a chain of 
quantum dots created by the gates. 
- Section IV: Mathieu equation. This part, which is highly relevant to this 
work, is very much reduced with respect to the lengthy discussion of the 
master equation (see previous comments). 
- It would be helpful to write down the charge density used to solve the 
Poisson equation numerically in Figure 4 (which needs a caption). What is 
neglected when decoupling Poisson and Schroedinger? Is that the backaction of 
the electrons from the CNT on the electric potential, and is it justified to 
neglect this? This would be a nice place to justify the need for 15 gates, 
for example by showing what happens for fewer gates. 
- I feel that stating clearly that the authors only model the bulk properties 
would also help the reader. 
- What is meant by "the equation can be solved asymptotically" (in which 
parameter? is it justified?). In the same sentence it is mentioned that the 
gap is obtained numerically, this seems contradictory. Also, as the semiconductor gap is used to find the 
value of $\delta$, what is its gap $\Delta_g$ and how was it chosen? Please explain in particular their relative magnitude! 
- The most important in this part would be to explain the physical reason for 
the appearance of the gap. This seems to have been thoroughly discussed 
already in Phys. Rev. B. 87, 807 (1952) by Slater. According to him, the 
potential creates the equivalent of "local" atomic potentials, or harmonic 
oscillators around each gate, in which the electrons are "trapped", hence the 
gap. The formula found for the frequency of these harmonic oscillators is 
$\sqrt{V_0 \delta}$, which directly enters in the expression for the gap that 
the authors found in the large gate voltage regime. Related to this, why do 
the authors keep the $-1$ in the large $\eta$ solution of the Mathieu 
equation? I feel that this simple physical picture by Slater should be 
included in the main paper as it gives an intuitive touch to most of the 
results. Notably, the fact that, in the large gate bias regime and if the 
only objective is to open a gap, the periodicity of the potential does not 
crucially matter, as each gate creates an "independent" harmonic oscillator. 
Furthermore, in a wave-particle duality view, $\delta$ looks like the quantum 
of kinetic energy of electrons in the "periodic lattice" created by the 
drive. Therefore, the relevant energies for the gap opening are the height of 
the potential barriers at each "site", $V_0$, and the kinetic energy of 
electrons in such a "crystal", $\delta$. 
This then confirms that the way to 
increase the gap size is to increase the gate modulation amplitude or to

- It would be nice to clarify the topological nature of the setup. Indeed, as 
the authors mentioned when referring to the bulk-boundary correspondence (and 
as is known from the surface state literature), on the edge of a periodic 
crystal, one would expect to see a surface state (Shockley state?). This edge 
state neither shows up in the conductance plots, nor in the top right panel 
of Fig. 
4 where one could expect the gap to be zero for the 1 and 15 gates. 
Looking for edge states might be out of scope but it would be nice to say a 
few words about this. 
## Specifically about the device and the measurements 
- It is not clear to me why the device is embedded in a waveguide cavity. 
- The authors mention that the robustness of the gap is due to the periodic setting 
of the spatial modulation. Is this why many gates are required (the local 
imperfections in the potential landscape would average out). It seems to me 
that this is not the case as the gap opening seems to be a single particle 
property, in the neighborhood of each gate. Is 15 the maximum number of 
gates the authors could achieve because of experimental constraints, or are 
there other reasons for this choice? 
- When saying that the charge density wave might not be stable enough to 
prevail, it would be nice to mention the competing effect (the impurities?). 
- The authors mention charging effects many times, but it is not clear where or 
how they are supposed to explain the discrepancies in the results, in 
particular how they break the left-right symmetry in the nanotube. In the 
results section the authors mention previously observed charging effects 
(where? Which reference?). Also in Fig. 4 there are large discrepancies 
between the red and black dashed curves, which deserve more explanations. 
- When experimental results do not match the modelling, the authors invoke 
inhomogeneous disorder or charging effects without providing any details. It 
would be nice however to give an intuitive explanation as to why this should 
change the expected behavior as observed. For example the asymmetry for 
positive and negative values of $\Delta V_g$ appears a few times, and would 
deserve being commented more. More importantly, when trying to take into 
account the disorder, in Figure 4 of the main text, it does not seem to give 
a better match with experimental data. 
- Section B, comparison of different gate points: The authors talk about "same 
qualitative behavior", but apart from the gap not being constant as a 
function of $\Delta V_g$ I do not see a same qualitative behavior. Also I do 
not see the gap closing, it just gets smaller.

Reviewer #4 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This interesting manuscript describes the experimental observation of a tuneable energy gap in a suspended carbon
nanotube (CNT), created by spatially modulating the local potential of multiple (15) gates below the CNT. The potential is
created by modulating voltage by a fixed value, above and below an average value, from one gate to the next. It is proposed
that such a potential creates a Peierls charge density wave (CDW), with an associated signature – the formation of an
energy gap, varying from 200μeV to 20meV, in transport measurements at low temperature. Single-electron effects are
observed following sweeping of one of the gates, leading to the observation of ‘Coulomb diamond’ (CD) characteristics in
the drain-source conductance vs. gate and drain-source voltage. Such a system can act as a synthesised Peierls system,
with potentially stable quantum states. Furthermore, small and large modulation amplitude on the gates lead respectively to
CDs which close to zero width in drain-source voltage, or CDs with a low current gap at low-drain-source voltage, which
cannot be modulated by the swept gate. It is argued that the gate voltage modulation leads to the gap, due to the Peierls
CDW. 
The paper has an interesting approach, in my view, to the generation of a CDW in a CNT. This require difficult fabrication
challenges to be overcome, e.g. ensuring a stable CNT which does not collapse onto the underlying gates and appropriate
gate dimensions with respect to CNT length. Furthermore, a nice supporting theoretical and experimental analysis is
conducted to show the plausibility of the proposed CDW mechanism, e.g. calculation of energy scales, which support
experimental observation, and in particular, experimental measurements of the induced energy gap using different gates. In
the latter case, the variation in gap width is small, and I agree that this is likely to indicate a homogenous system along the
CNT. 
I think the results presented are very interesting, and the supporting analysis demonstrates that a CDW induced energy gap
mechanism is certainly plausible. The results also have potentially high impact for the generation of tailored, stable quantum
states. I also like the fabrication work, which is non-trivial. Given the above, while I am happy to see that manuscript
published, I would like the point below to be addressed. 
I am somewhat concerned that the observed experimental result, i.e. the formation of a low-bias gap in the CD
characteristics, not modulated by the gate, might occur through other mechanisms as well. Very similar behaviour often
occurs in 1-D semiconductor based single-electron systems, due to additional quantum dots being induced by a change in a
given gate voltage (in the case of the current manuscript, this might be due to the change in modulation amplitude, perhaps
near the CNT edges), with these additional QDs coupling poorly to a swept gate. This would form a multiple tunnel junction /
QD system with varying gate coupling. As the manuscript appears to consider a metallic CNT, this effect may perhaps be
less likely. However, I would like the authors to consider and discuss this possibility in the manuscript, it would be nice to
rule this out – this would in my view strengthen the paper.

We thank the reviewers for this very important point and for pointing out this important paper
by Slater. This paper is equivalent to the “transmon” paper of Wilkinson et al. but it is more
directly related to the gap opening in electronic systems indeed. The Mathieu equation in the
large amplitude limit can be approximated by a series of harmonic traps in which each har-
monic oscillator has a quantized spectrum. This quantization is by construction gapped with
a gap which is the result of the curvature of the harmonic well and the kinetic energy. Like

same V0. This explains qualitatively why the periodic modulation is needed in order to obtain
a robust bandgap. It is worth noting that a similar mechanism is at work in the Quantum Hall
eﬀect which is insulating in the bulk and conducting in the edges. The Landau levels are the
harmonic oscillators in that case. The gap of the bulk is simply the level spacing of these Lan-
dau levels. We have expanded this discussion in the main text and modiﬁed ﬁgure 3 to bring
