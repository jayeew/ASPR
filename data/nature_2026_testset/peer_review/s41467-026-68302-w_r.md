Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This study introduces a Cr-doped Ni3S2 electrocatalyst that achieves selective UOR to nitrite alongside energy-saving
hydrogen production through Lewis acid-triggered hydroxyl spillover. The system demonstrates remarkable performance
metrics. While this study providing valuable mechanistic insights for catalytic engineering in sustainable energy conversion,
several fundamental and application-oriented concerns that need to be addressed before considering publication in Nature
Communications. Specific issues and recommendations are as follows: 
1. The manuscript inadequately presents the principle and current research status of hydroxyl spillover, it needs to more
clearly highlight the unique contributions of this work. 
2. Although the manuscript provides an in-depth investigation of UOR, the advantages of UOR over alternative small-
molecule oxidation processes should be explicitly addressed. 
3. Performance comparison under identical conditions with industrial benchmark catalysts (e.g., Pt or RuO2) for UOR activity
is required to assess practical relevance. 
4. The catalyst's resistance to poisoning in realistic/simulated wastewater environments (containing Cl-, PO43-, etc.),
particularly regarding other anionic groups competition for OH
⁻

⁻

exacerbated competing OER at higher potentials.

7. The pH-dependent onset potential shifts reflect proton/electron transfer kinetics in the rate-determining step (RDS) of
UOR. The authors should provide supporting data to conclusively demonstrate that the catalyst's mechanistic pathway

Comment 1:
The manuscript inadequately presents the principle and current research status
of hydroxyl spillover, it needs to more clearly highlight the unique contributions of
-thiswork.
Response:
Thank you for your valuable suggestions. The spillover effect in electrocatalytic
systems refers to the phenomenon where electroactive species (such as adsorbed
atoms, molecules, ions, or charged intermediates) migrate from one phase or site and
undergo chemical reactions or participate in reaction processes at another phase or
site1. This process can modulate the coverage between different sites, thereby
optimizing the catalytic reaction pathway and enabling a more efficient
electrocatalytic process2. Based on this, we propose a mechanism wherein strong
Lewis acid-base interactions at Cr sites induce hydroxyl spillover. This proposed
mechanism, which enables the selective oxidation of urea to nitrite/nitrate and the
efficient electrochemical synthesis of nitrogen oxyanions, is a key hypothesis and the
centralfocusofthisstudy.
According to your suggestions, we have added a detailed explanation of the
concept of "hydroxyl spillover" in the introduction of the manuscript to support the
rationale and importance of this effect, highlighted in yellow. The specific added
contentsintherevisedmanuscriptareasfollows:
At page 4 line 82-89: “The spillover process, a key phenomenon in
heterogeneous catalysis, involves the migration of activated species (e.g., OH⁻) from
their initial adsorption site to an adjacent, distinct active site30. This process enables
sites that lack intrinsic activity to participate in reactions by utilizing these migrated
species, thereby achieving synergistic catalysis and potentially altering the reaction
pathway, as demonstrated in various systems31. The strong Lewis acid-base effects at
Cr sites may initiate hydroxyl spillover. This spillover subsequently promotes the
selective oxidation of urea to nitrite/nitrate, ultimately facilitating the efficient

Comment 2:
Although the manuscript provides an in-depth investigation of UOR, the
advantages of UOR over alternative small-molecule oxidation processes should be
explicitlyaddressed.
Response:
Compared to other small molecule oxidation processes such as those using
hydrazine or ethanol, UOR offers distinct and attractive advantages3,4. First, UOR
utilizes widely available, easily accessible, and sustainable raw material urea. Urea is
not only produced as a fertilizer on a large industrial scale but is also the main
nitrogen-containing component in human and animal waste. This abundance makes
UOR a practical and valuable technology that can both produce value-added
chemicals and help remediate environmental pollution, particularly in treating
urea-rich wastewater. Second, the process is simple to operate and safer. Unlike
treating highly toxic, flammable and unstablefuels such as hydrazine (which requires
complex purification processes and strict safety regulations), urea is a relatively less
toxic and stable compound. It requires no high-energy pre-treatment steps,
significantly simplifying the design and operation of the reactor and reducing the
overall system cost. Finally, and most importantly, UOR produces nitrite (NO2⁻), a
high-value product used in chemical synthesis, food preservation and metallurgy5.
From a broader perspective, nitrite also serves as a stable, transportable nitrogen
carrier that can be electrochemically or chemically reduced into more valuable
products such as ammonia. The UOR-NO2⁻-NH3 pathway establishes a closed
nitrogencycle,convertingwaste(urea)intostrategicresources(nitrite/ammonia).
We have expanded the Introduction to explicitly highlight the key advantages of
UOR, including its use of an abundant and sustainable feedstock, operational safety,
and its unique capability for producing the high-value product nitrite (NO₂⁻), which
establishes a valuable pathway for environmental remediation and resource recovery.

Comment 5:
The lower NO2- FE and yield for Ni3S2 compared to Cr-Ni3S2 necessitates
clarification on whether this stems from exacerbated competing OER at higher

Response:
ThelowerNO2⁻FEofNi3S2arisesfromthefollowingaspects:
(1) As shown in Fig. 3a-c, the LSV measurement (Fig. 3a) results indicate that
whenthepotentialexceeded1.45VRHE,thecurrentdensityofNi3S2reachedaplateau,
demonstrating significant passivationbehavior.Simultaneously,product analysis with
extended measurement times and reduced error bars confirms that the Faradaic
efficiency (FE) for OER significantly increased with the applied current density.This
phenomenon suggests that Ni3S2 exhibited insufficient selectivity toward the
urea-to-NO2⁻ at higher potentials, while the competing OER intensified, thereby
leadingtoanotableFEdeclineofthetargetproductNO2⁻.
(2)AstestifiedinFigs. 4and 5,theCrsitesinCr-Ni3S2enhancedtheadsorption
ofOH⁻ions,inducingahydroxylspillovereffectthatpromotedtheconversionofurea
into NO2⁻. In contrast, Ni3S2 lacks sufficient OH⁻ supply to facilitate C-N bond
cleavage, leading to a dominant urea-to-N2 pathway (Fig. 3c), which accounts for its
lowerNO2⁻FE.
(3) The accelerated electron transfer induced by Cr doping in Cr-Ni3S2,
combined with hydroxyl spillover, promoted a dynamic Ni2+/Ni3+ cycle. This cycle
was sustained by the facile oxidation of Ni2+ and the subsequent reduction of Ni3+
back to Ni2+ by urea (Supplementary Figs. 42-44). This process effectively alleviated
Ni3+ accumulation and suppressed OER. Conversely, Ni3S2 suffered from continuous
Ni3+ accumulation under high current densities, favoring OER and thereby reducing
theFEofNO2⁻.
