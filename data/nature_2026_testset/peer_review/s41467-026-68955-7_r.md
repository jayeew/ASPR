(Remarks to the Author) 
Metallocenes with layered structures have had a significant impact on the development of organometallic chemistry. In
particular, metallocenes with high catalytic activity and electrochemical redox ability play a very important role in their
applications. This paper proposes a new group of compounds called "Inverse palladocenes", which are different from the
general "palladocenes", which are metallocenes based on palladium. These groups of materials have a high degree of
novelty in the fields of metal organic chemistry and cluster chemistry. Furthermore, these researches have already been
investigated at a high level. 
Minor comments are shown below.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript presents a comprehensive study on the synthesis, characterization, and photothermal properties of a novel
class of Pd-based nanoclusters "inverse palladocenes." The work is highly original and addresses an under-explored area
in organometallic chemistry involving "nonmetal-to-metal" coordination and Pd aromaticity. The inverse palladocenes
demonstrate rigorous structural/electronic characterization and promising applications, particularly in photothermal
conversion and laser shielding. 
The study is in general well-organized and the major conclusions are supported by the results, which is suitable for the
audience of Nature Communications. However, there are a few issues should be addressed: 
1. In Figure 4f, the RGD plot appears with many jagged edges, making it difficult to fully observe the π-π stacking interaction.
We kindly suggest considering an increase in the number of grid points to improve the visualization. 
2. Is there any electron transfer occurring between the phenyl group of the DPPM ligand and the nearby metal five-
membered ring? We kindly suggest calculating the orbital distribution based on the crystal structure to analyze the orbital or
hole-electron distribution within the aggregate. Additionally, it may be helpful to calculate the electrostatic potential of
inverse palladocycles for further analysis. 
3. Should the authors provide more details about the testing conditions for the absorption spectra of inverse palladocenes?
For instance, are the samples in the form of powder, thin films, or solutions? If the samples are in solution, it would be
interesting to explore how different polarities of solvents affect their absorption spectra. On the other hand, if the samples are
in the form of powder or thin films, the author could investigate any differences in their absorption spectra under various
conditions. 
4. The inverse palladocences synthesized by the author show diverse photothermal conversion capabilities. Referring to the
Jablonski energy level diagram, might a structure with relatively lower photothermal conversion efficiency exhibit
photoluminescence? If this material features near-infrared luminescence properties, its potential application could be greatly
broadened. 
5. The author noted that the photothermal conversion originates from the conjugation of the five-membered palladium
aromatic ring of inverse palladocences. However, we also observe five-membered palladium aromatic rings in Pd8 and Pd6.
Why Pd8 and Pd6 don't demonstrate sufficiently strong photothermal conversion capabilities? 
6. In the final paragraph of the article, the author mentions that Pd5-C encapsulated in polystyrene assist in the degradation
of polystyrene at 368 °C. However, in supplementary figure 25, the Pd5-C decomposed at ~300 °C and reached a mass loss
of 60% by ~50 °C. Considering this information, why the reaction might specifically occur at 368 °C?

8. In which solution was the photothermal conversion tested (Figure 6a), water or toluene?

Question 1: What is the essential difference between Reference 15 (Angew. Chem. Int.
