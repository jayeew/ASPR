Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript by Tsopoulidis et al. examined the functional impacts of Nudt21 on cell proliferation/self-renewal and
differentiation using mouse models. The Hochedlinger group, by using shRNAs and siRNAs, previously reported a
significant role of Nudt21 in cell differentiation and dedifferentiation in the context of induced pluripotent cells. Here they use
in vivo systemic or cell type-specific (basal stem cells of squamous epithelia) knockout (KO) mice as well as KO cells with
varing levels of Nudt21 expression through inducible expression of exogenous Nudt21. The major finding is that Nudt21
expression at different levels can exert distinct functions on cell proliferation/self-renewal and differentiation. In KO cells, cell
proliferation is inhibited. At the molecular level, transcripts with miRNA target sites are upregulated, in line with the notion
that 3’UTR shortening caused by Nudt21 deficiency mitigates miRNA targeting. 
In hypomorphs with ~70% reduction of
expression, the authors found less potent 3’UTR shortening and weaker the miRNA effect. Interestingly, while the
hypomorphs have limited effects on cell proliferation/self-renewal, cell differentiation is affected, indicating that
proliferation/self-renewal can be uncoupled from differentiation. Moreover, the authors found that many protein complexes
are downregulated in KO cells, such as the nuclear pore complex whose expression and functions were specifically
examined in this work. The authors suggest that 3’UTRs may play roles in co-translational complex assembly. Overall, this
study was carried out with good rigor. The conclusions are somewhat expected based what have already been known. But
this work does add useful information, especially with the in vivo data, on how Nudt21 exerts its functions. I have only some
minor comments and suggestions. 
- The authors speculate that the suppression of protein complex expression and formation in Nudt21 deficient conditions
might be due to defects in 3’UTR-mediated co-translational assembly. This is an interesting hypothesis. However, it might
also be possible that some chaperone proteins responsible for protein complex assembly are particularly sensitive to 3’UTR
size regulation. The authors may want to discuss this as well. 
- Some previous studies indicated that global 3’UTR shortening could lead to alteration of miRNA targeting through
competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) networks. It would be informative and helpful to the field if the authors could use their
data to evaluate this aspect of APA function. 
- Fig. 3f, y-axis, it would be more appropriate to use ‘shortened’ instead of ‘short’ 3’UTRs. Likewise, ‘lengthened’ should be
used in place of ‘long’.

Reviewer #3 
(Remarks to the Author) 
Tsopoulidis et al., generated conditional KO mice for Nudt21, the most important regulator of APA. This KO, together with a
Nudt21 ‘hypomorph’, which was obtained by rescuing the KO with approximately 25% of Nudt21 expression allows the
authors to investigate the biological consequences of impaired APA. The authors observed that full deletion of Nudt21 in
adult mice results in their lethality within 5-7 days. This is a striking finding and demonstrates the biological importance of
APA. As Nudt21 is highly expressed in the stem cell compartment, the authors then focused on stem cell biology to
investigate how the loss of Nudt21 results in animal death. They observed that highly proliferative cell types, such as the
gastrointestinal tract or hematopoietic system are strongly affected, whereas tissues without regeneration are not affected,
but become affected during regeneration stress. All these aspects were very nicely demonstrated, which is the strength of
the paper. These findings should definitely get published and are very interesting for the community. 
The authors then try to determine the molecular mechanism by which loss of Nudt21 affects proliferation (cell cycle) or
differentiation. They perform PAS-seq and mass spectrometry analysis and pursue different hypotheses, including miRNA
regulation and loss of nuclear pores as the potential underlying mechanisms. However, these investigations are not
performed very rigorously and are only modestly convincing. 
Specific points: 
1. The miRNA analysis does not make the paper stronger. It is only correlative and does not add real insights. Even if loss of
miRNA regulation would cause slight upregulation of some of the factors shown in Fig. 3i, it is unclear whether this has any
relevance for the phenotype observed. In my opinion, the paper would benefit from leaving this part (Fig. 3f-3l and Ext Data
Fig. 4f-4g) out as it does not add anything to the proposed mechanism by which loss of Nudt21 causes death. 
2. Nudt21 affects the nuclear pore. 
It is currently unclear, what the authors think is the molecular mechanism by which Nudt21 loss affects the nuclear pore. If
Nup160, Nup107 and Seh1L are essential genes and they are knocked-down, it is not surprising that the cells show
decreases in cell cycle progression. Moreover, the figure with the chromatin bridges in Ext Data Fig. 5j, k should be
removed. If the authors think that formation of the chromatin bridges are causative, it needs to be demonstrated. 
If they think that decreased Nup160 protein levels are causative for the phenotype, it is important to restore Nup160 protein

level to investigate if this rescues some of the phenotypes seen in Nudt21 loss of function cells. 
Or, if the authors think that the longest 3’UTR isoform of Nup160 is responsible for protein complex assembly with other
proteins in the nuclear pore, then, direct evidence needs to be shown. At the current state of the manuscript, lots of data are
presented, but the overall message of this part is not clear. 
3. The data in Fig. 4g showed upregulation of 26S proteasome complexes in the KO. As an alternative explanation, could
this contribute to the observed protein destabilization, and therefore not all observed decreases in protein levels are direct
consequences of APA-dependent protein complex formation? Importantly, most essential genes, not just genes regulated by

In the critical Fig./Extended Fig 5, which should help to establish causal relationship between APA and the phenotype, there

decrease in the mRNA level of the longest isoform and of Nup160 protein levels. 
4. A strength of the paper is the genome-wide and proteome-wide analyses in a time course of Nudt21 depletion. As the
authors seem to have problems of pinpointing to the mechanism responsible for the observed phenotype, it is essential to
provide the entire dataset for other researchers to use (as supplementary table). This should include all the data on APA, all
the mass spectrometry data (not just the data on the essential differentially expressed proteins). Also, the data on the
hypomorph should be included in this table. If this table is presented well (with p-values and fold-changes), it would be a
useful resource for the community to test alternative hypotheses on the mechanism by which Nudt21 has such strong effects
on self-renewal and differentiation.

We thank this reviewer for their overall interest and conclusion that our study was performed with good rigor and adds useful information to the field.  - The authors speculate that the suppression of protein complex expression and formation in Nudt21 deficient conditions might be due to defects in 3’UTR-mediated co-translational assembly. This is an interesting hypothesis. However, it might also be possible that some chaperone proteins responsible for protein complex assembly are particularly sensitive to 3’UTR size regulation. The authors may want to discuss this as well. We thank the reviewer for suggesting this alternative interpretation. To test whether chaperones are directly impacted by Nudt21 loss, we examined 3′UTR shortening and protein abundance of known chaperones in Nudt21 KO cells. Among the 12 chaperones detected in our proteomic data, only three (Dnaja1, Hsp90aa1, and Hspa1a) showed 3′UTR shortening upon Nudt21 KO. Importantly, none of these chaperones was significantly downregulated at the protein level, arguing against a direct effect of 3’UTR shortening on chaperone destabilization in Nudt21 KO cells. However, we note that certain chaperones were upregulated in Nudt21 KO cells even though their corresponding mRNAs did not exhibit 3′UTR changes, including components of the “chaperonin-containing T-complex” and “protein folding chaperone complex”. These upregulated proteins likely reflect secondary changes caused by failed protein complex assembly rather than direct 3′UTR-dependent regulation of chaperones themselves. Deregulation of ceRNA networks in Nudt21-depleted cells is exemplified by the APA target Sec24a. Sec24a is critical for the Nudt21-dependent differentiation defect, and its long APA isoform contains 28 predicted miRNA binding sites (see updated Figure 3i). We thank the reviewer for this insightful question and experimental suggestion, which we have tried to address in four different ways:  (1) To determine whether the 3′UTR isoforms of Nup160 influence cell cycle progression, expression, or localization of any protein-coding genes, we performed transfection experiments using in vitro transcribed (IVT) RNA constructs encoding either an in-frame GFP-Nup160 ORF or a GFP ORF fused to short, medium, and long Nup160 3′UTR isoforms. We chose IVT RNA to avoid confounding effects from DNA-based expression constructs, which might be subject to Nudt21-dependent APA regulation. Overexpression of long but not short 3’UTR Nup160 isoforms linked to the GFP-Nup160 ORF led to cell cycle arrest in WT cells, confirming strikingly different biological effects of distinct Nup160 3’UTR isoforms and suggesting that endogenous Nup160 3’UTR size needs to be tightly regulated endogenously to sustain cell division (Extended Data Fig. 10a). Nup160 3’UTR isoforms linked to GFP had only subtle effects on cell cycle progression (Extended Data Fig. 10a). Moreover, GFP-Nup160 ORF constructs with the long 3’UTR led to reduced GFP levels than the short and medium 3’UTRs, supporting an isoform-dependent effect on protein levels. Notably, reduced protein levels were also observed with the long 3’UTR GFP construct compared to the medium and short 3’UTR GFP constructs, consistent with a coding sequence-independent effect on protein levels (Extended Data Fig.10c-f). No significant changes in protein localization were observed between different 3’UTR isoforms (Extended Data Fig.10g). (2) To determine whether different Nup160 3’UTR isoforms are associated with different proteins, we performed RNA immunoprecipitation (RNA-IP) using biotinylated versions of the three IVT Nup160 mRNA isoforms. This revealed that the long Nup160 3′UTR isoform preferentially binds to ribosomal subunits, supporting the hypothesis that an extended 3′UTR facilitates ribosome recruitment or engagement, perhaps by enabling the co-translational assembly of Nup160 in the nuclear pore complex (Extended Data Fig.11). These findings align with the previously proposed model of 3′UTR-mediated coordination between translation and protein complex assembly3 .  (3) Overexpression of the long Nup160 3’UTR isoform was insufficient to rescue the cell cycle block caused by Nudt21 KO, suggesting that cell cycle-dependent expression is critical for proper Nup160 function and/or that dysregulation of additional APA targets contributes to the cell cycle defect of Nup160 KO cells (Extended Data Fig. 10b). (4) Finally, we performed disome selective ribosome profiling (DiSP) in collaboration with Bernd Bukau’s lab to assess the global effect of Nudt21 loss on co-co-translationally assembled proteins. We discovered that nearly half of all co-co-translational protein assembly events detected in control cells were reduced in the absence of Nudt21, and affected proteins were enriched for cell cycle regulators. These observations are consistent with our cell cycle phenotype and support the notion that APA plays a critical role in co-co-translational assembly mechansims. We appreciate the reviewer’s comment regarding the mechanistic role of the long 3′UTR isoform of Nup160 in nuclear pore complex assembly. Mechanistically, we propose a model whereby the long 3′UTR of Nup160 contributes to nuclear pore complex assembly or stability by facilitating the co-translational interaction of subunits. In support of this notion, we found that the long 3′UTR isoform interacts more strongly with ribosomal subunits using pull-down experiments with in vitro transcribed (IVT) and biotinylated Nup160 transcripts (Extended Data Fig.11, see response to reviewer 2, question 9). Consistent with this model,  3′UTRs have recently been suggested to help orchestrate spatial translation, folding, and complex assembly3,4. To experimentally support this model, we performed DiS, which captures co-co-translational protein assembly events. Co-co-translational assembly is defined as a translational process in which a nascent polypeptide chain interacts with another nascent polypeptide chain, in contrast to co-post-translational assembly, where a nascent polypeptide chain interacts with an already folded protein. Although Nup160 did not score in the DiSP assay, we found that almost half of all co-co-translationally assembled proteins are impacted by Nudt21 KO and that most of these factors are involved in cell cycle processes, supporting a hitherto unknown function of Nudt21 in the regulation of co-co-translational assembly processes (Extended Data Fig. 12, lines 570-590 of the text, page 13, see response to reviewer 2 question 9). To address the question of how the 3’UTR of Nup160 influences nuclear pore assembly, future experiments outside the scope of the current manuscript (e.g. co-post translational assembly assays) are required to definitively prove this model. Of relevance, recent evidence suggests that conserved 3′UTRs can modulate the biogenesis of intrinsically disordered region (IDR)-containing proteins by acting as RNA-based chaperones5 . In this framework, 3′UTRs engage in multivalent interactions with IDRs during translation, influencing oligomerization states and folding kinetics in condensate-like environments. It is conceivable that such a mechanism also applies to Nup160, which contains extended domains with structural flexibility, and may help explain how its long 3′UTR contributes to the assembly of stable nuclear pore complexes. While we acknowledge that additional experiments will be required to distinguish between these mechanisms, our data and models provide a useful conceptual framework for follow-up experiments (e.g., direct co-post translational assembly assays or tests for 3′UTR–IDR interactions).
