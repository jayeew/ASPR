Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
The manuscript clearly presents an innovative extension of guided-wave OCE for characterizing nonlinear viscoelastic and
anisotropic properties of arterial tissues. Methodological advances are substantial, particularly the use of a two-layer
viscoelastic guided-wave model, which significantly enhances the interpretation of complex arterial biomechanical
properties. The experimental setup and data analysis are robust, and the clear visualization (figures and schematic
diagrams) is helpful. Application of selective chemical treatment to elucidate the contributions of collagen and elastin fibers
provides valuable mechanistic insights into arterial biomechanics. 
Overall, the manuscript is well organized and clearly written. While only minor revisions are needed, the manuscript could
be further improved by addressing the following points: 
1. The Discussion already acknowledges limitations, including boundary conditions (air vs. blood; fluid vs. tissue), curvature,
and the plan to move toward non-contact ultrasound excitation. This is excellent. Because excitation physics affect mode
content and dispersion, a one-sentence clarification would help readers: the present contact PZT probe predominantly
exerts mechanical surface forcing at relatively low center frequencies (set by tip contact length and coupling), whereas non-
contact ultrasound would predominantly rely on acoustic radiation force if higher frequency ultrasound is being used,
potentially altering the excited mode mix and adding controllable focal size but also introducing new variables. This is
consistent with the manuscript’s own limitations and future-work statements; I’m simply suggesting you make the mechanism
distinction explicit for broader readership. 
2. In the Results, A0/S0 are first referenced (Line 98) and then defined a bit later as quasi-antisymmetric and quasi-
symmetric modes (Line 100). For readability, please give the full names at the first mention and consider adding a very brief
description about the basic physics about the induced elastic wave.

Reviewer #4 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this work, the authors measured the biomechanical properties of porcine aortas using optical coherence elastography. A0
and S0 wave propagation modes were fitted to various models to determine the optimal approach to extract biomechanical
information. Aortic biomechanical properties were measured as a function of axial stretch, and changes to mechanical
properties due to collagen degradation were also examined. This work is recommended for publication following minor
revisions. 
1. The tissues are oriented in the water bath so that the intima is exposed to air. What steps are taken to ensure hydration of
the intima? 
2. Excitation was performed with a custom tip mounted on a PZT, and pure tone stimuli were used to perform wave
propagation. Was each frequency excited individually, or was a chirped signal used? If frequencies were excited
individually, how many cycles was each excitation? 
3. In the discussion section, the authors describe using noncontact approaches when translating to in vivo. Is a noncontact
OCE approach necessary when the procedure to access vascular tissue will be invasive? 
4. What is the highest speed that the OCE system can measure in the configuration used for these sets of experiments?
Understanding that could help clarify why S0 modes failed at higher stretch ratios. 
5. The authors suggest that excitation velocities can become unreliable due to low excitation efficiency at higher stretch
ratios, could the authors clarify what is meant by “excitation efficiency”? 
6. While the authors have done extensive theoretical and simulation studies developing and validating their models,
demonstrating model validity on tissue mimicking phantoms would dramatically improve the strength of the work.
