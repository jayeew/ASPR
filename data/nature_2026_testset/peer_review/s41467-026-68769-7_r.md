Reviewer #1 
(Remarks to the Author) 
This manuscript proposes a novel method to predict unobserved interactions in food webs. As such, it participates to the
recently growing body of literature on this question (for food webs and for other types of networks). Consequently, one may
perceive this effort as incremental. However, this contribution stands out on several aspects. 
Firstly, while the choice of model stacking is by itself not a breakthrough (one may indeed expect that the combination of the
results of different existing models will be superior to the results of any individual model), it is based here on a large number
of simple predictors that were, to my knowledge and for most of them, not used before for this purpose. Also, the information
used is not only based on the network itself (structure-only models), but also integrates external information on the nodes,
with a sensible distinction between assortative and disassortative descriptors. 
Secondly, the work is really extensive, by the use of a large number of predictors, by the careful test of the approach with
synthetic data, and by the application of the method to a large dataset of 290 published food webs. 
Thirdly, the analyses of the results are comprehensive: which predictor and which type of information perform best; how
these results differ between the different types of networks (grouped according to the habitat type); how model performance
correlates with food web properties. 
Finally, the discussion of the results with regard to the existing literature is interesting and exhaustive, which is very useful
for future work on this topic. There is however one topic that should be treated in this section. You mention that food webs
sometimes come with additional complexity, among them edge weights (Li. 876). By giving the same importance to all edges
in qualitative networks, a full description of a food web will obscure rather than clarify its structure (see e.g. Tylianakis et al.
2007, Nature 445, 202–205, for a practical example). Consequently, edges of food webs described extensively should
ideally be weighted (with relevant metrics). In this respect, it is useful to indicate if, and how, your method could predict edge
weights (a priori, it should be straightforward to adapt the predictors to quantitative information). 
I have a single technical reservation on the work. I do not understand the choice of a linear regression coefficient for the
analysis of the relationships between food web properties and performance (ROC-AUC and PR-AUC). From Fig. S4 and
S5, it is clearly inappropriate since values larger than 1 can be predicted. Also, the distribution of the points may not be
linear (e.g. in Fig. S4d). I recommend replacing the slopes with non-parametric correlation coefficients (Kendall or
Spearman), which are perfectly adequate here. This requires rerunning a lot of analyses (Figs. 7 and S6 to S12), but this
should be straightforward to adapt the code for this purpose. Also, I would replace the regression lines in Fig. S4 and S5 by
lines obtained e.g., by moving average or generalized additive models.

Reviewer #2 
(Remarks to the Author) 
In this manuscript, the authors present a stacked model approach to predict missing links in food webs. The novelty lies in
the use of stacked generalization, which appears to improve predictive performance by optimally combining information from
both structural models (which predict links based solely on network topology) and trait-based models (which use species-
level information). To adapt this methodology to ecological networks, the authors thoughtfully modify established network
metrics, taking into account the specific characteristics of food webs such as directionality and trophic hierarchy. They
convincingly argue that this combined approach outperforms previous methods in missing link prediction. 
I find this article interesting and timely, as improving the resolution of ecological networks is becoming increasingly important
for advancing both theoretical and empirical studies in community ecology. While I am not a specialist in machine learning, I
found the methodology to be clearly described, well executed, and the results effectively communicated. In particular, I
appreciate the authors’ effort to explain how standard predictors were adapted to the ecological context; I thank them for the
clarity of writing throughout the manuscript. 
Although I cannot fully assess the methodological novelty from a machine learning perspective, I believe the manuscript will
be of broad interest to researchers working with ecological networks. 
I, however had difficulty locating the data/code accessibility statement, so I recommend to include it more clearly in the
manuscript. Also, consider improving the readme file to more clearly explain the workflow, as it is very difficult to follow it
how it is included now. 
That said, I only have some minor comments: 
Reference 106 and Reference 3: Missing journal information—please complete. 
Line 105: There is an unmatched quotation mark ("). 
Line 359: I recommend briefly explaining what ROC-AUC represents the first time it appears. 
Line 465: Indicate more precisely where in the Supplementary Information the empirical distributions can be found (e.g.,
"Fig. S17–S20").

either within or between webs for 83 nodes" is unclear. It is not clear what "nodes" refers to in the second instance—do the
authors mean "webs" or something else? Please clarify. 
Lines 576 & 601: Consider repeating the figure number where the referenced result is shown to help guide the reader.

in the individual ones. For instance, does the “log mass ratio” feature appear only in the trait-based model (blue) or also in
the combined model (orange)? Please clarify this in the figure legend or main text. 
Line 644: The phrase “may be in alignment with prior work” is vague. Either confirm the alignment (e.g., "These results are
consistent with...") or rephrase cautiously (e.g., "These results appear to follow similar patterns to those reported in..."). 
Line 896: The sentence with “respectively” is incomplete. Please revise it to clarify what is being compared.
