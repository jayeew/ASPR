Reviewers' comments:
 
Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
In this manuscript, one pot method for synthesizing self assembled flower like superstructures from InP/ZnSe/ZnS QDs was
reported. It has an absolute photoluminescence quantum yield of 87%. Moreover, the synthesis process, surface chemical
regulation, formation mechanism of the superstructure, and optimization of optical properties were described in detail.
However, the study has a major flaw in that it lacks detailed information on the mechanism of action of the TOP ligand and
its specific impact on the superstructure properties. Therefore, some revision has be done before this manuscript could be
accepted for publication in this Communications Materials. 
1. In the “DFT calculation for InP/ZnSe/ZnS QDs” section, it is suggested to add a correlation analysis with the experimental
TRPL to make it more convincing. 
2. Besides the zeta potential data in Figure S4 supporting the reduction of electrostatic repulsion, it is suggested to further
explore the specific mechanism of superstructure formation, including how TOP promotes the arrangement of quantum dots.
And the role of TOP was only qualitatively demonstrated through FTIR analysis. 
3. Literature citation: Please ensure that you cite the most recent relevant studies to reflect the current progress in the field,
especially in the introduction section 
4. In the “Stability and repeatability”section, It is recommended to provide additional experimental data regarding the long-
term stability and reproducibility of the synthesized superstructures to enhance the reliability of the results. For example,
supplement the inter-batch repetitive data (at least n=3). 
5. Figure 1 and 8, etc. are not arranged neatly and uniformly. 
6. In the “Reference” section, please note the uniformity and standardization of the references. 
7. It is suggested that the language in some paragraphs be simplified to make the expression clearer and more
straightforward. 
Reviewer #2 (Remarks to the Author): 
The authors present a one-pot synthesis approach of InP/ZnSe/ZnS QD assemble superstructures which is supported by a
strong set of characterizations like TEM and SAXS. In my opinion, the data quality is remarkable and above average for
what has been previously published in this domain. I suggest the authors to provide clarity on a few comments in order to
further strengthen their overall work presented in this manuscript. 
(1) The authors use the terminology : “single crystalline” for the superstructure. And the reasoning behind using that
terminology is the TEM fringes that are observed across the flower-like structure that is formed due to OA. I would like to
disagree with that entire understanding. Reason being – oriented attachment is a term utilized for arrangement of multiple
single crystalline components that eventually form a polycrystalline structure. To claim it as single crystalline, I believe its
essential to have a clear understanding of the unit cell, which I totally understand is not straightforward given the complexity
of this work. All in all, I would suggest that the authors re-think and revise this terminology and maybe provide a stronger
justification if they still believe it to be a single crystalline structure. 
(2) I believe the 
size calculations using SAXS need to be included in the manuscript or the SI. This pertains to the values in
Table 1. The authors do mention “spherical particle model”, but can the authors provide the differences in calculation and
compare the values for spherical vs non-spherical models? I am guessing the authors are using some form of the Scherrer
equation. 
(3) Comparison of the optical properties for this QD with previous reports is essential to compare performance with the
developed methodology. 
(4) I would suggest including XPS spectra results of the InP, InP/ZnSe and InP/ZnSe/ZnS QD superstructures. This would
provide valuable information about how the coordination alters with added molecules.

Reviewer #1 (Remarks to the Author): 
 
In this manuscript, one pot method for synthesizing self assembled flower like 
superstructures from InP/ZnSe/ZnS QDs was reported.It has an absolute 
photoluminescence quantum yield of 87%. Moreover, the synthesis process, surface 
chemical regulation, formation mechanism of the superstructure, and optimization of optical 
properties were described in detail. However, the study has a major flaw in that it lacks 
detailed information on the mechanism of action of the TOP ligand and its specific impact on 
the superstructure properties. Therefore, some revision has be done before this manuscript 
could be accepted for publication in this Communications Materials. 
1. In the “DFT calculation for InP/ZnSe/ZnS QDs” section, it is suggested to add a correlation 
analysis with the experimental TRPL to make it more convincing. 
We thank the reviewer for this valuable suggestion. In the revised manuscript, we have added 
the following discussions to understand the correlation between the experimental TRPL results 
and the DFT calculations. 
The TRPL measurements show an increasing average lifetime with shelling from InP core to  
InP/ZnSe/ZnS QDs, which is consistent with our DFT results indicating a reduction of mid-gap 
trap states and enhanced radiative recombination in the shelled structures. Furthermore, the 
presence of TOP ligands reduces mid -gap states even further, which co rrelates with the 
experimentally observed enhancement in PL intensity and longer carrier lifetimes. This 
combined analysis strengthens the conclusion that shelling and ligand passivation suppress 
non-radiative pathways, thereby improving PLQY and carrier dynamics. 
 
2. Besides the zeta potential data in Figure S4 supporting the reduction of electrostatic 
repulsion, it is suggested to further explore the specific mechanism of superstructure 
formation, including how TOP promotes the arrangement of quantum dots. And the role of 
TOP was only qualitatively demonstrated through FTIR analysis. 
We thank the reviewer for this insightful comment. We have now clarified the mechanism of 
superstructure formation by explicitly linking interparticle distance, ligand chemistry, and 
interaction free energy. TEM analysis reveals that oleylamine-capped InP quantum dots 
remain well separated with interparticle surface to surface distance ~3–3.5 nm, indicating 
dominant steric and electrostatic repulsion that prevents ordered assembly. In contrast, the 
presence of TOP during synthesis leads to a sharp reduction in interparticle surface to 
surface distance ~1.0–1.2 nm, consistent across different TOP concentrations. 
This behavior can be explained by a distance-controlled transition in the interparticle 
interaction potential. From the experimental and theoretical evidenced reported, we have 
seen that TOP which is a short chain ligand, when present during nucleation and growth, 
dominates surface passivation despite the coexistence of oleylamine.TOP being a short 
chain ligand reduces the interparticle separation unlike the long chain bulky ligand 
oleylamine. this results in a compact ligand shell that reduces both steric and electrostatic 
repulsion while enabling short-range van der Waals attraction. As a result, the interaction 
free energy develops a well-defined minimum at short separation, favoring ordered packing 
rather than random dispersion or irreversible aggregation. This behaviour is consistent with a 
transition from a purely repulsive interaction regime to one characterized by a distance-

defined free-energy minimum that stabilizes ordered quantum dot assemblies .Increasing 
TOP concentration improves long-range order without further reducing interparticle spacing, 
supporting a self-limited, thermodynamically driven assembly mechanism. More detail 
discussion have been provided in SI(discussion  S1), where we have added detail 
mathematical expressions of interparticle interaction forces and expanded the discussion to 
describe the interaction free energy as a sum of van der Waals, electrostatic, steric, and 
ligand-mediated contributions. These additions provide a physically grounded and 
quantitative framework for understanding the role of TOP in promoting ordered 
superstructure formation. One schematic showing the superstructure formation has also 
been added (fig 9) in the manuscript. 
We have now also added XPS analysis for InP QDs with and without TOP samples which 
provides direct evidence for TOP-dominated surface coordination in InP QDs with TOP. In 
InP QDs synthesized without TOP , a clear N 1s peak is observed, confirming oleylamine 
binding to surface indium sites. However, in the presence of TOP , the N 1s signal is 
completely suppressed, indicating displacement or negligible coordination of oleylamine. In 
addition, the phosphorus atomic percentage is significantly higher in the TOP-assisted 
sample, consistent with phosphine-derived surface species. These observations are in good 
agreement with the binding energy simulations and FTIR results, collectively confirming that 
TOP preferentially coordinates to the InP surface and dominates the surface chemistry even 
in the presence of oleylamine.

We thank the reviewer for raising this important crystallographic point. We agree that the term 
“single crystalline ” requires rigorous structural validation, typically involving unambiguous 
determination of a continuous unit cell acro ss the entire structure which is beyond the scope 
of the present work. 
We fully agree with the reviewer that oriented attachment does not necessarily imply the 
formation of a true single crystal, and that such assemblies are more appropriately described 
as mesocrystalline or crystallographically oriented superstructures. 
Accordingly, we have revised the manuscript to replace the term “single crystalline 
superstructure” with “crystallographically oriented (mesocrystalline) superstructure ” 
throughout the text. We believe this revision more accurately reflects the structural nature of 
the superstructures while remaining consistent with the experimental evidence. 
 
(2) I believe the size calculations using SAXS need to be included in the manuscript or the

We thank the reviewer for this important comment regarding the SAXS size analysis 
reported in Table 1. 
The particle size values were extracted from small-angle X-ray scattering (SAXS) data using 
model fitting implemented in the SASVIEW software, and not using the Scherrer equation. 
We regret that this distinction was not made sufficiently clear in the original manuscript. 
In our analysis, the SAXS profiles were fitted using a spherical particle form-factor model, 
which assumes isotropic particles with a narrow size distribution. This assumption is justified 
based on (i) the absence of anisotropic features in the SAXS patterns and (ii) consistency 
with TEM observations, which show predominantly quasi-spherical nanoparticles. The 
extracted radii correspond to the volume-weighted mean particle size obtained from the best 
fits. 
To address the reviewer’s request, we additionally tested non-spherical form-factor models 
(ellipsoidal model) within SASVIEW and found it’s a bad fit with χ2(chi square) value 108 
which is unacceptable. Spherical model shows the χ2 value to be <1 indicating a very good 
fit.The fitted datas are compared in fig.S5 in supporting information. 
We have now included a detailed description of the SAXS fitting procedure, model 
assumptions, and comparison between spherical and non-spherical models in the 
Supporting Information method section. 
 
(3) Comparison of the optical properties for this QD with previous reports is essential to 
compare performance with the developed methodology. 
We thank the reviewer for this important suggestion. To enable a meaningful assessment of 
the optical performance achieved using the developed methodology, we have now included 
a detailed comparison of the key optical properties of our quantum dots with representative 
reports from the literature. Specifically, absorption onset, photoluminescence (PL) peak 
position, full width at half maximum (FWHM), and photoluminescence quantum yield (PLQY) 
have been compared with previously reported values for similar material systems and size 
regimes. 
This comparison, has now been added in the revised manuscript (Table S3 ) which shows 
that the optical characteristics obtained in this work are consistent with, and in some cases 
improved relative to, prior reports. The narrow PL linewidth and high PLQY indicate good 
size uniformity and effective surface passivation, validating the effectiveness of the 
developed synthetic and assembly methodology. These results demonstrate that the present 
approach yields optically high-quality quantum dots without compromising emissive 
performance relative to established methods.

spectra of the InP, InP/ZnSe, and InP/ZnSe/ZnS quantum dot superstructures in the

incorporated into the main manuscript.
