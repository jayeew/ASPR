Gravitationally induced stratiﬁcation during self-assembly often leads to
density-driven vertical segregation, resulting in an inherent density gradient
that severely limits the synthesis of metastable nanoﬁlms requiring inverted
architectures. Here we show an antigravity conﬁned interfacial self-assembly
approach based on a liquid-liquid interface formed between hydrophilic and
hydrophobic porous membranes, where capillary forces suppress gravita-
tional effects to enable precise molecular organization. Experimental data,
supported by quantum chemistry, density functional theory, and Fick’s ﬁrst
law, demonstrate that capillary forcesenhance local concentration and inter-
action probability, yielding highly ordered, stable nanoﬁlms. Our approach
achieves a 17-fold increase inﬁlm area than gravity-limited methods and a 109-
fold improvement over unconﬁned techniques. These nanoﬁlms exhibit the
stability and mechanical property, showing promise for green enhanced oil
recovery and multifunctional material development.Furthermore, our strat-
egy offers a paradigm for nanoﬁlm mechanical characterization, paving the
way for future advances in the design and application of nanomaterials.
Conﬁned interfacial self-assembly is a material synthesis process where
molecular systems spontaneously organize into ordered structures at
interfaces or surfaces under con ﬁned conditions1–3. This approach
represents a versatile and powerful approach for designing and syn-
thesizing materials with tailored functionalities, offering broad appli-
cations in nanotechnology, environmental science, and biomedical
engineering
4,5. In materials synthesis, it offers precise control over the
formation of nanostructures, enabling the design of customized
nanomaterials with speciﬁc properties. By utilizing con ﬁned spaces,
this technique allows for theﬁne-tuning of the self-assembly process at
the nanoscale, which is essential for creating functional materials, such
as nanoﬁlms.
However, the gravitationally induced strati ﬁcation during self-
assembly causes density-driven vertical segregation, typically accu-
mulating heavier molecular components at the bottom and lighter
ones at the top
6,7. This inherent density gradient critically limits the
synthesis of metastable nano ﬁlms requiring inverted architectures
where dense components must occupy upper layers to ensure struc-
tural integrity. Such forced positional inversion disrupts thermo-
dynamic equilibrium, leading to localized stress concentration and
ultimately destabilizes the interfacial assembly process for compro-
mising the synthesis stability of the material
8.

assembly (ACIS) for synthesizing self-supporting nano ﬁlms,

In this ACIS, the capillary force of the hydrophilic nylon and
hydrophobic polytetraﬂuoroethylene (PTFE) membranes can effec-
tively counteract the gravity effect to form a conﬁned interface, which
enables ﬁlm formation under the same conditions where previous
attempts failed (Fig.1a). It restricts the movement direction of CD and
brings them into the reduced dimensionality, enhanced proximity, and
increased diffusion to interface, thus increasing the host-guest inter-
action and hydrogen bonding between CDs and dodecane. This
interaction results in the formation of surfactant-like compounds that
possess the unique capability to congregate at the water-alkane
interface, thereby facilitating directional self-assembly to form the
stable and highly ordered CSNs. Multi-scale modeling, based on Fick’s
ﬁrst law to model the material diffusion process, combined with den-
sity functional theory calculation to analyze the interaction between
molecules, to reveal the driving force and in ﬂuencing factors of the
diffusion process from different scales (Supplementary Figs. 1–4).

Results
Design, synthesis, and characterization of the CSNs
The synthesis of nano ﬁlms at the liquid-liquid interface is widely
recognized as a straightforward, efﬁcient, and eco-friendly process for
producing compact, robust, and easily controllable ﬁlms6,8,19.B y
leveraging capillary forces, liquids can be con ﬁned within micro-
structured porous solid matrices to form the con ﬁned interfaces20,21.
Unlike current systems, the asymmetric hydrophilic –hydrophobic
porous membranes employed here are designed to overcome grav-
itational limitations, signiﬁcantly expanding their potential applica-
tions across diverse scienti ﬁc ﬁelds22. Through the strategic
arrangement of solid matrices with contrasting properties to encap-
sulate two immiscible liquids, a stable antigravity con ﬁned interface
can be established. This interface is particularly susceptible to elec-
trostatic interactions and van der Waals forces, providing an optimal
environment for the synthesis of CD nanoﬁlms.

pressure required for gas to penetrate interfacial ﬁlms formed

mechanical stability and resistance to gas permeation.
We also examined β-CD behavior in alkanes with varying chain

Compared with prior methods for synthesizing interfacialﬁlms, a
key advantage of the ACIS is its capacity to form large-area interfaces
with the same volume. The dimensions of the nano ﬁlms are deter-
mined by the membrane area over which the β-CD solution and
dodecane are distributed (Supplementary Fig. 13). Using 6 μLo f
dodecane and 26 μLo f β-CD solution infused into 25 mm-diameter
membranes, the ACIS produces an interface congruent with the
membrane area. In contrast, gravity interfacial self-assembly (GIS)
yields an interface of only ~28 mm², and uncon ﬁned interfacial self-
assembly (UIS) results merely in peripheral contact between the two
liquids, leading to minimal ﬁlm formation. As summarized in Fig. 2f,
the nanoﬁlm area achieved via ACIS is approximately 17 times larger
than that from GIS and 109 times greater than that from UIS, high-
lighting the ef ﬁciency of this approach for producing large-area
nanoﬁlms under equivalent volumetric conditions.

Based on our previous investigations into the design of aqueous and
alkane phases for the properties of the CSNs, we selected β-CD solu-
tion as the aqueous phase infused in a nylon membrane and dodecane
as the alkane phase within a PTFE membrane. Figure 3a illustrates the
molecular mechanism at the antigravity conﬁned interface, where β-
CD molecules eventually polymerize via hydrogen bonding. Initially,β-

host-guest interactions, forming a complex that reduces interfacial
tension and drives the interfacial assembly. Subsequently, these
complexes undergo interfacial polymerization through hydrogen
bonding withβ-CD molecules in the bulk phase, enhancing the stability
and mechanical strength of the ﬁlm.

Applications
We further demonstrate the versatility of this method across multiple
liquid-liquid systems, with potential applications in materials science,
emulsion processing, and the petroleum industry (Fig.4a, Right). The
morphology of the nanoﬁlms is determined by the shape of the solid
substrates used to con ﬁne the β-CD solution and dodecane. As illu-
strated in Fig. 4a, Left, employing nylon and PTFE membranes pat-
terned as circles, hearts, triangles, ﬁve-pointed stars, and leaves
enables the synthesis of correspondingly shaped CSNs via the ACIS.
Comparisons between ACIS and GIS are showed in Fig. 4a, Right.
As shown in gray, GIS in certain systems suffers from the formation of
interfacial ﬁlms with poor mechanical strength and limited area, and
also exhibits signiﬁcant limitations in practical applications. However,
ACIS in these systems can effectively solve some limitations of GIS, as
shown in pink. As shown in Fig. 4b and Supplementary Fig. 21a, the
temperature of dodecane under ACIS conﬁnement remains 3 °C higher
than that under GIS after 3 min, underscoring the potential of CSNs in
insulation materials. A ﬁnding further validated in food insulation
applications (Supplementary Fig. 22).
The ACIS also facilitates unique emulsion behaviors. Unlike GIS,
where alkane and CD solution rapidly separate into distinct layers, the
ACIS allows the dispersion of CD solution droplets within the alkane
phase (Fig. 4c). This emulsi ﬁcation process, stabilized by interfacial
nanoﬁlms, suggests promising applications in areas such as pesticide
emulsiﬁer formulation.
Aligned with global sustainable development goals, green petro-
leum extraction remains a critical endeavor. As a result, enhanced oil
recovery has become an essential and indispensable part of this
strategy
37. The tertiary oil recovery technology based on oil-water
interface behavior regulation has received widespread attention all

applications in green enhanced oil recovery (Supplementary Fig. 23
and Table 7). A key challenge in chemicalﬂooding for the Enhanced Oil
Recovery (EOR) is the preferentialﬂow of water through high-porosity

not only simpli ﬁes the displacement mechanism but also synergisti-

synergy between the methodological approach and the material

critical in controlling the ﬂow characteristics of oil-water mixtures:

example, we tested the in-situ CSNs synthesized by the two types of
petroleum, and the North three area oil exhibited a larger transmem-
brane pressure difference, indicating stronger ﬁlms and better dis-
placement performance.

Methods
Synthesis of the antigravity conﬁned interfacial self-assembly
The water, 0.004 mol/L of α-CD, 0.001 mol/L of β-CD, 0.002 mol/L of
β-CD, 0.003 mol/L ofβ-CD, 0.004 mol/L ofβ-CD, 0.005 mol/L ofβ-CD,
and 0.004 mol/L of γ-CD solutions were used as the functional liquids
infused to the nylon membrane used as the porous material in the
upper layer of the ACIS. And in the lower layer of the ACIS, the octane,
the decane, the dodecane, and the hexadecane were used as the
functional liquids infused to the PTFE membrane used as the porous
material. The ACIS was the overlap of the nylon membrane infused
with the CD solution and the PTFE membrane infused with the alkanes
to form the conﬁned antigravity interface.
Characterizations of membranes
Microstructures and energy spectrum diagrams of the nylon mem-
brane, the nylon membrane infused with the CD solution, the PTFE
membrane, and the PTFE membrane infused with the alkanes in the
ACIS were measured by using Field emission SEM (Zeiss
GeminiSEM 500).
Transmembrane critical pressure measurements
The CD solutions were in ﬁltrated into the nylon membrane, and the
alkanes were in ﬁltrated into the PTFE membrane. And then, the gas
was injected to pass through the overlapping the nylon membrane
with CD solutions and PTFE membrane with the alkanes. Transmem-
brane critical pressures for gas passing through the ACIS system were
measured by using the liquid gating intelligent tester and analyzer
(GIFT-E1 Pro) from Gating Inspired Future Technology Co., Ltd. (Xia-
men, China). In all experiments, the gasﬂow pressures were produced
by a syringe pump with a pressurization rate of 2 mL/min. Unless
otherwise speciﬁed, the pore sizes of the nylon membranes and the
PTFE membranes with diameter of 25 mm are 5 μm. All experiments
were repeated at least three times.
Stability of ﬁlm
The gas was injected to pass through overlapping the nylon membrane
inﬁltrated with β-CD solution and PTFE membrane inﬁltrated with the
dodecane. When gas passes through the system, theﬁlm is destroyed.
After the gas input stops, theﬁlm continues to synthesize. After several
times of gas injection and gas removal, the transmembrane pressure of
the ﬁlm is stable, indicating that if the liquid-liquid interface exists, the
ﬁlm will stably synthesize. Transmembrane critical pressures produced
by the compression of the gas to pass through the CD self-supporting
nanoﬁlms (CSNs) were measured by using the liquid gating intelligent
tester and analyzer (GIFT-E1 A). In all experiments, the pressures were
produced by the 2 mL/min of gas ﬂow by a syringe pump. The pore
sizes of the nylon membranes and the PTFE membranes with diameter
of 25 mm are 5 μm. All experiments were repeated at least three times.
Characterization ofﬁlm area
In this part, three methods are used to synthesize the ﬁlms. By the
ACIS, because the 26 μLo f β-CD solution will in ﬁltrate in the nylon
membrane, and 6μLo fd o d e c a n ew i l li nﬁltrate in the PTFE membrane,
the area of ﬁlm formed will be consistent with the area of the mem-
branes. By the gravity interfacial self-assembly (GIS), 6 μLo ft h e
dodecane ﬂoats on 26 μLo ft h e β-CD solution. By the uncon ﬁned
interfacial self-assembly (UIS), when the 26 μLo f β-CD solution tou-
ches the 6 μL of dodecane, it pushes the spread 6 μLo fd o d e c a n e
toward the edge, so that only the edge of the β-CD solution forms an
interface with the dodecane. The areas ofﬁlms are calculated using the
radius measured by the ruler.
Interfacial tension measurements
The water, 0.004 mol/L of α-CD, 0.001 mol/L of β-CD, 0.002 mol/L of
β-CD, 0.003 mol/L ofβ-CD, 0.004 mol/L ofβ-CD, 0.005 mol/L ofβ-CD,
and 0.004 mol/L of γ-CD solutions were prepared by the deionized
water. 10 μL of these solutions were injected into the alkanes to
directly measure via the pendant drop method on the OCA100 system
(Dataphysics, Germany) at room temperature. All experiments were
repeated at least three times.
The binding energy calculations
Quantum Chemical (QC) methods were used to investigate non-
covalent interactions, such as hydrogen bonds and van der Waals
forces, which are essential for the stability of CD complexes. To
investigate the host-guest interactions between CDs and alkane
molecules, we adopted a multi-level computational strategy. Given the
substantial size of CD complexes, the semiempirical quantum
mechanical methods GFN1-xTB
33 and GFN2-xTB34 were ﬁrst applied for
efﬁcient conformational sampling. Then, the single point energies and
binding energies based on the structure with the lowest energy were
further calculated under B3LYP/6-311 G(d) level using Gaussian 16
program, with Grimme’s D3BJ dispersion correction applied through-
out to enhance accuracy. Solvation effects were incorporated via the
Solvation Model based on Density (SMD) as an implicit solvent model.
Finally, the binding natures in the studied complexes were revealed
with IRI method
35 using the software Multiwfn36, and binding energies
were obtained based on the following equation:
Ebinding = Ecplx opt /C0ð ECD opt + Eguest opt Þ ð1Þ
All the visualization of molecular structures and contour surfaces
were performed by VMD46. For the conformer search, the temperature
is 298.15 K, and the energy window is 6 kcal mol⁻¹.

Different shapes material synthesis
The nylon and PTFE membranes are cut into different shapes, includ-

Insulation experiment
25 mL of the β-CD solution and the dodecane samples are heated to
85 °C and transferred to a beaker to observe the temperature change
of the samples. In the GIS system, where the dodecane is on top, the
dodecane dissipates heat faster. In the ACIS system, if the PTFE
membrane and nylon membrane areﬁxed, the upper layer of theβ-CD
solution will notﬂow into the lower layer, and the system will slow heat
dissipation. The sample withoutﬁlms cooled faster within 3 min.
Dispersion system preparation
There are two methods for dispersion system preparation. The GIS
uses the dense β-CD solution phase as the dispersing phase and the
dodecane as the dispersing substance. The ACIS uses the dense β-CD
solution phase as the dispersing substance and the dodecane as the
dispersing phase. The dispersing substance is placed into the syringe
and dripped through the corresponding nylon and PTFE membranes
with conﬁned space to the dispersing phase.
Oil displacement experiment
A uniform Berea core with a gas permeability of 30 × 10 −3 μm2 was
vacuumed for 4 h at room temperature and saturated with deionized
water to calculate pore volume and porosity. The core was then heated
for 12 h at a constant temperature of 25 °C. The original oil saturation
of crude oil was calculated by using the oil to ﬂush water. Next, the
deionized water orβ-CD solution was injected into the core. When the
cumulative injection volume reached 2 PV, the water orβ-CD solution
was stopped. In this work, it is worth noting that weﬁrst injected 0.5 PV
of the deionized water or β-CD solution, and then suspended for 2 h
before further injected remaining 1.5 PV of liquid. Dynamic pressure
and recovery were recorded during displacement. During the experi-
ment, the injection rate was constant at 0.35 mL/min. A similar core is
used when the injection liquid is changed. Each experiment was
repeated three times.
