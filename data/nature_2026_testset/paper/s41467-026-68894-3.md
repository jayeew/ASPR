and carbon emission reduction. However, low production capacity and the

FDCA yield (96.2%) at 100% single-pass conversion efﬁciency (SPCE). This stack
operates stably for over 100 hours with a space-time yield (STY) of 367.2 mg h−1
cm−2. A membrane separation device is employed to purify FDCA with an

Discussion
In this work, we have developed high-performance hundred-watt
scale (164.8 W) AEM-HMFOR stacks for continuous FDCA production
at a high STY
FDCA of 367.2 mg h–1 cm–2 (32.2 t year −1 m−2). The stack
operated stably at 80 A for over 100 h, enabling 100% SPCE of HMF at
an industrial concentration of 500 mM with a single-pass mode,
achieving both high FE
FDCA (94.6%) and yield FDCA (96.2%). Based on
this, a whole green chemical process is proposed for high-purity
FDCA production, including HMF puri ﬁcation, AEM-HMFOR, and
membrane separation modules. The FDCA purity increased from
99.2% to 99.8% through the membrane separation process, sig-
niﬁcantly improving the quality of polymerized PEF. The ﬁndings of
this work demonstrate the technical feasibility and economic viabi-
lity of this AEM-HMFOR technology, providing the basis for con-
tinuous production of high-purity FDCA from lab scale to industrial
scale. In the future, AEM-based systems for biomass upgrading in
practical applications require further exploration.

anode, Ni-Mo/NF as the cathode, and PAP-TP-85 as the membrane
(internal resistance obtained from the Nyquist curve). For multi-batch

the stability of the stacks was investigated by chronopotentiometry
(CP) using an electrochemical workstation (100 A current booster) at
selected currents. All electrolyzer operating conditions are summar-
