Pure water-fed membrane electrode assembly (MEA) electrolyzers represent a
signiﬁcant advance for practical deployment of electrochemical CO2 reduc-
tion, yet suffer from poor reaction kinetics and high ohmic resistance due to
the lack of alkali cations and low intrinsic ionic conductivity. Here, we address
interfacial mass transport limitations arising from inefﬁcient H2Oa n dO H-
transport in anion exchange membrane (AEM)-based pure water MEAs. A
permeable intimate membrane (PIM) electrode is developed by casting iono-
mer emulsion directly onto the catalyst layer (CL) to form the anion exchange
layer (AEL) in situ, which creates an intimately bonded CL/AEL interface and
allows the ionomer to permeate the CL, establishing continuous internal
channels for efﬁcient H
2Oa n dO H− transport. Consequently, the PIM-based
MEA achieves over 90% CO selectivity across a wide current density range
under pure water conditions, with a system energy efﬁciency 1.35 times higher
than conventional MEAs. Characterization reveals that the intimate catalyst-
ionomer interface reconstructs the hydrogen-bonded network of interfacial
water, accelerating the hydrogenation kinetics of the key *COO
− intermediate.
Electrocatalytic conversion of CO2 into chemicals and fuel is promising
for promoting efﬁcient utilization of green electricity and realizing a
closed-loop carbon cycle1–3. Recently, membrane electrode assembly
(MEA)-based electrochemical CO2 reduction (ECR) electrolyzers have
garnered signiﬁcant attention due to their speci ﬁcz e r o - g a pc o nﬁg-
uration, where catalyst layers are directly in contact with the ion-
exchange membrane. Compared to conventional three-chamberﬂow
cells, MEA systems substantially reduce ohmic resistance, enhance
reactant transport ef ﬁciency, and enable operation at signi ﬁcantly
higher current densities, laying a crucial foundation for the scale-up of
ECR technology
4–7. However, state-of-the-art high-performance MEA-
ECR systems typically rely heavily on the addition of electrolytes (e.g.,
KHCO
3, KOH) in alkaline or neutral aqueous solutions. While these
additives effectively boost ionic conductivity and optimize the local
reaction microenvironment 8–10, they introduce several persistent
challenges such as increased system complexity and maintenance
costs due to salt precipitation and complex product separation
11–13.
Consequently, developing MEA systems operating with a pure water-
fed electrolyte, devoid of any added supporting salts, is regarded as a
promising approach to overcome these bottlenecks and advance ECR
towards practical application
14,15. However, the lack of alkali cations in
such system results in suboptimal CO 2 activation, inhibiting ECR
reaction kinetics and reducing selectivity toward desired products.
Furthermore, the intrinsic low ionic concentration of pure water, with
a conductivity of merely 0.055μSc m
−1, is markedly lower than that of
standard electrolytes. Consequently, this leads to a rise in system
resistance and requires elevated voltages to maintain the reaction rate
and subsequently diminishes energy efﬁciency (EE) of the system
16,17.

In pure-water MEA system, AEMs are normally employed to sup-
press the competing hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) by inhibiting
the transfer of protons produced on the anode to the cathode side.
While H
2Oa n dC O 2 molecules diffuse from the anode electrolyte
chamber and the cathode gas chamber to the catalyst surface,
respectively18,19.I ti st h eO H - ions produced during the ECR process
been conveyed through the AEM to the anode side to complete the
charge transfer loop
20–22. Therefore, optimizing the transportation
behavior of H2Oa n dO H- between the cathode catalyst layer (CL) and
the AEM is critical for enhancing the ECR performance in pure water
MEA system. Traditional MEA fabrication processes either involve
laminating the ion-exchange membrane with gas diffusion electrodes
(GDEs) pre-coated with CL, or coating the CL directly onto the ion-
exchange membrane followed by lamination with gas diffusion layers
(GDLs)
23,24. Due to the smooth surface of the ion-exchange membrane
and the porous, rough nature of the CL, voids or poor interfacial
contact often occur between the CL and the membrane (Fig. 1). This
compromises mass transport (particularly ion conduction), posing a
signiﬁcant detriment especially for pure water-fed MEA systems.
To address the interfacial incompatibility and limited mass
transport in pure water-fed MEA, it is essential to construct
continuous and ef ﬁcient pathways for H
2Oa n dO H - transport
between the CL/AEM interfaces. Inspired by the progressive
densiﬁcation interface strategy in solid-state batteries
25–27,w e
designed a permeable intimate membrane (PIM) electrode by
casting ionomer emulsion directly onto the CL to form an AEL in
situ. This approach facilitate st h ef o r m a t i o no fa ni n t i m a t e
interface between the CL and the AEL (Fig. 1), which could bene ﬁt
efﬁcient mass transport across the interface. Simultaneously, the
ionomer emulsion could permeate into the CL, establishing
internal continuous channels for H
2Oa n dO H - transport. Conse-
quently, we accomplished over 90% CO selectivity under pure-
water conditions across a broad current density range, with EE of
the system improved by 1.35 times compared to conventional
MEAs. In a large-scale electrolyzer operating at a current of 3.2 A,
the PIM-based MEA demonstrated continuous and stable opera-
tion over 200 h, validating its robustness and scalability for
practical deployment. Further characterizations revealed the
intimate catalyst-ionomer interfa ce also reconstructs the hydro-
gen bonded network of interfacial water, thereby facilitating CO
2
activation under pure water conditions and accelerating the
hydrogenation kinetics of the critical *COO
− intermediate.

Characterization of PIM electrode
The Ag nanoparticles was selected as a model catalyst for the elec-
trochemical reduction of CO 2 (Fig. s1a–f). In the PIM and CCS elec-
trodes, the Ag-based CL was constructed by directly spraying ink onto
the hydrophobic GDL (Fig. s2). In contrast to the method of directly
pressing AEM onto the GDE in CCS electrode, the AEL of the PIM
electrode is fabricated by casting ionomer resin solution onto the Ag-
based GDE. The quaternary ammonia poly(N-methyl-piperidine-co-p-
terphenyl) (QAPPT), Poly(aryl piperidinium) (PiperIon A40), and Sus-
tainion (imidazolium-functionalized polystyrene ionomer) ionomers
are employed to fabricate PIM electrodes. These electrodes are refer-
red to as PIM-Q, PIM-P, and PIM-S, respectively.
The integrated ionomer and CL electrode of PIM electrode
ensures intimate interfacial contact without physical interval. Scanning
electron microscope (SEM) images, shown in Fig.2a, b and Fig. s3a, b,
reveal a smooth and ﬂat ionomer membrane with QAPPT ionomer
onto the PIM-Q electrode, with no apparent crevices. The surface of
the ionomer membrane was further examined using an atomic force
microscope (AFM), which conﬁrmed a root-mean-square roughness of
23.6 nm (Fig. 2ca n dF i g .s 4 ) .
Cross-sectional SEM images in Fig. 2d, e reveals a well-de ﬁned
sandwich electrode in the PIM-Q electrode, comprising a GDL, a CL
with controlled thickness of 5.2 to 6.5 μm, and an AEL with average
thickness of 25.6μm. This is markedly different from the conventional
CCS electrode, which exhibits a discernible physical gap between the
CL and membrane. The intimate integration between the CL and AEM
is further con ﬁrmed by cross-sectional SEM interfacial mapping
(Fig. 2f–i and Fig. s5), which shows a continuous interface without
delamination or voids. The PIM-P and PIM-S electrode exhibit the
smooth surface and dense cross-sectional structure similar to that
observed with the PIM-Q electrode (Fig. s6a–g and Fig. s7a–g).

To evaluate the impact of the electrode fabrication method,

more uniform coverage and improved ECR performance.

In addition to selectivity, there is a notable difference in the cell
voltage between PIM-Q and CCS electrodes (Fig. 3c). The PIM-Q elec-

50 mA cm

current density of 400 mA cm−2, the cell voltage remains at 4.28 V. As
the temperature rises to 60 °C, the cell voltage would decrease further.
With the increase of current density, the different of cell voltage for
PIM-Q and CCS electrode increases. The lower potential and high
selectivity brings in the enhancement of EE. Upon assessment over a
broad current density range of 50 to 400 mA cm
−2, it was observed that
the PIM-Q electrode exhibited an enhancement in EE by more than 1.35
times relative to the CCS electrode (Fig. 3d), and the EE of PIM-Q
electrode reached 43.0% ± 1.6% at a temperature of 25 °C and a current
density of 50 mA cm
−2. Unfortunately, although further increasing the
temperature reduces the overall cell voltage, the elevated temperature
also accelerates the competing HER (Fig. s13b). As a result, at 60 °C, the
PIM-Q electrode achieves a maximum EE of only ~32%, which is 1.38
times higher than that of the CCS electrode under the same conditions
(Fig. s13c).
The electrochemical active surface area is estimated by cyclic
voltammetry (CV) measurement at different scan rate from 20 to
200 mV s
−1 in non-Faradaic potential region (Fig. s21a, c). The PIM-Q
electrode displays an enhanced double-layer capacitance (C dl)o f
55.0 μFc m−2, in comparison to the 30.2 μFc m−2 of the CCS electrode
(Fig. s21b, d). This suggests that the PIM-Q electrode has 1.82 times
more reaction active sites than the CCS electrode. Alternatively, the
integrated catalyst-ionomer electrode of PIM-Q electrode can expand
the electrochemical reaction area into the inner surface of the CL,
rather than being con ﬁned to the interface between CL and the
membrane. The expanded electrochemical reaction area can be
attributed to the constructed internal continuous H
2Oa n dO H- trans-
portation channel in PIM-Q electrode, which impels the internal active
site to participate in ECR reaction. The technique of in-situ electro-
chemical differential mass spectrometry (DEMS) is employed to con-
ﬁrm the ECR onset potential of both PIM-Q and CCS electrodes,
utilizing a step voltage from 0 V to −0.8 V (Fig. s22a, b). The PIM-Q
electrode identiﬁes the CO signal (m/z = 28) at −0.1 V, while the CCS
electrode identiﬁes it at −0.4 V (Fig. s22c). The DEMS results of PIM-Q
electrode with lower onset voltage indicates favorable thermo-
dynamics, which further verify the faster mass transfer rate. In con-
clusion, the optimized mass transfer of the PIM-Q electrode exhibits
reduced cell voltage and favorable selectivity for ECR within MEA,
leading to enhanced energy utilization efﬁciency.

Discussion
To conclude, this work addressed the critical interfacial mass transport
limitations in pure water-fed AEM electrolyzers for ECR by developing
a PIM-Q electrode, fabricated through in situ casting of QAPPT iono-
mer emulsion onto the Ag CL. This strategy created an intimately
bonded CL/AEL interface and established internal continuous channels
for efﬁcient H
2Oa n dO H- transport, overcoming the voids and poor
contact inherent in conventional CCS electrodes. The PIM-Q electrode
demonstrated favorable performance in pure water, achieving >90%
CO selectivity across a broad current density range (50–400 mA cm−2),
a 1.35-fold improvement in EE, and stable operation for over 200 h in a
large-scale electrolyzer. Comprehensive characterization, including
DRT analysis and in situ ATR-SEIRAS, revealed that the intimate
interface fundamentally reconstructs the hydrogen-bonded network
of interfacial water, signiﬁcantly increasing the proportion of sh-H
2O.
This optimized environment facilitates CO2 diffusion, enhances CO2
adsorption, and critically promotes the critical *COO − intermediate
protonation step (*COO− to *COOH), thereby accelerating ECR kinetics
and suppressing the competing HER. The PIM design, validated by TEA
showing a substantial reduction in CO production cost (down to ~463
USD/tonne), provides a transformative interface engineering strategy
for high-performance, electrolyte-free CO
2 electrolysis towards prac-
tical deployment. While the PIM strategy is effective in regulating
interfacial water structure and enhancing selectivity toward C
1 pro-
ducts, its direct extension to C 2+ formation on Cu-based catalysts is
limited. Due to the C2+ pathways involve more complex multi-electron/
proton transfer processes and high energy barrier of C-C coupling
steps, which require more reﬁned control over catalyst structure and
interfacial microenvironment.

Techno-economic analysis
The TEA conducted in this study is predicated on the daily production
of CO (QCO of 100 tonnes day−1), with a capacity factor of 0.9 (32,850
tonnes annually). This analysis assumes a facility with a lifespan (tlife of
20 years). The cell voltage (Vcell, 2.93 V for PIM-Q electrode, and 3.71 for
CCS electrode) and current density (j of 50 mA cm−2) from our stability
test are used as the calculation parameter.
