The ‘Strangelove ocean’(a state of ocean, almost devoid of living organisms) documented in the early
Cambrian strata in South China, has been thought to be driven by an extraterrestrial impact, but this
viewpoint has been doubted for emerging geochemical evidence. Volcanism may be a trigger for the
oceanic crisis. However, such a linkage has not yet been established. Here, we conduct an
investigation into the timing and origin of the early Cambrian K-bentonites (a type of clay rock formed
from volcanic ash) of the Yangtze and Baoshan blocks. The results suggest that the K-bentonites may
represent early Cambrian explosive super-eruptions occurred in the north-western margin of
Gondwana. The eruptions might trigger the anoxia of the contemporaneous ocean and coincided
temporally with a bio-crisis preceding the summit of the Cambrian explosion. We propose that they
may have initiated the ‘Strangelove ocean’and delayed the climax of the Cambrian bio-radiation.

Large-scale volcanic eruptions in geological history have been con-
sidered as one of the initial causes of environmental and evolutionary
events17,18. For example, the global environmental changes and the mass
extinctions during the Ordovician–Silurian transition and the end-Permian
have been argued to be caused by volcanic activity18–20. In the past decades,
widely distributed K-bentonite beds representing volcanic eruptions have
been identiﬁed in the basal Shiyantou Formation and its equivalents (the
basal Niutitang and Shuijingtuo formations) recording the ‘Strangelove
ocean’ and between the two episodes of the Cambrian explosion in the
Yangtze Block (Figs.3 and 4)16,21–25. Recently, we discovered an isochronous
K-bentonite in the upper Gongyanghe Group in another block; the Baoshan
Block (Fig. 4F). Systematic study on the timing and origin for these
K-bentonites may cast light on the link between the‘Strangelove ocean’ and
the contemporaneous volcanisms. However, such a work is lacking, and
therefore, the hypothesized relationship remains unclear.
Here, we sampled the K-bentonites from the Yangtze and Baoshan
blocks, and then performed zircon separation, clay mineral composition
analysis, as well as major- and trace-element content analysis for the sam-
ples. Meanwhile, U-Pb ages, trace-element contents and Hf isotope com-
positions of zircons in the samples were analyzed. The aim is to unravel the
origin of the K-bentonites and, based on the detailed genetic evidence,
provide insights into the potential link between the volcanic eruptions
represented by the K-bentonites and the‘Strangelove ocean’.
Geological settings
During the early Cambrian, the Yangtze Block of South China was adjacent
to the north-western margin of Gondwana26,27. In this period, the Yangtze
Block evolved from a rift basin to a passive continental margin 23.T h e
Yangtze Block can be divided into the platform interior, slope, and basin
from northwest to southeast, extending along a NE-SW strike (Fig. 2B)23.
Upwardly, the early Cambrian successions in the shallow-water platform
interior include the Zhujiaqing Formation (or the Yanjiahe Formation) and
a unit comprising the Shiyantou and Yu ’anshan formations, which is
equivalent to the Shuijingtuo or Niutitang formations (Figs. 2Ba n d 3).
Those in the deep-water slope and basin of the block include the topmost
Liuchapo Formation and the Niutitang Formation
16,28. These successions
consist mainly of chert, phosphorite, dolostone, limestone, black shale,
siltstone, and silty shale (Figs. 2Ba n d3)8,23,29. K-bentonites have been dis-
covered at the base of the Shiyantou (Fig.4A, B)21, Niutitang (Fig.4C)23 and
Shuijingtuo (Fig.4D)16 formations in the platform interior, as well as at the
bottom of the Niutitang Formation (Fig.4E) of the slope.
The Baoshan Block was near the north-western margin of
Gondwana during the early Cambrian 30,31. The Gongyanghe Group
(Precambrian to early Cambrian in age), the oldest unit of this block, was
intruded by early Paleozoic and Cenozoic granites and outcrops on the
western part of the block (Fig. 2C)
31. The lower part of the group consists
mainly of metasandstone and slate, while the upper part comprises slate,
sandstone, chert, and black shale (Fig.2C). We discovered a greenish gray
K-bentonite sandwiched in the cherts of the upper Gongyanghe
Group (Fig. 4F).

Mineralogy
The K-bentonites consist mainly of illite and/or mixed-layer illite-smectite
(Supplementary Fig. 3). Speciﬁcally, the contents of illite in the K-bentonites
from the Meishucun, Songlin, Taoying, and Mucheng sections are 1%, 70%,
82%, and 74%, respectively, whereas those of mixed-layer illite-smectite in
the K-bentonites of the corresponding sections are 99%, 30%, 18%, and 4%,
respectively. The illite in the mixed-layer illite-smectite of the K-bentonites
accounts for≥80%. Zircons from the K-bentonites are 40–240 μml o n ga n d
30–80 μm wide, with length-to-width ratios of 1 –4. Most crystals are
euhedral to sub-euhedral and prismatic. Some grains exhibit oscillatory
zoning while some show homogeneous internal structure, as revealed by
cathodoluminescence (CL) imaging (Supplementary Fig. 4).

Discussion
In the outcrop, our K-bentonites have sharp contacts with their immediately
underlying and overlying horizons (Fig.4). Geochemically, the K-bentonites
contain 4.99–8.65 wt% K 2O (Supplementary Table 1) and exhibit REE
patterns with negative Eu anomalies but without signiﬁcant Ce anomalies
(Supplementary Table 2). Mineralogically, they consist predominantly of
mixed-layer illite-smectite and illite (Supplementary Fig. 3). Most of the
zircons from the K-bentonites are e uhedral and prismatic. Some grains
show obvious oscillatoryzoning, while some present homogeneous internal
structure (Supplementary Fig. 4). These zircons typically have REE patterns
characterized by depleted LREE, enriched HREE, positive Ce anomalies,
and negative Eu anomalies (Supplementary Fig. 5), with Th/U ratios >0.1
(Supplementary Table 5). Therefore, the ﬁeld, geochemical, and miner-
alogical characteristics are consistent with those of typical
K-bentonites

magmas of the K-bentonites from both the Yangtze and Baoshan blocks are

The K-bentonites may represent early Cambrian (around 520 Ma)
explosive super-eruptions in the Iran Block, which is based on the following
reasons. Firstly, these K-bentonites are records of isochronous eruptions
and have a shared origin, which provides two prerequisites for that they
represent intensive eruptions f rom the same region. Secondly, the
subduction-related settings of the source volcanoes of the K-bentonites and
their felsic source magmas agree withthe magmato-tectonic conditions for
intensely explosive eruptions. For instance, the Toba (approximately 74,000
years ago, in Sumatra, Indonesia), Tambora (in 1815, in Sumbawa, Indo-
nesia), and Tonga (in 2022, in the TongaKermadec arc) eruptions generated
ashes that covered˃2.0 × 10

These large-scale explosive eruptions typically occurred in subduction-
related settings and commonly erupted felsic magmas. Finally, source ashes
of the K-bentonites may have a long-distance subaerial transportation and
could have covered a large area. We have proposed that the source volcanoes

Simulating the timing of magmatic activities related to the sub-
duction of the proto-Tethys Ocean
A large number of magmatic rocks rel ated to proto-Tethyan subduction
have been found in Turkey, Iran, Lhasa, South Qiangtang, Himalaya, and
Sibumasu blocks along the north-western margin of Gondwana. We
compiled the ages of the rocks (Supplementary Table 7). Then, we used
Monte Carlo model to simulate the timing of magmatic activities in the
blocks based on this dataset. During Monte Carlo simulations, each date
generated 10,000 possible ages, constrained by the published age and error
values. We grouped the outputs of this exercise into 0.25 Myr bins and
produced probability density estimates for each bin. For each of the above
six blocks, we averaged across each bin to obtain a probability density of each
0.25 Myr, and then yielded a probability density curve characterizing the
timing of the magmatic activities in each block. All simulations were per-
formed using Python (version 3.7.2).
