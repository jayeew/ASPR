Peritonitis is an in ﬂammation of the peritoneum caused by bacterial
leakage from an intestinal perforation, leading to a severe acute
inﬂammatory response. If peritonitis is not well controlled, it can
progress to sepsis, leading to what remains a high mortality rate of
19.7% as of 2017
1–3. Despite advances in medical care, there are no
deﬁnitive treatments to prevent the progression from peritonitis to
sepsis. Thus, new perspectives are needed to understand early-stage
inﬂammation in peritonitis and to develop effective immune-based
therapies.
Recent studies have shown that adipose tissue, classically
recognized as an energy storage organ, contains numerous immune
cells; thus, it is considered an important immune organ

immune regulation during acute peritonitis. However, the mechan-
isms by which MAT regulates immune responses during the acute
inﬂammatory phase of peritonitis are poorly understood and require
further study.

Results
Involvement of MAT-ILC1s in CLP-induced acute peritonitis
To clarify the role of ILC in MAT during acute peritonitis, we ﬁrst
analyzed mice subjected to CLP, a commonly used in vivo model of
sepsis11. After CLP surgery, the mice had a decreased rectal temperature
at 6 hours (CLP 6 h) compared to sham-operated control mice (sham
control), which normalized by 24 hours (CLP 24 h) (Fig.1A, B, Supple-
mentary Fig. 1A). Consistent with this, disease severity scores indicated
worsened pathology at CLP 6 h (Supplementary Fig. 1B). Meanwhile,
these mice exhibited a low mortality rate ( < 20%) even 7 days after CLP
(Fig. 1C), indicating that our CLP mice could be used as a recovery
model with the strongest inﬂammatory condition occurring at around
6 h post-CLP among the examined time points. Consistent with these
physiologicalﬁndings, the expression of inﬂammatory genes Il1b and
Il6 was upregulated in the MAT at CLP 6 h (Fig. 1D). Furthermore, ele-
vated IL-6 protein levels were detected in both the MAT and plasma at
CLP 6 h, corresponding to the peak inﬂammatory time point (Fig.1E, F,
and Supplementary Fig. 1C). Additional inﬂammatory mediators, GM-
CSF and S100A8, which are known to be involved in septic
conditions
12,13, were also upregulated in the MAT (Fig. 1E). In addition,
similar trends were observed in the production of in ﬂammatory che-
mokines, including CCL2, CCL4,CCL5, CCL11, CXCL1, and CXCL10, in
the MAT at CLP 6 h (Supplementary Fig. 1D), indicating that the MAT
could attract numerous immune cell types during acute peritonitis.
We next assessed whether ILCs are involved in the acute perito-
neal inﬂammation induced at CLP 6 h in the MAT. Each ILC subset was
ﬁrst characterized in the innate population according to the expres-
sion of NK1.1, ST2 (IL-33R), EOMES, GATA3, and ROR γt, respectively
(Supplementary Fig. 1E)
14. NK cells, which share characteristics with
ILC1s, were clearly distinguished based on the level of EOMES
expression
15 (Supplementary Fig. 1E). Notably, the number of ILC1s
signiﬁcantly increased at CLP 6 h, concomitant with the peak of
inﬂammation. In contrast, NK cells and ILC2s were unchanged com-
pared to sham controls, and ILC3s were virtually absent at all time
points examined (Fig. 1G). In contrast, granzyme B-expressing cyto-
toxic NK cells were signi ﬁcantly increased at CLP 24 h, suggesting a
role for NK cells in the resolution phase of CLP-induced MAT in ﬂam-
mation (Supplementary Fig. 1F). No signiﬁcant changes in the numbers
of NK cells and any ILC subset were detected in sham-operated mice at
6 and 24 hours (Supplementary Fig. 1G).
These ﬁndings suggest that ILC1s in the MAT are associated with
acute inﬂammation initiated by bacterial leakage after CLP and might
be involved in regulating pathogenesis in acute peritonitis.

Upregulation of PD-L1 on MAT-CD127+ ILC1s at CLP 6 h
Since MAT-CD127+ ILC1s in mice at CLP 6 h exhibited less IFN- γ pro-
duction during inﬂammation (Fig.2F–I), we next attempted to identify
the key factors, other than IFN-γ, regulating inﬂammation in the MAT
within cluster 0, identiﬁed as MAT-CD127+ ILC1s (Fig.2D, E). According
to the differential gene expression analysis,Cd274, the gene encoding
PD-L1, was elevated in cluster 0 compared with clusters 1–3, together
with the upregulation of the Pdcd1lg2 genes (Fig. 5A). Indeed, the
intensity of PD-L1 expression was highly upregulated in MAT-CD127 +
ILC1s at CLP 6 h compared to CD127− ILC1, whereas the level of PD-L1
on NK cells was considerably lower (Fig.5B, C). In accordance with this,
the frequency and absolute number of PD-L1-expressing CD127+ ILC1
were also greatly increased in CLP 6 h (Fig. 5D–F). These results sug-
gest that CD127+ ILC1s could contribute to immune regulation during
peritonitis via PD-L1 expression.

Discussion
In this study, we delineated a spleen-MAT axis as part of ILC1 migration,
in which CXCR3-expressing Ly6C+CD127− ILC1s could migrate to the
MAT, where CXCL10 is abundantly produced. Upon recruitment to the
MAT, these unique ILC1s undergo phenotypic changes, characterized
by low IFN- γ-production along with up-regulation of CD127. Acute
inﬂammation altered the local environment by reducing IL-7 produc-
tion in the MAT, which facilitated CD127 upregulation on CD127
− ILC1s.
Furthermore, these ILC1s expressed PD-L1 and negatively regulate TNF
production by γδ T cells. Collectively, these mechanisms might be
required to restrain excessive in ﬂammation during acute peritonitis
and could potentially help prevent progression toward sepsis.
Although direct cell-cell interactions were not demonstrated in
this study, our ﬁndings suggest that γδ Tc e l l sa r et h et a r g e t so fM A T -
ILC1s via the PD-1/PD-L1 axis. However, the current study does not
necessarily provide direct evidence that PD-L1 ⁺ ILC1s primarily reg-
ulate γδ Tc e l l s .I n d e e d ,γδ T cells are unlikely to be the only source of
TNF during peritonitis. Other populations, such as Th1 cells and
additional T-cell subsets, are also present in the MAT and may con-
tribute to the in ﬂammatory response. Further investigation is war-
ranted to determine whether ILC1s also regulate these T cell
populations and to clarify wheth er such regulation is mediated
through co-stimulatory molecules or cytokine-mediated pathways. We
also showed that the capacity of TNF production by γδ Tc e l l si s
restricted following CLP-induced in ﬂammation, showing a trend
opposite to that observed in the majority of T cells. Although this may
partly explain why overall TNF production in the MAT does not
increase after CLP (Fig. 1E), the pathophysiological contribution of
ILC1-mediated modulation of TNF⁺ γδ T cells should be interpreted
cautiously. This is because γδ T cells represent only a minor TNF-
producing subset, andγδ T cell depletion in the ILC1-deﬁcient setting
was technically challenging. These limitations complicate the inter-
pretation of our data and remain to be addressed. In future studies,
in vivo cell–cell interaction mapping approaches, such as those using
LIPSTIC/uLIPSTIC mice
31, together with γδ T cell-speciﬁc TNF ablation
models, should help clarify direct ILC1–γδ T cell interactions and the
pathophysiological impact of TNF ⁺ γδ T cells during CLP-induced
inﬂammation.
Interestingly, CD127⁺ ILC1s induced at CLP 6 h persisted at lower
levels even during the recovery phase, at CLP 24 h, suggesting that
some cells remained in the tissue after the inﬂammation condition and
maintained their phenotypic change. Supporting this idea, our CITE-
seq data revealed that CD127 ⁺ ILC1s express Rora (Supplementary
Fig. 2B), a transcription factor known to be expressed by long-lived
ILC1s and critical for their survival
32. These long-lived ILC1s are also
characterized by CD127 expression and are known to circulate via
CXCR3 and CXCR6
33, suggesting that the MAT-CD127⁺ ILC1s identiﬁed
in this study may represent a similar long-lived subset. Notably, pre-
viously described long-lived ILC1s show limited direct evidence for
enhanced effector functions, such as IFN- γ or granzyme production,
upon secondary challenge after sensitization, and their precise func-
tional roles remain unclear
32. Importantly, in our study, the MAT-
CD127⁺ ILC1s expressed PD-L1 during peritonitis, indicating that they
may contribute to immune modulation rather than to classical
inﬂammatory responses.
Taken together, our ﬁndings suggest the intriguing possibility
that a ‘regulatory-like ILC1s′ exists within the MAT. These cells exhibit
RORα-dependent long-term survival and, unlike conventional ILC1s
that promote in ﬂammation, may contribute to the maintenance of
tissue homeostasis. These observations highlight the need for fur-
ther mechanistic studies to clarify the precise roles of such
regulatory-like ILC1s. Since secondary infections during the immu-
nosuppressive phase are a major cause of mortality in septic patients,
it is also worth investigating whether these ILC1s can enhance their
functional capacity through trained immunity in response to sec-
ondary challenges.

evaluated as follows: 0 = eyes fully open; 1 = slightly closed; 2 = half

brisk aversive response; 1 = responds to stimuli but reduced activity; 2
= delayed response, unable to walk more than two steps; 3 = shows

hunched posture or slightly reduced responsiveness; 2 = clearly hun-

pendent of the CLP procedures.
