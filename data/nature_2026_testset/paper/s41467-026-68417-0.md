Synapsis, the physical bridging of two broken DNA ends, is a critical step in
non-homologous DNA end joining (NHEJ), the primary pathway for repairing
DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) in vertebrates. While NHEJ synapsis has been
well characterized using blunt-ended DNA, how diverse DNA end conﬁgura-
tions inﬂuence synaptic complex formation remains much less clear. Here,
using single-molecule FRET, we show that end conﬁgurations play a decisive
role in synapsis pathway choice, with end pairing compensating for XLF in
facilitating synaptic complex transitions. Notably, 3-nucleotide (nt) micro-
homology at DNA ends signiﬁcantly enhances close synaptic (CS) complex
formation mediated by Ku70-Ku80 (Ku) and XRCC4-Ligase IV (X4L4), even in
the absence of XLF. Although XLF is nonessential for synapsis with 3-nt
microhomology, it further promotes CS formation when present. The FRET
analysis reveals that the single-stranded and double-stranded junctions of the
DNA ends are in close contact within the primary CS complex, while the
overhangs ﬂip out of the duplex, potentially stabilizing the complex. These
ﬁndings underscore the pivotal role of DNA end conﬁgurations in regulating
synapsis and their broader implications for NHEJ repair efﬁciency and ﬁdelity.

cells. DSB can be caused by both endogenous factors, such as active
stresses from common metabolic processes, and exogenous reasons,
such as genotoxic reagents1. If not repaired promptly, DSBs can result
in cell cycle arrest, apoptosis, or senescence. Two major repair path-
ways— homologous recombination (HR) and non-homologous end
joining (NHEJ)— are applied in cells to cope with DSBs

has roles in the S and G2 phases of the cell cycle. NHEJ can directly
rejoin the broken DNA ends with no to little homology and can
function throughout the entire cell cycle

sequences from homologous templates, it can largely restore the ori-

and often leaves an ‘information scar’ after repair2,3. However, many

and NHEJ, therefore, is the predominant pathway for repairing DSBs
within cells

Here, using single-molecule FRET (smFRET), we directly evaluate
the effects of DNA end conﬁgurations on NHEJ synapsis by core NHEJ
proteins – Ku, X4L4, and XLF, and investigate the structures of
synaptic complexes and the dynamics of synapsis determined by the
end conﬁgurations. We ﬁnd that DNA end structures critically in ﬂu-
ence both the efﬁciency of NHEJ synapsis and the architecture of the
synaptic complexes formed. The presence of 3-nucleotide (nt)
microhomology at DNA termini promotes ef ﬁcient CS complex for-
mation by Ku and X4L4 even in the absence of XLF. While XLF is not
essential for close synapsis of ends with 3-nt microhomology, it
enhances the efﬁciency of CS complex. FRET analyses of the synaptic

NHEJ synapsis, highlighting their importance across all stages of NHEJ
repair.

DNA end conﬁgurations inﬂuence NHEJ synapsis
Using blunt-ended DNA, we found that Ku70-Ku80 (Ku) together with
XRCC4-DNA ligase IV (X4L4) efﬁciently mediate NHEJ synapsis, form-
ing a ﬂexible synaptic (FS) complex, which is characterized as having a
FRET peak around 0.1; and some of the formed synaptic complexes
exhibit FRET ﬂuctuations, indicating the mobility of the two dsDNA
molecules within the FS complex (Supplementary Fig. 1a – e). XLF
promotes the formation of a close synaptic (CS) complex, which is
characterized as exhibiting a FRET peak around 0.8– similar to that of
the pre-ligated substrates– indicating end-to-end contact of the DNA
molecules (Supplementary Fig. 1c, f, g). Dwell-time analysis of the
formed synaptic complexes showed lifetimes of 9.9– 16.5 s for the FS

To directly test the effect of overhangs on NHEJ synapsis, we
prepared duplexes with 3’four-nucleotide complementary overhangs

Discussion
Bringing two broken dsDNA ends together is theﬁrst and most critical
step for the repair of most double-strand DNA breaks and is essential
for their eventual ligation. Core NHEJ proteins — Ku, X4L4, and XLF —
have previously been shown to mediate synapsis of blunt ends,
forming two distinct types of synaptic complexes — ﬂexible synaptic
(FS) and close synaptic (CS) complexes
9,20. Ku and X4L4 are essential
for the initial synapsis of blunt ends, but they only support the for-
mation of FS complexes, which are ligation-incompetent. XLF must be
present for mediating the transition of the FS complex to the ligation-
competent CS complex. However, our previous work did not establish
whether, or how, the end sequences in ﬂuence synapsis and thereby
affect the ligation outcome. Although blunt ends are useful for iden-
tifying the essential components required for NHEJ synapsis
9– 11,t h e y
do not reﬂect the diversity of broken DNA ends encountered in vivo–
especially those generated by physical or oxidative damage 3,19,20.
Ensemble ligation studies have shown that DNA end conﬁguration can
dictate the speci ﬁc requirements of NHEJ components for end
joining2,21. Here, we demonstrate that DNA end conﬁguration also has
critical roles in determining NHEJ synapsis (Fig.6), contributing to the

Single-molecule imaging
The smFRET strategy used for the NHEJ synapsis investigation is similar
as previously described9,32.B r i eﬂy, two duplexes (74– 85 bp) with var-
ied end con ﬁgurations were prepared with Cy3 or Cy5-labeled at a
distance of 4 bp from the double-stranded (ds) terminus and close to
the single-stranded (ss) overhang. The Cy5-labeled dsDNA is immobi-
lized on the slide, the Cy3-labeled dsDNA is free in solution and has a
loop at the opposite side to the Cy3 dye. The simultaneous appearance
of Cy3 and Cy5 signals indicates the occurrence of synapsis, and the
changes in FRET (E
FRET) signals re ﬂect the dynamic interactions
between two dsDNA ends.
The 5’OH/P-dsDNA was immobilized on the surface of the cover-
slips by a biotin-streptavidin-biotin strategy at a concentration of
300 pM. 10 nM loop-containing and Cy3-labeled 5 ’OH/P-dsDNA was
used in the solution. Unless otherwise speciﬁed, the reaction buffer in
this study contained 20 mM Tris-acetate pH 7.5, 75 mM KCl, 10 mM
MgCl2, 3% glycerol, 1 mg/ml BSA, 1 mM DTT, 0.5 mM ATP, 0.25% Tween
20, 2 mM Trolox (Millipore Sigma, 238813) and an oxygen-scavenging
system, which contains 0.8 % (w/v) D-glucose, 1 mg/ml glucose oxidase
(Millipore Sigma, G2133-50KU), and 0.04 μg/ml catalase (Millipore
Sigma, C3556). Theﬁnal concentrations of the proteins were 50 nM Ku,
100 nM X4L4, and 100 nM XLF. Proteins and looped dsDNA were
added to the reaction buffer, mixed, and immediately injected into the
imaging chamber. Images were captured immediately after sample
injection. All reactions were carried out at room temperature (22 °C).
The exposure time of the CCD camera was set to 100 ms and each
movie lasted approximately 2 min, with at least three movies taken for
each sample. Each sample was repeated at least three times in this
study. In all, 532 nm laser was used constantly to excite FRET pairs
during each ROI imaging session. In all, 532 nm laser cannot directly
excite the Cy5 dye, the Cy5 dye only brightens when it is exposed to
energy transferred from the excited Cy3 dye. The appearance of both
Cy3 and Cy5 signals or the FRET signal indicates the occurrence of
synapsis. The emitted ﬂuorescence signals of Cy3 and Cy5 were split
into two channels using a splitter (FF640-FDi01-25×36, Semrock). The
Cy3 and Cy5 imaging channels were registered using the BZ45/
ZhaoBZ49 dsDNA probe, which contains Cy3 and distance-rational
Cy5 dyes.

FS and CS complexes were distinguished based on FRET levels
observed in synapsis events. Classiﬁcation was guided by the full width
at half maximum (FWHM) of FRET distributions obtained from pre-
ligated DNA substrates. Speci ﬁcally, the pre-ligated blunt-end sub-
strate— with an 8-nt Cy3 – Cy5 separation— was used as a reference to
identify junction-contacting CS complexes. Because 2-nt micro-
homology is insufﬁcient for stable CS formation by Ku and X4L4, the
highest FRET cutoff (0.66) derived from pre-ligated 3 ’ GGG/CCC
overhangs (peak FRET = 0.60; FWHM = 0.11; Supplementary Fig. 4a)
was also adopted as a threshold for de ﬁning junction-contacting CS.
Thus, synapsis events with FRET ≥ 0.66 were classi ﬁed as junction-
contacting CS complexes. This cutoff is consistent with the lowest
cutoff (0.69) observed for pre-ligated blunt ends (Supplementary
Fig. 1g or Fig. 2c), which also de ﬁnes junction-contacting CS com-
plexes. The gap-containing CS complex formed by 3 ’ GGGG/CCCC
overhangs was expected to yield the lowest CS FRET due to the 13-nt
Cy3– Cy5 separation. This conﬁguration was modeled using pre-ligated
3’ GGGGG/CCCCC overhangs (Supplementary Fig. 2h), which gave a
cutoff of 0.48 at FWHM. Accordingly, synapsis events with FRET > 0.48
were categorized as CS (including gap-containing, nick-containing,
and junction-contacting CS), whereas events with FRET ≤ 0.48 were
classiﬁed as FS. This 0.48 threshold is further supported by FRET his-
tograms of 3’G/C and 3’GG/CC overhangs (Fig.3f, g), which form only
FS complexes with Ku and X4L4 and predominantly exhibit FRET
below 0.48. Following classiﬁcation, the formation efﬁciency and dwell
times of FS and CS complexes were calculated for each DNA
substrate set.
Synapsis time analysis
The dwell-time analysis was based on the time window from the
appearance to the disappearance of each FRET pair of molecules. All
the dwell-time histograms and the corresponding exponential ﬁts
corresponded only to synapsis events that begin and end within the
detection time window. Distributions were ﬁtted with single-
exponential decay functions.
Quantiﬁcation and statistical methods
The Gaussian ﬁtting of the E FRET distribution and the exponential ﬁt-
ting of the dwell time distribution were performed using OriginLab
Origin 2024b. The statistical analysis was performed using the
unpaired two-tailedt test built-in Excel and statistical signiﬁcance was
considered when theP value was less than 0.05. Statistical analysis was
performed with at least three independent experiments.
