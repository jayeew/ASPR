Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is driven by genetic alterations in
the pancreatic epithelium (e.g.,KRAS) coupled with dysregulated innate
immunity that triggers tumor-promoting chronic inﬂammation. However, the
identity of innate immune molecular regulators as therapeutic targets in PDAC
is ill-deﬁned. Here, we show in PDAC patients that elevated tumoral expression
of the inﬂammasome adaptor protein ASC and its downstream effector
Caspase-1 is primarily colocalized tothe pancreatic ductal epithelium and
prognostic for poor survival. In the mutant Kras-driven KPC PDAC mouse
model, global and conditional (pancreatic epithelial) ablation of ASC, or
nanobody-mediated targeting of extracellular ASC, suppresses pancreatic
tumorigenesis. Whole transcriptome proﬁling and multiplex immuno-
ﬂuorescence reveal that the tumor-promoting activities of epithelial-derived
ASC align with molecular pathways for mitochondrial respiration, metabolism
(glycolysis), and immune responses.Our discovery that ASC-containing
inﬂammasomes promote PDAC by acting as a molecular bridge between
innate immunity, mitochondrial dysfunction and metabolic reprogramming
provides the rationale to therapeutically target ASC in cancers.
Pancreatic cancer is predicted to be the second most lethal cancer in
Western countries by 2030 and is associated with a low overall 5-year
survival rate of 10–12%
1–3. Poor survival outcomes for pancreatic cancer
are linked to late detection in 80% of patients diagnosed with inop-
erable locally advanced or metastatic late-stage disease, and the lim-
ited effectiveness of current standard-of-care chemotherapy regimens,
and more recently immunotherapy
3,4. The overwhelming majority
(~90%) of cases present as pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC),
driven by genetic alterations in the pancreas epithelium (e.g., KRAS,
90%; TP53, <70%) coupled with a highly heterogenic tumor micro-
environment (TME) that invariably exists in an immunosuppressive
state
3–5.

Elevated expression and activation of inﬂammasome compo-
nents ASC, Caspase-1, and IL-18 in human PDAC are prognostic
for poor survival
Global transcriptomics on PDAC patient primary tumor versus non-
tumor pancreas tissues in independent datasets (Victorian Pancreatic
Cancer Biobank, VPCB; The Cancer Genome Atlas, TCGA; GSE15471)
identiﬁed that PYCARD mRNA levels were signiﬁcantly upregulated in
PDAC tumor biopsies at all stages of disease (Fig. 1a, Supplementary
Fig. S1a,b). In addition,PYCARD gene expression was elevated in PDAC
patient tumors irrespective of distinct molecular subtypes previously
assigned to PDAC
28 (Supplementary Fig. S1c, d). Furthermore, elevated
PYCARD gene expression signiﬁcantly correlated with impaired survi-
val in both VPCB and TCGA patient cohorts, indicating thatPYCARDis a
poor prognostic indicator in PDAC (Fig. 1b, Supplementary Fig. S1e).
Increased ASC protein expression levels were also detected in PDAC
patient tumors by immunoblotting and immunohistochemistry, the
latter revealing pronounced staining of ASC-positive cells pre-
dominantly in the pancreatic ductal epithelium (Fig. 1c, d). Opal mul-
tiplex immuno ﬂuorescence staining of PDAC patient tumors
conﬁrmed that ASC strongly colocalized predominantly with CK19-
positive epithelial cells, while ASC staining in CD45-positive pan-
immune cells was not as pronounced (Fig. 1e, f, Supplementary
Fig. S1f,g). Notably, throughout PDAC patient tumors, ASC staining in
epithelial (and immune) cells localized to both the nucleus and cyto-
plasm, and frequently appeared punctate, suggestive of ASC“specks’,
with evidence also of ASC extracellular staining (Fig. 1e, f, Supple-
mentary Fig. S1f,g).
The above observations suggest that heightened activation of
ASC-containing in ﬂammasomes may be a common feature of
human PDAC. Indeed, mRNA levels for CASP1 were signi ﬁcantly
elevated in PDAC patient tumors, which positively correlated with
PYCARD and were indicative of poor patient survival (Supplemen-
tary Fig. S2a –c). Gene expression levels for in ﬂammasome-
associated PRRs, except for NLRP3, were also signi ﬁcantly upregu-
lated in PDAC patient tumors (Supplementary Fig. S2d). Immuno-
blotting indicated that protein expression levels of pro (p45) and
cleaved (p20) forms of Caspase-1, the latter indicative of ASC
inﬂammasome activation, were signi ﬁcantly elevated in PDAC
patient tumor versus non-tumor tissues (Fig. 1c). Immunohis-
tochemistry also demonstrated signi ﬁcantly increased numbers of
cleaved Caspase-1-positive cells in the ductal epithelium of human
PDAC tumor versus non-tumor tissue sections, with immuno-
ﬂuorescence depicting strong nuclear colocalization of cleaved
Caspase-1 largely in the cytokeratin-positive epithelium of PDAC
tumors (Fig. 1e–g, Supplementary Fig. S1f, g). Moreover, strong
nuclear, and to a lesser extent cytoplasmic, colocalization of
cleaved Caspase-1 with ASC was observed in PDAC patient tumors
(Fig. 1e, f, Supplementary Fig. S1f, g).
We next investigated whether the expression levels of inﬂamma-
some effector cytokines IL-1β and IL-18 were upregulated in indepen-
dent PDAC patient cohorts. ELISA re vealed that protein levels of pro
and mature forms of IL-18, but not IL-1β,w e r es i g n iﬁcantly increased in
serum and/or tumor tissue lysates from PDAC patients, with an
incremental increase in IL-18 protein levels observed from early (I/II)
through to advanced (III, IV) stages (Fig.1h, i, Supplementary Fig. S3a).
Furthermore, IL18, but not IL1B, tumoral mRNA levels were con-
sistently and signiﬁcantly upregulated in PDAC patient cohorts, with
only elevated
IL18 mRNA levels prognostic for impaired patient sur-
vival (Fig. 1j, k, Supplementary Fig. S3b, f). In addition, serum IL-18
protein levels positively correlated with tumoral PYCARD and CASP1
mRNA levels in PDAC patients (Fig. 1l). Taken together, these data
support the presence of activated in ﬂammasome complexes in
human PDAC.

ASC-containing inﬂammasomes promote PDAC in KPC mice via
selective upregulation of mature IL-18
To investigate whether ASC-containing in ﬂammasomes promoted
PDAC, we generated KPC mice globally deﬁcient for ASC (KPC:ASCKO)
(Supplementary Fig. S5a). At 10 and 20 weeks of age, the pancreas size
and mass of KPC mice were markedly elevated compared to WT mice
(Fig. 3a, b, Supplementary Fig. S5b, c). By contrast, compared to age-
and sex-matched KPC littermates, the pancreas of 10wo and 20wo
KPC:ASC
KO mice were visibly smaller and signiﬁcantly reduced in mass
by ~65% and ~75%, respectively, with no observable sex bias (Fig.3a, b,
Supplementary Fig. S5b, c). Histopathological assessment of 10wo
H&E-stained pancreas cross-sections indicated a marked suppression
in the early development of PDAC in KPC:ASC KO mice, with a 7-fold
reduction in the area of pancreas affected by lesions (49% KPC versus
7% KPC:ASC
KO) comprising only PanIN versus ductal carcinomas of
various differentiation grades in KPC mice (Supplementary
Fig. S5d –g). Similarly, 20wo KPC:ASC KO mice displayed a 3.5-fold
reduction in pancreas area (65% KPC versus 18% KPC:ASC KO) com-
prising lower grade well and moderate differentiated lesions com-
pared to the more severe poor differentiated lesions in 50% of age-
matched KPC mice (Fig. 3c–e, Supplementary Fig. S5g). Furthermore,
metastatic lesions were observed in the livers of ~19% (7/37) of KPC
mice aged 20 or more weeks (Fig. 3a), compared to 0% (0/33) in age-
matched KPC:ASC
KO mice. Therefore, these data indicate that elevated
ASC-containing inﬂammasome activity promotes the onset and pro-
gression of PDAC in KPC mice.
The suppressed PDAC phenotype in KPC:ASC KO mice associated
with reduced in ﬂammasome activation, with immunohistochemistry
and immunoﬂuorescence showing that elevated numbers of cleaved
Caspase-1-positive cells within the transformed pancreatic epithelium
of 10wo and 20wo KPC mice were signiﬁcantly reduced in the pancreas
of age-matched KPC:ASC
KO mice (Fig. 3f, g, Supplementary Fig. S5h).
Immunoblotting also conﬁrmed that cleaved Caspase-1 protein levels
were markedly lower in the pancreas of 20wo KPC:ASCKO compared to
KPC mice (Fig. 3h). Similarly, total GSDMD cellular staining was sig-
niﬁcantly upregulated in the pancreatic ductal epithelium of KPC
compared to WT and KPC:ASCKO mice (Fig. 3i). Intriguingly, immuno-
blotting showed that the increased GSDMD levels in the pancreas of
KPC mice (versus WT and KPC:ASC
KO) corresponded to full-length
GSDMD protein, rather than the N-terminal cleaved GSDMD p30
fragment (lowly expressed among all genotypes) which facilitates
plasma membrane pore formation and the extracellular release of
cytosolic contents (Fig. 3j). In further support of reduced in ﬂamma-
some activity in KPC:ASC
KO mice, ELISA demonstrated that protein
levels of both secreted IL-18 in serum and mature IL-18 in pancreatic
tissues from KPC:ASC
KO mice were signiﬁcantly reduced compared to
elevated levels in KPC mice (Fig. 3k, l, Supplementary Fig. S5i). Taken
together, these data strongly suggest the requirement of ASC for the
inﬂammasome-mediated processing of mature IL-18 during pancreatic
tumorigenesis.

ASC-driven PDAC is associated with impaired mitochondrial
biogenesis and metabolic reprogramming towards aerobic
glycolysis
Considering mitochondrial biogenesis encapsulates the transcription
and translation of mitochondrial- and nuclear-encoded genes 32,t h e
impaired expression of mitochondrial ETC components in KPC mice
(Fig. 8a, b) suggests that over-activation of ASC-containing inﬂamma-
somes in the pancreas may disrupt mitochondrial homeostasis.
Indeed, mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) copy number, which is a bio-
marker of mitochondrial biogenesis
33, was signiﬁcantly reduced in the
pancreas of KPC mice compared to the higher pancreatic mtDNA copy
number of KPC:ASC
KO (and WT) mice (Fig. 8c). We next explored the
ASC-mediated functional consequences of impaired mitochondrial
biogenesis by assessing levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS), which
in many cancer types, including oncogenic Kras-induced PDAC, accu-
mulate in response to dysfunctional mitochondrial respiration and
OXPHOS
34–36. Immunodetection of carbonyl groups on proteins, an
indicator of protein oxidation by ROS, demonstrated that signiﬁcantly
elevated levels of protein oxidation in pancreas lysates from tumor-
bearing KPC mice (versus WT controls) were reduced towards wild-
type levels in KPC:ASC
KO mice (Fig. 8d, e). Furthermore, immunohis-
tochemical staining for the cellular marker of oxidative stress, 4-
hydroxy-2-nonenal (4-HNE), which is upregulated in PDAC
35,37,w a s
more pronounced in pancreatic epithelial ductal regions of KPC mice
versus WT and KPC:ASC
KO mice, as well as KPC:ASC ﬂ/ﬂ mice (Fig. 8f).
The downregulation of mitochondrial-associated OXPHOS in the
transformed pancreas of KPC mice is consistent with the hypoxic state
of the TME in Kras-addicted PDAC, which fosters a switch in
mitochondrial-driven energy metabolism from OXPHOS to aerobic
glycolysis resulting in the biased conversion of glucose to lactate
(‘Warburg effect’)
38–40. Indeed, elevated lactate levels in pancreatic
tissues of KPC (versus WT) mice were substantially lower in KPC:ASCKO
mice (Fig. 8g). Collectively, these data suggest that ASC-containing
inﬂammasomes can contribute to the metabolic reprogramming of
cancer cells in PDAC by in ﬂuencing mitochondrial biogenesis, result-
ing in skewed energy metabolism towards aerobic glycolysis.
To determine the spatial cellular context of changes to mito-
chondrial biogenesis within the pancreatic TME and their localization
with activated ASC-containing in ﬂammasomes, we next performed
multiplexed immunoﬂuorescence on KPC versus WT pancreas sec-
tions with an antibody panel against ASC, cleaved Caspase-1, ductal
epithelial (CK19) versus immune (CD45) cells, NDUFB8 (complex I),
and UQCRC2 (complex III). Consistent with our earlier ﬁndings, the
comparable staining pattern of ASC and cleaved Caspase-1 pre-
dominantly colocalized with CK19-positive ductal epithelial (~30%)
versus CD45-positive immune (~8%) cells in the pancreas of KPC mice,
which was not observed in the pancreas of WT mice comprising >80%
CK19-negative acinar cells (Fig. 8h, i, Supplementary Fig. S11c –e).
Moreover, colocalization of dual-labeled ASC and cleaved Caspase-1
cells with mitochondrial markers NDUFB8 and UQCRC2 (~20%) was
observed in CK19-positive ductal epithelial lesions in KPC, but not WT,
mice (Fig. 8h, i, Supplementary Fig. S11c –e). Furthermore, both the
conditional and nanobody-mediated therapeutic targeting of ASC in
KPC mice resulted in a marked reduction in the mitochondrial colo-
calization of ASC and cleaved Caspase-1 with NDUFB8 and UQCRC2 in
the CK19-positive pancreatic ductal epithelium, with the suppressive
effect on in ﬂammasome activity more pronounced upon nanobody
(VHH
mASC) treatment (Supplementary Fig. S12, S13). Therefore, these
in vivo data support the notion that the expression (and activation) of
ASC-containing inﬂammasomes is upregulated in the pancreatic ductal
epithelium and localizes with mitochondrial markers during PDAC.

promoting function for ASC-containing in ﬂammasomes in the pan-

preferential maturation of the IL-18 effector cytokine. Some limitations
of our current study include the unknown identity of the
inﬂammasome-associated PRR(s) that contribute to PDAC, as well as
formal evidence (such as via targeting IL-18 in KPC mice) that IL-18

RNA-seq read alignment and UMI counting were performed in R
(v4.1.2)

data68. Multiplex RNA-seq FASTQ ﬁles had already been demulti-

with scPipe. Read alignment was performed using the RSubread
package (v2.6.4)

were mapped to exons using the sc_exon_mapping function with the

BAM ﬁles were processed, and reads mapping to exons were asso-
ciated with each individual sample using the sc_demultiplex func-
tion, taking the UMI into account, and an overall count for each
gene per sample was generated using the sc_gene_counting func-
tion (with UMI_cor = 1).
