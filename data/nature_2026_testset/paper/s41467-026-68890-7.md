Mixed ionic-electronic conductors have attracted tremendous atten-
tion due to their unique properties and applicability in a wide range of
devices, such as batteries

transistors3,4. Organic mixed-ionic electronic conductors, particularly

PEDOT:PSS in its pristine form possesses a low electrical con-
ductivity, near 1 S/cm 3,7,10,11. The addition of high boiling point
solvents11, “secondary dopants” such as ionic liquids and salts12,13,a n d
post-deposition acid treatment14 can substantially enhance electrical
conductivity by three orders of magnitude over their pristine coun-
terparts. In addition to the signiﬁcant boost to conductivity, ionic and
molecular additives have also been linked to an increase in the
stretchability and ﬂexibility of PEDOT:PSS, a key property for fabri-
cating wearable electronics
7,12,13.
A large body of work has focused on studying structure-function
relationships in PEDOT:PSS under different processing
conditions
7,12,13,15– 19. Despite these efforts, a holistic picture describing
the PEDOT:PSS morphology remains elusive, in part due to morpho-
logical complexities and the lack of real space imaging techniques
conveying the solution and solid-state properties. For instance, we
commonly accept that PEDOT:PSS microstructure consists of core-
shell micelles with PEDOT-rich cores and PSS-rich coronas. Despite this
common assumption, the reported core-shell size extracted from
neutron, X-ray, and light scattering techniques ranges from a few
nanometers
15 to hundreds of nanometers 20,21. The role of additives
further complicates the morphological picture 3.X - r a ya n dn e u t r o n
scattering measurements point to the formation of rod-like
structures
21 in PEDOT:PSS with added salts, as well as an increase in
t h ed e g r e eo fc r y s t a l l i n i t y7 of PEDOT:PSS through the breakdown of
core-shell and phase segregation of PEDOT and PSS. Nevertheless,
important aspects of the PEDOT:PSS morphology remain unresolved,
such as the size and distribution of crystalline domains and the cor-
relation between rod-like aggregates and crystalline phases. Despite
the partial success of the core-shell breakdown model, this description
fails to explain multiple PEDOT:PSS properties, such as the simulta-
neous increase in electrical conductivity and increase in mechanical
compliance, in particular of modern formulations of PEDOT:PSS
7,22.
This behavior is in contrast with other conjugated polymer systems,
where a trade-off exists between charge transport (and crystallinity)
and mechanical compliance
23,24. We propose that the lack of a com-
prehensive morphological model predicting the physical properties of
PEDOT:PSS mainly stems from reliance on scattering and spectroscopy
techniques to characterize the complex global and local morphology
of PEDOT:PSS.
Here, we reveal how the microstructure impacts the mechanical
and electrical properties of PEDOT:PSS using a combination of cryo-
genic electron microscopy (cryo-EM), electron energy loss spectro-
scopy (EELS), and X-ray scattering. Exploiting high-resolution cryo-EM
reveals the nanostructure evolution of PEDOT:PSS dispersions upon
the addition of ionic and nonionic additives. We used bis(tri-
ﬂuoromethane)sulfonimide lithium (Li-TFSI) and 1-ethyl-3-
methylimidazolium bis(tri ﬂuoromethylsulfonyl)imide (EMIM-TFSI)
salts due to their strong cation exchange capability with PEDOT:PSS
22.
Polyethylene glycol diacrylate (PEGDA) was selected as a nonionic
additive because it can enable highly-conductive, stretchable PED-
OT:PSS-based bioelectronics
9,10. Our results reveal that ionic and
PEGDA additives lead to the formation of elongated ﬁbers and chain
packing in solutions. Further analysis of cryo-EM results reveals that
elongated ﬁbers form through the coalescence and self-assembly of
PEDOT:PSS micelles that often exhibit an outer layer of extended and
stacked chains. The presence of these heterostructural ﬁbers has
direct implications on the mechanical and electrical properties of
PEDOT:PSS.
High-resolution cryo-EM imaging of solid ﬁlms, which are pro-
cessed from PEDOT:PSS solutions with the various additives, conﬁrms
the formation and growth of crystalline domains and heterostructural
ﬁbers in solid samples. Furthermore, by employing a combination of
cryo-EM, EELS, and an automated crystal feature extraction framework
(called Graph based Analysis of Transmission Electron Microscopy,
GRATEv2
25), we show how exposure to moisture reorganizes the
PEDOT:PSS nanostructure leading to smaller crystalline domains while
heterostructuralﬁbers swell. The former observation is consistent with
the reduction in electrical conductivity of PEDOT:PSS in the presence
of moisture
26,27, while the latter explains the complex mechanical
properties of PEDOT:PSS with ionic and PEGDA additives. Our results
also show that additives can enhance the hydrophilicity of PEDOT:PSS,
leading to higher moisture absorbance and resulting in higher
mechanical compliance and a larger crack onset strain in these sam-
ples. Subsequently, the combination of heterostructuralﬁbers swelling
and reorganization of crystalline domains leads to the formation of
simultaneously conductive and stretchable PEDOT:PSS thinﬁlms. Our
work provides a framework for the complex interplay between mor-
phological, mechanical, and electrical properties of modern formula-
tions of PEDOT:PSS used in different applications, such as implant
electrodes
5, wearable electronics 9, and organic electrochemical
transistors8,28.
Results
Cryo-EM of PEDOT:PSS dispersions
Beam damage is one of the main factors limiting the resolution and
detailed analysis of organic structures in real space. Cryo-EM has been
extensively used in the study of biological samples
29,30 to both mini-
mize electron beam damage 31– 33 and enable studies of hydrated
(vitriﬁed) samples 31– 33. We used cryo-EM to track PEDOT:PSS self-
assembly with salt additives. Previously, we showed that the addition
of ionic species leads to the formation and self-assembly of elongated
ﬁbers in the solution phase of PEDOT:PSS
3. Other recent studies have
also reported elongated ﬁbrils in doped PEDOT:PSS 17,34,35.D e s p i t e
these initial observations, the lack of high-resolution structural ima-
ging of PEDOT:PSS in either solution or solid ﬁlms precludes a com-
plete description of PEDOT:PSS phase formation and microstructure.
Speciﬁcally, the distribution of crystalline domains and the inner
structure of elongated ﬁbers in highly conductive and stretchable
PEDOT:PSS remain underexplored.
Solution cryo-EM measurements were performed on vitri ﬁed
PEDOT:PSS samples at liquid nitrogen temperature (see the Methods
section for details). Figure 1a– b shows a schematic of the plunge
freezing of TEM grids and the chemical structure of PEDOT:PSS and
ionic and PEGDA additives, respectively. Schematics of PEDOT:PSS
dispersions containing isolated micelles and aggregated micelles in
heterostructural ﬁbers are shown in Fig. 1c– d, respectively. High-
resolution cryo-EM shows the presence of two distinct domains in neat
PEDOT:PSS solutions, (i) isolated micelles with a size of 4.1 ± 1.3 nm,
and (ii) elongated ﬁbers (Fig. 1e– g). On the other hand, PEDOT:PSS
solutions containing ionic and PEGDA additives, in addition to the
higher density of elongated ﬁbers, also show lattice planes of PED-
OT:PSS in solution (Fig. 1h– p).
Previous work has used X-ray scattering measurements to pro-
pose that PEDOT:PSS micelles consist of a PEDOT-rich nanocrystal-
line core and a PSS-rich corona
15. The higher mass density of the
crystalline PEDOT-rich core when compared to the amorphous PSS-
rich corona leads us to assume that electron scattering from the
micelles is dominated by the core region. As a result, we associate the
ca. 3 – 4 nm features in cryo-EM micrographs with the PEDOT-rich
core of PEDOT:PSS micelles. At this point, the size of the PSS-rich
corona region cannot be independently extracted from cryo-EM,
mainly due to the small scattering contrast between solvated PSS-
rich regions and water.

of neat PEDOT:PSS (3.9 ± 0.8 nm) (see Figs. S1 – S2). A reduction in
micelle size upon the addition of salts to PEDOT:PSS has been pre-
viously reported, which has been attributed to the screening of
interchain charge repulsion

Solid-liquid Interactions
We expect different responses from crystalline domains and hetero-
structural ﬁbers when exposed to moisture. To investigate this
hypothesis, we exposed PEDOT:PSS+Li-TFSI thin ﬁlms to moisture
(100% relative humidity (RH)) before plunge-freezing the samples with
liquid ethane (see Methods section for further details). This approach
allows us to monitor the morphological evolution in high-conductivity
PEDOT:PSS thin ﬁlms in the presence of water.
As shown in cryo-EM images in Fig. 2c, upon exposure to moist-
ure, PEDOT:PSS+Li-TFSI thin ﬁlms show a uniform morphology with
some inhomogeneity observed in the density of PEDOT:PSS micelles.
We also show HRTEM images of PEDOT:PSS+Li-TFSI recorded at 30%
and 100% relative humidity under varying defocus conditions in
Fig. S16. Moisture exposure improves the contrast of polymer micelles,
as can be seen in Fig. 2d. Given the expected thickness increase of
PEDOT:PSS ﬁlms upon hydration
42,43, the improvement in scattering
contrast cannot be explained by reduced inelastic electron scattering
or a reduced number of overlapping features in hydrated samples.
Subsequently, we deduce that upon exposure to moisture, the PSS-rich
shell of the micelle swells, decreasing the scattering from the shell and
improving the contrast between the PEDOT-rich core and the swollen
PSS-rich corona. This shows that heterostructuralﬁbers consist of self-
assembled core-shell PEDOT:PSS micelles that swell upon exposure to
water. Figure2f shows the 1D small angle X-ray scattering (SAXS) SAXS
proﬁles of drop cast neat PEDOT:PSS and PEDOT:PSS+Li-TFSI at two
different RH levels. While the SAXS proﬁles from neat PEDOT:PSS show
minimal differences between 40% and 85% RH, PEDOT:PSS+Li-TFSI
samples show a clear change that corresponds to the formation of
mesoscale structures upon exposure to higher moisture.

Electrical and mechanical properties of PEDOT:PSS

factors driving the widespread use of this mixed ionic-electronic
polymer in various applications
3,7,9,49. In this section, we explore the
correlation between morphology, conductivity, and mechanical
properties of PEDOT:PSS in the presence of ionic additives. Consistent
with prior studies
7,12,13,22, we observe an increase in the electrical con-
ductivity of PEDOT:PSS thinﬁlms by two to three orders of magnitude
followingﬁlm processing using ionic additives and water treatment of
salt-containing ﬁlms (Fig. S18). The signi ﬁcant increase in electrical
conductivity of PEDOT:PSS is often closely correlated with an increase
in crystallinity of PEDOT:PSS processed with different additives
7,12,13.
This is consistent with the larger number of crystalline domains
observed in cryo-EM of PEDOT:PSS+Li-TFSI thinﬁlms (See Fig. 3d).

increasing trend in COS is also observed in PEDOT:PSS + PEGDA

with additives, including salts

To further investigate the effect of ionic additives and their sub-
sequent removal upon water treatment, we performed thermogravi-
metric analysis (TGA) on neat PEDOT:PSS and PEDOT:PSS+Li-TFSI;
results are shown in Fig.4b. Neat PEDOT:PSS show two main mass loss
transitions: theﬁrst transition completes around 100 °C; this transition
is due to the evaporation of moisture within the PEDOT:PSS structure.
The second transition at ~275 °C is associated with the chemical
degradation of PEDOT:PSS. On the other hand, PEDOT:PSS+Li-TFSI
shows a signiﬁcantly different TGA proﬁle. TGA from PEDOT:PSS+Li-
TFSI does not show clear thermal transitions, but instead a continuous
loss of sample mass, with an overall larger mass loss percentage
compared to neat PEDOT:PSS. After water treatment (removing added
salts), the thermogram reverts to the two-step pro ﬁle for neat PED-
OT:PSS, conﬁrming that water absorption due to Li-TFSI is responsible
for the broadened, continuous loss of sample mass (Fig. S20). The
water uptake ofﬁlms containing Li-TFSI likely leads to the formation of
water-salt complexes within the PEDOT:PSS structure
51,52.
We also measured the water uptake due to Li-TFSI using scanning
transmission electron microscopy-electron energy loss spectroscopy
(STEM-EELS) at room temperature and cryogenic conditions (Fig. S21).
STEM-EELS allows us to establish a compositional-morphological
relationship in PEDOT:PSS thin ﬁlms processed with hydrophilic
additives. Although ionic additives are linked to the improved
stretchability of PEDOT:PSS, upon water treatment, the stretchability
of PEDOT:PSS ﬁlms signiﬁcantly drops due to the removal of these
additives
13. We associate this behavior with the formation of water-salt
complexes in PEDOT:PSS and subsequent elimination of these com-
plexes after water treatment. This is consistent with the increase in
oxygen composition (as an indica tor for water absorption) when we
compare PEDOT: PSS+Li-TFSI thin ﬁlms before and after salt removal
(Fig. 4c and Fig. S22).

representation to cluster those skeletal elements into distinct crystal-
