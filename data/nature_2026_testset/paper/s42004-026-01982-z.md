The tetrahydroisoquinoline scaffold is an important structural motif in many natural products and
pharmaceuticals, known for its broad biological activities. Fused tetrahydroisoquinoline polycyclic
heterocyclic structures have increasingly attracted attention, yet their synthetic pathways remain
limited. Herein, we developed an efﬁcient, metal-free strategy for assembling tetrahydroisoquinoline-
fused polycycles via Vilsmeier-reagent promoted decarbonylative cyclization reaction. The protocol
features mild conditions, excellent functional-group tolerance, and high ef ﬁciency. Furthermore,
biological evaluations demonstrated that the constructed derivatives exhibit antiproliferative activity in
cancer cell lines, thereby providing potential starting points for further optimization.
The tetrahydroisoquinoline (THIQ) structural motif constitutes a vital
scaffold in numerous bioactive natural products and semisynthetic deri-
vatives, exhibiting potential a pplications across a spectrum of ﬁelds
1–4.
Notably, fused THIQs are extensively distributed in alkaloids and chemi-
cally synthesized substances with signiﬁcant biological activities. This class
of structures displays a diverse array o f bioactivities, encompassing anti-
tumor effects
5–8, inhibition of protein tyrosine phosphatases such as TC-
PTP and PTP1B9–13, and anti-HIV activity14–18. For instance, Tetrazomine,
an antibiotic featuring a THIQ-fused six-membered nitrogen-containing
heterocyclic structure, has been isolated from the bacterium Streptomyces
variabilis. It demonstrates inhibitory activity against both Gram-positive
and Gram-negative bacteria. Moreover, Tetrazomine exhibits potent cyto-
toxicity against P388 leukemia cells
19,20. PD 102807, a novel M4 muscarinic
receptor antagonist with a six-membered fused heterocyclic scaffold, has
demonstrated antiparkinsonian effects in animal studies21–26.A d d i t i o n a l l y ,
an anti-CRC (colorectal cancer) agent possessing a novel fused THIQ
scaffold with excellent in vitro and in vivo anti-CRC activities by targeting
Hsp90 to disturb Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, which lays a foundation
for discovering more effective CRC-targeted therapies
27. Moreover, some
molecules with a seven-membered fused heterocyclic core have also
demonstrated excellent pharmacological activities, including the inhibition
of 5-HT2
28 and related protein kinases29,30 (Fig. 1A). A multitude of other
THIQ-fused polycyclic heterocycl ic compounds with pharmacological
activities have been identiﬁed, which collectively expand the conceptual and
methodological frameworks for drug development.
So far, synthetic strategies for the construction of THIQ-fused seven-
membered heterocycles have rarely been reported31,32. In 2024, Cui and co-
workers developed a multicomponen t reaction that assembles seven-
membered heterocycles from indole, formaldehyde, and ammonium
chloride, yet the process requires prolonged heating, and the substrate scope
remains limited. Additionally, more research has focused on the construc-
tion of fused six-membered heterocycles, with methods including tradi-
tional chemical synthesis, organo catalysis, and other approaches
33–41.
Particularly, Seidel reported an expeditious and ef ﬁcient method for the
synthesis of benz[ e][1,3]oxazine scaffolds through acetic acid catalysis 42.
Especially, Baruah devised a catalyst-free three-component reaction for the
synthesis of 1,3-oxazine via naphthol, aldehyde, and tetrahydroisoquino-
line, representing a multicomponent C–H functionalization reaction con-
ducted in water and employing molecular oxygen as the sole oxidant,
thereby eliminating the need for any ca talyst or chemically synthesized
oxidant, and marking theﬁrst instance of a C–H functionalization reaction
occurring in an aqueous medium
43. Besides, Maycock’s group reported a
stereoselective copper-catalyzed intramolecular annulation method for the
synthesis of various dihydro-1,3-oxazines, using inexpensive

Carboxylic acids are valued for their stability, low toxicity, and ready
availability, and direct de carbonylative/decarboxylative transformations
allow efﬁcient conversion into diverse structures. Decarbonylative cycliza-
tion entails the expulsion of CO or CO₂ from a carboxylic acid or its deri-
vatives concomitant with ring formation, enabling rapid access to complex,
functional polycyclic architectures. To date, decarbonylative transforma-
tions have been dominated by transition-metal catalysis 45, visible-light-
mediated radical decarboxylation 46, and electrochemical protocols 47
(Fig. 1C). Transition-metal-catalyzed decarbonylationvia carbon−carbon
bond cleavage constitutes a pivotal synthetic manifold, wherein the aryl- and
alkyl-metal intermediates generated during the decarbonylative event can
engage in a diverse array of downstre am transformations. However, the
majority of metal-catalyzed examples still require temperatures >130 °C and
exhibit limited selectivity. For instance, Dong ’s rhodium-catalyzed dec-
arbonylative coupling delivers 2-quinolinone derivatives ef ﬁciently, yet
necessitates 160 °C and otherwise harsh conditions
48. Consequently, the
discovery of more efﬁcient catalyst systems and milder reaction conditions
remains a central challenge in the ﬁeld. Visible light-mediated decarbox-
ylation readily furnishes radical intermediates at ambient temperature, yet
heavy metal Ru- or Ir-based photocatalysts are hampered by toxicity and
cost49, whereas organic photocatalysts s uffer from poor photostability.
Furthermore, electrochemical decarbonylation has advanced rapidly in
recent years, operating under amb ient conditions with exceptional
functional-group tolerance. Nevertheless, its nucleophile scope remains
narrow, and readily oxidized primary amines or phenols still present for-
midable challenges.
Our group is dedicated to p erforming chemical modiﬁcation of tet-
rahydroprotoberberine derivatives through developing highly effective
synthetic strategies for a long time and successfully constructing a library of
small molecules with different scaffolds
50–54. Herein, we report a dec-
arbonylative annulation promoted by the Vilsmeier reagent, providing
rapid access to THIQ-fused heterocyclic scaffolds. This method allows for
the one-step construction of a novel THIQ-fused seven-membered het-
erocyclic scaffold at room temperature. Furthermore, we have successfully
extended this approach to the synthesis of fused six-membered heterocycles.

Results and discussion
Reaction optimizations
At the outset of our investigation, 1,2,3,4-tetrahydroisoquinoline-1-car-
boxylic acid derivative (1a) was selected as our model substrate. Initially, the
reaction was carried out using dich loromethane (DCM) as the solvent,
adding oxalyl chloride and N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF) at −20 °C,
subsequently raising the temperature to−10 °C. Excitingly, the new product
2a was generated in an 18% yield after reacting for 4 h (Table1, entry 1), and
its structure was conﬁrmed via single crystal X-ray crystallography (Sup-
plementary Fig. 7 and Supplementary Table 4 and Supplementary Data 1).
Crucially, the control experiment uncovered that no target product was
obtained in the absence of DMF (Supplementary Table 1, entry 1)
55,56.T o
enhance the reaction efﬁciency, we embarked on a systematic optimization
of the reaction conditions, exploring various parameters to reﬁne the pro-
cess. Firstly, we investigated the impactof different activating reagents on
the reaction, including thionyl chlo ride/DMF, phosphorus oxychloride/
DMF, bis(Trichloromethyl)Carbonate (BTC)/DMF, phthaloyl dichloride/
DMF, phosphorus pentachloride/DM F, (chloromethylene)dimethy-
lammonium chloride (Vilsmeier reagent)
57–64 and triﬂuoroacetic anhydride
(TFAA)65 (Table1, entries 2-8). Among these, Vilsmeier reagent emerged as
the most effective activating reagent, signiﬁcantly improving the yield of2a
to 43% (Table 1, entry 7). Subsequently, we optimized the amount of the
Vilsmeier reagent and found that 6.0 equiv afforded 2a in 54% yield.
(Table 1, entries 9 –12). Afterward, a series of solvents was successively
explored and showed that DCM remained the optimal solvent (Table 1,
entries 13–16; Supplementary Table 1, entries 2 –8). The reaction tem-
perature was deﬁned in the following explorations (Table1,e n t r i e s1 7–19),
and we noticed that this process could be proceed smoothly to afford2a in a
relatively high yield (75%) when the activating reagents were added at 0 °C
and the reaction was carried out at room temperature. Reaction time was
screened (Supplementary Table 1, entries 9 –1 2 ) ,b u ts h o r t e ro rl o n g e r
durations gave lower yields. Based on the above ﬁndings, the optimal
reaction conditions are as follows: Vilsmeier reagent (0.72 mmol) was added
to a solution of1a (0.12 mmol) in DCM (2.0 mL) at 0 °C, and then warming
to room temperature for 4 h.
Evaluation of the substrate scopes
With the optimized reaction conditions in hand (for detailed procedures, see
“Methods” section), we explored the substrate scope of diverse 1,2,3,4-
tetrahydroisoquinoline-1-carboxylic acid derivatives1a-1z, as illustrated in
Scheme 1 (The NMR spectra are provided in Supplementary Data 3.). We
systematically investigated the impact of various substituents on the indole
and THIQ moieties of the substrates on the reaction yield. First, introducing
a methyl group at the 3-position of the indole ring afforded product2b in
66% yield. Furthermore, various carboxylic acids having substituents at the
5- and 6-positions of the indole rin g with both electron-donating and
electron-withdrawing groups, including methyl, methoxy, halogen (ﬂuoro,
chloro, and bromo), and ester, reacted to form the desired products2c-2l in
24-90% yields, attesting to the exceptional functional-group tolerance of this
protocol. In addition, when dimethylamino or methoxy substituents were
introduced into the phenyl ring of the THIQ fragment, the corresponding
products were obtained in good to excellent yields (2m, 51%; 2n, 90%). To
our delight, the introduction of a methyl or ﬂuoro at the ortho position
relative to the methoxy group was all compatible ( 2o, 48%; 2p, 59%).
Substrates bearing double substitutions, such as benzyloxy and methoxy,
also afforded the product in moderate yield ( 2q, 58%). We also explored
substrates1r and 1s, which featured fused ring structures on the phenyl ring
of the THIQ fragment, and these substrates also showed good reactivity in
this reaction to give the target productssuccessfully. Notably, replacing the
phenyl ring of the THIQ fragment with a thiophene ring resulted in the
desired product2t with a 67% yield, and its derivatives2u-2y were obtained
in moderate yields (27-56%). Additionally, when the tetrahydroisoquinoline
ring is fully hydrogenated, the substrate molecule can still cyclize to form the
desired product in good yield ( 2z, 66%). Regrettably, replacing the tetra-
hydroisoquinoline core with a piperidine or substituting the indole moiety
with a pyrrole ring failed to deliver the corresponding products under the
standard conditions (see the Supplementary Information for details, Sup-
plementary Fig. 1). Overall, our comprehensive investigation of the posi-
tions and types of substituents in the substrate fragments demonstrated that
a wide range of functional groups are well tolerated by this reaction system.
This ﬁnding provides a convenient and efﬁcient approach for constructing
structurally diverse THIQ-fused seven-membered heterocyclic compounds,
thereby expanding the scope of potential applications in chemical synthesis.

yields (23-80%). Moreover, the reactiontolerated the substitution of halogen
atoms at the ortho position to the phenolic hydroxyl group ( 4i, 67%; 4j,
62%). We also attempted to introduce methyl,tert-butyl, and nitro groups at
the 5-position of the phenyl fragment and found that the desired products

Mechanistic investigation
In order to gain insight into the mechanism of this reaction, a substrate
analogue 2-benzyl-1,2,3,4-tetrahydroisoquinoline-1-carboxylic acid ( 5a)
was subjected to reaction under standard conditions. The resulting product
was isolated and puriﬁed (Fig. 2A), and was subsequently identiﬁed as 2-
benzyl-3,4-dihydroisoquinolinium-2-ium (6a) through the combined
application of nuclear magnetic resonance hydrogen spectroscopy and mass
spectrometry. Based on these ﬁndings, we hypothesized that the reaction
involves extrusion of the carboxylate group and designed experiments to
capture the reaction intermediates (Fig. 2B). The model substrate 1a was
reacted under standard conditions, and the reaction mixture was sampled
after 1 h. Then, high-resolution massspectrometry was employed to analyze
the mass spectra of the intermediat es in the reaction mixture (Supple-
mentary Fig. 2). Through this analysis, we successfully detected the imine
intermediate, thereby proposing that the reaction proceedsvia extrusion of
the carboxylate unit followed by nucl eophilic addition. Concurrently, we
employed the singlet enantiomer(+)-1a, derived from the resolution pro-
cess, as the substrate for the reaction and obtained the racemic product,
thereby further corroborating our hypothesis (Supplementary Fig. 3 and
Supplementary Fig. 4). Finally, we designed an experimental setup to collect
the gas generated during the reaction, corroborating that the carboxyl group
is extruded as CO and thus validating and completing the proposed
mechanistic manifold. (see the Supple mentary Information for details,
Supplementary Fig. 5).

Gram scale synthesis and practical applications
To verify the viability and prospective applicability of the method, we
initially undertook a gram-scale experiment (Fig.3A). Following puriﬁca-
tion, the target product 2a was isolated in an impressive yield of 70%,
thereby highlighting the potential of this method for large-scale application.
Besides, to explore the potential pharmacological activities of these syn-
thesized novel compounds with fused heterocyclic scaffolds, some selected
compounds were screened in antitumor proliferativeactivity assay. Excit-
ingly, ﬁve compounds (2j, 2t, 2u, 2v, 2w ) exhibited signiﬁcant inhibitory
activity against the proliferation ofMCF-7 tumor cells (human breast cancer
cells), with an inhibition rate of >70% at 10 μM (Fig. 3B). These results
demonstrated that fused heterocycles built on thiophene –indole cores
possess measurable anti-MCF-7 potency. Speciﬁcally, 2t showed excellent
anticancer activity in MCF-7 cells, with IC
50 value of 1.16μM. Preliminary
structure–activity relationship analyses revealed that introducingﬂuoro into
the 6-position of the indole ring (2u) had little effect on activity, whereas
introduction of a methoxy group at the same position (2w) led to a moderate
decrease in antiproliferative poten cy. Similarly, a 5-position chloro
substituent (2v) elicits a modest decline in ef ﬁcacy. Methylation of the
thiophene ring (2x) caused a marked reduction in activity, and replacement
of the thiophene with a b enzothiophene ring ( 2y) essentially abolished
activity. Based on these observations, we tentatively propose that the
unsubstituted thiophene ring within this compound class is critical for the
observed antiproliferative activity. Moreover, compound2t was tested for its
inhibitory activity against the other two human cancer cell lines, including
HeLa (cervical cancer) and HCT116 (colon cancer). To our delight, com-
pound2t displayed excellent inhibitory activities against HeLa and HCT116
cells, with IC
50 values of 0.41μM and 0.87μM, respectively (Supplementary
Fig. 6 and Supplementary Data 4). Collectively, these results reveal that the
fused heterocyclic compounds we have synthesized possess promising
anticancer potential.
Conclusions
In summary, we have developed an ef ﬁcient strategy for the convenient
synthesis of the novel THIQ-fused polycyclic heterocyclic skeleton via a
Vilsmeier reagent-promoted decarbonylative annulation reaction at ambi-
ent temperature. This protocol is characterized by wide functional group
tolerance, mild reaction conditio ns, and it can occur in a moderate to
excellent yield. Furthermore, biological evaluation revealed that the novel
frameworks constructed by this method ology possess antiproliferative
activity against cancer cell lines, thereby providing new insights into the
discovery of bioactive small molecules.

Methods
General procedure for the synthesis of target products
In a pre-dried sealed tube, Vilsmeier reagent (0.72 mmol, 6.0 eq.) was added
to a solution of the 1,2,3,4-tetrahydroisoquinoline-1-carboxylic acid deri-
vative (1 or 3,0 . 1 2m m o l ,1 . 0e q . )i nC H2Cl2 (2.0 mL) at 0 °C. Subsequently,
the tube was sealed, and the reaction mixture was stirred at room tem-
perature for 4 h. Upon completion, the reaction was quenched with satu-
rated sodium bicarbonate aqueous solution and extracted with CH
2Cl2
(10 mL × 2). The combined organic layer was washed with brine (30 mL),
dried over anhydrous Na
2SO4, and concentrated in vacuo. The residue was
puriﬁed by column chromatography to afford the desired product.
