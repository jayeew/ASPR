Physiological and neuro-electrophysiological characterization
of the effects of LL-VNS
To develop a clinically applicable LL-VNS system, we ﬁrst established
optimal stimulation parameters that could modulate cardiac function
while maintaining safety margins. During the measurement process,
gold electrodes were used to connect the nerve stimulation receiver
and stimulator to the cervical vagus nerve, enabling it to receive and
transmit electrical signals (Supplementary Fig. 1).
The threshold of LL-VNS was deﬁned by Li’s research team in 2009
as the maximum intensity for maintaining sinus rhythm. Half of the
stimulation threshold is typically used by researchers as a safety
parameter for long-term LL-VNS. Therefore, we designed a neural sti-
mulation protocol to explore the LL-VNS threshold in experimental
mice to determine the ﬁnal stimulation intensity. We adopted a fre-
quency of 2 Hz on the basis of heart rate and FDA-approved VNS
applications. Moreover, we set up a current source nerve stimulator
with low current and high resistance to ensure the accuracy of the VNS
intensity (Supplementary Fig. 2a).
Heart rate variability (HRV), changes in electrocardiogram wave-
forms (which are closely related to cardiovascular status), and high-
frequency power can re ﬂect the physiological state of mice and the
excitation state of the vagus nerve and were used as criteria for
exploring the LL-VNS threshold. As shown in Fig.2a, during 60-sec LL-
VNS, the heart rates of the mice decreased. The heart rate decreased
slowly at 0–8 μA (pp.) and decreased rapidly at 10μA (pp.), indicating
that 8 μA (pp.) was still within the stimulation threshold range. Fig-
ure 2b shows the heart rate and HRV data. With increasing stimulation
intensity, the HRV increased within the range of 0–15 μA (pp.), which
was not obvious after 8μA (pp.). Within the range of 0–15 μA( p p . ) ,t h e
rate of change in heart rate decreased signiﬁcantly and then increased
slowly afterward. As mentioned earlier, LL-VNS with an absolute heart
rate change rate of 15% and an HRV value within 0.5 should be within
the safe range, which indirectly proves that 8 μA (pp.) is the LL-VNS
threshold. The calculatedΔHRV and vagus nerve signal power density
values indicate that the HF power reaches the maximum value at 8μA
(pp.), and theΔHRV value is close to the 0 point. which also proves that
8 μA (pp.) is the LL-VNS threshold (Fig. 2c). Overall, a stimulation
intensity of 8μA (pp.) was proven to be the LL-VNS threshold, and half
of the threshold (4 μA) is typically regarded as a safe parameter for
long-term neural stimulation, which was also demonstrated in our
experiment (Supplementary Fig. 3).
We further veri ﬁed the changes in the ECG (Fig. 2e) and down-
stream vagus nerve signals (Fig. 2f) of the mice before, during and
after stimulation at a nerve stimulation intensity of 4 μA (pp.)
(Fig. 2d), and the results indicated that the frequencies of the ECG
and downstream vagus nerve signals remained basically unchanged
before and after stimulation at this intensity. Short-term Fourier
transform was used to analyze the MSA of downstream vagus nerve
signals in the low-frequency region (0 –50 Hz) and the high-
frequency region (500 Hz), and the results indicated that this sti-
mulation intensity caused excitation of the vagus nerve in the low-
frequency region (Fig. 2g, h, and Supplementary Fig. 2b –d). More-
over, an analysis of neural signals before and after stimulation
revealed that vagus nerve function was still not affected. These
ﬁndings establish 4 μA peak-to-peak current at 2 Hz as optimal LL-
VNS parameters for chronic cardiac therapy, representing 50% of
the identi ﬁed threshold with demonstrated safety margins and
effective vagal modulation. This intensity achieved therapeutic
vagal activation while preserving normal cardiac conduction and
autonomic function, providing the foundation for subsequent
device design and therapeutic evaluation.
Design and working principle of the BTENG
The BTENG developed in this study adopts a material system and
structural design to meet the requirements of long-term electrical
stimulation therapy. On the basis of the LL-VNS test results, a biode-
gradable triboelectric nanogenerator whose output can provide a
neural stimulation output of 4 μA (pp.) was designed. The BTENG is
composed of multiple functional components: the positive tribo-
electric layer comprises a composite system of polyvinyl alcohol (PVA)
and microiron powder, and the negative triboelectric layer is a pure
PVA ﬁlm. Metal Mo was sputtered on the reverse side of the PVAﬁlm to
form an electrical circuit. These two triboelectric layers achieve elec-
trical output through low-frequency contact separation. The spacer
layer is made of a fully freeze-dried hydrogel material to ensure the
effective operation of the dynamic contact separation mechan-
ism (Fig. 3a).
The encapsulation layer comprises a polycaprolactone-
polylactic acid (PCL-PLA) composite system, and controllable
degradation was achieved through ultrasonic responsive design.
The PCL coating provides waterproof performance and anti-
degradation performance, whereas the PLA substrate is used to
ensure the mechanical properties of the device. Under the special
design of the modulus, the PCL layer and the PLA layer are more
prone to stress concentration and damage in a high-frequency
ultrasonic environment, which has also been proven in COMSOL
physical ﬁeld simulations (Supplementary Fig. 4a –d). This protec-
tion mechanism can not only maintain the functional stability of the
device in the humoral environment but also accelerate the degra-
dation process through ultrasonic triggering.
The working process of the TENG is shown in Fig.3b. In the design,
the contact-separation mode is employed, which minimizes the stress
on the triboelectric layer actuator. In the initial state, the top layer is
separated from the bottom layer, with no triboelectric potential
existing between the electrodes. When force is applied to the device,
the upper end deforms, causing the upper and lower triboelectric
layers to come into contact. As the force increases, the contact area
between the two triboelectric layers gradually increases. When full
contact is achieved, the triboelectric potential reaches its peak. If an
external circuit is used, an electric currentﬂows during deformation of
the TENG. When the force is remo ved, the upper and lower tribo-
electric layers revert to their original positions due to the action of the
middle layer, causing the electrons to be driven back to their original
positions by the triboelectric potential, thereby completing an elec-
trical cycle.
As an implantable electrical stimulation output device, output
efﬁciency and stability are the most important parameters. Thus,
the output performance and operational stability of the device
were characterized experimentally. As shown in Fig. 3c, d, the open-
circuit voltage of the device was approximately 9.07 ± 0.43 V,
and the short-circuit current (peak-to-peak, pp.) was 4.19 ± 0.14 μA
(pp.). Notably, owing to the characteristics of the BTENG, the
device output is an AC output, and the positive value and amplitude
of the short-circuit current output differ signi ﬁcantly. To
better calibrate the output current intensity of the device, we use the
peak-to-peak value of the current for characterization in the
following text.

Evaluation of the biocompatibility and degradation perfor-
mance of the BTENG
Following successful demonstration of therapeutic output capability,
we evaluated the biodegradation and biocompatibility proﬁles essen-
tial for clinical translation of temporary cardiac stimulation devices.
Controlled degradation eliminates the need for surgical device
removal while ensuring complete biocompatible clearance, addressing
a critical limitation of permanent implantable stimulators and redu-
cing long-term patient risks. The packaging layer plays a key role in
maintaining the normal operation of the implantable vagus nerve sti-
mulation device. In an environment containing bodyﬂuids, PCL slowly
peels off due to contact with the body ﬂuids, is hydrolyzed into
monomers, and eventually dissolves. According to reports in the lit-
erature and in vivo implantation results, without external interference,
the PCL packaging layer can ensure normal operation of the device for
4–6 weeks. During this process, the packaging layer will degrade and
eventually rupture. After the packaging layer breaks, body ﬂuids
penetrate the device, and the BTENG quickly loses its function. Before

Device degradation is facilitated by the use of ultrasound as a
driving source, potentially impacting device performance. To miti-
gate the in ﬂuence of ultrasound on the triboelectric layer. We
designed the thicknesses of both the packaging layer and the upper
triboelectric layer of the device. Physical ﬁeld simulations were used
to simulate the wave behavior of the device under ultrasonic con-
ditions. The simulation employed an experimental ultrasonic inten-
sity of 3 W/cm
2 as a parameter and investigated two commonly used
ultrasonic frequencies, 1 MHz and 0.1 MHz, as degradation para-
meters (Supplementary Fig. 4a –d). According to the physical ﬁeld
simulation, the 1 MHz ultrasonic stress was more concentrated on
the lower surface of the packaging layer material. In contrast, at
0.1 MHz, sound pressure and other related factors were more con-
centrated in other areas than in the packaging layer. These results
indicate that the unique material design used in this experiment is
more ef ﬁcient in degrading the device at 1 MHz. Similarly, in an
ultrasonic degradation environment, the device ruptured and started
degrading within 7 days (Supplementary Fig. 7). To assess systemic
handling of degradation products, serum iron and molybdenum
concentrations were measured at multiple timepoints throughout
the degradation period. Both elements remained within normal
physiological ranges with no signi ﬁcant accumulation detected
(Supplementary Fig. 8), con ﬁrming safe metabolic clearance of
degradation products. To validate the safety of ultrasound-triggered
degradation, we performed comprehensive tissue analyses on mice
receiving ultrasound exposure (1 MHz, 3 W/cm²) for 7 days post-
implantation compared to sham controls. Cardiac H&E staining
revealed preserved myocardial architecture with no evidence of
thermal injury or necrosis (Supplementary Fig. 9a). TUNEL staining
showed no signi ﬁcant increase in apoptotic cells in ultrasound-
exposed hearts (Supplementary Fig. 9b). qPCR analysis of cardiac
inﬂammatory markers (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α) demonstrated no elevation
in in ﬂammatory gene expression following ultrasound treatment
(Supplementary Fig. 9c). Additionally, H&E staining of skin at the
implantation site showed preserved normal histological architecture
with no evidence of thermal damage. H&E staining of major organs,
including liver, spleen, lungs, and kidneys, revealed no pathological
changes following ultrasound exposure (Supplementary Fig. 9d).
These ﬁndings con ﬁrm the safety of ultrasound-triggered device
degradation across multiple tissue types.

degrading situation after the device was implanted. In our experi-
ments, the device output was monitored continuously over a 56-day
period. For the ultrasonic degradation groups, ultrasound exposure
was initiated on day 14 and day 28, respectively. Results showed that in
both groups, the device output dropped to nearly undetectable levels

device output during theﬁrst week following ultrasound intervention,

These experimental results furthe r demonstrate the stability of the
device’s output both before and after degradation, as well as the

LL-VNS prevents cardiac remodeling and HF
To evaluate the prophylactic ef ﬁcacy of LL-VNS, treatment was initi-
ated one week after AB surgery, prior to cardiac structural and func-
tional alterations. Transthoracic echocardiography was performed at
2-, 4-, and 8-weeks post-surgery, with cardiac tissue collection con-
ducted in week 8 for endpoint analyses (Fig. 4a).
Cardiac function was assessed via echocardiographic measure-
ments of the left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) and fractional
shortening (LVFS) in weeks 1, 2, 4, and 8 post-surgery (Figs.4b, 4c). No
signiﬁcant intergroup differences in cardiac function were observed in
week 1. In week 2, AB surgery induced signiﬁcant cardiac dysfunction,
with the mean EF decreasing to 51% compared with that of the sham
group (80.9%; p < 0.0001). Device implantation alone had no ther-
apeutic effect (EF: 52%). LL-VNS treatment resulted in a minimal EF
reduction with 20-min and 10-min stimulation protocols (decreasing
from 74.6% to 71% and 73.25% to 73.5%, respectively). While the
implantation-only and untreated groups showed progressive EF
decline, the LL-VNS groups exhibited attenuated deterioration, with
modest improvement in later stages (Figs.4b, c). Notably, an extended
stimulation duration did not confer additional bene ﬁts for cardiac
function. Endpoint M-mode echocardiography (Fig. 4d) con ﬁrmed
that LL-VNS mitigated pressure overload-induced cardiac dysfunction.

Animals and treatment
Male C57BL/6 mice (8 weeks old, 25–27 g) were obtained from Institute
of Laboratory Animal Science, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences
and used to establish a left ventricular pressure overload model
through aortic banding (AB) surgery. Male mice were selected to avoid
potential hormonal variation attr ibuted to estrogen production in
female specimens, ensuring experimental consistency. Animals were
housed under stringent speci ﬁc pathogen-free conditions at Wuhan
University’s Experimental Animal Center, maintaining controlled
temperature (22-24 °C), controlled humidity (40-70%), and a 12-hour
light-dark cycle with unrestricted access to food and water. A mini-
mum 7-day acclimatization period was provided before experimental
procedures.
Sample sizes were determined based on power analysis using
preliminary data from pilot studies. With an expected effect size of
20% difference in LVEF between treatment groups,α =0 . 0 5 ,a n dp o w e r
= 0.80, a minimum of 8 animals per group was calculated, with group
sizes of 8 –12 used to account for potential attrition. Animals were
randomly assigned to experimental groups using computer-generated
randomization sequences, and investigators performing echocardio-
graphic assessments and histological analyses were blinded to group
allocation.
The aortic banding procedure involved precise thoracotomy
under isoﬂurane anesthesia (2–3% in oxygen), followed by transverse
ligation of the ascending aorta using 7-0 silk suture and a 26-gauge
needle as a spacer, which was subsequently removed to create stan-
dardized constriction. Doppler echocardiography veri ﬁed complete
aortic constriction immediately post-surgery. Sham-operated controls
underwent identical surgical procedures without aortic ligation. Post-
operative analgesia was provided using buprenorphine (0.05 mg/kg
subcutaneously) every 12 h for 48 h. Animals were monitored daily for
signs of distress, and prede ﬁned humane endpoints included severe
cardiac decompensation or weight loss exceeding 20% from baseline.
Cardiac functionality was assessed at baseline and at 1-, 2-, 4-, and
8-weeks post-AB through transthoracic echocardiography. Primary
outcomes included left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) and frac-
tional shortening (LVFS), while secondary outcomes encompassed
morphometric indices (heart weight/body weight, heart weight/tibia
length, lung weight/body weight ratios), histological changes, and
molecular markers.

RNA-seq
In our investigation, we conducted a detailed analysis of gene

collected at week 8 post-AB surgery (study endpoint), with n = 3 bio-
