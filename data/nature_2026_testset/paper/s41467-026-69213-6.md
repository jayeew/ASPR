Methods for accurate, rapid blood typing and antibody titration at the point of
care are constrained by cold-chain dependent red blood cell (RBC) reagents,
time-consuming serial dilutions, and subjective visual interpretation. To
overcome these limitations, here we develop a method based on bioengi-
neered red-blood cells. We engineer hemoglobin-depleted RBC membranes
that preserve ABO surface antigens, label them withﬂuorescent nanoparticles,
and lyophilize them, demonstrating at least 2 years of stability at room-
temperature. We capture hemagglutination on a dual-layer paper pad and
quantify it by ﬂuorescence in approximately 8 minutes, eliminating the need
for serial dilution. We conduct a registered clinical trial (ChiCTR2300078864)
and benchmark performance against the gel microcolumn assay. In a clinical
cohort (n = 641), ABO typing achieves 100% concordance. In antibody-titration
samples, quantitative titers agree with the gel microcolumn assay (coincidence
80.75%, κ = 0.8623). In summary, we develop a cold-chain-independent,
handheld paper-based assay that enables simultaneous ABO grouping and
quantitative antibody titration with clinical-grade performance, supporting
scalable manufacturing, cross-site standardization, and transfusion decision-
making in emergency and resource-limited settings.

bodies relative to blood type antigens in a blood sample. This test is
clinically valuable for several reasons: (1) Type O blood lacks both A
and B antigens but contains anti-A and anti-B antibodies, which can
lead to hemolytic transfusion reactions if present at high titers

hematopoietic stem cell (HSC) and organ transplants, monitoring ABO

10. (4) Blood type incompatibility between a

Traditional methods for blood typing and antibody titration pri-
marily rely on tube testing and GMA13. Tube testing, though simple, is
time-consuming and subject to observer variability. GMA uses gel
columns and a centrifuge to assess antibody presence based on RBC
sedimentation, offering greater accuracy but requiring complex
equipment and skilled operators, making it less suitable for time-
sensitive or resource-limited settings, such as emergencies, primary
care, or battle ﬁeld conditions. Paper-based diagnostics (PADs) have
emerged to address portability and speed. Commercial formats
include consumer agglutination cards and professional GLIF-type
tests, which deliver rapid ABO/Rh results but remain qualitative and
restricted to forward typing. Point-of-care (POCT) blood typing has
attracted considerable attention, and a number of paper- and ﬁber-
based platforms have already been reported. Li et al. developed a
paper-based blood typing device that reports the patient’s ABO/RhD
type “in writing,” demonstrating that simple capillary-driven paper
cards can support clear visual readout outside conventional
laboratories
14. Guan et al. later introduced a barcode-like paper sensor
for smartphone-assisted ABO typing, showing the feasibility of cou-
pling paper cards with digital imaging for semi-quantitative
diagnostics
15. On the bioactive paper side, Yeow et al. established a
paper-based assay for red blood cell antigen typing using the indirect
antiglobulin test, thereby extending paper platforms to secondary
blood group phenotyping under IAT conditions
16. Hertaeg et al.
reported a rapid droplet-wicking paper method for ABO blood typing,
further simplifying on-paper handling and reducing assay time 17.
Manderson et al. developed droplet-based paper assays with laser
incubation for blood group antibody screening, and subsequently
showed that laser-assisted paper platforms can accelerate the detec-
tion of red cell alloantibodies in human samples
18,19.S e v e r a lo t h e r
groups have proposed related POCT-oriented formats. Songjaroen
et al. designed a simple, low-cost portable paper-based ABO blood
typing device for point-of-care testing
20. Chomean et al. reported a
paper-based device combined with image-based high-throughput

substrates can support POCT-compatible blood group testing,
including secondary blood group phenotyping, when used with fresh
red blood cell reagents. However, existing platforms generally rely on
short-lived cell reagents with limited ambient stability and are not
designed to provide long-term preserved red cell reagents that
simultaneously support rapid ABO reverse grouping and quantitative
IgM antibody titration.
Several research teams have attempted to develop freezing
methods for rare blood type RBCs
23,24. However, progress has been
limited due to the challenge of preventing ice crystal formation in the
cytoplasm during freezing, which causes RBC membrane rupture
during rehydration. Furthermore, prolonged freezing increases the
likelihood of ice nucleation, which can alter the structure of membrane
proteins structures, reducing their ability to bind to antibodies
25,26.
Thus, preserving the antigenicity of RBCs for antibody testing remains
as i g n iﬁcant challenge.
Antibody titration typically involves serial dilution of serum, fol-
lowed by mixing each dilution with antigen-positive RBCs to determine
the highest dilution at which agglutination occurs4. Despite its use, this
method has limitations: (1) Sensitivity and precision, especially when
the antibody concentration falls between two dilution points; (2)
Resource and time consumption; (3) Cumulative dilution errors.
To address these limitations, we propose a rapid method for
detecting IgM anti-A and anti-B antibodies using bioengineered RBCs
and ﬂuorescent labeling. The key innovation lies in the modi ﬁcation
and freeze-drying of RBCs to create long-lasting RBC membranes that
preserve functional antigens. RBCs are converted into RBC mem-
branes using a gentle punching solution, which removes cellular
components like hemoglobin while preserving the antigenicity. This
process, combined with freeze-drying, allows long-term storage at
room temperature. Fluorescent nano-labeling technology enables
agglutination to be visualized and analyzed quantitatively using a
ﬂuorescence analyzer, eliminating the subjectivity of visual inter-
pretation. We developed a paper-basedﬂuorescent assay (PFA) based
on agglutination principles, in which antibody level correlates with the
size and number of agglutinated clumps. Blood typing and antibody
titration are completed simultaneously in less than 8 min, meeting
point-of-care testing needs.
Following establishment of this method, we conducted a clinical
trial (ChiCTR2300078864) to assess assay performance. The RBC
bioengineering approach is extensible to the storage of rare blood
type antigens and is adaptable for membrane protein preservation.
These innovations reduce operational complexity and equipment
dependency, providing a solution for rapid testing in specialized
environments. More importantly, the ﬂuorescent labeling system
enables precise quanti ﬁcation, presenting a potential breakthrough
for IgG/IgM antibody quantitative detection techniques.

PFA design for BG-Ab testing
Our research focuses on developing a POCT assay for BG-Ab analysis
(Fig. 1). Traditional BG-Ab testing relies heavily on fresh RBC reagents,
requiring cold chain storage. These reagents induce agglutination of
RBCs by speciﬁc BG-Ab. Major blood group antigens such as ABO, Rh,
Kell, and Duffy are inherent components of theRBC membrane, rather
than being adsorbed from plasma onto the RBC surface (e.g., Lewis
system)
27, and storage-related damage can compromise antigenicity28.
RBCs consist of a cell membrane and contents such as hemoglo-
bin and enzymes, but lack nuclei and organelles 29, we hypothesized
that removing the RBC contents while maintaining the membrane
structure would extend the storage life of RBC reagents. To test this,
we use a mild punching solution to release RBC contents and prepare
RBC biomembranes (RBC
bm), preserving the antigenicity of the RBC
membrane surface.
Since removing hemoglobin eliminates color indication on RBCs,
we label the RBCbm with red FNPs using click chemistry, creating nano-
markers to visualize and monitor agglutination. The resulting RBCbm
labeled with FNPs is referred to as RBCbm@Fluo. The design allows for
the separation of RBC-based clots from free ones, making it possible to
directly read the agglutination extent via ﬂuorescent signals. Thus
eliminating subjective interpretation and bypasseing the lengthy
dilution process (Fig. 1a). Furthermore, freeze-drying enhances the
practicality of RBC
bm@Fluo. After freeze-drying, the reagent can be
stored at room temperature for extended periods, making it ideal for
emergency settings, such as ambulances,ﬁeld environments, aircraft,
and ships. It is also suitable for laboratory use. As shown in Fig. 1b,
RBCbm@Fluo works based blood agglutination principles. When a test
sample containing BG-Ab is added, an antigen-antibody reaction

To facilitate on-site BG-Ab testing, we developed a paper-based
ﬂuorescent assay (PFA) that integrates the RBCbm@Fluo (Fig. 1c). The
device, constructed using 3D printing, offers signiﬁcant advantages for
producing cost-effective prototypes. RBCbm@Fluo is freeze-dried onto
the reaction zone’s wells, and unaggregated RBCbm@Fluo is removed
via on-site ﬁltration before result reading. The ﬁltration membrane
consists of two layers: an upper pre-ﬁlter that retains larger aggregates,
while the lower ﬁne ﬁlter membrane that captures smaller aggregates.
Additionally, a location well is included for sample positioning and
calibration, aiding auto-focusing and image correction. As a result, the
concentration of blood group antibodies can be precisely quanti ﬁed
using a portable ﬂuorescence analyzer.
The current focus of the PFA platform is on detecting IgM anti-
bodies against A/B antigens (Fig.1d). Additionally, the platform can be
adapted to detect IgG antibodies by adding anti-human globulin (AHG)
reagents and incubating at 37°C. This expanded application is parti-
cularly useful for diagnosing hemolytic disease of the newborn (HDN),
irregular antibody screening, and direct/indirect Coombs ’ tests,
thereby providing a broader diagnostic toolset for clinical applica-
tions. In future developments, we plan to enhance the PFA ’sp e r f o r -
mance by integrating automation. We aim to integrate a rotating disk
with an automated manipulator, which will increase sample processing
capacity and improve detection ef ﬁciency (Fig. 1e). For instance, a
single disk could accommodate 32 samples, enabling up to 240 results
per hour—more than a 30-fold increase in efﬁciency.

Establishment of BG-Abs analysis method
We developed a paper-based POCT device for on-site BG-Ab analysis
with high accuracy. To optimize the device’s design, we explored three
design: baf ﬂe type, push-pull type, and ﬂip type (Supplementary
Figs. 12 and 13). The ﬂip-type was selected as the optimal choice to
minimize operational errors. The device consists of two distinct zones:
a foldable reaction zone and a ﬁltration zone (Fig. 4a). The front view
(Fig. 4b) shows three reaction wells in the reaction zone and corre-
sponding ﬁltration wells below in the ﬁltration zone. Freeze-dried
RBC
bm@Fluo is uniformly distributed at the bottom of the reaction
wells, where rehydration occurs, and BG-Ab-induced agglutination
takes place.
Once the agglutination process is complete, the operator folds
the reaction zone, allowing the mixture toﬂow into the ﬁltration zone
under gravity. To eliminate background signals from unaggregated
free RBC

Blood type antibody detection is crucial in clinical transfusion, organ
transplantation, and perinatal checks, directly contributing to
advancements in transfusion medicine and hematology

Firstly, long-term ambient preservation of RBC reagents with
stable antigen activity represents a major advantage. A mild RBC
punching solution releases hemoglobin while preserving the integrity
of the RBC

allows ambient storage without extreme cold, unlike fragmented RBC

decay of FNP-based indicators, shelf life could potentially extended
further. This innovation in solid-phase, long-term storage can also be
applied to IgG-sensitized RBCs and rare antigen preservation, offering
a cost-effective alternative to cold-chain techniques.
Secondly, the PFA enables rapid and accurate quanti ﬁcation of
blood type antibodies. Unlike tube test or GMA, which rely on sub-
jective interpretation, the PFA provides an objective readout in
approximately 8 min for simultaneous antibody titer and blood
group determination. Since agglutination-based blood typing relies
only on the visible onset of agglutination rather than full agglutinate
formation, the assay time can be substantially shortened. When
blood group determination is the sole objective, results can be
obtained in under 3 min. Furthermore, through further optimization
of experimental conditions, the detection time may be reduced to
less than 3 min. RBC

Supplementary Table 3), despite a nearly 49% reduction in antigen

original hemoglobin in RBCs or alternative indicators such as gold
nanoparticles and latex particles. FNPs were chosen for their sensi-
tivity, whereas colloidal gold or latex beads remain viable for low-
precision detection.
Another advantage of the PFA is the elimination of labor-intensive
serial dilutions and centrifugation. Direct quanti ﬁes of blood type
antibodies improves efﬁciency and reduces errors. Adjusting testing
conditions, such as dilution factors, further enhanced sensitivity,
making the PFA suitable for precise titer detection in autoimmune
hemolytic anemia and pre-transplant evaluations.

and accuracy. Tube test is time-consuming, procedural, and operator-

ﬂow cytometry

Preparation of RBCbm
RBC suspensions were obtained from Changchun Bioxun Biotechnol-
ogy Co., Ltd. The blood was sourced from healthy adult donors who
underwent screening for ﬁve markers in compliance with national
regulations, including transaminase, hepatitis B, hepatitis C, syphilis,
and HIV. A1 cells contained both A and A1 antigens, B cells contained B
antigens, and O cells contained neither A nor B antigens. The RBC
suspension was centrifuged at 1000 × g for 10 min to separate the
packed red blood cells from the preservative solution. The supernatant
was discarded, leaving behind the packed RBCs, which were then
subjected to extensive washing and lysis steps to isolate the RBC
RBC
bm.T h eR B Cbm were prepared using the following three methods:
(1) Hypotonic lysis method: 1 mL of RBC precipitate was suspended in
10 mL of 0.25× PBS (Wuhan Servicebio Technology Co., Ltd), causing
the cells to swell and rupture, releasing hemoglobin and leaving
behind the RBC membrane. The suspension was then washed 3 –5
times with an equal volume of distilled water to separate the RBCbm.( 2 )
Repeated freeze-thaw method: 1 mL of RBC precipitate was frozen at
−30 °C and then rapidly thawed. This freeze-thaw cycle was repeated
3–5 times, after which the ruptured RBCs were centrifuged. The RBC
bm
were then washed 3 –5 times with 1 mL of distilled water. (3) Mild
punching method: 1 mL of RBC precipitate was mixed with 1 mL of
punching solution. The mixture was centrifuged to rupture the RBCs,
and the RBC
bm were separated by washing 3 times with 1 mL of
distilled water.
Preparation of RBCbm@Fluo
Take 10 μLo f ﬂuorescent nanoparticles (FNPs, MF02, Madenew
Biotechnology, China) (solid content: 5.03%, 200 nm, red ﬂuores-
cence) and disperse them in ultrapure water. Add 200 μL of freshly
prepared 1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl) carbodiimide (EDC,
22981, Thermo Fisher Scienti ﬁc, US) solution (15 mg/mL) to the dis-
persion, and sonicate for 1 min to ensure uniform particle dispersion.
Incubate the solution at room temperature for 30 min to fully acti-
vate the carboxyl groups on the nanoparticle surface. After activa-
tion, add 200 μg of RBC
bm, prepared using the mild punching
method with gentle lysis buffer. Wash with 1 mL of ultrapure water
and centrifuge at 6000 × g for 5 min to remove unbound materials.
Finally, resuspend the engineered RBC biomembrane (RBCbm@Fluo)
in Alsever’s solution (R1016, Solarbio, Beijing, China) and store at 4 °C
for future use.

IgM anti-A and anti-B detection using the PFA
To achieve rapid blood type antibody detection, the following steps
are performed. First, 60 μL of plasma is directly added to the sample
reaction well without dilution for blood type detection. For antibody
titer detection, 10μL of plasma is mixed with 70μL of saline to achieve
a dilution factor of 8. The subsequent steps are identical for both types
of detection. The sample is gently mixed by the operator for 5 min to
ensure uniformity. The reaction zone of the detection card is then
inverted to allow the liquid to ﬂow into the ﬁltration zone, and 150μL
of saline is added to rinse the free RBC
bm@Fluo. Finally, ﬂuorescence
intensity is measured using a portable ﬂuorescence analyzer to
determine both the blood type and antibody titer.
IgM anti-A and anti-B agglutination titers using GMA
All procedures were carried out according to the manufacturer ’s
instructions. A 50 μL aliquot of serially diluted serum and 50 μLo fa
0.8% RBC suspension of either group A or B were added to the gel card
microcolumns (ABO reverse typing card, Bioxun). The card was then
centrifuged, and the titer was deﬁned as the highest dilution showing
1+ agglutination.
