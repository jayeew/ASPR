Crinkler effectors are iconic toxins deployed by pathogens to suppress host
immunity, a function long thought exclusive to pathogenic species. Here, we
uncover a striking evolutionary twist: an ADP-ribosyltransferase-associated
Crinkler-like protein that has repurposed its toxin-derivedactivity to function
as an epigenetic parasite in free-living eukaryotes, challenging the paradigm
that Crinkler effectors are exclusively pathogen-restricted virulence factors.
The ADP-ribosyltransferase-associated Crinkler-like protein retains a func-
tional mono-ADP-ribosyltransferase domain homologous to diphtheria toxin,
yet exhibits degeneration of N-terminal host-targeting domain, severing ties to
canonical pathogenic functions. This activity enables dual subversion of host
mechanisms: the ADP-ribosyltransferase-associated Crinkler-like protein
mono-ADP-ribosylates the EZH2, reducing H3K27me3-mediated silencing and
reactivating its own locus and adjacent transposons to drive replicative
ampliﬁcation; simultaneously, it modiﬁes the immune adaptor TRAF6, sup-
pressing NF-κB and IRF3 pathways to weaken innate immune surveillance and
facilitate sustained propagation withinthe host genome. Notably, this parasitic
strategy is countered by host detoxiﬁcation: TRAF6 ubiquitinates ADP-
ribosyltransferase-associated Crinkler-like protein, targeting it for proteaso-
mal degradation. Together, our ﬁndings reveal how a toxin-derived effector
escaped pathogenic contexts to evolve as a selﬁsh genomic element, lever-
aging toxin-like activity to subvert epigenetic and immune barriers and rede-
ﬁning the evolutionary potential of Crinkler toxins as drivers of genomic

Unexpectedly, recent comparative genomics surveys have

exclusive to pathogenic taxa

fundamental questions about their evolutionary persistence: why do

tions is essential to expanding our understanding of effector evolution
and function beyond classical pathogen-host paradigms.
Effector genes often reside within dynamic genomic contexts
shaped by transposable elements and mobile genetic elements that
facilitate their mobilization and diversi ﬁcation in pathogens

symbiotic interactions, which constrains the spread of virulence-

Here, we report the discovery and functional characterization of a
CRN homologue, ADP-ribosyltransferase-associated CRN-like
(AACRNL), whose homologues are broadly distributed across free-
living eukaryotic lineages. Unlike canonical CRNs, AACRNL have
retained functional toxin-like enzymatic module but exhibit degen-
eration of N-terminal targeting domain, uncoupling them from clas-
sical host translocation. Remarkably, AACRNL co-opts its ART activity
to mono-ADP-ribosylate EZH2, eroding H3K27me3-dependent
repression and enabling the activation and replication of its own
locus and adjacent transposons. Concurrently, AACRNL modi ﬁes
TRAF6 to suppress NF-κB/IRF3 signaling, creating an immunologically
permissive environment for its expansion. This dual strategy reveals
how a virulence-derived effector can be repurposed as an epigenetic
parasite in free-living eukaryotes, challenging conventional views of
CRN biology and illuminating broader principles of genome con ﬂict
and adaptation.
Results
Distribution and degeneration of AACRN(L) in free-living
eukaryotes
The canonical CRN effector consists of an N-terminal domain
responsible for host targeting and a toxin-related C-terminal domain
(Fig. 1a). Based on this modular architecture, we identi ﬁed a distinct
type of CRN effectors, termed AACRN, characterized by an N-terminal
UBL domain and a C-terminal ADP-ribosyltransferase (ART) domain
(Fig. 1b). In addition, we identiﬁed a related variant in which the UBL
domain has undergone extensive degeneration; we refer to this lineage
as AACRN-Like (AACRNL) (Fig. 1b). Although AACRN effectors are
typically associated with pathogenic functions, AACRN(L) homologs
are also present in several free-living eukaryotes. Speci ﬁcally, com-
prehensive search across multiple phyla revealed that the AACRN(L)
family were detected in free-living eukaryotes like Porifera, Cnidaria,
Echinodermata, Chordata, Rotifera, Nematora, and Arthropoda of
animalia (Fig.1c and Supplementary Fig. 1). Notably, the occurrence of
AACRN(L) genes is limited to a minority of taxa and can vary con-
siderably even within the same phylum. For example, within Chordata,
the AACRNL family was identiﬁed exclusively in Actinopteri, a class of
bony ﬁsh, which is also the highest living organism distributed by the
AACRNL family (Fig.1c). Given that the N-terminal domain is critical for
host targeting, we examined its integrity in these free-living organisms.
We observed pronounced degeneration of the N-terminal regions in
AACRN effectors. For instance, the AACRNL homologues from Aci-
penser ruthenus and Porites lobata, the UBL domain has degenerated
and lost the essential RGG motif required for ubiquitin chain formation
(Fig. 1d, e). Overall, these ﬁndings demonstrate that AACRNL homo-
logs persist in the genomes of certain free-living eukaryotes, and their
N-terminal domain responsible for host targeting has also degraded,
which may reﬂect a potential functional shift.

Discussion
Effector proteins have long been viewed as specialized virulence fac-
tors evolved exclusively by pathogenic organisms to suppress host
immunity and facilitate colonization
23–25. Here, we challenge this
paradigm by demonstrating that CRN homologues, AACRNL, persist
across diverse free-living eukaryotic lineages despite the absence of
overt pathogenic lifestyles. This conservation implies that effector
repertoires can be selectively retained when their activities are co-
opted into novel functions, notably the facilitation of sel ﬁsh genetic
element propagation. The pronounced degeneration of their
N-terminal host-targeting domains highlights a clear functional diver-
gence from canonical virulence roles, while the preservation of intact
catalytic modules suggests strong selective pressure to maintain their
biochemical potency.
A central feature of AACRNL is its potent mART activity. Bio-
chemically, M. miiuy AACRNLβ directly modi ﬁes TRAF6, a pivotal
adaptor that integrates NF- κB and IRF3 signalling to mount innate
immune responses
26,27. By targeting TRAF6, AACRNL β effectively
suppresses the activation of immune pathways. This strategy mirrors
the mode of action of diverse bacterial virulence factors —including
diphtheria toxin and Pseudomonas ExoS —which employ ADP-
ribosylation to disable host defence mechanisms
28,29.I nf r e e - l i v i n g
eukaryotes, however, the maintenance of such enzymatic toxicity
likely confers a different selective advantage: dampening immune
surveillance to create an environment permissive for transposon
activation and proliferation. The persistence of catalytic function, even
absent pathogen-associated delivery domains, underscores the evo-
lutionary stability of toxin-like effectors and their potential to remain
disruptive long after decoupling from obligate parasitism. Consistent
with this view, our data show that AACRNL activation during viral
infection induces DNA damage and robustly elevates LINE and ERV
transcripts, supporting the idea that AACRNL behaves as a pathogen-
like element whose catalytic activity enables episodic bursts of trans-
poson mobilization.
Our evolutionary analyses further reveal a dynamic balance
between host-driven degeneration and the element’s intrinsic capacity
for self-propagation. Across vertebrates, multiple AACRNL homologs
exhibit signatures of functional decay: for instance, zebra ﬁsh
AACRNLγ1a n d γ4 retain intact coding sequences but remain tran-
scriptionally silent, and several species (including M. miiuy, A. alosa,
and P. platessa ) carry obviously degenerated AACRNL copies with
disrupted catalytic residues or codon-disrupting mutations. These
patterns suggest strong host-imposed repression—likely via epigenetic
silencing and purifying selection —to mitigate the deleterious con-
sequences of AACRNL activity. Yet this degeneration is counter-
balanced by episodic reactivation that permits the selective
ampliﬁcation of functional AACRNL copies. Publicly available tran-
scriptomic datasets reveal that while all ﬁve zebra ﬁsh AACRNL γ
members remain silent during embryogenesis (likely due to
H3K27me3-mediated repression), three members ( γ2, γ3, γ5) are
rapidly induced upon SVCV infection. Similarly,M. miiuy AACRNLβ is
strongly upregulated following SCRV challenge. Once activated,
AACRNLβ promotes DNA damage and elevates the expression of other
transposon RNAs, providing a transient “permissive window ” for
transposon mobilization. This tension between host restriction and
element-driven propagation provides a mechanistic explanation for
why AACRNL retains intact mART catalytic activity despite widespread
structural decay. The catalytic domain must remain functional to
exploit rare but crucial activation opportunities during infection,
ensuring long-term evolutionary persistence.
Beyond immune suppression, AACRNL exerts a striking impact on
local chromatin states by overcoming PRC2-mediated
H3K27me3 silencing. In eukaryotes, H3K27me3 constitutes a central
layer of epigenetic control that restricts transposable element activa-
tion and preserves genome stability
30,31. Analogous strategies are
observed in oomycete plant pathogens that deploy histone demethy-
lases to derepress effector loci
32 and in herpesviruses that manipulate
Polycomb proteins to reactivate latent viral genomes33.A A C R N L’sA R T
activity appears to subvert this repression through direct modiﬁcation
of chromatin components or regulatory factors, reactivating tran-
scription of its own locus and adjacent transposable elements. The
infection-induced expression data strongly suggest that this epige-
netic derepression is not a constitutive state but an inducible process
triggered by environmental cues —particularly viral infection —that
transiently shift the host chromatin landscape toward a more per-
missive conﬁguration.
One of the most striking implications of this epigenetic subver-
sion is its link to replicative expansion via hAT and mariner elements.
Although traditionally classiﬁed as cut-and-paste transposons
34,h A T
and mariner families can also engage in replicative transposition under
certain conditions
35,36. In this model, staggered cleavage coupled with
incomplete donor-site repair during S-phase yields duplicated copies
ﬂanked by intact TIRs
36. Our genomic analyses revealed precisely such
duplication of AACRNL and adjacent hAT or mariner sequences,
implicating replicative rather than canonical excision-based transpo-
sition. Because AACRNL is embedded within transposable elements, its
mobilization depends on the activation of the corresponding trans-
posase activity. We propose that by relieving H3K27me3-mediated
repression, AACRNL can not only reactivate its own transcription but
also enhance the recruitment and recognition of the necessary trans-
posase machinery to adjacent transposable elements, thereby tightly
coupling local chromatin remodeling to replicative ampliﬁcation. This
synergistic mechanism may explain how AACRNL achieves ef ﬁcient
copy number expansion while minimizing deleterious excision-
associated genetic footprints, thereby markedly enhancing its evolu-
tionary persistence.
An additional layer of complexity arises from the reciprocal
antagonism between AACRNLβ and TRAF6. While AACRNL β mono-
ADP-ribosylates TRAF6 to suppress immune signaling, TRAF6 in turn
polyubiquitinates AACRNLβ, targeting it for proteasomal degradation.
Such dynamic interplay parallels toxin –antitoxin systems, where
counteracting activities maintain equilibrium between persistence and
toxicity. Analogous mechanisms have been described in nematodes,
