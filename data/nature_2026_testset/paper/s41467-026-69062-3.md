Neointimal hyperplasia (NIH) is a major pathological feature of vascular injury,
yet its cellular and molecular drivers remain elusive. Here, using a mouse
model of ligation-induced carotid artery NIH, we identify a population of

The adventitial layer of blood vessels is heavily innervated; how-
ever, the interplay between the peripheral nervous system (PNS) and
immune system in the progression of vascular diseases, including NIH,
remains unclear. In atherosclerosis, increased nerveﬁber density in the
adventitia brings sensory nerve endings into close proximity with
immune cells, establishing a dynamic interplay between the nervous,
immune, and cardiovascular systems
6. For instance, severing sympa-

lizes plaques, while activation of cardiac sympathetic nerves drives
monocyte aggregation and macrophage activation
6,7. Beyond cardio-
vascular systems, neuro-immune interactions are also prominent in
inﬂammatory skin conditions such as psoriasis. Sensory nociceptors
expressing TRPV1 and Nav1.8 engage with dermal dendritic cells (DC)
to stimulate IL-23 production, driving IL-17-mediated inﬂammation8,9.
Moreover, disrupting these neural signals through denervation or
peripheral nerve blockade ameliorates psoriatic inﬂammation in both
humans and rodent models
8.T h e s eﬁndings underscore the relevance
of sensory nerve-driven in ﬂammation in diverse pathologies, raising
questions about their role in NIH.
Tenascin C (TNC), an extracellular matrix glycoprotein, has
emerged as a critical regulator of nerve growth, in ﬂammation, and
ﬁbroblast activity in various pathological contexts. In myocardial
infarction, TNC induces cardiacﬁbroblast differentiation into MFs and
promotes their migration to lesion sites10–12. Similarly,Tnc knockout in
models of aortic endovascular intima hyperplasia reduces proteogly-

insights, the precise cellular sources and mechanisms by which TNC

unclear.

Discussion
The cellular state transition of AFs to MFs, accompanied by neural
abnormalities and neuro-immune interactions, is pivotal in the pro-
gression of NIH
25. However, the intricate interplay and regulatory
mechanisms of these factors within the in ﬂammatory microenviron-
ment of NIH remain poorly understood. Through scRNA-seq analysis,
we identiﬁed a subset of AFs that upregulate Tnc expression during
differentiation into MFs in the setting of vascular NIH. Functional
experiments revealed that TNC secreted by these Tnc
+ MFs exert
paracrine effects, establishing a positive feedback loop that drives
further MF differentiation. Notably, the spatial co-localization ofTnc
+
MFs, CD11c + macrophages and neurites of sensory neurons was
observed in the NIH region using lineage-tracing models coupled with
3D immunoﬂuorescence imaging. Moreover, ﬁbroblast-speciﬁcc o n -
ditional Tnc-knockout and pharmacological sensory neuron inhibition
experiments conﬁrmed that Tnc
+ MF-derived TNC supports aberrant
neurite growth and promotes CCL2 release, which recruits macro-
phages and stimulates the secretion of IL-1β and TGF-β1, accelerating
NIH progression (Supplementary Fig. 7). Ourﬁndings uncover a novel
indirect role of TNC in promoting vascular remodeling by driving
neuro-immune interactions, distinct from its traditionally recognized
effects on ﬁbrosis.
The role of TNC in chronic in ﬂammatory and ﬁbrotic diseases is
well established. It is typically absent in healthy tissues but is upregu-
lated in various cardiovascular conditions, including atherosclerotic
plaque
26, myocardial infarction19, and hypertensive cardiac ﬁbrosis27,
highlighting its potential as a diagnostic marker for related
diseases28,29. Our study adds a new dimension to this understanding by
demonstrating that TNC, released during AF-to-MF transition, not only
facilitates ﬁbrosis but also promotes aberrant sensory nerve growth
and reshapes the immune microenvironment. This was evident from
the reduced MF differentiation and NIH severity observed in Tnc-
knockout ﬁbroblasts and sensory neuron-targeted interventions.
These ﬁndings align with TNC’s known ability to inﬂuence macrophage
polarization and migration
30, but they also reveal a previously unrec-
ognized role in modulating neuron-immune networks. The functional
diversity of TNC is further shaped by alternative splicing. Variants

To resolve cellular composition within these multicellular

smooth muscle cells (SMCs), to generate robust reference signatures.
Based on the quantitative cell abundance estimates derived from
the model, we established a thresholding strategy to de ﬁne spatial
cellular identity. Spots were classi ﬁed as Lineage-Dominant if the
estimated proportion of a single cell type exceeded 70%, with con-

concurrent high abundance for both SMCs (>0.45%) and ﬁbroblasts
(>0.45%) were deﬁned as “Mixed” spots. Deconvolution results were
further cross-validated by mapping the spatial expression of lineage-

Statistical and reproducibility
Each experiment was repeated independently at least 3 times -very
often many more times- with similar results. For in vitro experiments,
the cells were evenly suspended and then randomly distributed into
each well. For in vivo experiments, the animals were randomly dis-
tributed into various treated groups. For immunohistochemistry ana-
lysis of patients’ samples: 1 embolized popliteal artery sample and 1
carotid intimal hyperplasia tissue sample were used; no technical or
biological replicates were undertaken. For immunoﬂuorescence ana-
lysis of patients’ samples: 1 embolized popliteal artery sample, 1 normal
splenic artery and 1 normal abdominal aorta were used; no technical or
biological replicates were undertaken.
No statistical method was used to predetermine sample size.
Required experimental sample sizes were estimated based on previous
established protocols in the ﬁeld. The sample sizes were adequate as
the differences between experimental groups were reproducible.
Data were excluded only when transgenic mice were unavailable or
when tissue sections were damaged during processing. Animals
were randomly assigned to treatment groups. The investigators were
not blind to group allocation. All n values are clearly indicated within
the ﬁgure legends. All data were given as means ± standard deviation
(SD). The data were statistically analysed with two-tailed, paired/
unpaired Student’s t-test and a one-way or two-way ANOVA with post
hoc Tukey’s test (SigmaStat, San Jose, CA). GraphPad Prism 10 was
used for statistical analysis and data plotting. Signi ﬁcance was set
at P < 0.05.
