To accomplish the intense desire of high-strength materials for enhanced energy-ef ﬁciency, recent
research applies a combined strategy of additive-manufacturing and precipitation-strengthening in
high entropy alloys. In a context, Al
0.2Co1.5CrFeNi1.5Ti0.3 nanoprecipitation-strengthened system was
developed, demonstrating very convincing strength and toughness. Moreover, additive-
manufacturing facilitated additional strength by well-decorated cell-boundaries with blocky L2
1
precipitates and homogeneously distributed L1 2 precipitates. However, fatigue research of this alloy
remained unexplored despite being the main precursor for structural applications. In this study low-
cycle fatigue behavior of this alloy in both as-built and precipitation-strengthened (aged) conditions
has been explored, combined with in-situ neutron diffraction investigation. Findings revealed a
substantial cyclic-stress proﬁle and a notable fatigue-life below ±0.50% strain-amplitude, exceeding
10
5 cycles at ±0.30% strain-amplitude. These demonstrate the potential to carry higher payloads with
marked engineering-reliability. Residual-stress estimation revealed strain-compatibility between the
matrix and L12 precipitate, indicating a crack-initiation immune interface. A comparative examination
of dislocation character revealed shifting towards pure edge-character in aged alloy indicates
precipitates promoted planar-slip during deformation.
The development and discovery of materials with high mechanical per-
formance have always been in demand for energy efﬁciency. Great fatigue-
resistant materials are the utmost requirement for economics, engineering
reliability, and safety for structural applications. Comprising a higher fatigue
stress proﬁle and enhanced fatigue life is worthwhile to accomplish higher
payload and durability underﬂuctuating loads. Enhancing fatigue strength
is also critical for achieving higher payload and avoiding failures caused by
undesirable load ﬂuctuations. The proposed idea of the multi-principal
element alloys (MPEAs) design and high-entropy effect on phase stability
1,2
has revolutionized the exploration of the central region of multi-component
phase diagrams, offering great opportunities for the discovery of new and
exciting materials. Miracle et al.3 critically reviewed previous studies and
revisited the de ﬁnition, thermodynamic aspects, and phase-formation
trends in high-entropy alloys (HEAs). They proposed a taxonomy for the
HEAs, highlighted the vastness and opportunities, outlined the efforts made
towards their mechanical and functional applications, and provided broad
prospects and future possibilities. The research excitement of these alloys is
deeply rooted in their outstanding mechanical attributes, having UTS 1.0 to
1.5 GPa and phase stability in the cryogenic to high-temperature range
3–6.

restricted by the phase-formation complexity and limited to a certain

Discussion
The comparative observation of exper imentally recorded fatigue-stress
proﬁle shows a signi ﬁcant strengthening contribution by the dual-
precipitates while maintaining a rema rkable fatigue life at lower strain-
amplitudes compared to various structural alloys. The fatigue life of the as-
built alloy is dominant above the 0.50% strain, while the fatigue life of the
aged alloy dominates below 0.50%. T he observed dominating fatigue
property of aged alloy at lower strain amplitudes is associated with the
planar-slip mode of deformation, as observed through SEM and TEM of the
fatigued sample. Planar-slip is generally reported to be associated with a
lower dislocation recovery rate and good deformation reversibility
54,55 and
enhances fatigue strength and life. Although it has a planar-slip mode of
deformation at higher strain amplitudes, several other factors, such as
precipitate shearing, inherent AM defects, and microcracking at the L2
1
interface (Supplementary Fig. S4) due to strain-localization, may shorten the
fatigue life.
The stress-proﬁle pattern of the as-built alloy does not have much
variation with applied strain-amplitude and shows similar hardening-
softening at all strain amplitudes except at a 0.30% strain amplitude. The
initial hardening is due to the dislocation generation during initial
cycles, and softening is due to the dislocation rearrangement and
annihilation during later cycles, as re ﬂected in the dislocation density
proﬁle and the dislocation arrangement M parameter estimated through
an in situ neutron-diffraction experiment. The decreased fatigue-life and
early hardening-softening transition with increasing strain-amplitude
are related to the accelerated crack-propagation because of the higher
applied strain-energy dissipation per cycle
68. The increased striation
spacing with increased strain-amplitude presented in Supplementary
Fig. S3 also re ﬂects the higher crack-propagation rate. Quantitative
estimation reveals that crack-initiation consumes a substantial amount
of fatigue cycles. Hence, it can be inferred that the uniform-deformation
process governs the substantial portion of fatigue-life while strain-
localization and crack-initiation occur at a later stage of fatigue defor-
mation. The lateral surface morphology indicates that plastic defor-
mation is accommodated by wavy slip, resulting in the formation of
persistent slip bands (PSBs) at the surface, which usually serve as sites
for surface-crack initiation. Dislocation accumulation at the initial cell
boundary observed through TEM shows that these initial dislocation
cells act as soft barriers to dislocation motion, which does not fully stop
the plastic-deformation governing dislocation partials but traps them
temporarily
69. Hence, it provides strengthening, as well as avoiding
localized stress-concentration for crack-initiation. Dislocation motion
can also be impeded by the coherent internal stress due to the lattice-

In the aged alloy, hardening-softening behavior is dependent on
strain-amplitude. There is almost no cyclic hardening during the initial
cycles at lower strain-amplitudes while hardening is much more domi-
nant with increasing strain amplitudes. The observed hardening behavior
is consistent with the reported shifting of single to multiple slip activation
and increased density of slip-band formation

Lomer-Cottrell locks, as observed through TEM. Twinning at higher
strain amplitudes also acts as barriers to dislocation movement and
provides hardening. The increasing softening rate with the increasing
strain-amplitude is also observed in the stress proﬁle. Besides dislocation
re-arrangement and annihilation, several other mechanisms are also
proposed to describe the softening phenomena in precipitation-
strengthened materials. These mechanisms involve inhomogeneous
plastic-deformation caused by strain-localization, as well as precipitate-
dissolution and precipitate-shearing
46,74,75. At higher strain amplitudes,
precipitation shearing was observed, which provides an easy glide path
for dislocations, resulting in a comparatively higher softening rate. The
dislocation rearrangement and dislocation annihilation at microcracks
observed near L2

The planar-slip mode of deformation is generally associated with
enhanced deformation reversibility and strain uniformity. Hence, the
value of damage-defusing capability (higher β) increased. However, due
to twinning at higher strain amplitude, the strain-localization is higher
and consequently, more susceptible to crack-initiation.
Overall, the plastic deformation in the as-built alloy appears to be
dominated by wavy glides of dislocati on. Due to slip irreversibility asso-
ciated with wavy glide, conspicuous intrusion and extrusion were formed at
the surface during deformation. Those intrusion-extrusions at the surface
lead to crack-initiation with the subsequent cyclic deformation. The plastic
deformation of the aged alloy appearsto be dominated by planar glides of
dislocation. At lower strain-amplitudes, dislocation activity is mostly
restricted to the fcc matrix and piles up at the precipitate boundaries. With
the increasing strain-amplitude, multiple-slip systems are activated and
shear precipitates. At higher strain-amplitudes, twinning phenomena are
also activated, which contribute to hardening, as well as act as strain-
localized regions initiating microcracks. Strain-localization at the matrix-
L2
1 interface also acts as a crack-initiation site, as several microcracks were
observed at the interface, depicted in Supplementary Fig. S4. The disloca-

Material and experimental procedure
Methods
Sample fabrication . The speci ﬁed composition of the
Al0.2Co1.5CrFeNi1.5Ti0.3 alloy has been printed layer by layer using a
L-PBF technique employing a zig –zag scanning strategy with successive
rotation of 67 °. The laser-process parameters included a laser power of
260 W, spot size of 58 μm, hatch spacing of 70 μm, and scanning speed
of 1000 mm/s applied on a 50 μm powder layer in an argon atmosphere.
The speci ﬁed parameter is optimized to obtain the highest density,
higher than 97%, as speci ﬁed in the earlier published literature
26.
The specimens were built on a carbon steel plate preheated to 200 °C
and removed using electrical discharge machining for subsequent
investigations. The part of the L-PBF as-built sample was directly aged
for 50 h at 750 °C to obtain the optimum precipitation, as speci ﬁed by
ref. 26.
Mechanical testing and microstructural investigation . For LCF test-
ing, specimens with speci ﬁed dimensions as shown in Fig. 9, were pre-
pared from both the L-PBF as-built and aged blocks. Completely reversed
tension-compression LCF tests were conducted at 0.30%, 0.50%, 0.75%,
1.00%, and 1.25% strain amplitudes with a triangular loading waveform
under a continuous-loading condition. A materials test system (MTS)
extensometer was used to measure all the strains. The pre-deformation
and post-deformation microstructural features, distribution of
precipitates, elemental-distribution mapping, and fatigued fracture sur-
face were characterized using scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and
transmission electron microscopy (TEM). For SEM, samples were
ground, polished, and then electrochemically etched in 20 vol% phos-
phoric acid at 5 V for 15 s using a copper electrode. Microstructural
features were quantitatively measured using the ImageJ software.
In situ neutron-diffraction measurement . To investigate the detailed
mechanistic insights during deformation, we conducted in situ neutron-
diffraction measurements subjected to fully reversed strain cycling at
room temperature, using an MTS load frame with an attached ther-
mocouple in the VULCAN Engineering material diffractometer at the
Spallation Neutron Source (SNS), Oak Ridge National Laboratory
(ORNL), as shown in Fig. 10a. Diffraction data are collected for inter-
mittent fatigue cycles ( N)( N = 1, 4, 8, 12, 30, 50, 100, 250, 500, and so
on) to trace the different deformation stages of fatigue. Diffraction data
are recorded at seven different points of the hysteresis loop, as men-
tioned in Fig. 10c. Among the multiple detector banks, two detector
banks at ±90 ˚, simultaneously record a complete diffraction vector
pattern from the grain parallel (loading direction) and perpendicular
(transverse direction) to the applied load, respectively, as illustrated in
Fig. 10a, b. The details of the experimental setup can be found in the
reference (2019 instrument paper, MRS Bulletin
78). The time-of- ﬂight
diffraction was acquired with a count time of about 6 min to achieve
sufﬁcient scattering intensity for analyzing the diffraction pro ﬁles. The
collected data were reduced and analyzed through the VULCAN data
