The 2025 Myanmar earthquake caused heavy casualties and economic losses in Myanmar and
surrounding regions. The mechanism of the unexpectedly long rupture and the effect of sediments on
its rupture dynamics remain unclear. Here we integrate seismic and geodetic observations with
kinematic inversions and dynamic rupture simulations, revealing that the rupture extended ~450 km
with a maximum slip of 6.9 m concentrated within the top 10 km of the upper crust. The rupture
propagated ~70 km northward (subshear to supershear) and ~380 km southward (subshear to
supershear and back to subshear). The long southward-propagating supershear rupture created a
highly energetic front that resulted in an unexpectedly long surface rupture. Moreover, we ﬁnd the
coexistence of shallow subshear and deep supershear around station NPW due to the in ﬂuence of
sediments. Our results highlight the importance of combining data-driven inversions and physics-
based modeling to decipher the rupture dynamics of large earthquakes.
The moment magnitude (Mw) 7.7 Myanmar earthquake occurred on 28
March 2025 and caused extensive destruction and thousands of casualties in
Myanmar and neighboring Thailand1. It is the largest earthquake recorded
in Myanmar over the past one and a half centuries2,3. This event produced a
maximum surface displacement of ~ 6 m, generated surface ruptures
e x t e n d i n g~ 4 5 0k m( F i g .1), and is one of the longest continental strike-slip
earthquakes ever recorded. Notably, the surface rupture traversed through
two major cities (Mandalay and Nay Pyi Taw) in Myanmar (Fig. 1A),
causing substantial damage
4. Furthermore, strong ground motions induced
severe shaking that was felt as far as Bangkok5, located ~1000 km from the
epicenter.
This event occurred in the central-southern segment of the ~1200-km-
long Sagaing fault (Fig. 1A), which extends across Myanmar in a north-
south direction and serves as a principal plate boundary to accommodate a
right-lateral strike-slip movement6 of ~2 cm yr−1 due to the oblique con-
vergence of the Indian and Sunda Plates7 of ~3.5 cm yr−1. About 60% of the
total strain is accommodated at the India-Sunda plate boundary. Since 1929,
several earthquakes with a magnitudegreater than 7.0 have occurred along
the Sagaing fault8 (Fig. 1), with the 2025 Mw 7.7 Myanmar event being the
largest. As expected, the 2025 Myanmar earthquake nucleated within the
seismic gap between Mandalay and Nay Pyi Taw (Fig. 1B), rupturing the
~260-km-long seismic gap
8,9. The rupture propagated bilaterally into
adjacent segments of the seismic gap (Fig. 1B), reactivating the northern
(1956 Mw 7.1) and southern (1929 M 7 and 1930 Mw 7.3) historical rupture
zones
8. Four segments of the Sagaing fault, namely the Sagaing segment, the
Meiktila segment, the Nay Pyi Taw segment, and the Pyu segment (Fig.1B),
were ruptured during this mainshock, with a total rupture length that is
much longer than the seismic gap de ﬁned in Hurukawa and Maung
Maung
8, posing a challenge for evaluating the maximum magnitude of the
earthquake in the region. The physical mechanisms controlling whether an
earthquake ruptures a single segment or multiple segments of the fault
remain unclear. Moreover, previous studies using ambient noise, receiver
functions, and surface wave imaging show that the rupture segments of this
earthquake involve relatively thick sedimentary layers
10,11 (~3–5k m t h i c k
around the fault). Previous studies have revealed that sediments sub-
stantially inﬂuence earthquake rupture dynamics 12 and strong ground
motions13. A detailed examination of how subsurface material properties
(e.g., sediment covers) affect the rupture processes of the Mw 7.7 mainshock
is needed.

earthquake, integrating kinematic inversion and dynamic rupture simula-

Supershear at the camera site
One remarkable feature of the 2025 Mw 7.7 Myanmar earthquake is that it
generated supershear rupture during its southward propagation (Fig.3D). A
publicly available Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV) camera (~124 km
south of the USGS epicenter, Fig.1A) located on the coseismic rupture zone
within 20 m (Fig. 4A, B), captured the entire surface process when the
dynamic rupture passed through it. Several studies have measured the time
series from the only available video recording
15,45–47. However, most of
them utilized the 720p video that was uploaded online, and their results
were not consistent with each other. For example, Hirano et al. argued that
the Myanmar mainshock started propagating southward as a supershear
rupture, temporarily slowed down near the camera location, and then
reverted to a supershear rupture farther south
47. Nevertheless, their result
was primarily based on the initial hypocenter from the USGS near 22°N.
Several recent studies, such as Peng et al.
3 and Wei et al.19, have shown that
the initial hypocenter of the M7.7 mainshock was likely 20 –40 km
farther south, which would argue mo re for a predominantly subshear
rupture before the camera site. Here we utilize this footage with the
original high-deﬁnition (1080p) video to reveal additional details of rupture
dynamics. We select representative point targets distributed on the ﬁrst
frame of the video (Fig. 4B) and track them on subsequent frames using
the cross-correlation method. The pixel offset history of these point
targets shows distinct ground motion time series, and the consistency of the
motion waveforms across all poin ts supports the robustness of our
results (Fig. 4C).

Another notable characteristic of thisevent is that the surface rupture length
is unexpectedly long, possibly over 450 km18,19,30,32,33. Historically, the strike-

120 years, there have been only a few cases worldwide: (1) the 1906 Mw
7.7–7.8 San Francisco earthquake, which produced a surface rupture
exceeding 300 km

in a surface rupture of ~426 km51; (3) the 2002 Mw 7.8 Denali earthquake,
which generated ~341 km of surface ruptures52; and (4) the 2023 Mw 7.8
Kahramanmaraş earthquake, which produced surface ruptures of
~380 km53. Furthermore, the empirical relationship54 between the surface

Conclusions
In this study, we adopt the geodetic inversion and physics-based dynamic
rupture simulations to investigate the kinematic and dynamic properties of
the 2025 Mw 7.7 Myanmar earthquake. Our results show the duration of
this event is ~100 s, and the rupture extended ~70 km to the north from
subshear (~3 km s
−1) to supershear (~5.5 km s−1), which may be halted by
the low-stress zone where the 2012 Mw 6.8 earthquake occurred. Mean-
while, it propagated ~380 km to the south, transitioning from subshear
(~3 km s
−1) to supershear (~5.5 km s −1) and then back to subshear
(~3 km s−1), and the rupture decelerated and ultimately was terminated by
the southernmost fault bend and the relatively dif ﬁcult-to-rupture zone
where the 1929 M 7 event and the 1930 Mw 7.3 earthquake ruptured.
Moreover, our analysis reveals that sediments around the NPW station
greatly inﬂuence the rupture dynamics: the co-existence of shallow subshear
and deep supershear, mitigating the destructiveness of strong shaking
generated by the deep supershear rupture. It is worth noting that our
model can only capture the ﬁrst-order rupture characteristics of the
mainshock due to limited available data. Future studies could consider more
complex scenarios, such as the effects of historical strong earthquakes,
variations ofﬂuid pressures, and the heterogeneity of frictional parameters
on the fault plane.

Geodetic data processing
We collect multi-track Sentinel-1 synthetic aperture radar (SAR) images

Numerical method
We conduct dynamic rupture simulations and perform a broader range of
strong ground motion modeling separately. Weﬁrst conduct the dynamic
rupture simulation to calculate the spontaneous rupture process. We then
simulate the strong ground motions using the preferred dynamic model to

curved-grid ﬁnite difference method (CG-FDM) to simulate the dynamic
rupture process

area. The split-node technique imp lements fault boundary conditions,

faults65. The CG-FDM stands out for its computational ef ﬁciency and its
