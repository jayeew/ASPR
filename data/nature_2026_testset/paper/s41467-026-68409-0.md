Results
Patient disposition and baseline characteristics
Between November 3, 2021, and November 15, 2022, 161 patients were
screened, and 117 were enrolled and randomized: 78 to the NALIRIFOX
group and 39 to the gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel group (Fig.1). At
the data cutoff (June 30, 2024), three patients in the NALIRIFOX group
and none in the gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel group remained on
treatment.
Baseline demographic and disease characteristics were generally
well-balanced between treatment groups (Table 1). The majority of
patients had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) perfor-
mance status (PS) of 1, stage IV disease, and liver metastases.
Efﬁcacy
At the data cutoff (June 30, 2024), the median follow-up time was 18.7
months (interquartile range [IQR], 7.5 –22.1) for NALIRIFOX and
12.1 months (IQR: 6.4–14.8) for the gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel. 53
patients (67.9%) in the NALIRIFOX group and 30 (76.9%) in the gem-
citabine plus nab-paclitaxel group had experienced disease progres-
sion or death. Median progression-free survival (PFS) was signiﬁcantly
longer in the NALIRIFOX group (7.6 months; 95% con ﬁdence interval
[CI], 5.52 –9.23) compared to the gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel
group (3.7 months; 95% CI, 3.38–5.32), with a hazard ratio (HR) of 0.56
(95% CI, 0.35 –0.88; nominal P = 0.0115) (Fig. 2A). PFS bene ﬁtw a s
consistently observed across prespeci ﬁed subgroups (Fig. 3A),
including those with liver metastasis, ECOG PS of 1, female sex, and
stage IV disease. A numerically greater PFS beneﬁt was seen in patients
with baseline CA19-9 levels <37 U/mL.
At the time of analysis, 60 patients (76.9%) in the NALIRIFOX
group and 32 (82.1%) in the gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel group had
died. Median overall survival (OS) was 12.9 months (95% CI,
8.84–16.43) in the NALIRIFOX group versus 8.9 months (95% CI,
6.90–12.62) in the gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel group (HR, 0.72;
95% CI, 0.47 –1.11; nominal P = 0.1371) (Fig. 2B). OS trends favored
NALIRIFOX across most subgroups (Fig.3B), with particular beneﬁti n
patients with liver metastases, ≥3 visceral metastases, and stage IV
disease at baseline. A numerically greater OS beneﬁtw a sa l s oo b s e r v e d
in patients with CA19-9 < 37 U/mL.
The conﬁrmed objective response rate (ORR) was higher in the
NALIRIFOX group than in the gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel group
(32.1% vs. 20.5%; between-group difference, 11.5%; 95% CI, 6.91 to 27.17)
(Table 2). The median time to response (TTR) was 3.7 months in both
groups. duration of response (DoR) was notably longer with NALIR-
IFOX (8.5 months vs. 3.8 months, P = 0.0234) (Fig. 2C). Overall, 37
(47.4%) of 78 patients in the NALIRIFOX group and 21 (53.8%) of 39
patients in the gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel received subsequent
anticancer therapy (Supplemental Table 2).
Both groups received a median of 4.0 treatment cycles. The
median treatment duration was 4.71 months with NALIRIFOX and
3.45 months with gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel. The mean relative
dose intensities were comparable between the two regimens: liposo-
mal irinotecan (81.3% ± 14.8%), 5-FU (76.5% ± 14.6%), leucovorin
(82.8% ± 14.1%), and oxaliplatin (80.9% ± 15.0%) in the NALIRIFOX
group, and gemcitabine (82.8% ± 21.2%) and nab-paclitaxel
(82.9% ± 21.4%) in the gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel group.
Safety

thrombocytopenia (66.7% vs. 69.2%), nausea (73.1% vs. 48.7%), and
fatigue (65.4% vs. 51.3%).

Discussion
FOLFIRINOX and gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel are both recom-
mended as ﬁrst-line therapy for metastatic pancreatic
adenocarcinoma12. While some retrospective and real-world
studies11,13,14 suggest that FOLFIRINOX may be superior or equivalent
to gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel, a prospective head-to-head com-
parison trial— JCOG1611, GENERATE
15— demonstrated signiﬁcantly bet-
ter ef ﬁcacy for gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel. Nonetheless, the
clinical utility of FOLFIRINOX is often limited by its considerable
toxicity proﬁle, including grade 3–4 vomiting and diarrhea in 14.5% and
12.7% of patients, respectively4. Consequently, gemcitabine plus nab-
paclitaxel remains a commonly used regimen in clinical practice, par-
ticularly among patients less suited for intensive combination che-
motherapy. In real-world settings, the administration schedule of
gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel is frequently modi ﬁed— from three
times in four weeks to twice in four weeks— to improve tolerability
16,17.
Therefore, there is an urgent need for alternative regimens that offer
enhanced efﬁcacy with an improved safety proﬁle.
Supported by the global phase III NAPOLI-3 trial, NALIRIFOX
(liposomal irinotecan plus 5-FU, leucovorin, and oxaliplatin) demon-
strated superior OS over gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel and subse-
quently received US FDA approval in February 2024 for the ﬁrst-line
treatment of metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma
10. It has since
been incorporated into the 2024 CSCO guidelines as a Category 1 A
Level II regimen and designated as a preferred Category 1 option for
metastatic disease in the NCCN 2025 guidelines
12.
In this randomized phase II study, NALIRIFOX yielded clinically
meaningful improvements in PFS compared with gemcitabine plus
nab-paclitaxel in Chinese patients with metastatic pancreatic ductal
adenocarcinoma, with consistent bene ﬁt observed across all pre-
speciﬁed subgroups. These ﬁndings are congruent with those of the
NAPOLI-3 trial, which primarily enrolled patients of non-Asian
descent
10. The underrepresentation of Asian patients in NAPOLI-3 —
only 5% of the total population— has limited the generalizability of its
ﬁndings to Asian cohorts and is a primary reason why NALIRIFOX has
remained a Level II recommendation in Chinese treatment guidelines.
The present study directly addresses this gap, providing high-quality
evidence from a Chinese population and supporting an upgrade in the
recommendation level of NALIRIFOX in national guidelines.
The median PFS in our study for NALIRIFOX and gemcitabine plus
nab-paclitaxel (7.6 and 3.7 months, respectively) was comparable to
that reported in NAPOLI-3 (7.4 and 5.6 months, respectively)

results (11.1 vs. 9.2 months). Notably, our population had a higher
proportion of patients with ECOG PS 1— 83% versus 57% in NAPOLI-3—

Delayed diarrhea is a known adverse effect of irinotecan and
typically occurs ≥24 h after administration. When irinotecan is com-
bined with 5-FU/ leucovorin, the incidence of delayed diarrhea can
reach 70%–80%. In our study, 55.1% of patients experienced delayed
diarrhea, with a notably lower incidence among patients aged 65 years
or older (17.9%), suggesting that NALIRIFOX may be more tolerable in
older individuals.

COVID-19 pandemic, which adversely affected both patient recruit-
ment and protocol adherence. Consequently, the statistical power of
our analyses was reduced, and the results should therefore be inter-
preted with appropriate caution. Additionally, no stratiﬁcation factors
were indicated in this study due to the limited sample size. However,
based on baseline characteristics, the incidence of liver metastases was
similar between the NALIRIFOX and gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel
groups. In addition, the proportion of subjects with an ECOG score of 1
was slightly higher in the NALIRIFOX group compared to the gemci-
tabine plus nab-paclitaxel group. Despite this imbalance, the NALIR-
IFOX group still demonstrated signiﬁcantly superior efﬁcacy over the
gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel group. Moreover, the 2:1 randomiza-
tion ratio resulted in a relatively smaller control group, which may have
limited the statistical power for between-group comparisons. While
the use of a two-sided alpha level of 0.10 exceeds the conventional
threshold, it is appropriate for proof-of-concept research aimed
at evaluating the ef ﬁcacy and safety of NALIRIFOX versus
gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel in Chinese pancreatic cancer patients.
Furthermore, the absence of BRCA status information means that
whether the ﬁndings of this study can be applied to BRCA-mutated
subjects needs to be further validated. Patient enrollment in this study
was restricted to those aged <70 years, unlike the NAPOLI 3 trial, which
had no upper age limit. Therefore, the generalizability of ourﬁndings
to older populations warrants further investigation. Despite these
limitations, the observed efﬁcacy and safety outcomes were consistent
with those reported in the global NAPOLI-3 trial, reinforcing the
robustness of our ﬁndings.
In conclusion, this randomized phase II study demonstrated that
NALIRIFOX provides meaningful clinical beneﬁt and acceptable toler-
ability as a ﬁrst-line treatment for Chinese patients with metastatic
unresectable pancreatic adenocarcinoma. These ﬁndings support its
adoption as a preferred therapeutic option and provide critical data to
inform regional treatment guidelines.

reduced or de ﬁcient dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPYD)

Endpoints and measurements
The primary endpoint was PFS, which was de ﬁned as the time from
randomization to the earliest occurrence of either radiologically
documented tumor progression (per RECIST 1.1) or death from any
cause. If a patient had neither disease progression nor death by the
data cutoff date or before initiating any subsequent anti-tumor
therapy, PFS would be censored at the date of the last tumor
assessment prior to the data-cutoff date or prior to the start of the
subsequent anti-tumor therapy, whichever occurred ﬁrst. If a patient
experienced progression or death after missing two or more con-
secutive tumor assessments, PFS would be censored at the date of
the last valid radiological tumor assessment prior to the missed
assessments. Secondary ef ﬁcacy endpoints included OS (de ﬁned as
the time from the start of the study drug to death from any cause),
ORR (de ﬁned as the proportion of subjects with a best overall
response of complete response or partial response), disease control
rate (DCR, de ﬁned as the proportion of subjects with a best overall
response of complete response, partial response, and stable disease),
and DoR (de ﬁned as the time from the start of the ﬁrst tumor
assessment of complete response or partial response to the ﬁrst
assessment of disease progression or death from any cause). Tumor
response was evaluated using RECIST v1.1 criteria. Additional sec-
ondary endpoints included safety, pharmacokinetic parameters, and
evaluation of the relationship between UGT1A1*6 and UGT1A1*28
polymorphisms and safety. Adverse events (AEs) were classi ﬁed
using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA)
version 25.1 and graded according to the Common Terminology
Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.
Exploratory analyses aimed to investigate correlations between
UGT1A1 genotypes and the incidence of grade ≥3 diarrhea and neu-
tropenia in patients receiving liposomal irinotecan therapy, as well as
the association of efﬁcacy with CA19-9 levels.

This trial was designed as a randomized, open-label phase II study to
evaluate the PFS bene ﬁt of NALIRIFOX compared with gemcitabine
plus nab-paclitaxel in Chinese patients with advanced pancreatic
adenocarcinoma. Based on prior data from the MPACT trial and real-
world studies, the median PFS for the gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel
regimen was assumed to be approximately 5 months

of 0.63— corresponding to a median PFS improvement from 5 months
to 7.9 months— with 80% power and a two-sided alpha of 0.10, a total of
125 PFS events were required. To account for patient attrition and
ensure a sufﬁcient number of PFS events, the total planned sample size

Time-to-event endpoints, including PFS, OS, and DoR, were ana-
lyzed using the Kaplan–Meier method. The 95% CIs for median time-to-

estimated with Cox proportional hazards models. The ORR and dis-
ease control rate (DCR) were estimated using the Clopper –Pearson
exact method.
All efﬁcacy analyses were performed in the intention-to-treat (ITT)
population, deﬁned as all randomized patients, regardless of whether
they received study treatment. Safety analyses were conducted in all
patients who received at least one dose of study medication. Phar-
macokinetic and pharmacogenomic analyses were conducted in all
patients who received liposomal irinotecan and had valid plasma
samples or UGT1A1 genotype data, respectively.
Patients who were alive without d ocumented progression at the

graphic assessment. Similarly, patients lost to follow-up were censored
at the date of last contact. Missing data for binary endpoints (e.g.,
response status) were not imputed; patients without post-baseline
tumor assessments were considered non-responders in the response
analyses. Sensitivity analyses were conducted to assess the robustness
of primary endpoint results under various censoring scenarios.
