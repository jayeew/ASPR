Antibiotic (AB) resistance often arises through mutations that alter AB targets, reduce membrane
permeability, or increase ef ﬂux activity, yet its physiological costs remain poorly understood. In
Caulobacter vibrioides,a ΔtipR mutant that constitutively upregulates the RND ef ﬂux pump
AcrAB2NodT exhibits heightened sensitivity to copper (Cu), revealing a trade-off linked to excessive
pump expression. Deleting acrAB2nodT or impairing its transport activity restored cell envelope
integrity and, ultimately, Cu resistance, uncoupling the effects of pump abundance from active ef ﬂux.
The ΔtipR mutant also showed increased sensitivity to other metals, including zinc, nickel, and
cadmium, indicating a broader vulnerability to metal stress. Mechanistically, pump overexpression
depleted the proton motive force and lowered ATP levels, key processes for metal resistance. These
ﬁndings uncover the physiological cost of ef ﬂux upregulation and suggest a potential therapeutic
vulnerability: targeting the metabolic burden of resistance mechanisms could improve antibiotic
efﬁcacy by exploiting ﬁtness trade-offs.
Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) represents an urgent and growing global
health threat, with projections estimating up to 8.22 million AMR-
associated deaths annually by 2050
1. Although AMR is well-studied in
clinical settings, environmental contamination is now recognized as a major
driver of the spread and evolution of AMR. Human activities, including
insufﬁciently treated wastewater discharge, the extensive use of AB in
agriculture, and various industrial practices, have led to the accumulation of
AB in natural habitats
2. Moreover, environmental bacteria are frequently
exposed to other pollutants, such as heavy metals, organic toxins, and
industrial chemicals. This chronic exposure exerts selective pressure that
promotes the development and di ssemination of diverse resistance
mechanisms
3. Among these, resistance-nodulation-division (RND) efﬂux
pumps have emerged as key elements o f bacterial defense against toxic
compounds4. RND systems, prevalent in Gram-negative bacteria, are tri-
partite complexes spanning the inner and outer membranes and are pow-
ered by the proton motive force (PMF). These complexes consist of an inner
membrane RND transporter, a periplasmic membrane fusion protein, and
an outer membrane factor, functioning together to export a broad range of
harmful substrates out of the cell
5.
RND efﬂux pumps are generally classiﬁed into two major subfamilies
based on substrate speciﬁcity: the hydrophobic and amphiphilic efﬂux RND
(HAE-RND) pumps, which primarily expel AB and detergents, and the
heavy metal efﬂux RND (HME-RND) pumps, which mediate metal ion
resistance
6.I nt h em o d e lo r g a n i s mEscherichia coli, the HAE-RND pump
AcrAB-TolC and the HME-RND pump CusCFBA have been extensively
characterized for their respective roles in AB and heavy metal detoxiﬁcation,
respectively
7,8. These systems are tightly reg ulated at the transcriptional
level, typically via local repressorssuch as AcrR for AcrAB-TolC and CusR
for CusCFBA7,8. Notably, clinical isolates often harbor mutations in these
regulatory elements, leading to constitutive overexpression of efﬂux systems
and multidrug-resistant phenotypes9.
While RND efﬂux pumps are extensively studied for their role in AMR,
particularly in clinical pathogens, the broader physiological consequences of
their overexpression remain poorly understood
10. This gap is especially

Results
Collateral Cu sensitivity of the ΔtipR mutant
A genetic screen performed on aC. vibrioidesmini-Tn5 transposon mutant
library to seek Cu-sensitive mutants led to the identiﬁcation of thetipR gene
(CCNA_00852)( F i g .1A). TipR encodes a one-component transcriptional
regulator that was shown to repress the expression of the neighboring efﬂux
pump AcrAB2NodT14. To validate the genetic screen, we deleted the tipR
gene in the WT strain. We monitored the growth of the resulting ΔtipR
mutant (hereafter referred to asΔR) over 24 h in rich liquid peptone yeast
extract (PYE) medium under 150 μM CuSO ₄, and 190 μM CuSO ₄ stress
conditions, and on solid PYE medium under 100 μM CuSO₄ stress. These
concentrations were chosen based on the sensitivity of each assay: 150 μM
allowed clear discrimination between WT and mutant growth in liquid
culture, while higher concentrations (190 μM CuSO ₄) were required for
shorter or more stressful conditions. TheΔR mutant exhibited an increased
sensitivity to Cu stress on solid (Fig.1B) and in liquid (Fig.1C) PYE medium
but displayed a WT-like growth under control condition (Supplementary
Fig. 1A). Cu sensitivity correlated with increasing CuSO₄ concentrations,
with the ΔR mutant displaying reducedﬁtness at 190 μM Cu stress (Sup-
plementary Fig. 1A). Ectopic expression oftipR from the low-copy plasmid
pMR10 under the control of the constitutive lac or native P
tipR (ΔR + R)
restored the WT phenotype (Fig. 1B, C, and Supplementary Fig. 1B),
indicating thattipR deletion is not associated with polar effects.
To determine whether the sensitivity of theΔR mutant was speciﬁct o
Cu, we exposed the ΔR mutant to Zn, Ni, and Cd. The ΔR mutant also
exhibited an increased sensitivity to ZnSO₄ in liquid PYE medium (Sup-
plementary Fig. 1C), and showed enhanced sensitivity to ZnSO₄,N i S O₄,a n d
CdSO₄ on solid medium (Supplementary Fig. 1D). The rest of the study will
be focusing on the Cu sensitivity.
We hypothesized that the Cu hypersensitivity of theΔR mutant might
result from increased intracellular Cu levels. To test this hypothesis, we
quantiﬁed total intracellular Cu concentr ations using atomic absorption
spectroscopy (AAS). Surprisingly, theΔR mutant exhibited no signiﬁcant
increase in Cu accumulation compared to the WT (Fig. 1D). This was
further corroborated by theΔpcoAB, lacking a multicopper oxidase and Cu
efﬂux pump, which served as a control for Cu accumulation (Fig. 1D).
Similarly, Cu levels in a mutant lacking the AcrAB
2NodT efﬂux pump
(ΔABT) remained comparable to those of the WT (Supplementary Fig. 1E).
These results demonstrate that the Cu sensitivity of theΔR mutant is neither
due to elevated intracellular Cu nor to a direct role of AcrAB2NodT in Cu
efﬂux, revealing an unexpected uncoupling between metal sensitivity and
total cellular Cu content.
Owing to the ability of Cu to trigger ROS production15, we decided to
determine whether the increased Cu sensitivity of the ΔR mutant could
result from a higher oxidative stress. Upon exposure to 190 μM Cu stress, we
observed an increase in ﬂuorescence of the ROS-sensitive, turn-on probe
CellROX deep red and the cytoplasmic redox-sensitive, turn-off biosensor
rxYFP, conﬁrming that Cu induces oxidative stress. However, no signiﬁcant
difference in CellROX (Fig.1E) and rxYFP (Fig. 1F) ﬂuorescence could be
measured between the WT and ΔR strains, which is consistent with the
similar Cu content of these two strains (Fig.1D).
Together, these data indicatethat the Cu sensitivity of theΔR mutant is
independent of increased Cu accumulation and oxidative stress.
Inactivation of the AcrAB2NodT efﬂux pump restores Cu resis-
tance in the ΔtipR mutant
To dissect the mechanisms underlying Cu toxicity in the ΔR mutant, we
performed a suppressor screen in the ΔR mutant seeking mutants that
would exhibit a WT-like Cu resistance. Nine suppressors were isolated with
varying degrees of Cu resistance (Supplementary Fig. 2A). Whole-genome
sequencing identiﬁed four suppressor mutations as indels within acrA,
which encodes the periplasmic component of the AcrAB
2NodT ef ﬂux
pump. The remainingﬁve suppressor mutations were nucleotide substitu-
tions located in the intergenic TipR-binding region between thetipR gene
and the acrAB2nodT operon16 (Fig. 2A). TipR deletion triggers the upre-
gulation of the AcrAB2NodT efﬂux pump (Fig. 2D) that confers resistance
to penicillin16 and cephalosporin16, such as cefuroxime (CXM) (Fig. 2C).
CXM concentrations were chosen based on the MIC of the WT strain
(50 μg/mL, Supplementary Fig. 1F) to highlight sensitive and resistant
phenotypes. One could argue that the upregulation of the AcrAB 2NodT
efﬂux pump plays a key role in the collateral Cu sensitivity of theΔR mutant.
Consistent with this hypothesis, a double tipR, acrAB2nodT operon
(ΔRΔABT) mutant exhibits a WT-like Cu resistance in both solid (Fig.2B)
and liquid PYE medium (Fig. 2C), while losing its resistance to CXM
(Fig. 2B, C). Therefore, the Cu sensitivity of the tipR mutant likely arises
from the indirect consequences of ef ﬂux pump overexpression, since
AcrAB2NodT is neither induced (Fig.2D) by Cu nor involved in Cu efﬂux
(Supplementary Fig. 1E).

Metabolic burden of AcrAB2NodT upregulation
RND efﬂux pumps use the PMF to actively expel toxic compounds. We
hypothesized that overexpression of an active AcrAB2NodT efﬂux system in
the ΔR mutant imposes a physiological burden through excessive con-
sumption of the PMF, which could impair essential cellular processes such
as ATP synthesis, motility, and nutrient transport and, in turn, affect Cu
stress response.
To test this hypothesis, we measured the membrane potential as a PMF
proxy using the ratiometric dye DiOC₂(3), where a decrease in red ﬂuor-
escence indicates a loss in membrane potential. Under control conditions,
the ΔR mutant exhibited a marked reduction inﬂuorescence compared to
the WT strain, suggesting lower membrane potential likely due to PMF
depletion (Fig.5A). In contrast, both the ΔRΔABT and ΔRa c r B* mutants
exhibited WT ﬂuorescence, indicating that reduced AcrAB
2NodT abun-
dance and/or efﬂux activity restores membrane potential (Fig.5A). Under
Cu stress, both WT and ΔR strains showed a further drop in membrane
potential, while the ΔRΔABT and ΔRa c r B* mutants remained largely
unaffected (Fig.5A). Consistent with a loss of PMF, theΔR mutant exhibited
a defective motility under both contr ol and Cu stress conditions, while
motility was restored to WT levels in theΔRΔABT and ΔRa c r B* mutants
(Fig. 5B). The ΔpleC, lacking a histidine kinase required for motility, was
included as a control for motility defects (Fig.5B).
To directly test whether PMF disruption alone is sufﬁcient to confer Cu
sensitivity, we treated WT cells with gramicidin D, an ionophore that dis-
sipates the proton gradient
22. Gramicidin-treated WT cells exhibited slight
sensitivity, and the addition of Cu fu rther exacerbated growth defects
(Supplementary Fig. 4A). Strikingly, the growth curve of WT cells treated
with gramicidin and Cu closely aligned with that ofΔR mutant cells exposed
to Cu alone (Supplementary Fig. 4B). This result indicates that PMF
depletion caused by misregulated AcrAB
2NodT activity is a major driver of
the Cu-sensitive phenotype of the ΔR mutant, emphasizing the need for
tight regulation of this efﬂux pump.
In line with the PMF impairment, the ΔR mutant exhibited a sig-
niﬁcant reduction in intracellular ATP levels relative to the WT strain
(Fig. 5C). Accordingly, energy metabolismpathways are upregulated in the
ΔR mutant, including ATP producing pathways such as fatty acid oxidation,
folate metabolism, as well as ATP consuming processes such as DNA repair,
ABC transporters, kinase activity, and motility, all of which rely on sustained
energy availability (Fig.5D).
These data collectively support a model in which loss oftipR leads to
the upregulation of the AcrAB2NodT efﬂux system, resulting in depletion of
the PMF and increased energy demand. This metabolic strain leads to ATP
depletion, ultimately contributing toheightened sensitivity to Cu-induced
stress.
Discussion
RND efﬂux pumps have long been recognized as critical components in
bacterial defense mechanisms against antimicrobial agents. Previous
research has primarily focused on the protective bene ﬁts conferred by
overexpression of such RND pumps under stressful conditions
23. However,
the potentialﬁtness costs associated with elevated expression of these large,
energy-intensive complexes tend to be overlooked 10.I nt h i ss t u d y ,w e
highlight the double-edged nature of ef ﬂux pump upregulation, demon-
strating that such systems can enhance stress resistance and compromise
cell envelope integrity, thereby imposing aﬁtness cost.
While AcrAB
2NodT is best known for exporting AB, our results show
that its upregulation viatipR deletion destabilizes the cell envelope, in turn
sensitizingCaulobacterto metal stress. This phenotype does not result from
increased intracellular Cu or ROS but instead reﬂects the physiological and
structural burden directly caused by AcrAB2NodT overexpression.
In the WT strain, envelope homeostasis is maintained by the coordi-
nated action of the TAM complex, thetwo-component systems ChvGI and
UzcRS, and the TonB-dependent receptors BugA and ChvT (Fig.6). Upon
Cu exposure, these pathways are partially induced, re ﬂecting a metal-
responsive adaptive response that preserves membrane integrity (Fig. 6).
BugA and ChvT, known to mediate nutrient uptake and to respond to
antibiotics and metals, contribute to envelope homeostasis
24–26.U n d e rC u
stress, BugA was upregulated (Supplementary Fig. 5A), suggesting a shared
metal-responsive regulatory pathway. Notably, UzcRS, previously shown to
activate acrAB2nodT transcription under Zn stress, did not increase
AcrAB2NodT protein levels under Cu, indic ating post-transcriptional or
stress-speciﬁc regulation.
In contrast, the ΔR mutant, which constitutively overexpresses the
AcrAB2NodT, has an overall upregulation of genes related to lipid meta-
bolism, proteolysis, transport, and signaling, accompanied by visible outer
membrane deformation (Fig.6). This deformation is consistent with pre-
vious work showing that AcrAB 2NodT overexpression reduces TamAB
levels through the small RNA AbnZ, transcribed from the 3 ′ end of the
acrAB2nodT operon27. AbnZ represses the TAM complex, which is essential
for assembling autotransporters involved in nutrient uptake, adhesion, and
virulence
28, and its disruption leads to membrane defects and reduced
viability27. This TAM downregulation likely exacerbates envelope fragility
and contributes directly to theﬁtness cost of pump overexpression observed
in the ΔR mutant. Additionally, the ChvGI two-component system, known
to respond to osmotic stress and ensure envelope homeostasis 29, is upre-
gulated in this mutant (Supplementary Fig. 5B), highlighting chronic acti-
vation of envelope stress pathways even in the absence of Cu. When Cu is
then applied to the ΔR mutant, membrane deformation worsens, but the
regulatory landscape remains largel y unchanged, emphasizing that the
underlying fragility is driven primarily by pump overexpression and its
downstream consequences via AbnZ and TAM rather than by the metal
itself (Fig. 6).
Together, these ﬁndings support a model in which AcrAB
2NodT
upregulation is the primary driver of envelope instability, creating a fragile
physiological state that is exacerbated by exposure to transition metals, with
Cu representing the best-characterized case in this study. This highlights the
double-edged nature of ef ﬂux pump overexpressi on: while enhancing
antibiotic resistance, it compromises envelope integrity and overallﬁtness.

phenomenon, whereby the inactivation or overexpression of one ef ﬂux
