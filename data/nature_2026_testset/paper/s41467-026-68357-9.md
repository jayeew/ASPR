Perovskite-based solar water splitting systems are promising candidates for
addressing environmental challenges, exceeding the commercialization efﬁ-
ciency of solar-to-hydrogen (STH) at >10%. However, the operational stability
remains suboptimal due to insufﬁcient in situ/operando insights into charge
carrier dynamics. Here, we investigate the role of charge accumulation on
operational stability through operandocatalytic modulation via near-infrared
(NIR) toggling on a photothermal catalyst. Electrochemical analyses under
operando NIR toggling demonstrate enhanced hydrogen evolution reaction
kinetics and reduced charge recombination. In situ analyses conﬁrm that
reduced charge accumulation suppresses ion migration in the perovskite
layer, thereby enhancing operational stability. The NIR-irradiated cathode
delivers a photocurrent density of 25.48 mA cm
–2, maintaining 90% of its initial
photocurrent density at 0 VRHE for 310 h. A parallelly-illuminated coplanar
system with NIR-irradiatedperovskite-based water splitting cathode operates
without bias, achieving a STH efﬁciency of 15.18%, retaining 70% of their initial
performance for 115 h.

investigate the electrochemical behavior of an immersed perovskite-
based water splitting electrode paired with a commercial Pt-carbon-
coated carbon cloth (Pt-C/CC) catalyst. NIR laser irradiation of the
catalyst elevates its temperature via the photothermal effect, thereby
improving the overall performance of the device, in operando. Elec-

only enhanced hydrogen evolution reaction (HER) kinetics but also
reduced charge accumulation within the multilayer structure. In a
hybrid device combining a water splitting cell and a solar cell, in situ
monitoring during operational stability tests reveal that reduced
charge accumulation suppresses ion migration within the perovskite
layer via accelerated electrochemical kinetics, thus improving long-
term stability. Our results identify catalyst degradation as the domi-

degradation become more signi ﬁcant in later stages, thereby eluci-
dating the degradation mechanism of the immersed perovskite-based
solar water splitting system. Through this understanding, we demon-
strate that charge accumulation is a primary cause of performance
deterioration, signi ﬁcantly impacting overall stability. The NIR-

Design and performance of immersed perovskite-based water
splitting cathode
The device con ﬁguration and performance of perovskite solar cells
(PSCs) are detailed in Supplementary Note 2. Based on the PSCs, a
perovskite-based water splitting cathode was fabricated by positioning
a Cu tape and Cu wire on the top Au electrode (Fig. 1a) (see Supple-
mentary Fig. 2, Supplementary Note 3 and 4, and Methods for addi-
tional fabrication details about the device). The Pt-C/CC photothermal
catalyst was placed at the bottom electrode, physically separated from
the perovskite. All experiments were conducted under ambient con-
ditions (RH 15–20%, 23–25 °C), unless speciﬁed. The temperature ele-
vation of the catalyst on the cathode, immersed in 0.5 M KPi
(potassium phosphate) (pH 7) electrolyte under NIR irradiation, was
monitored via thermal imaging using an infrared camera, as shown in
Supplementary Fig. 3. The catalyst ’s temperature increased sig-
niﬁcantly within a short period despite being submerged in the
electrolyte, while epoxy encapsulation effectively prevented heat
transfer to the photo-absorber layer. The HER performance of the
cathode was evaluated using linear sweep voltammetry (LSV)
(Fig. 1b) (see Supplementary Fig. 4 for the detailed experimental
setup of performance measurements with NIR irradiation). Despite
being immersed in the electrolyte, the perovskite-based cathode
stably operated without any degradation, indicating that our
encapsulation strategy effectively prevented water permeation
(Supplementary Fig. 5). The NIR laser intensity used was 2.5 A, with
the optimization process described in Supplementary Fig. 6. The
immersed perovskite-based cathode initially demonstrated a satu-
rated photocurrent density ( J
ph) of 24.08 mA cm –2 at 0 VRHE (Vv s .
reversible hydrogen electrode) and an onset voltage ( Von) of 0.96
VRHE. Notably, following NIR laser irradiation of the catalyst, the
cathode’s performance improved, exhibiting aJph of 25.48 mA cm–2 at
0 VRHE and Von of 1.04 VRHE. The increased onset voltage under NIR
irradiation originates from enhanced catalyst performance (detailed
explanation in later sections, see Supplementary Fig. 22), rather than
change in band gap or photovoltage provided by the perovskite
layer. Note that the thermodynamic potential of the HER does not
vary due to the catalyst surface temperature (Supplementary Note 5).
Operando performance observations were conducted with the NIR
laser alternately turned on and off (Supplementary Fig. 7). The dark
current of the water splitting cell was measured respective to the NIR
irradiation (Supplementary Fig. 8). The presented dark current data
via various measurements that the photocurrent density under light
illumination originates from photo-induced processes rather than
unfavorable attributions such as current leakages or side reactions
apart from HER. Enhanced performance was observed under NIR
irradiation, which reverted to the original control (without NIR)
levels when the NIR laser was turned off. This indicates that the
increased temperature enhanced the catalytic activity of Pt-C/CC,
leading to improved overall performance of the cathode.
The incident photon-to-current conversion ef ﬁciency (IPCE)
spectra demonstrated enhanced photon harvesting across all wave-
lengths, with integrated IPCE valu es under NIR irradiation closely
matching the measured J

improvement in IPCE across the entire wavelength range suggests that
the photothermal effect induced by NIR irradiation increased the
catalyst’s temperature, thereby accelerating HER kinetics and enhan-
cing both J

length range of the IPCE spectra under both control and NIR-irradiated
conditions indicates that the temperature increase of catalyst due to
NIR irradiation is local and does not alter the band gap of the per-
ovskite layer due to the thermal insulation of epoxy resin. By com-
paring the in situ photoluminescence (PL) spectra before and after NIR
irradiation (Supplementary Fig. 9), it was observed that the PL peak
position remained unchanged, and the intensity also maintained the
same, which is consistent with IPCE results.

Degradation mechanism of perovskite-based water
splitting system
Figure 5 illustrates the overall degradation mechanism of the
perovskite-based water splitting cathode and the direct impact of the
photothermal effect on its operational stability. When NIR irradiation
is applied to the Pt-C/CC, the local temperature rapidly increases,
enhancing the HER kinetics. Also, temperature gradient is formed,
inducing thermal Marangoni convection, which accelerates the bubble
departure. The rapid gas evolution, characterized by smaller bubble
departure sizes, prevents the detachment of Pt particles, thereby
improving operational stability. This catalyst degradation mechanism
dominates the initial stage of stability decline in the perovskite-based
cathode. In addition, the photothermal effect facilitates rapid electron
extraction for HER, signi ﬁcantly reducing charge accumulation and
iodide migration over extended period of operation, thereby sup-
pressing perovskite degradation. In contrast, without NIR irradiation,
sluggish HER kinetics result in accumulation of electrons on the FTO
surface and accumulation of holes in the HTL, resulting in increased
iodide migration into the HTL. This migration introduces trap states
within the HTL, disrupting overall charge transport and accelerating
perovskite degradation. These charge transport interruptions worsen
with prolonged operation, leading to exacerbated iodide migration
and further degradation of the perovskite. Based on theseﬁndings, we
propose that the degradation of perovskite-based water splitting
devices during operation is determined by the stability of the catalyst
and the efﬁciency of charge extraction through electrochemical reac-
tions, both of which are critical factors inﬂuencing the overall stability
of perovskite-based water splitting systems.

Discussion
In summary, we present direct operando and in situ observations of
charge accumulation in perovskite-based water splitting cathodes and
its impact on operational stability utilizing the photothermal effect.
This was achieved by integrating a Pt-C/CC catalyst with the cathodes,
signiﬁcantly enhancing the HER kinetics through photothermal acti-
vation upon NIR irradiation. The enhanced performance resulted in a
ABPE of 20.69%. Moreover, the operational stability of the perovskite-
based cathodes was extended from 6 h to 310 h, maintaining ~90% (t
90)
of its initial photocurrent. Electrochemical analysis under operando
conditions effectively monitored suppressed charge accumulation
within the multilayer device using identical specimen sets. In situ
analyses of the device during operation revealed that reduced charge
accumulation led to suppressed iodine migration at the perovskite/
HTL interface, with ion migration predominantly occurring during the
later stabilized stage of operation. Observations of bubble dynamics
showed that NIR irradiation accelerated gas evolution with smaller
bubble sizes, effectively preventing detachment of Pt particles and
thus enhancing operational stability during the initial stage. Based on
these ﬁndings, a degradation mechanism for perovskite-based water
splitting devices during operation is proposed, providing operando
insights into charge accumulation within the perovskite layer, high-
lighting its role in achieving enhanced operational stability. With
improved performance and stability, bias-free solar water splitting
achieved a STH ef ﬁciency of 15.18%, maintaining 70% of the initial
performance for 115 h in a parallelly illuminated coplanar system.
