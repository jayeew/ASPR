Control of chiral assembly across length scales is desirable but challenging.
Herein, we demonstrate controllable construction of microscopic chiral twists
through hierarchical assembly of enantiomeric styrylpyridinium-derived glu-
tamide amphiphiles (L/D-SPG). By combining variable-temperature spectro-
scopy with in-situ real-time optical microscopic observation, the dynamic
chirality transfer process across spatial and length scales during assembly is
elucidated. It is revealed that hydrophobic interactions between alkyl chains
and intermolecular hydrogen bonds dominate the initial assembly of SPG
oligomers, which subsequently elongate into microscopic twists through
synergistic action of multiple non-covalent bonds under stereochemical con-
trol. The coherent cross-length-scalechirality transfer drives a dramatic
ampliﬁcation of chiroptical signals, evolving from undetectable circularly
polarized luminescence (CPL) in the monomeric state and weak CPL with a
small luminescence dissymmetry factor (|g
lum |=2 . 8×1 0 −3) in the non-
hierarchical nanostructures to a |glum| value of 0.11 among single-component
supramolecular gel systems. This work not only provides a direct spatio-
temporal observation of chirality transfer from molecular to micron scales, but
also establishes a supramolecular design strategy for high-performance CPL
materials through optimized hierarchical organization.
Biological systems are masterful at constructing functional materials
across multiple length scales in nature. From the precise folding of
polypeptide chains into functional proteins to the cross-scale inte-
gration of organelles and tissues, living organisms achieve extra-
ordinary functional complexity through the spatiotemporal
orchestration of non-covalent interactions (NCIs)
1– 4. This sophisticated
biological architecture has inspired supramolecular chemistry to
develop hierarchical assembly strategies that reproduce such struc-
tural sophistication. Through the stepwise and programmed incor-
poration of NCIs, arti ﬁcial systems achieve functional organization
across single-molecule to macroscopic scales
5– 13. Contemporary
research has amassed substantial design principles for constructing
intricate architectures via controlled multistep pathways. Examples
such as rosettes and ultralong nanoladders demonstrate how hier-
archical driving forces, including hydrogen bonding, ionic interac-
tions, microphase separation, yield ordered structures with emergent
properties
14– 23. Crucially, this hierarchical approach facilitates macro-
scopic supramolecular assembly while achieving precise dimensional
control across multiple length scales and concurrent functional
enhancement, particularly in terms of efﬁcient chiral expression and
ampliﬁcation
24.

Hierarchically self-assembled microscopic chiral twists and
asymmetry ampliﬁcation
Helical structures not only exemplify the elegance of natural hierarchy
but also serve as powerful model systems for probing the emergence
and propagation of chirality across scales. Inspired by the subunit
structure of DNA double helices in biological systems, a pair of chiral
cationic amphiphilic moleculesL/D-SPG was designed and synthesized

50,51. In this design, the glutamic acid moiety provided the

bonding involving three amide groups. The cationic conjugated
headgroup facilitated electrostatic interactions that were functionally
analogous to those of DNA phosphate groups, and participated in a
dynamic interplay withπ– π stacking, which contributed to stabilizing
the hydrogen-bonding orientation established by the stereocenter.

Real-time observation of the in-situ dynamic evolution
process by OM
Guided by the physicochemical properties of SPG molecules and the
co-solvent system, a real-time observation device was built seeking to
capture the in-situ evolution process of microscopic twists (Fig.3a). A
septum-capped quartz cuvette, ﬁlled with SPG molecules uniformly
dispersed in a DMF-H
2O co-solvent, was placed on the heating
microscope stage and gradually heated until a clear and homogeneous
hot solution was obtained (Fig.3b, left and upper). Due to the hermetic
environment, this device was capable of operating at a relatively high
temperature without solvent evaporation. Afterwards, as the system
temperature dropped in the air atmosphere, nascent ﬁlaments
emerged and underwent morphological evolution by progressively
elongating into one-dimensional microscopic twisted architectures
(Fig. 3b, right and bottom). Initially, there were no optically visible
assemblies until twistedﬁlaments with discernible pitches (yellow tri-
angles) appeared after 4 s (Fig. 3c and Supplementary Movie 1). Over
the next 10 s, theseﬁlaments further elongated in both axial and lateral
directions at the same time with their pitches maintaining. Well-
deﬁned microscopic twists with stable width and morphology were
essentially formed after 20 s, indicating the attainment of equilibrium
in the dynamic assembly system (Fig. 3f). To quantify the growth
dynamics, time-resolved measurements and curve ﬁtting were per-
formed on the width and length of the evolving microscopic twists
(Fig. 3d, e and Supplementary Figs. 19– 26). Statistical analysis revealed
that both dimensions increased linearly with time, described by
separate linear functions: W(t)= k
1t + b1 and L(t)= k2t + b2, where the
slopes k1 and k2 represent the lateral and axial growth rates, respec-
tively, and the intercepts b1 and b2 correspond to the initial sizes at
t =0 ,r eﬂecting the limited size for samples observable with OM under
this magniﬁcation. This linear kinetics indicated that the assembly was
interface-controlled, with growth rate governed by the incorporation
of pre-assembled subunits at the growth front of a single twist rather
than by the diffusion rate of SPG monomers in solution
52,53. The con-
sistency of growth rates across different twists evidenced byk1 and k2
values, respectively, demonstrated that interfacial addition proceeded
at a nearly constant rate, determined by a uniform energy barrier. This
macroscopic linear growth thus served as a strong indicator that the
system, within the observable time window, operated as a highly
ordered, cooperative, and programmable self-assembly process. Cru-
cially, the relatively insensitivity of the growth rate to changes in bulk
concentration provided additional, independent support for this
interface-controlled mechanism (Supplementary Figs. 27– 31 and Sup-
plementary Movie 3).

Dynamic assembly process at a molecular level
Spectroscopic techniques enable precise detection and real-time
tracking of atomic- and molecular-level changes (e.g., vibrational
modes, electronic states, and energy transitions), thereby providing
insights into a diverse array of NCIs. A suite of variable-temperature
(VT) spectroscopic methods was employed to probe hierarchical
assembly dynamics across scales, from molecular recognition to
emergent supramolecular architectures. VT-UV– vis and VT-CD spec-
troscopies delivered essential information on chiral polymerization
kinetics (Fig. 4a– c and Supplementary Table 5)
55– 57. During the VT

signiﬁcant intensity enhancement at λ = 353 nm and a concomitant
decrease in the absorption band at λ = 394 nm. This trade-off rela-

conﬁrmed the dynamic equilibrium between monomeric and aggre-

absorption with increasing temperature, indicating the disassembly of

ited a pronounced bathochromic shift of 41 nm in UV spectra, unam-
biguously con ﬁrming the formation of J-aggregates (Fig. 4aa n d

Based on comprehensive analysis, a hierarchical assembly
mechanism for the microscopic chiral twists was formulated (Fig.1e).
First, the assembly process was initiated by temperature-driven
hydrophobic association of the alkyl chains, which promoted mole-
cular proximity and facilitated the formation of SPG oligomers
through intermolecular hydrogen bonds among amide groups. In the
high-polarity solvent environment, the positive charges maintained a
relatively localized distribution. Upon crossing the critical tempera-
ture threshold of T
e,i n t e n s iﬁed hydrophobic interactions dramati-
cally reduced the intermolecular distances, triggering rapid
coalescence of SPG oligomers devoid of supramolecular chirality.
Crucially, the molecular chiral centers then orchestrated the align-
ment of chirality-directed hydrogen-bond networks andπ– π stacking
interactions, accompanied by positive charge delocalization, thereby
encoding chirality into the nascent assemblies. This chiral encoding
was structurally realized by the formation of a highly tilted bilayer
driven by the optimal f
w = 30% condition, which in turn enabled
effective inter-bilayer π– π stacking. The resulting interlocked
arrangement of the headgroups served as a pivotal stabilizing factor
for the entire assembly. After the nucleation stage, the growth pro-
ceeded in an interface-controlled manner, resulting in a relatively
constant growth rate and comparable elongation in both lateral and
axial dimensions, which was characteristic of the long-range ordered
chiral J-aggregates stabilized by Br
− counterions. This interlockedπ– π
stacking acted synergistically with hydrogen bonding and hydro-
phobic effects, constituting a multi-faceted stabilization mechanism.
The real-time evolution of structural asymmetry further evidenced
uninterrupted chirality transfer throughout the in-situ twisting path-
way. This time-resolved process excluded artifacts arising from post-
assembly perturbations such as bulk solution shear or drying-induced
stress. Such graduated chiral ampli ﬁcation through hierarchical
conformational re ﬁnement promoted exceptional microscale chir-
ality expression in precisely aligned microscopic twist architectures.
Consequently, the integration of real-time trackability and high dis-
symmetry factors in the SPG system provided an opportunity to
monitor and control chiral assembly dynamics, setting a benchmark
for future chiral luminescent systems.
In order to bolster the aforementioned mechanism, the global
minimum energy structure for L-SPG dimer was optimized via con-
formational search (Figs.4h and S49). The resultant J-type aggregation
distinctly exhibited the chromophores alignment with a calculated
distance between two π-conjugated aromatic rings of 3.8 Å, precisely
matching the π– π stacking distance derived from the diffraction peak
in PXRD. NCI analysis demonstrated that both π– π stacking and
hydrogen bonds played a pivotal part in stabilizing L-SPG dimers,
which could be reasonably extrapolated, combined with other
experimental results, to understand the formation process of the chiral
assembly. MD simulations indicated that the pre-assembled L-SPG
bilayers containing 32 molecules displayed a tightly packed arrange-
ment with a counterclockwise rotational direction, which facilitated
the generation of right-handed CPL with a high g
lum value (Fig. 4i).
Discussion
In summary, emergent microscopic twist architectures were con-
structed through hierarchical assembly of cationic glutamide-based
amphiphilesL/D-SPG, during which multiple NCIs were coordinated in a
spatially and temporally programmed manner. Through multiscale
dynamic characterization, including real-time observation of the in-situ
formation process, the spatiotemporal trajectory of chirality transfer
across dimensional scales was deciphered. It was revealed that the
hierarchical organization was driven by the synergism of hydrogen
bonding, van der Waals forces, and π– π stacking interactions, which
aligned chromophores into long-range chiral arrays with optimal three-
dimensional chirality expression. This assembly pathway was thermo-
dynamically favored by low Gibbs free energy, kinetically stabilized by
counterions and guided by solvent-mediated twisting, hence facilitating
the formation of macroscopic chiral structures. Particularly noteworthy
was that the ef ﬁcient cross-length-scale chirality transfer manifested
itself in both structural dimensionality and chiroptical signals, impart-
ing the upscaled twists with a 40-fold ampliﬁcation in CPL response in
contrast to antisolvent-induced non-hierarchical nanostructures. The
chiroptical enhancement culminated in a | g

the single-component gel system. By quantiﬁed real-time visualization
of hierarchical supramolecular twist dynamics, this study not only elu-
cidated the spatiotemporal supramolecular organization driven by
synergistic action of multiple non-covalent bonds under stereo-
chemical control, but also profoundly advanced the rational design and
efﬁciently control of high-performance CPL-active materials.
