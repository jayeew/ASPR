Geun-Hee Lee 1, Kyoung-Whan Kim 2 &K y u n g - J i nL e e1
Electrical control of magnetism is critical for advancing both fundamental
understanding and technological applications of magnetic systems. Although
gate-voltage-induced carrier modulation enables tuning of magnetic proper-
ties, current-induced mechanisms offer more dynamic and versatile control.
To date, such control has been investigated exclusively through spin exchange
interactions between itinerant and localized electrons, leaving the role of
orbital exchange interactions entirely unexplored. Here, we establish a theo-
retical framework for current-induced control of magnetism mediated by
orbital exchange interactions, incorporating the full orbital degrees of free-
dom, including orbital angular momentum and orbital angular position. We
show that nonequilibrium orbital densities generated by the orbital Hall and
orbital Edelstein effects induce not only damping-like andﬁeld-like torques,
but also current-driven modiﬁcations of the magnetic anisotropy, damping,
and gyromagnetic ratio. Our estimates suggest that orbital-exchange-
mediated effects can exceed their spin-exchange counterparts, positioning
orbital exchange as a dominant mechanism for magnetism control. We further
propose experimental schemes based on harmonic Hall and spin-torque fer-
romagnetic resonance measurements to quantify these effects. Theseﬁndings
uncover a previously unrecognized routefor electrical control of magnetism
and extend current-induced effects to a broader class of materials beyond
conventional dipolar magnets.
One of the most widely studied current-induced effects in magnetic
s y s t e m si ss p i nt o r q u e1– 3, which arises from spin currents interacting
with localized spins via spin exchange interactions. It includes spin-
orbit torque (SOT)
4, where spin currents are generated through the
spin Hall effect driven by spin-orbit interaction (SOI) 5. Spin torque
provides an ef ﬁcient mechanism for controlling magnetization
dynamics3,4,6, enabling switching, precession, and the motion of
domain walls and skyrmions.
Recent developments have highlighted two intriguing current-
induced effects: orbital torque7– 11 and control of magnetic anisotropy12.
Notably, both have been attribute d to spin exchange interactions.
Orbital torque arises from orbital currents generated by the orbital
Hall effect 13– 20– the transverse ﬂow of orbital angular momentum
(OAM) under an electric ﬁeld. The exisiting theory7 describes orbital
torque as resulting from orbital-to-spin conversion through SOI in
ferromagnets, with the converted spin currents exerting torque on
magnetization through spin exchange. Consequently, this orbital tor-
que effectively reduces to spin torque, with spin exchange serving as
the essential coupling. A recently observed current-induced mod-
iﬁcation of magnetic anisotropy in a transition metal ferromagnet, iron
(Fe)
12, was also interpreted based on spin exchange: a spin-current-
induced modiﬁcation of exchange splitting, leading to a change in
magnetic anisotropy.

current-induced effects: orbital exchange interactions between itin-
erant and localized electrons. We demonstrate that orbital exchange

Our theory also opens multiple promising directions for future
research. First, while we focus on spin dynamics in spin-orbit-coupled
ferromagnets, systems exhibiting equilibrium orbital magnetism in
moiré systems

SOI, may enable current-induced control even in the absence of SOI,

exchange interactions can be extended beyond OAM and OAP to
higher-order multipoles
21– 24. This raises the possibility of current-
induced multipole torques driven by nonequilibrium multipole cur-
rents in materials with intrinsic multipolar order. An example is
altermagnets
46, which hosts not only Néel order but also magnetic
multipolar order47,48.I ns u c hs y s t e m s ,m u l t i p o l ec u r r e n t sc a nn a t u r a l l y

multipole moments enables current-induced manipulation of multi-
polar order. Third, the natural involvement of OAP, which does not
carry angular momentum, in both current and interaction channels
suggests that even orbitally ordered but nonmagnetic systems could
be susceptible to current-induced control. Fourth, our results suggest
that ﬁrst-principles calculations of current-induced torques must go
beyond standard density functional theory (DFT), as the exchange-
correlation functional in DFT does not capture orbital exchange
interactions. These effects may be incorporated through extensions
such as DFT + U, DFT-DMFT (Dynamical Mean-Field Theory), or GW
approximation (G:G r e e n’s function, W: screened Coulomb interac-
tion), provided that the limitations of each method in accurately
treating orbital exchange interactions are properly addressed.
Although the on-site Coulomb Hamiltonian [Eq. ( 1)] and the Kugel ’-
Khomskii Hamiltonian [Eq. (2)] do not provide a complete description
of OAM and OAP exchange interactions in solids, they indicate that
OAM and OAP exchanges can be as signi ﬁcant as spin exchange. This
highlights the need for ﬁrst-principles approaches that properly
account for OAM and OAP exchange effects in order to achieve a more
complete understanding of current-induced torques. Altogether,
these directions substantially broaden both the conceptual basis and
practical scope of current-induced effects, paving the way for their
realization in material systems far beyond conventional dipolar
magnets.
