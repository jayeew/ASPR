Zinc-ion batteries depend on molecular design of electrolyte additives for performance optimization.
However, the in ﬂuence of molecular structure and steric effects on solvation behavior has not been
systematically quantiﬁed. This research combines Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
technology with large language models, establishing a system from intelligent literature analysis to
molecular screening. Through prompt engineering optimization, screening precision under de ﬁned
criteria improved from 30.0% to 100%, identifying two structurally distinct additives from over twenty
thousand molecules— rigid cyclic 2-methylimidazole (MI) and ﬂexible chain-like 3-aminopropanol
(AP). Through theoretical calculations and experimental validation, we demonstrate that MI ’s rigid
structure restricted solvation shell adaptability, whereas AP ’s conformational ﬂexibility enhanced
zinc-ion migration ef ﬁciency, suppressed hydrogen evolution and dendrite formation, and extended
battery cycle life. This study quantitatively elucidates molecular structure and steric effects in zinc
battery performance, establishes principles for electrolyte design, and develops an AI-driven precision
screening methodology.
The rapid advancement of global renewable energy has established the
development of efﬁcient, safe, and cost-effective energy storage systems as a
central challenge in the energy sector. Among various energy storage
technologies, aqueous zinc-ion batteries (ZIBs) have emerged as a pro-
mising candidate for next-generatio n energy storage solutions, offering
signiﬁcant advantages including high theoretical capacity (820 mAh g
–1),
low reduction potential (–0.76 V vs. SHE), abundant resource availability,
and environmental compatibility1–6. However, ZIB practical implementa-
tion faces several challenges, particu larly regarding zinc anode interface
stability, which leads to hydrogen evolution, zinc dendrite formation, and
cathode material degradation. These factors signi ﬁcantly impact battery
cycle life and electrochemical performance
7–9.
Electrolyte optimization represents a key strategy in addressing these
challenges10–12. In conventional aqueous electrolytes, highly reactive water
molecules readily undergo side reactions with the zinc anode, substantially
degrading battery performance
13. Organic small-molecule additives,
incorporating polar functional groups (hydroxyl, carbonyl, carboxyl),
effectively minimize zinc ion-water interactions, thereby suppressing side
reactions and enhancing interfacial stability 14–16. These additives enable
precise modulation of zinc-ion solvat ion structure and the electrolyte-
electrode interface, while improving solvation shell stability17,18.C o m p a r e d
to alternative additive types, organi c small molecules provide enhanced
ﬂexibility in tailoring electrolyte sy stems and demonstrate considerable
potential for improving aqueous zinc-ion battery performance19–21.
Recent research has shifted the development of electrolyte additives
from optimizing individual functionalities toward systematic molecular
design, wherein molecular structure and steric effects play fundamental
roles in regulating electrolyte performance. Regarding molecular structure,
Yang et al. investigated six stereoisomers of 2,3,4,5-tetrahydroxypentanal,
demonstrating that D-arabinose effectively reconstructs the Zn

Based on intelligent literature analysis from the RAG system, we
designed four progressive core questions to construct a systematic screening
framework: from molecular structure and solvation regulation mechanisms,

system interaction mechanisms, ultimately establishing screening criteria

Through iterative validation and analysis processes, we established three
core screening criteria: (1) single-ringor short-chain molecular structures to
satisfy experimental design parameters; (2) moderate water solubility to
ensure electrolyte compatibility while maintaining interfacial enrichment
capability; (3) presence of speciﬁc polar functional groups to optimize sol-

To ensure accuracy of large language models in complex chemical
screening tasks, we conducted systematic prompt engineering optimi-
zation work. To this end, we ﬁrst employed a Retrieval-Augmented
Generation (RAG) system to analyze cheminformatics-related literature,
extracting and integrating quantitative criteria and their chemical basis.
Subsequently, based on this literature-supported knowledge, we adopted
an iterative optimization strategy, evolving from simple qualitative
descriptions (V1) to systematic prompts containing detailed quantitative
standards and knowledge bases (V3). Speci ﬁcally, V1 prompts used
simple qualitative descriptions, mainly relying on the model ’s chemical
common sense for judgment; V2 prompts introduced speci ﬁc numerical
ranges and clearer standards; V3 prompts covered detailed molecular

comprehensive evaluation systems (Supplementary Figs. 11 –13), and
used the previously extracted quantitative criteria and their chemical
explanations as a database, signi ﬁcantly improving the accuracy and
interpretability of screening. To validate prompt optimization effective-
ness, we independently randomly selected 60 molecules from public
databases for testing veri ﬁcation. Results showed screening accuracy
signiﬁcantly improved from 30.0% for V1 to 86.7% for V2, with the ﬁnal
V3 version achieving 100% screening precision in benchmark tests
(Supplementary Table 2, detailed comparative data in Supplementary
Data 2–5). Based on the optimized V3 screening system, we conducted
systematic screening of over 20,000 molecules, ultimately identifying 18

Evolution of the ﬁrst solvation shell structure
Based on the selected target molecules, we performed molecular dynamics
(MD) simulations to elucidate the m echanistic behavior of MI and AP
molecules in the Zn2+ ﬁrst solvation shell. We systematically investigated the
coordination behavior of MI and AP molecules in the ﬁrst solvation shell
(Fig. 3a and Supplementary Fig. 15). Further, we precisely de ﬁned the
coordination angles and bond lengths of MI and AP (Fig. 3b) to examine
their correlation withﬁrst solvation shell geometric conﬁgurations.
The simulation results revealed that MI, constrained by its rigid cyclic
structure, exhibits two coordination modes: MI1-N and MI2-N, with angles
of 70–81° and distances of 3.1 –3.4 Å. In contrast, AP’s ﬂexible chain-like
structure enables three coordination modes: AP-N, AP-O, and O-AP-N,
with broader angle ranges (63–180°) and distances (2.5–5.1 Å) (Figs. 3c, d,
and Supplementary Fig. 16).
Time-evolution analysis (Figs.3e, f) demonstrated distinct adaptability
patterns of MI and AP molecules in dynamic environments. The MI
molecule maintained relatively constant coordination angles and distances
throughout the simulation, indicating that its rigid structure constrains
conformational ﬂexibility in dynamic electrochemical environments.
Conversely, the AP molecule exhibite d superior geometric adaptability,
particularly in the O-AP-N coordination mode. Its ﬂexible chain-like
architecture signiﬁcantly enhancedﬁrst solvation shell dynamic adaptability
(Supplementary Fig. 17).
Solvation space occupancy effect
Building on the coordination structure analysis, we employed MD simu-
lations combined with the Bondi method to quantitatively evaluate spatial
occupancy effects
43. We calculated dimensions and volumes of ﬁve repre-
sentative solvation shell structures (Fig. 4a and Supplementary Fig. 18,
Supplementary Table 4). The MI system exhibits signiﬁcantly larger sol-
vation shell volumes (695.91 –711.01 Å³) compared to AP
(456.49–550.15 Å³), indicating that MI ’s rigid structure substantially
inﬂuences solvent molecule arrangement within the shell.
The rigid structure of the MI molecule induces signiﬁcant disruption to
solvent molecule arrangement, nece ssitating their rearrangement to
accommodate the MI molecule. This phenomenon creates a “shell entry
barrier.” (the energetic cost required for additive molecules to integrate into
the solvation shell due to structural reorganization of coordinated solvent
molecules). Such rearrangement enhances intermolecular repulsion,
thereby constraining the free movement of solvent molecules within the
shell. In contrast, the AP molecule ’s ﬂexible chain-like structure enables
more effective adaptation to shell environment changes, substantially
reducing the “entry barrier” effect.
To validate this phenomenon, we performed MD simulations to
determine the box density for both MI and AP systems (Supplementary
Fig. 19). Results demonstrated tha t the MI system exhibits signi ﬁcantly
higher density than the AP system, re ﬂecting enhanced packing density
induced by MI ’s rigid structure. This density increase restricts solvent
molecule mobility within the shell, intensifying the“internal packing effect.”
(the restricted molecular mobility resulting from enhanced density and
spatial crowding within the solvation shell). Conversely, the AP molecule,
with itsﬂexible chain-like structure, establishes a more uniform distribution
in the dynamic environment, preventing excessive solvent molecule packing
within the shell.
Additionally, MD simulations revealed that the molecular volume in
AP-N mode is 498.65 Å
3, markedly reduced from 612.24 Å3 calculated in
AP2-N geometry optimization (Suppl ementary Fig. 20). This volume
reduction demonstrates that AP’s ﬂexible chain-like structure undergoes
conﬁgurational adjustments in dynam ic environments, forming a more
compact solvation shell. Consequently, this provides greater mobility for
solvent molecules within the shell, pr eventing excessive packing density.

Dynamic evolution of the interfacial hydrogen bond network and
polarization behavior
Based on a deeper understanding of ion transport dynamics, further studies
reveal that the introduction of additives signiﬁcantly restructures the ori-
ginal water –water hydrogen bond network, affecting the electrolyte ’s
microstructure and its dynamic evolution. Hydrogen bond analysis (Fig.6a
and Supplementary Fig. 25) demonstrates that the water-water hydrogen
bonds in MI and AP systems decrease to 1071 and 1025, respectively,
compared to the ZnSO
4 system, indicating a substantial weakening of inter-
water molecular hydrogen bonding9. Additionally, the AP system formed
more hydrogen bonds with water molecules. In contrast, the rigid ring
structure of MI shows certain limitations in hydrogen bond formation. This
suggests that the ﬂexible chain structure of AP, through its dynamic
adaptability, more effectively disrupts the water molecule hydrogen bond
network, making its regulation ability superior to that of MI.
The hydrogen bond length distribution analysis (Fig.6b) further elu-
cidates the impact of different add itives on the hydrogen bond network
structure. In the ZnSO
4 system, the proportion of hydrogen bonds in the
short bond region (1.6 –2.0 Å) is minimal, while the long bond region
(>2.0 Å) accounts for the predominant proportion. Upon the introduction
of additives, this distribution patte rn changes distinctly. The AP system
preferentially forms hydrogen bonds in the short bond region, enhancing
the compactness of the hydrogen bond network, while signiﬁcantly redu-
cing the proportion in the long bond region. In comparison, the MI system
exhibits a relatively lower hydrogen bond proportion in the short bond
region than the AP system, with a correspondingly higher proportion in the
long bond region. These results demonstrate that the rigid structure of MI
constrains the formation of short hydrogen bonds, resulting in a locally less
compact hydrogen bond network.

Conclusion
In summary, this investigation presentsan innovative approach integrating
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) technology with large language
models to establish an ef ﬁcient electrolyte additive screening strategy.
Through systematic prompt engineering optimization, we achieved sig-

30.0% to 100% in benchmark tests, and provided systematic quantitative
analysis of molecular structure and steric effects in regulating solvation
behavior at the microscopic level. Our theoretical calculations and experi-

structure, forms a more compact sol vation shell (ranging from 456.49 to
550.15 Å³), signiﬁcantly reducing steric hindrance effects compared to MI’s
rigid cyclic structure (ranging from 695.91 to 711.01 Å³), achieving multi-
mode synergistic coordination with z inc ions. From sustainability per-
spectives, MI is commercially available at industrial scale, while AP offers
bio-compatibility, low toxicity, and biodegradability, with the low required
concentration (0.1 M, <1 wt%) minimizing material consumption. Through
these cooperative mechanisms, AP signiﬁcantly enhances zinc ion migra-

tical framework, with design principles derived from rigid versus ﬂexible
architectures offering guidelines for rational additive design.
This research demonstrates the practical effectiveness of AI-driven

In this study, a Python-based molecular information acquisition system was
developed, enabling automated data retrieval through the REST API of the
PubChem public database. The system extracts key molecular parameters
via HTTP requests, including molecular formula, SMILES structural
representation, molecular weight, XLogP, hydrogen bond donor count,
hydrogen bond acceptor count, and topological polar surface area (TPSA),
among other physicochemical properties. The system employs the pandas
library for structured data processing and utilizes a random sampling
method to select 22,000 samples from the compound ID (CIDs) range
within the PubChem database. The co nstructed database is stored in a
standardized markdown format, ach ieving systematic management of
molecular structures and their physicochemical property parameters.
For data processing and analysis, this study developed an intelligent
literature analysis system integrating Retrieval-Augmented Generation
(RAG) technology, utilizing the DeepSeek-V3 large language model for
literature analysis due to its superior performance in scientiﬁc text proces-
sing, and the Claude 3.7 Sonnet model for molecular screening tasks
leveraging its hybrid reasoning capabilities for complex property evaluation.
Through specially designed systematic prompt frameworks and multi-stage
optimization strategies, literature data extraction and molecular screening

MD simulation
Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were conducted using the Castep
module within the Materials Studio software suite. The system was modeled
using the Amorphous Cell module, and the initial con ﬁguration was
determined by selecting the lowest-energy framework from ten generated
snapshots. Before the simulation, the geometry of all components (water
molecules, sulfate ions, and additive molecules) was optimized to ensure
that the system started from a reasonable structural state with accurate
intermolecular charge distribution. The ZnSO
4 electrolyte system consisted
of 1500 water molecules, 100 sulfate ions, and 100 zinc ions. For the additive
system, 300 additive molecules were added separately to the base electrolyte,
resulting in three simulated systems: ZnSO 4, MI, and AP. Each system
underwent 20 steps of annealing treatment to ensure stability and reason-
able conﬁgurations, with the temperature range set between 300 –500 °C.
After annealing, the system was equilibrated under NPT conditions, with a
temperature of 298 K and pressure of 0.1 GPa, using the Berendsen method
for temperature and pressure control.The equilibration process lasted for
3 ns, with a decay constant of 0.1 ps. Subsequently, the simulation was
carried out under NVT conditions using the NHL thermostat, with a decay
constant of 1.0 ps. The total simulation time for each system was 7 ns, with
the ﬁrst 4 ns used for equilibration and the remaining 3 ns for data analysis.
The time step for all MD simulations was set to 1.0 fs, and the cutoff radii for
van der Waals (vdW) and electrostatic interactions were both set to 25 Å.
Long-range electrostatic interactions were treated using the Particle-Particle
Particle-Mesh (PPPM) solver with a precision of 1 × 10
–5.
