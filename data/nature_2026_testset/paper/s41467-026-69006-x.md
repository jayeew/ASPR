An efﬁcient carbon capture and release system necessitates rapid CO2 trans-
port to and from active sites, a propertytypically associated with permanently
porous materials featuring large surface areas. Here, we present hydrophobic
organic crystals of alkylated monoethanolamine that, despite their nonporous
nature, undergo a rapid and reversible solid-to-solid phase transition upon
CO₂ uptake and release. Exposure to CO

forming a stable carbamate, aided by intermolecular interactions involving the
long side chains. This process is fully reversible under practicalﬂue-gas CO

capture conditions (>0.6% CO2,0 −100% relative humidity) and enables low-

Herein, we report a solid-state chemisorption process with
hydrophobic organic crystals of monoalkylated monoethanolamines
(Cn−MEAs) that undergo complete and reversible structural rearran-
gement upon CO

conditions. Chain melting—thermally induced reversible hydrocarbon
chain disordering in solid-state —and recrystallization of the non-
porous organic crystals at ambient temperatures enable rapid CO 2
uptake and spontaneous solid-to-solid transformation (Fig. 1). These
organic crystals exhibit high thermal and oxidation stability, a CO 2
capture capacity of 2.5 mmol/g, recyclability (>200 cycles), and low
volatility, making them ideal for carbon capture applications. Com-
bined with the simplicity and accessibility of MEAs, this approach
enables a signiﬁcantly lower operating cost for post-combustion car-
bon capture processes.
Results
CO2 induced solid-to-solid crystal transformation
We previously reported a water-lean CO2 capture system with amphi-
philic absorbents of the Cn-MEAs driven by entropic advantages in
saline solutions
38. These absorbents were prepared via a one-step
modiﬁcation of the parent monoethanolamine (MEA). The scalable
synthesis was optimized to afford a crystalline product of high yield
and purity (up to 88%) after a single recrystallization step (Fig.2A), with
a representative absorbent 2-(decylamino)-ethanol (C10-MEA).
Qualitatively, C10-MEA appears as soft, plastic, colorless, needle-
like crystals with a vitreous luster. These crystals rapidly transition to a
chalky-white ﬁbrous form when exposed to pure CO
2 at room tem-
perature in solid state (Fig. 2B). Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA)
revealed a maximum theoretical uptake of 0.5 molar equivalents and a
high gravimetric uptake of 2.5 mmol/g within 7 min (Fig. S18). Scanning
electron microscopy (SEM) disclosed a substantial morphological
transformation of the C10-MEA absorbent upon CO
2 exposure. Initi-
ally, the material exhibits well-deﬁned, elongated microstructures with
smooth surfaces and distinct boundaries (Fig. 2C, red). After CO 2
treatment, the crystals exhibit extensive fragmentation and the
emergence of a highly intricate, ﬁbrous network (Fig. 2C, blue). This
qualitative change of the crystals implies a crystalline phase transition
upon the exposure to CO
2.
To investigate the structural changes of hydrophobic absorbents,
time-dependent in situ X-ray powder diffraction (XRPD) analysis was
performed on pristine C10-MEA. We observed a fully reversible tran-
sition between two crystalline phases at room temperature under a
CO
2 atmosphere (Fig.3A, left) and for the CO2-loaded C10-MEA sample
(denoted C10-MEA-CO2) under N2 atmosphere (Fig.3A, right). This set
of experiments led us to propose a continuous and reversible solid-to-
solid transformation induced by the presence and the absence of CO
2
as a stimulus. We tested a series of other alkylated absorbents (C11-
MEA, C12-MEA, C14-MEA, and C16-MEA), all of which exhibited similar
reversible behavior with CO
2 under XRPD analysis conditions
(Fig. S10A–E).
Attenuated total re ﬂectance infrared (ATR-IR) spectroscopic
analysis of C10-MEA and C10-MEA-CO2, at ambient conditions, enabled
us to identify a characteristic carbamate carbonyl stretch at 1628 cm−1,
along with N-H stretching and bending frequencies observed at
3200 cm
−1 (broad) and 1540 cm −1, respectively (Fig. 3B, blue and
Fig. S11). The band at 1409 cm −1 was attributed to the C-N stretching
frequency.
Crystallographic investigations of C10-MEA and C10-MEA-CO 2
proved the CO2-induced phase transition (Table S1). These analyses39
revealed molecular rearrangement and the formation of an ordered
organic crystal that accommodates CO2 through a complex interplay
of covalent and non-covalent interactions. The pristine C10-MEA
exhibits a layered herringbone arrangement stabilized by hydrogen
bonding and van der Waals interactions (Fig. 3C, left, Table S2, and
Fig. S22). Speciﬁcally, the OH proton interacts with the N atom of an
adjacent molecule in the same plane (O···N = 2.792(2) Å), while the
oxygen weakly interacts with the NH proton (N···O = 3.088(2) Å) from
the molecule in the plane above. In contrast, the CO
2-loaded organic
crystal, i.e., C10-MEA-CO2, is characterized by a motif of four distinct
molecules, i.e., two crystallographically independent ammonium-
carbamate pairs, arranged head-to-tail in a basket weave pattern
(Fig. 3C, right, and Fig. S24). Each carbamate group acts as an acceptor
of 4 H-bonds from OH and NH
2
+ groups, forming an extensive network
of hydrogen bonds, with D···A distances ranging from 2.69(2) to
2.88(2) Å that stabilizes the crystal structure (Table S6 and Fig. S23).

Hoff analysis of CO 2 capture processes with C10-MEA at a various

chemisorption and desorption energies underpins the reversibility of
C10-MEA for carbon capture via a selective carbamate formation
(chemisorption) and chain-melting mechanism (low temperature
desorption).

CO

exempliﬁed by C10-MEA, demonstrate a high CO2 capture capacity as a
single organic compound, surpassing many state-of-the-art

high-purity CO2 stream at as low as 65 °C owing to the temperature-

materials will pave the way for a paradigm shift toward sustainable,
cost-effective CO2 capture solutions, with profound implications for
designingﬂexible and responsive absorbents and advancing industrial
decarbonization.

Synthesis procedure for Cn-MEAs and other derivatives
All Cn-MEAs were synthesized following the general procedure for the
synthesis of C10-MEA by using an appropriate alkyl bromide. N-(2-
methoxyethyl)decan-1-amine and 2-(decyl(methyl)amino)ethan-1-ol
were synthesized using 2-methoxyethan-1-amine and 2-(methylamino)
ethan-1-ol respectively, instead of monoethanolamine.
General procedure. Monoethanolamine (0.992 mol, 60 mL, 4 eq.) was
added to 1.5 L of absolute EtOH in a 3 L three-necked round-bottomed
ﬂask. The power output of the heating jacket was adjusted to maintain
the solution at 70 °C. When the desired temperature was reached, a
60 mL syringeﬁlled with 1-bromodecane (0.243 mol, 51.5 mL, 1 eq.) was
attached to a syringe pump with a long needle going through the
septum of the three-necked ﬂask. After the setup was complete, slow
addition was initiated at the rate of 1 mL/h over 60 h. After completing
the addition of alkyl bromide, the mixture was left to react for 24 h.
The reaction mixture was then transferred to a round-bottomed
ﬂask, and all EtOH was evaporated. To the resulting residue, 500 mL of
H
2O was added. The crude compound was extracted with 3 × 300 mL
CHCl3. The combined organic layers were washed with 300 mL H 2O,
dried over Na2SO4, ﬁltered, and concentrated under reduced pressure.
To the resulting light brown solids, 100 mL of ethyl acetate was
added, and the mixture was heated to 78 °C with stirring. The light
brown solution was slowly cooled to room temperature and left
overnight under aﬂow of N
2, resulting in the formation of long, white,
needle-shaped crystals, which were collected by ﬁltration using a
Büchner funnel and dried under reduced pressure.
All Cn-MEAs were recrystallized following the general procedure.
N-(2-Methoxyethyl)decan-1-amine and 2-(decyl(methyl)amino)ethan-1-
ol were distilled.
