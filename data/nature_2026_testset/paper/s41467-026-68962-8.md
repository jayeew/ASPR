Human cytomegalovirus (HCMV) is associated with bile duct disorders in
immunocompetent and immunocompromised individuals, including scleros-
ing cholangitis, biliary structures, AIDS cholangiopathy, biliary atresia (BA),
and post-transplant biliary complications. Despite these clinical associations, it
remains unknown whether HCMV can directly infect the bile duct epithelium
and cause pathogenesis. Here, we establish a human iPSC-derived cholangio-
cyte-like cell (CLC) organoid model that can be infected by HCMV, which
results in reduced organoid growth, a deformed structure, and a loss of barrier
function. Bulk RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) showed that the HCMV-infected
organoids had highly enriched expression of genes involved in the
epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) pathway. Importantly, blockade of
TGF-β signalling abrogates the EMT induction effect in the HCMV-infected CLC
organoids. Furthermore, single-cell RNA-seq indicates that HCMV infection
occurred in different clusters with distinct viral and host gene expression
proﬁles and the analysis shows increased expression of EMT and TGF-β sig-
nalling-related genes. Theﬁndings are conﬁrmed in BA patient liver tissues by
examining HCMV-DNA
+ cells. Two modes of infection by HCMV are found in
these CLC organoids, which show productive and latency-like features. This
study shows that HCMV infection induces EMT in cholangiocytes that may
contribute to the cholangiopathy seen in various diseases.

cause cholangiopathy?

HCMV is a ubiquitous DNA betaherpesvirus that typically exhibits
two modes of infection. The ﬁrst involves productive infection in
human ﬁbroblasts, where viral replication occurs, leading to host cell
lysis due to the ordered expression cascade of the ~200 viral genes
encoded by the virus. The second mode is latent infection, which
primarily occurs in human CD34 + progenitor cells; in this mode, the
virus remains dormant, with only a small subset of viral genes being
expressed
13–21. Reactivation from latency could lead to diseases in the
liver, which has been reported in autoimmune hepatitis, cirrhosis, and
immunocompromised AIDS patients
3,22–26.
HCMV can infect infants through congenital infection, postnatal
infection (<2 weeks after birth), perinatal infection (3–12 weeks after
birth), or acquired infection (>12 weeks after birth). The incidence of
congenital HCMV infection ranges from 0.4 to 5%
27 and can result in
various symptoms, including jaundice, vision and hearing loss, sei-
zures, petechiae, and developmental motor delay. The HCMV infection
rate in the perinatal period could range from 10 to 15%28; this period is
important for the development of the biliary tract in the liver, and
HCMV-infected patients often suffer from liver injury, such as chole-
static hepatitis
29,30. In addition, perinatally infected patients were
found to have increased levels of bile duct and liver damage-related
molecules, includingγ-glutamyl transpeptidase (γ-GT), direct bilirubin
(DBIL), total bile acid (TBA), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), and
aspartate aminotransferase (AST)
31. A recent report showed that
murine CMV infection could lead to bile duct damage in mice based on
experiments on CD14
+ biliary epithelial cells31. Clinical treatment using
antivirals against HCMV (valganciclovir or ganciclovir) can improve
serological biomarkers and patient outcomes32,33, including cholestasis
(e.g., biliary atresia (BA)) in infants34–37.
BA is a rare disease (incidence of 1 in 5000 –200,000 live births)
that primarily affects newborns. It is characterised by cholestasis in the
biliary tract that results from inﬂammation andﬁbrosis of the epithelial
cells of the bile ducts (cholangiocytes)
38–40. There are four types of BA
that are identi ﬁed clinically: syndromic BA (presents with splenic
malformation), cystic BA (presents with obliterated bile duct), isolated
BA (the most common type, presents with in ﬂammation and atretic
biliary tree), and HCMV-associated BA (IgM
+ and associated with
perinatal infection)41. HCMV is a potential aetiological factor in this
multifactorial disease38,39,42,43. Although HCMV is the most studied
human virus in association with BA, de ﬁnitive experimental evidence
establishing a direct causative relationship between HCMV infection
and BA development is lacking
5,44,45. Clinical studies have reported that
9.5 to 30.5% of anti-HCMV IgM+ BA patients have elevated anti-HCMV
IgM antibody levels and a poor prognosis, with a lower rate of jaundice
clearance, higher incidence of cholangitis, and more severe liver
ﬁbrosis and in ﬂammation following Kasai's portoenterosomy
(KPE)
12,46–48.
Recent studies have suggested that genetic predisposition,
epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), and beta-amyloid peptide
play roles in the development of BA 49. EMT is the biological process
in which epithelial cells undergo a transformation and start
expressing mesenchymal cell markers. This transition often results
in the cell acquiring more progenitor-like characteristics. The phe-
nomenon has been observed in the biliary ducts of patients with
BA
50–52. The onset of EMT can be triggered and mediated by
inﬂammation, the transforming growth factor beta (TGF- β), and
hedgehog signalling pathways 53,54. Although these signalling path-
ways are essential for normal tissue maintenance and development,
their excessive activation can lead to substantial ﬁbrogenesis
because of EMT
50,55,56. Moreover, in a sea lamprey model, TGF- β was
found to be associated with the development of BA through an
analysis of different transcriptional stages leading up to the devel-
opment of the observed phenotype
57. In addition, TGF- β immuno-
histochemical staining was noted in the liver tissues of patients
with BA
58.
A few of the ~200 genes encoded by HCMV have potential roles in
liver and bile duct damage. Immediate-early gene 1 (IE1) was found to
be expressed in infected biliary epithelial cells
31 and could affect
CYP450 enzymes, altering metabolic pathways 59. IE2 regulates
SREBP1c, which could promote lipid accumulation and lead to hepatic
steatosis60. US28, a viral chemokine homologue, can induce cellular
signalling pathways, such as ROCK/ β-catenin signalling 61.I ng l i o -
blastomas, US28 can activate NF-κB and the IL-6-JAK1-STAT3 signalling
axis, which could induce a shift to a mesenchymal state62,a n dt r i g g e r
the RIP2/NF-κB signalling pathway, leading to the upregulation of the
expression of mesenchymal cell markers (N-cadherin and
vimentin)
63–65. UL144 can activate NF- κB in a TRAF6-dependent man-
ner, leading to the onset of in ﬂammation66, while US5-encoded
microRNA can promote TGF- β to mediate EMT and ﬁbrosis67.T h e
HCMV interleukin-10 (IL-10) homologue can induce the release of TGF-
β from monocytes, as well as the expression of IE1 protein, which
facilitates glioblastoma invasion68. However, there is a lack of viral gene
expression data for cholangiocytes and uncertainties surrounding
HCMV’s precise role in bile duct pathogenesis.
The uncertainties regarding the link between HCMV infection and
cholangiopathy are due to a lack of an appropriate model to investi-
gate the direct effects. To establish such a model, we used a human
induced pluripotent stem cell (hiPSC)-derived cholangiocyte-like cell
(CLC) organoid model
69 for HCMV infection. Although CLCs do not
recapitulate all the functions of cholangiocytes, they are a useful
model for studying the phenotype of the biliary tree, such as differ-
entiation phenotypes, pathological responses (e.g., ﬁbrosis and cho-
lestasis), and the destruction of the biliary structure to model
cholangiopathies such as BA
70. Using a GFP-expressing virus, our data
shows that HCMV can infect cholangiocytes to produce GFP+ and GFP-
cell sub-populations. The GFP+ cells exhibited characteristics of pro-
ductive infection with de novo synthesis of viral particles for trans-
mission. The GFP
- cells displayed a latency-like mode of infection, with
limited expression of a subset of viral genes according to single-cell
RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq). Five clusters of cholangiocytes were
identiﬁed, with one fully supporting HCMV lytic infection and showing
high GFP expression. Although bulk RNA-seq suggested the upregu-
lation of the EMT pathway, the inhibition of HCMV-induced TGF- β
signalling abrogated the increased expression of EMT and ﬁbrotic
genes following infection. In addition, HCMV infection disrupted the
integrity and barrier functions of the organoids. We also detected
HCMV viral DNA in cholangiocytes from 65.5% of the liver tissues
obtained from patients with BA compared with 35% of the non-BA liver
tissues. The HCMV-DNA
+ cells exhibited increased EMT-associated N-
cadherin and TGF-β protein expression. Taken together, theseﬁndings
demonstrate that HCMV can directly infect cholangiocyte organoids
and induce the TGF-β/EMT pathway in cholangiocytes.

HCMV can infect cholangiocyte organoids
Using the previously described hiPSC-derived cholangiocyte-like cell
(CLC) organoid model69, we performed direct HCMV infection using
the TB40/E-eGFP virus strain, which expresses GFP upon lytic infec-
tion. By day 4 post-infection (PI), GFP
+ signals in the CLC organoids
exposed to HCMV were observed (Fig. 1a). Compared with the mock-
infected circular organoids that increased in size over time, the HCMV-
infected organoids became deformed and were characterised by

+ (mean ± SD:
1.50 ± 0.67μm) and GFP¯ organoids (1.22 ± 0.94 μm) compared with
the mock-infected organoids (0.50 ± 0.13 μm) (Fig. 1a). The number
and size of the organoids in the HCMV-infected culture at day 4 PI were
similar to those in the mock infection but there was a signi ﬁcant
reduction at day 10 PI (Fig. 1b, c).

secretory function of the organoids. The mock-infected organoids
showed rhodamine accumulation in their cells and rhodamine
release into the lumen. Luminal accumulation was prevented by the
MDR1 inhibitor verapamil (Fig. 3f, g). The HCMV-infected CLC orga-
noids showed a diffused staining pattern, with rhodamine signals
accumulating toward the centre. Even after verapamil treatment, the
rhodamine dye continued to accumulate in the lumen of the HCMV-
infected CLC organoids (Fig. 3f, g). In addition, a colourimetric assay
found that HCMV infection resulted in decreased alkaline phospha-
tase (ALP) activity level, which indicates a loss of cholangiocyte
functionality (Fig. S5b). Together, these data show that HCMV
infection induced substantial changes in the barrier integrity and
function of the CLC organoids.

In addition to its role in mediating EMT in cancer 82–84, TGF- β is

Discussion
A causative role of HCMV in cholangiopathy has not been clearly
proven. Here, we provide the ﬁrst evidence that HCMV infection can
directly cause EMT, which underlies cholangiopathy, by establishing a
human iPSC-derived CLC organoid infection model. We demonstrated
that HCMV can infect CLC organoids through both productive and
latency-like modes and result in an overall deformed morphology and
a loss of structural integrity. Second, HCMV infection upregulated the
expression of genes associated with EMT and ﬁbrosis, which was
mediated by HCMV-induced TGF-β signalling. Third, HCMV infection
drastically affected the CLC organoids’ barrier functions. Fourth, our
scRNA-seq data revealed that HCMV infection can occur in different
cell clusters, potentially resulting in different outcomes for the infec-
ted cholangiocytes. Fifth, we detected higher levels of HCMV DNA
accompanied by higher TGF-β and N-cadherin expression in the cho-
langiocytes in BA liver tissues compared with non-BA liver tissues,
indicating a possible role for HCMV in mediating cholangiopathy.
Productive HCMV infection in human ﬁbroblasts or latent infec-
tion in myeloid progenitor stem cells is well established
105–107.P r e v i o u s
studies have reported that HCMV infection of endothelial cells results
in a persistent lytic infection characterised by lower production of
infectious virions compared with infection of ﬁbroblasts
108–110.T h i s
persistent infection in endothelial cells leads to chronic inﬂammation
due to the recruitment of neutrophils and monocytes111,112,i n d u c e st h e
expression of proinﬂammatory cytokines and cell adhesion molecules,
alters the integrity of the endothelium, and stimulates responses from
CD4
+ T cells113–118, which may lead to various diseases such as athero-
sclerosis and coronary heart disease 119,120. In BA, the pathogenesis
involving the epithelial cells lining the bile duct (cholangiocytes) may
be a response to HCMV infection. Others have suggested that HCMV
can infect bile duct epithelial cells via CD14/CD55
31, which has been
demonstrated in liver grafts8,121. Our detection of HCMV in both cho-
langiocytes and hepatocytes using a sensitive probe against HCMV
DNA con ﬁrms the previous ﬁndings. Additionally, the experimental
data showed that HCMV can infect CLC organoids, as demonstrated by
the presence of HCMV DNA and pp28 protein, the expression of viral
genes, the transmission of the virus to ﬁbroblasts, and the disruption
of the integrity and function of the cholangiocyte organoid layer. More
importantly, we discovered that not only did infection result in pro-
ductive infection, a portion of cells exhibited a latency-like infection, as
evidenced by the expression of a limited subset of viral genes,
including latency-associated genes, a low viral DNA copy number, a
lack of infectious virus production, and evidence of reactivation after
prolonged co-culture withﬁbroblasts. While HCMV-infected lytic cells
will likely rapidly undergo cell death, the persistence of latency-like
infected cells could be vital for the sustained and progressive bile duct
pathology observed in liver diseases such as BA. The roles of the
detected viral genes in cholangiocytes that may mediate the EMT and
the TGF-β pathway should be studied (Table S3) to determine if this is
the mechanism through which HCMV contributes to the development
or progression of BA and to explore their potential application as
biomarkers for the diagnosis of HCMV-related BA.
Intrahepatic small and large cholangiocytes line the small and
large bile ducts, respectively, in the afferent direction from the liver,
and they exhibit distinct transcriptional characteristics
122–124.T h e s et w o
types of cholangiocytes have distinct functions: small cholangiocytes
can respond to carbon tetrachloride-induced bile duct damage and are
considered proliferative or migratory, whereas large cholangiocytes
can respond to secretin and somatostatin, primarily have proliferative
functions during bile duct ligation, and display in ﬂammatory
activity
125–129. Studies have been conducted using cholangiocytes from
rodents, but there are no studies examining HCMV infection of small

Virus
The TB40/E-eGFP virus was generated by transfecting a bacterial arti-

effect was observed (at 10–14 days) before collecting the media, which
