Reporter gene assays are widely employed to investigate activation of G-protein-coupled receptors
(GPCRs). However, the increasing complexity of GPCR signaling — particularly the capability of many
receptors to couple with multiple G α subunits— has created a growing need for reliable methods to
dissect individual G α-mediated pathways. Recent insights into the crosstalk and interdependence
among Gα subfamilies have further complicated our understanding of the overarching GPCR signaling
landscape. While previous studies have linked speci ﬁcG α-proteins to distinct transcriptional
response elements, the precise speci ﬁcity of these associations remains unclear. Here, we
systematically identiﬁed the relationship between G α proteins and four commonly used GPCR-
regulated response elements — CRE, SRE, NFAT-RE, and SRF-RE — using a panel of G α knockout
(KO) cell lines. Contrary to the traditional model of near-exclusive G α–response element pairings, our
results reveal that each reporter is modulated by multiple G α subfamilies. Each response element
exhibited varying degrees of activation by different G α proteins, with CRE being predominantly
regulated by G αs/olf, and SRE, NFAT-RE, and SRF-RE primarily regulated by G αq/11. Based on these
results, we provide an updated framework that rede ﬁnes the subfamily-speci ﬁcG α regulation of
downstream transcriptional responses and underscores the need for caution when interpreting
reporter assays to assess G α-protein activity.

convert extracellular signals into intracellular responses. Upon activation,
GPCRs engage with Gα proteins to initiate a signaling cascade. The human
genome encodes 16 Gα proteins, which are grouped into four subfamilies—
Gαs/olf,G αi/o,G αq/11,a n dGα12/13— each of which regulates distinct second
messengers or downstream effectors, thereby eliciting a wide range of cel-
lular responses

Evaluation of GPCR-mediated transcriptional response using
parental cells
We ﬁrst examined a total of nine Family A GPCRs (Supplementary Table 1),
namely theβ3 adrenergic receptor (β3), vasopressin type 2 receptor (V2), μ-
opioid receptor (μ), histamine 1 receptor (H1), angiotensin II type 1 receptor
(AT1), thromboxane A 2 receptor (TP), neurotensin receptor 1 (NTS 1),
cholecystokinin A receptor (CCK1), and neurokinin 1 receptor (NK1)i nt h e
parental HEK293 cells. These nine GPCRs were chosen to encompass
receptors with both selective and promiscuous coupling proﬁl e sa sc u r a t e d
from the database (Supplementary Fig. 1). We selectedβ3 over the otherβ-
adrenergic receptor subtypes due to its low endogenous expression in the
HEK293 cells and minimal basal activity, which provided a higher signal-to-
background ratio. The remaining eight GPCRs also exhibit low levels of
endogenous expression, with the exception of TP, which is known to have a
relatively higher endogenous expression in HEK293 cells. For each of the
nine GPCRs, we assessed agonist-inducedtranscriptional activation across
four transcriptional response elements (CRE, SRE, NFAT-RE, and SRF-RE)
(Fig. 1). All tested GPCRs exhibited robust, concentration-dependent
reporter activation at least by one reporter, whereas mock-transfected cells
showed no detectable activity, indicating that the observed activities were
solely attributable to exogenously expressed receptors.
For each GPCR, we compared log-transformed relative intrinsic
activities (LogRAi; see“Materials and methods” section for calculation) of
reporter responses with the coupling proﬁles for detailed examination of
transcriptional activity patterns (Fig. 2). We employed LogRAi as the
parameter accounts for both E
max and EC 50,w h e r eE C50 reﬂects the
receptor’s intrinsic Gα-protein activation properties. Based on hierarchical
clustering of coupling proﬁles, we categorized the receptors into four clus-
ters. β3 and V2 were characterized by dominant CRE activation relative to
other response elements, consistent with primary Gαs/olf-protein coupling. μ
selectively induced SRF-RE activity. H1,N T S1,A T1,T P ,C C K1,a n dN K1
comprise receptors with strong G αq/11-protein coupling. Notably, these
receptors displayed relatively promiscuous reporter activation compared
with the other receptors, suggesting that Gαq/11 proteins may broadly reg-
ulate multiple response elements. These observations are further supported
by hierarchical clustering of LogRAi values, which segregated the reporters
into three groups: CRE, NFAT-RE, and a combined SRE/SRF-RE cluster.
The close association of SREand SRF-RE suggests shared Gα-protein reg-
ulatory mechanisms.

dual G α subfamilies for each transcriptional element using genetically
modiﬁed cell lines. We employed a panel of individual G α-subfamily
knockout (KO) HEK293 cell lines (ΔGα

highlight the central role of the Gαq/11 subfamily in mediating NFAT-RE
activation, while also indicating that other Gα proteins provide a secondary
contribution to the overall transcriptional response.
Discussion
This study investigates GPCR-media ted transcriptional regulation by
directly evaluating activation of four response elements— CRE, SRE, SRF-
RE, and NFAT-RE. Although the prevailing model depicts a near one-to-
one relationship between the Gα-protein subfamily and response element2,
our ﬁndings reveal that this relationship is considerably more complex than
previously assumed. We show that these response elements detect signals
from a wide range of G α-protein subfamilies, serving as relatively non-
selective reporters of G α-subfamily activity in GPCR signaling assays
(Fig. 6). These results suggest that GPCRs, through engagement of multiple
Gα-signaling pathways, generate an intricate and promiscuous signaling
network that governs transcriptional gene regulation.
Overall, our results reveal that classical response element –based
reporter assays lack the Gα-protein selectivity that has often been assumed,
thereby limiting their utility for pathway-speciﬁc interpretations of GPCR
signaling. We agree that the activities measured using these reporters can
provide coarse insights into the GPCR activity and the Gα-protein coupling
proﬁles of GPCRs
26,27. However, our ﬁndings show that the response ele-
ments tested here are inﬂuenced by signals from multiple Gα subfamilies
and thus exhibit substantially lower selectivity than previously appreciated.
This limitation of the transcriptional reporter assay is further compounded
by the fact that our study was perf ormed in HEK293 cells using repre-
sentative family A GPCRs, and it remains unclear whether similar patterns

the Gα-subfamily KO cells may also be explained by common signaling
molecules that contribute to transcri ptional regulation independently of
speciﬁcG α proteins. One such molecule is the G βγ subunits of the het-
erotrimeric G-protein complex. In the GPCR signaling paradigm, G βγ
subunits released upon Gα activation enable themto regulate downstream
effectors. Previous studies have demonstrated that Gβγ subunits participate
in multiple Gα-mediated pathways. For example, Gβγ enhances Gαs-pro-
tein-mediated cAMP generatio n from endosomal compartments 33,34.
Because endosomal G αs-protein signaling is known to enhance nuclear
localization of CREB23, this suggests a role for G βγ subunits in regulating
CRE activity. Furthermore, Gβγ subunits contribute to Gαq/11–PLCβ sig-
naling as well as the RhoGEF–RhoA pathway35,36. Therefore, the reduction
in total Gα-protein levels in the Gα-subfamily KO cells may limit the pool of
dissociated Gβγ subunits after agonist stimulation, thereby contributing to
the observed decrease in reporter activity.
Apart from such a common molecule afﬁliated to Gα-protein signal,
Gα protein–response element promiscuity may also arise from effectors that
broadly facilitate the activation of four transcriptional response elements. A
key example is ERK. Past studies have shown that ERK modulates key
transcription factors and kinases associated with the response elements
studied. For instance, MEK –ERK axis promotes the phosphorylation of
CREB
37; ERK also facilitates SRF activation by promoting nuclear translo-
cation of megakaryoblastic leukemia 1 (MKL1) 38, and enhances NFAT-
mediated transcription through complex formation with
calcineurin–NFAT
39. These past ﬁndings support the notion that reduced
reporter activity observed in the Gα-subfamily KO cells may, at least in part,
reﬂect attenuated ERK signaling.
Although SRE has long been used as a readout of ERK –TCF sig-
naling, its regulation in the context of GPCR pathways remains poorly
deﬁned. TCF activation has been attributed to ERK-mediated phos-
phorylation, a concept primarily supported by studies in receptor tyrosine
kinase (RTK) pathways
40. These earlier ﬁndings led to the prevailing view
that SRE is regulated downstream of G proteins, distinct from those
regulating SRF-RE. However, our results show that SRE and SRF-RE are
regulated in a similar manner. Notably, to the best of our knowledge, no
direct evidence currently demonstrates ERK-dependent TCF activation
downstream of GPCR signaling. Given the mechanistic distinctions
between RTK and GPCR pathways
41,42, it is plausible that GPCR-mediated
TCF regulation may operate in a more nuanced manner compared to
RTKs. These ﬁndings suggest the need to reconsider the conventional
view of SRE and SRF-RE speci ﬁcity and to further investigate the mole-
cular underpinnings of SRF and TCF regulation in the context of GPCR
signaling.
In conclusion, we present a revised model of G α protein–response
element coupling that departs from the classical paradigm. Our ﬁndings
demonstrate that each response element exhibits considerable promiscuity,
integrating signals from multiple Gα protein subfamilies. Altogether, the
multiplexed regulatory mechanism revealed in the current study suggests
that transcriptional regulation by GPCRs is more complex and integrative
than previously appreciated.
