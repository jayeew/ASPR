Noble metal-based core/shell catalysts show great promise for ethanol oxi-
dation reaction (EOR) through regulateddehydrogenation or bond-breaking,
yet their performance remains constrained by limited interphase electron
interactions accessible through conventional structure-conserving modula-
tion strategies. Herein, we report a strategy of conﬁned heterostructural
transformation within the core to enhance interphase electron interactions,
electrochemically transforming ordered hexagonal close-packed (HCP)/ face-
centered cubic (FCC) PtPdBiSn nanoplates into disordered hexagonal non-
close-packed (H-nCP)/FCC heterostructured catalyst for efﬁcient EOR.
Advanced characterization and density functional theory (DFT) calculations
reveal that the conﬁned transformation induces interphase electron redis-
tribution: electron transfer fromcore to shell. This effect reconﬁgures the
electronic structure, optimizing intermediates' adsorption energetics and
reaction energy barriers. This reconstructed catalyst exhibits the mass and
speciﬁc activity of 28.17 A mg
−1
NM (noble metals) (18.4 × Pt/C) and 85.7 mA cm−2
(26.1 × Pt/C), retains 80.3% activity after 20,000 cycles, and demonstrates
enhanced CO tolerance. This work transcends conventional Pt-based HCP/FCC
heterostructure design by elucidating conﬁned heterostructural
transformation-induced interphase electron redistribution enhancement
mechanisms, offering a potential strategy for developing efﬁcient analogous
catalysts.
Direct ethanol fuel cells (DEFCs) have attracted signi ﬁcant attention
for their high energy density, safety, environmental compatibility, and
ease of utilization
1–3. However, the commercialization of DEFCs is
hindered by the sluggish kinetics of the ethanol oxidation reaction
(EOR), which involves intricate C –C bond cleavage and multi-step
dehydrogenation steps requiring multifunctional active sites, coupled
with the low catalytic activity and poor stability of anode materials
4–6.

Results
Synthesis of ER-PtPdBiSn HPs
The synthesis of electrochemically reconstructed PtPdBiSn hexagonal
nanoplates (ER-PtPdBiSn HPs) involves two key steps. Firstly, PtPdBiSn
hexagonal nanoplates (PtPdBiSn HPs) were synthesized by a two-step
heating method in an oil bath with a holding time of 1 hour at both
80 °C and 220 °C utilizing platinum acetoacetone [Pt(acac)
2], palla-
dium acetylacetonate [Pd(acac) 2], bismuth acetate [Bi(act) 3], and
stannous chloride (SnCl2) as metal precursors, ascorbic acid (AA) as
reducing agent, cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) as dis-
persant, and oleylamine (OAM) and octadecene (ODE) as solvents. ER-
PtPdBiSn HPs were then obtained from PtPdBiSn HPs by cyclic vol-
tammetry (CV) scanning in Ar-saturated alkaline ethanol electrolyte at
a slow scan rate for 25-30 cycles. As a class of Pt-based core/shell
structured nanomaterials, we have employed a range of characteriza-
tion techniques to investigate the structures of PtPdBiSn HPs and ER-
PtPdBiSn HPs.
Characterization of (ER-)PtPdBiSn HPs
Electrochemical cycling drives the reconstruction in PtPdBiSn HPs,
converting the ordered hexagonal close-packed (HCP) core into a
disordered hexagonal non-close-packed (H-nCP) phase to establish H-
nCP/FCC heterostructures (Fig. 1a). This structural reorganization,
accompanied by the dissolution of Sn and the increase of the ratio of Bi
atoms in the core, reconﬁgures the atomic coordination and electronic
landscape. The transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and high-
angle annular dark- ﬁeld scanning transmission electron microscopy
(HAADF-STEM) images con ﬁrm the synthesis of monodisperse
PtPdBiSn HPs with well-deﬁned crystallinity (Fig. 1b and Supplemen-
tary Fig. 1), exhibiting an average diameter of 20.52 ± 0.06 nm, with a
thickness of about 16.23 nm as quantiﬁed by atomic force microscopy
(AFM; Supplementary Fig. 2). In contrast, TEM, HAADF-STEM, and
high-magniﬁcation HAADF-STEM images reveal that ER-PtPdBiSn HPs
still maintain hexagonal morphology but exhibit obvious changes in
the core/shell lining (Fig. 1c, d and Supplementary Fig. 3). This mor-
phological evolution con ﬁrms that the electrochemical process
reconﬁgures the heterostructure. The selected-area electron diffrac-
tion (SAED) pattern conﬁrms that the phase transformation from an
ordered HCP to a disordered H-nCP structure at a macroscopic scale
(Supplementary Fig. 4). The X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern conﬁrms
that PtPdBiSn HPs exhibit an HCP structure analogous to binary PtBi/
PtSn intermetallics (Supplementary Fig. 5), with peak shifts arising
from quaternary alloying-induced lattice distortion while retaining
structural coherence. The ratio of Pt/Pd/Bi/Sn is determined by
inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectrometer (ICP-OES)
to be 35.2/12.2/31.6/21.0 (Supplementary Fig. 6), which is consistent
with scanning electron microscope energy dispersive spectrometry
(SEM-EDS) results (34.6/14.4/26.2/24.8). Moreover, comparative
aberration-corrected (AC)-HAADF-STEM-EDS elemental mappings
reveal distinct redistribution dynamics: pristine PtPdBiSn HPs exhibit
uniform elemental distribution, with Bi preferentially localized in the
core (Fig. 1e and Supplementary Fig. 7), while ER-PtPdBiSn HPs
demonstrate Bi retention in the core alongside Pt/Pd/Sn surface
enrichment (Fig.1f and Supplementary Fig. 8). The atomic structure of
PtPdBiSn HPs is uncovered by the AC-HAADF-STEM characterizations.
AC-HAADF-STEM images along the [001] direction reveal a periodic
stacking sequence in PtPdBiSn HPs mirroring binary HCP PtBi/PtSn
intermetallics (Fig.1g and Supplementary Fig. 9)

tion modiﬁes the interphase electron interactions (interphase electron
redistribution) between the core and shell. Charge density difference
calculations and Bader charge statistics reveal the complete polarity
reversal and transfer ampliﬁcation (Fig.3a and Supplementary Fig. 30):
the pristine HCP/FCC heterostructure in PtPdBiSn HPs exhibits elec-

amplifying electron transfer by 2.38 ×. Thereby, this modulation,
direction reversal coupled with transfer enhancement, establishes
strong interphase electron interactions, concentrating electron den-

regions. Further two-dimensional cross-sectional analysis of the

Discussion
Through electrochemical reconstruction, we transform HCP/FCC
heterostructured PtPdBiSn HPs into H-nCP/FCC heterostructured ER-
PtPdBiSn HPs. In the con ﬁned transformation, the H-nCP/FCC struc-
ture exhibits an interphase electr on redistribution, promoting the
enrichment of electrons occurring in the shell layer. This effect alters
the electronic structure of the heterostructure, including enhancingd-
p orbital coupling, elevating the d-band center, and intensifying elec-
tron localization near the E
F. The ER-PtPdBiSn HPs/C catalyst exhibits
the mass and speci ﬁc activity of 28.17 A mg −1
NM (18.4 × Pt/C) and
85.7 mA cm−2 (26.1 × Pt/C), and retains 80.3% activity after 20,000
cycles, alongside enhanced *OH adsorption and CO tolerance. DFT
calculations further reveal that the H-nCP/FCC heterostructure opti-
mizes the adsorption energies of key intermediates and reduces
reaction energy barriers. This work demonstrates that inducing het-
erostructural transformation within the core of the core/shell serves as
an effective strategy to enhance interphase electron interactions. More
broadly, it establishes core phase transformation engineering as a
viable design principle for regulating interphase electronic structure in
multimetallic heterogeneous systems, providing both theoretical gui-
dance and experimental validation for developing advanced
electrocatalysts.

Computational setup
All of the calculations are performed in the framework of the spin-
polarized density functional theory with the projector augmented
plane-wave method, as implemented in the Vienna ab initio simulation
package (VASP)
68,69. The core/shell heterostructures of HCP/FCC and
H-nCP/FCC were constructed based on the experimental results of
PtPdBiSn HPs and ER-PtPdBiSn HPs, respectively. The lattice constants
were initially set to a = 11.288 Å, b = 8.328 Å. The generalized gradient
approximation (GGA) proposed by Perdew, Burke, and Ernzerhof
(PBE) is selected for the exchange-correlation potential
70,71. The long-
range van der Waals interaction is described by the DFT-D3 approach72.
The cut-off energy for the plane wave is set to 480 eV. The energy
criterion is set to 10
−5 eV in the iterative solution of the Kohn-Sham
equation. All the structures are relaxed until the residual forces on the
atoms have declined to less than 0.02 eV/Å. Data analysis and visuali-
zation are carried out with the help of the VASPKIT code and VESTA
73,74.
The diffusion barrier at different adsorption sites was explored by
using the Nudge Elastic Band (CI-NEB) method75.
