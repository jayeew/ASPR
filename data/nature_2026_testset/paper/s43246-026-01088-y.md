Interfaces play a crucial role in strain hardening, slip accommodation, and plasticﬂow stability of dual-
phase alloys. The orientation relationship (OR) between phases critically governs the crystallographic
discontinuity and imposes geometric constraints on the activation of deformation modes. In dual-
phase 19Al-20Fe-20Co-41Ni high-entropy alloys, we demonstrate that weakening the Kurdjumov-
Sachs (K-S) OR between the L1
2 and B2 phases via moderate plastic deformation and
recrystallization, signiﬁcantly improves ductility without sacriﬁcing strength. The threefold increase in
ductility compared to the as-cast alloy is attributed to a B2 →body-centered tetragonal (BCT)
transformation, promoted by weakening the interface OR. In contrast, the as-cast alloy with K-S OR
constrained B2 remains untransformed. In situ synchrotron tensile testing con ﬁrms the phase
transformation evolves progressively. Our analysis of the expected strain contribution reveals that
transformation preferentially occurs in B2 grains adjacent to highly deformable neighbors. These
ﬁndings highlight the critical role of OR in governing phase transformation and deformation.

tightly constrained by surrounding L1₂ lamellae with a K-S OR, remains
stable and does not undergo transformation.
Results
Interface orientation weakened by thermo-mechanical
treatments
The NE-cast/NE-T alloys exhibit a dual-phase structure, as shown in the
XRD pattern (Fig. 1a), consisting of an FCC structure and an ordered B2
phase, evidenced by the distinct (100) su perlattice diffraction peak. TEM
analyses (Fig. 1b) using selected area electron diffraction (SAED) further
conﬁrm this dual-phase structure, with clear superlattice spots corre-
sponding to the ordered L1 2 and B2 structures. EBSD phase mappings
(Fig. 1c) demonstrate that both NE-cast/NE-T alloys have a heterogeneous
dual-phase lamellar structure along with some coarse grains. Additionally,
Fig. 1c1 reveal that that the NE-cast alloy exhibits a lamellar microstructure
with two characteristic layer thicknesses, averaging ~3.0μm and ~10.0 μm.
In addition to the lamellae, coarse grains are identiﬁed as B2 phase with an
average equivalent diameter of ~29.1μm. Notably, in the NE-cast alloy, the
lamellar regions consist of large, continuous grains extending across mul-
tiple layers, as indicated by the limited number of grain boundaries within
individual lamellae. Although the B2 and L1 2 phases are conventionally
associated with the stoichiomet ric compositions of NiAl and Ni 3Al,
respectively, our alloy exhibits no table deviations from these ideal
formulations. Speciﬁcally, the L1 2 phase has an average composition of
12.5Al-24.7Fe-23.1Co-40.2Ni (at%), while the B2 phase consists of 24.8Al-
15.6Fe-17.0Co-42.6Ni (at%). When compared to the overall alloy compo-
sition of 18.8Al-19.6Fe-19.9Co-41.7Ni (at%), the Ni content remains nearly
constant across both phases. The primary compositional variation arises
from the partitioning of the Al element, which is apparently enriched in the
B2 phase and depleted in the L1
2 phase. After cold rolling and subsequent
annealing at high temperature (Fig. 1c2), these lamellar grains undergo
deformation and full recrystallization. As summarized in Supplementary
Table 1, the resulting recrystallized lamellae exhibit increased thicknesses of
~7.0 and ~15.0 μm. Within these layers, most recrystallized B2 and L1

grains exhibit near-equi axed morphologies, with average grain sizes of
~14.5 μma n d~ 1 2 . 3μm, respectively, along the lamellar direction. Their
widths are limited by the layer thickness due to phase boundaries.
Quantitative analyses were conducted using several large-area EBSD
datasets. Supplementary Fig. 1 shows the representative phase maps toge-
ther with the corresponding inverse poleﬁgures (IPFs) for the NE-cast and
NE-T samples. We performed a systematic comparison between the com-
monly reported FCC/BCC ORs, namely the K-S and Nishiyama-
Wasserman (N-W) ORs. The results indicate that the majority of L1

interfaces in the NE-cast sample follow the K-S ORs. As shown in Fig.1d1
(total analyzed area >4 mm2), more than half the phase boundaries show
K-S orientational relationships within a deviation angleΔθ < 3°. In contrast,
the thermo-mechanically treated (NE-T) alloy exhibits a more random

2),

recrystallization process, during which stored defects such as dislocations
are annihilated or rearranged, therebyproviding numerous nucleation sites
for new grains with diverse crystallographic orientations.
Ductility enhanced by new emergence of BCT phase
Mechanical properties of the NE-cast alloy and the NE-T alloy were com-
pared by conducting in/ex-situ tensi le tests. Since plastic deformation
during rolling may introduce crystallographic texture and be constrained by
lamellar structure, six NE-T tensile specimens were machined along dif-
ferent orientations with respect to the rolling direction (0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 75°,
and 90°) to assess potential mechanical anisotropy. As shown in the stress-
strain curves (Fig.2a), all NE-T samples exhibit nearly identical mechanical
responses, indicating minimal lamellar orientation dependence. The NE-
cast alloy has a ductility less than 5%, due to the coarse primary B2 phase. As
shown in Supplementary Fig. 2, the fracture surface reveals prominent
cracks propagating through the coarse B2 grains. The NE-T alloy demon-
strates a remarkable improvement in ductility (~18 %), while maintaining
comparable yield strength (~500 MPa) and ultimate tensile strength
(~1050 MPa). Interestingly, the NE-cast sample shows a signiﬁcantly higher
strain-hardening rate than the NE-T samples. Such a high hardening rate
could be attributed to the geometrical constraints associated with strong OR
interfaces
33. In contrast, the evolution of strain-hardening rate with strain
(Fig. 2b) reveals a low initial work-hardening capacity in the NE-T samples,
followed by gradual increase after ~5% strain. This behavior suggests a
transition in the dominant deformation mechanisms as plastic strain
progresses.
The XRD pattern of NE-T sample after tensile deformation (Fig. 2c)
reveals the coexistence of L1

to the (111) plane in the L1

existence of all three phases after deformation. EBSD phase mapping

Discussion
The B2→BCT phase transformation results in a signiﬁcant elongation along
the <001> direction35,36, and thus it is typically activated under high-pressure
environments23,24 or certain compositional conditions37–39.F o ral a y e r e d
structure, such a large uniaxial expansion (~10.5%) will impose considerable
mechanical constraints on the surrounding L12 grains. Correspondingly,
the L12 grains must activate appropriate slip systems to absorb or redis-
tribute the strain generated by the expanding BCT phase. In the present
study, tensile testing revealed that the NE-cast sample did not undergo a
B2→BCT phase transformation. In contrast, such a transformation was
clearly observed in the NE-T sample. Microstructural analysis further
indicates a weakening of the interface orientation between the L1

phases in the NE-T samples, suggest ing that this weakened interfacial
orientation may facilitate the B2→BCT transformation.

interphase constraints and slip system compatibility in governing defor-

Methods
Material preparation
The near-eutectic 19Al-20Fe-20Co-41Ni (at%) alloy (labeled as NE-cast)
was fabricated from commercially pure elements (purity >99.9 wt%) in a
vacuum induction melting furnace into an ingot of ϕ100 × 200 mm2.T o
mitigate chemical segregation introduced during solidiﬁcation, the as-cast
ingot was homogenized at 1200 °C for 2 h after casting. The phase evolution
observed experimentally is in good agreement with thermodynamic pre-
dictions obtained via Thermo-Calc calculations (TCNi10 database, Sup-
plementary Fig. 7), which indicate th e initial formation of a primary B2
phase from the melt, followed by the concurrent solidiﬁcation of B2 and L1
2
phases through a eutectic reaction.
Furthermore, these ordered intermetallic phases (B2 and L1 2)a r e
thermodynamically predicted to transform into disordered BCC and FCC
phases upon annealing at lower temperatures. To verify the precipitation
behavior of the alloy, samples were annealed for 1 h at different tempera-
tures after rolling. Disordered FCC precipitates were observed in the matrix
when the alloy was annealed at 1100 °C and below (Supplementary Fig. 8).
Therefore, an annealing temperature of 1200 °C was selected for the present
study. The homogenized sample was then cold-rolled (CR) with a thickness
reduction of 30%, followed by annealing at 1200 °C for 1 h. Rapid quenching
in water was employed immediately afterward to suppress secondary phase
precipitation. A second CR –annealing cycle was conducted to further
reduce the inherited casting orientation. The resulting alloy is designated as
NE-T.
The actual alloy composition, determined by inductively coupled
plasma optical emission spectroscopy (ICP-OES, Avio 500), was 18.8Al-
19.6Fe-19.9Co-41.7Ni (at%). The precise chemical compositions of each
phase in both NE-cast and NT-T samples were measured using an electron
probe micro-analyzer (EPMA) equipped with a wavelength-dispersive
spectrometer (WDS) and summarized in Supplementary Table 4. Overall,
the measured bulk composition is in close agreement with the nominal alloy
design.
Mechanical property measurements
Flat dog-bone-shaped tensile sp ecimens with a gauge dimension of
4×2×1m m 3 were cut sectioned at various angles relative to the rolling
direction (0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 75°, and 90°) using electrical discharge machining
(EDM). Prior to testing, all specimens were mechanically polished using
5000-grit SiC abrasive paper to ensure a uniform surface ﬁnish. Uniaxial
tensile testing was performed at room temperature on a universal test
machine (MTEST 5000 W Tensile Stage, GATAN) at a strain rate of
1×1 0
−3 s−1. To further investigate the macroscopic deformation behavior,
digital image correlation (DIC) was conducted on larger tensile specimens
with gauge dimensions of 12.5 × 4 × 2 mm³ using an Instron 5982 tensile
testing machine.
Microstructural characterization
Microstructural characterization was conducted using a Zeiss Gemini 300
scanning electron microscope (SEM) operated at an accelerating voltage of
20 kV. Electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) analysis was performed on
the same instrument equipped with an EBSD detector, using a 30 kV
accelerating voltage and a step size of 0.5 μm. To ensure high-quality EBSD
data acquisition, the specimens were sequentially ground with SiC abrasive
papers ranging from 300 to 5000 grit, followed by polishing with 3 μm
diamond suspension andﬁnal ﬁnishing using a 50 nm colloidal silica (SiO
2)
suspension. EBSD data were processed and analyzed using the MTEX
toolbox in MATLAB.

of 2°/min. Synchrotron radiation X-ray diffraction (SXRD) measurement
