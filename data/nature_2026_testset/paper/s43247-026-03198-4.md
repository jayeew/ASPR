The escalating frequency of extreme heatwaves coupled with the deep decarbonization of the power
system pose growing challenges to the reliable operation of metropolitan power systems. In megacity
clusters, heatwave-induced blackouts leave densely populated and vulnerable communities exposed
to prolonged thermal stress, signi ﬁcantly elevating urban health risks. In response, we proposes a
health-aware decarbonization framework that integrates meteorology, power systems, and urban
health— offering a comprehensive, systems-level solution to support the development of resilient, and
sustainable cities under intensifying climate stress. Our results indicate that while decarbonizing the
power system is crucial for meeting climate goals, it may unintentionally increase heat-related deaths
in large urban areas. In the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao megacity cluster, the number of cities with
excess death rates over 3% is expected to rise from 1 in 2030 to 9 by 2050. However, health-aware
decarbonization strategies can cut excess deaths by 55.38% -65.01% and reduce total annual costs
by 8.71% -13.63%.

also increase the likelihood of power outages in densely populated urban
grids. As illustrated in Fig. 1, the extensive use of air conditioning in the

factors often result in unforeseen po wer failures. Power outages during
heatwaves expose vulnerable urban populations to extreme temperatures,
heightening the risk of mortality. With the future deep decarbonization of
the power system, these urban health risks are likely to worsen, as the grid’s
ability to cope with heatwaves may be further reduced.

In this study, we explored the link between power system dec-
arbonization and urban health resilience in a megacity cluster, proposing a
health-aware decarbonization framework for the urban power grid. This
approach accounts for the impact of outages caused by high temperatures
on human health under the inevitable trends of global warming and the
widespread adoption of renewable energy. By integrating meteorological,
energy, and public health data, we quantify the associated health risks and
economic losses under future heatwave scenarios aligned with ambitious
carbon neutrality goals. To uncover the interconnections between
meteorology, power, and urban health, we ﬁrst develop a heat exposure-
mortality model to accurately esti mate excess deaths during heatwave-
induced power outages. This model incorporates the mechanisms of
heatwave-induced mortality, capturing the effects of both current and
cumulative heat exposure on mortality risk. The model’s parameters are
calibrated using real-world data on excess deaths during heatwaves. Next, to
characterize heatwave-induced power outages, we construct an annual
operation model for urban power grids that incorporates the impacts of
heatwave. This model uses hourly power demand data and detailed multi-
voltage power network conﬁgurations to simulate power outage events with
high precision. Finally, to mitigate urban health risks, we strategically deploy
power generation resources and energy storage to enhance the resilience of
the power grid through a health-awaredecarbonization model. This model
not only captures the diversity and uncertainty of the power grid’sh o u r l y
operations but also incorporates future projections of increased extreme
heatwave frequency and associated social health risks for urban
communities.
We take the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao megacity cluster— one of
China’s three world-class megacity clusters— as a case study to explore the
interconnection between power system decarbonization and urban health
resilience. The results indicate that the pursuit of carbon neutrality can
substantially intensify electricity shortages and excess mortality during
heatwaves, particularly in the densely populated and energy-intensive core
areas. The average share of heatwave-related deaths in total annual mortality
across the region is projected to rise from 0.47% in 2030 to 2.78% by 2050,
with about 40% of cities expected to exceed a 3% share. To mitigate the
excess deaths under power system decarbonization, we analyzed prospective
expansion planning schemes from 2030 to 2050 that integrate urban health
considerations into the decarbonization process. Our ﬁndings show that
strategic investments can signiﬁcantly reduce excess deaths during heat-
waves by 55% to 65% with only a 14% to 18% increase in power generation
resources. However, relying solely on power generation resources such as
renewable generation and gas turbines for resilience enhancement can be
cost-prohibitive due to the signiﬁcant waste of generation capacity caused by
heatwave-driven spikes in demand. Integrating energy storage, such as
hydrogen, into the urban power grid offers an effective solution for opti-
mizing resource utilization
44. The simulation results show that an increase in
capital investment of about 15% for h ydrogen storage can reduce load
shedding by approximately 35% compared to the baseline level. Further-
more, optimizing load-shedding schedules through a balanced generation
mix can signi ﬁcantly reduce daily cumulative heat exposure and, conse-
quently, urban health risks. In particular, photovoltaic (PV) power gen-
eration synergizes well with ambient temperature levels so that a larger share
of PV generation tends to reduce heat exposure risks. Beyond health ben-
eﬁts, proposed framework delivers growing economic gains. As capital costs
for clean energy technologies decline and heatwaves become more frequent,
the total annual cost savings are projected to increase from 8.71% in 2030 to
13.63% in 2050. These results underscore the importance of integrating
public health resilience into sustai nable energy transition strategies —
ensuring that cities remain both livable and climate-resilient under future
extremes.
Results
Model and scenarios
We utilize real data from the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao megacity
cluster as a credible example to discuss this issue (Fig. 2 (a)). This urban
agglomeration, located in southern China, spans approximately
180,900 km
2 and is home to over 135 million people. It experiences a typical
southern subtropical monsoon marine climate and holds signi ﬁcant
potential for urban-scale renewable energy development. While actively
advancing toward carbon neutrality, the region is also increasingly exposed
to extreme heatwave events, making it a compelling case for exploring the
interconnection between urban power systems, climate conditions, and
metropolitan urban health risks. Accordingly, we assess the spatial and
temporal distribution of urban health risks associated with power system
decarbonization in the megacity cluster from 2030 to 2050.
To mitigate the impacts of heatwaves on power system operation and
urban health risk, we propose a health-aware decarbonization framework
for urban power grids (Fig. 2 (b)). This framework incorporates two key
models: a temperature-power imbalance model, which simulates power
outages during heatwaves, and a hea t exposure-mortality model, which
estimates the relative risk of death (urban health risk) due to heat exposure,
to strengthen grid and health resilience. Details on the modeling framework,
formulation, assumptions, and parameters are provided in the“Methods”
section and the supplementary material section 1.
To accurately evaluate and effectively mitigate health risks associated
with heatwaves, the proposed healt h-aware decarbonization method
incorporates several key factors and novel features as follows.
1) Precisely modeling the spatial an d temporal distribution of power
outages is essential for assessing urban health risks. To this end, we
selected the Zhuhai power grid— part of the Guangdong-Hong Kong-
Macao megacity cluster— as a case study to explore health-conscious
decarbonization pathways for urban power systems. A high-resolution
urban power grid model of Zhuhai was developed using geographic
information systems, covering 110, 220, and 500 kV voltage levels
(Fig. 2 (c)). Real annual datasets on power generation and demand
enable the simulation of actual grid operations and outage scenarios.
Since electricity consumption de nsity typically correlates with
population density
45, the number of affected individuals is estimated
based on load-shedding amounts andper capita electricity consump-
tion, allowing for a comprehensive evaluation of the grid’s performance
during heatwaves and its social impact.

establishes a direct link between heat exposure and health risks to
estimate the excess deaths during heatwaves. We ﬁrst compile
temperature-relative health risk data from eight cities to construct an
interval-based representation, in which the lower bound, mean, and
upper bound correspond to conservative, interval-average, and
aggressive risk scenarios, respectively. These empirical intervals are
then embedded in a parametric e stimation framework: using
polynomial regression that captures both current and cumulative
exposure effects, we estimate scenario-speciﬁcc o e fﬁcients from multi-
city temperature-mortality data.

leading to diminished power generation capacity, while also causing

neutrality goal
We adopt the 2050 carbon-neutral expansion pathway for the Guangdong-
Hong Kong-Macao cluster from ref.46, to assess potential excess mortality

Carbon neutrality goal may induce more severe power outages. The
energy-intensive core areas of the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao
metropolitan cluster are particularly vulnerable to power outages during
severe heatwaves. Severe heatwaves have caused a substantial increase in
electricity demand across the region, with regional loads rising by
~10–20% due to the widespread use of air conditioning and cooling
systems. At the same time, extreme temperatures signiﬁcantly reduce the
transmission capacity of the power network — by ~5 –15%— thereby
limiting the delivery of electricity from renewable energy sources located
in the eastern and western peripheries of the urban cluster, as well as
interprovincial power imports (Fig. 3 (a)). Consequently, electricity
cannot be ef ﬁciently transmitted to the core consumption zones —
namely Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Hong Kong, Macao, and Zhuhai— where
demand is most concentrated. The mismatch between surging demand
and constrained supply leads to acute electricity shortages in these urban
cores. As these core zones also represent the most economically dense
areas, power outages during heatwaves can result in substantial economic
losses, highlighting the urgency of enhancing climate resilience in critical
metropolitan energy hubs. In this analysis, our manuscript does not
account for differences in supply-security priorities or outage preferences
across cities; instead, outage decisions are derived directly from the
annual power-ﬂow simulations.

arbonization strategy
The core zones of the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao megacity cluster,
including Zhuhai, face elevated health risks from heatwaves under power
system decarbonization. Considering that grid congestion signi ﬁcantly
affects the spatiotemporal distribution of power outages and associated
health risks, we use the Zhuhai power grid— with a high-resolution multi-

adopt the interval-average health-risk model and design three health risk

strategies. In these cases, R_Low does not account for the impacts of extreme
heatwaves, R_Med considers only the effects of extreme heatwaves on power
systems, while R_High further integrates health risks into the planning
model based on R_Med through a health-aware decarbonization approach.

Beyond the growth in power generation capacity, power outage

Optimized power outage scheduling reduces excess deaths . The
timing of load shedding signi ﬁcantly impacts the cumulative daily heat
exposure of vulnerable populations, indirectly affecting urban health
risks. Large-scale power outages during the daytime, rather than at night,
would substantially increase excess mortality due to the strong correla-
tion between heatwave-induced deaths and ambient temperature.
However, optimizing power outage scheduling through increased PV
integration can effectively mitigate health risks during heatwaves. For
instance, compared to case R_Low, the planning strategy in case R_High
increases PV capacity and its share by 35.26% and 14.43%, respectively,
helping to reduce daytime blackouts. Consequently, the operational
strategy in R_High not only lowers the total load-shedding but also
optimizes its timing. Daytime load-shedding is reduced by over 90%, with
most outages occurring at night (Fig. 5b). This strategy minimizes the
overlap between power outages and peak heat exposure, signi ﬁcantly
reducing excess deaths. Sensitivity analysis indicates that for every 1%
increase in PV penetration, excess deaths can be effectively reduced by
1–3% (supplementary material section 4.3). Thus, by strategically
increasing PV penetration to manage outage schedules, power systems
can more effectively safeguard vulnerable populations during extreme
heat events.
Hydrogen storage enhances urban power grid resilience. Moderately
increasing the PV penetration can mitigate excess deaths, but the ces-
sation of solar power generation at night may create a substantial power
gap. Deploying hydrogen storage in urban power grids can effectively
address this issue through a power-to-hydrogen-to-power technology.
Hydrogen storage not only releases hydrogen during the day to reduce
daytime load-shedding but also generates electricity at night on a larger
scale, compensating for the shortfall caused by the cessation of PV gen-
eration. Additionally, during peak demand periods, renewable energy
curtailments resulting from grid congestion are converted into hydrogen
for storage, providing a backup to counteract future power shortages
(Fig. 5c). Following the integration of hydrogen storage, renewable
energy consumption increased by 10.06%, while the daily load-shedding
rate decreased by 40.93% during heatwaves. Moreover, investing in
hydrogen storage not only performs well in case R_High. Sensitivity
analysis shows that, regardless of the power generation mix, investing in
hydrogen storage— representing about 15% of the total investment cost—
can consistently reduce load shedding by 32.8% to 41.2% (supplementary
material section 4.3).
Economic analysis of health-aware decarbonization strategy
To address the growing burden of excess deaths from more frequent and
severe heatwaves with higher penetration of renewable generation, we
applied the health-aware decarbonization model to the 2030–2050 expan-
sion period, emphasizing the healthcare beneﬁts and economic feasibility of
the proposed health-aware strategies in Zhuhai.
From 2000 to 2050, the global average intensity of heat waves is pro-
jected to increase by over 20%, while the number of heatwave days is
expected to rise by ~8%
50. Zhuhai, situated in a subtropical region
(23.5∘N-40∘N), has experienced signi ﬁcantly more severe extreme
heat waves than the global average. Under the relatively neutral SSP2-4.5

The increasing frequency and severity of heatwaves due to global warming,
combined with the decarbonization i mperatives for sustainable power
systems, are prolonging power outages during extreme weather conditions.

vulnerable to these intersecting climate and energy stresses. To mitigate

Conclusions
Climate change-induced extreme heatwaves pose serious threats to public
health and the reliable operation of u rban power grids. Resulting power
outages and cooling disruptions particularly endanger vulnerable popula-
tions. Focusing on the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area—
one of the world’s largest megacity clusters with 23 cities and 135 million
people— we offer a high-impact, real-world case study that rethinks urban
energy transition from a coupled health-energy perspective.
Our analysis reveals d eep decarbonization of urban power systems,
while essential for climate goals, can unintentionally exacerbate urban
health risks during extreme heatwaves. These risks are rising over time and
concentrating in dense urban cores; in the 11 core cities of the Guangdong-
Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area, the projected annual share of
heatwave-related excess deaths climbs from 0.99% in 2030 to 5.72% in 2050
under the interval-averaged health r isk scenarios. To reconcile climate
mitigation with public health ri sk, we propose a health-aware dec-
arbonization strategy that co-optimizes energy planning and health resi-
lience, advancing environmental (carbon), social (health), and economic
(cost) objectives; simulations indicate that this approach can cut excess
deaths by 55.51 –65.01% and reduce annual total costs by 8.72 –13.63%
under the SSP2-4.5 climate pathway, underscoring the value of integrating
health-focused, resilience-enhancing measures into urban energy
transitions.
However, some limitations must be acknowledged. First, our health-
risk model operates at the macro level and does not integrate micro public-
health data (e.g., neighborhood-level demographics, income and energy
poverty, or prevalence of pre-existingconditions). As a result, intra-urban
heterogeneity in heat risk is not resolved and estimation accuracy may be
limited. Meanwhile, our macro-level health model highlights associations
between heat exposure and mortality but, due to potential confounding
factors, is not sufﬁcient to clearly identify the causal chain from outages to
health risks. In addition, the framework currently omits mitigation and
built-environment modiﬁers— such as greenspace access, public cooling
facilities, urban design and behavioral responses, and building character-
istics (built volume, density, materials)— which can bias heat exposure and
health-risk estimates. Finally, the present application is limited to the
Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area; validation in other
megacity clusters (e.g., India, the United States, Europe) is needed to
establish generality and broader applicability.
Future work will extend the framework to multiple megacity clusters in
other countries and regions. We will incorporate intra-urban, micro-level
public health data into the risk model and apply instrumental-variable or
quasi-experimental approaches to strengthen causal identi ﬁcation. In
addition, integrating urban heat-mitigation measures (such as greenspace
and public cooling facilities) together with building characteristics (envelope

Method
Data-driven generation method for heat wave scenarios
To address the uncertainty in heat wave intensity, duration, and frequency,
we propose a data-driven method for generating heat wave scenarios. First,
we collected anew Zhuhai’s historical temperature records and future pro-
jections from the CMIP6, spanning 1980–2070, for three climate pathways
— SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5, and SSP5-8.5. We then extract monthly temperature
data from database to develop their probability distribution functions.
Because a heatwave is characterized by a period of signiﬁcantly higher-than-
usual temperatures, we de ﬁne speci ﬁc temperature thresholds for each
month to identify heatwaves. The intensity of high temperatures is deter-
mined by accumulating the abnormal temperature values over a speciﬁed
duration
50.
To account for the uncertainty in he at wave intensity, we categorize
historical temperature data into K groups (S1, S2,… ,SK ) based on high-
temperature intensity. For each group, a k-means clustering algorithm is
applied to extract a representative annual temperature scenario
S
k(k =1 ,2 ,..., K) consisting of 8760-time steps. To alleviate computational
complexity, we then select a representative week, spanning 168 h, from each
month to capture the variation in heat waves. This procedure results in new
heatwave scenariosS0, each comprising 12 typical weeks.
To forecast how heatwave frequency evolves, we assemble a tem-
perature dataset spanning 1980 –2070 by combining historical obser-
vations with CMIP6 projections for each climate pathway (e.g., SSP1-
2.6, SSP2-4.5, SSP5-8.5). For each year under each pathway, we compute
the annual high-temperature intensity and classify the year into groups
based on that intensity. For any analysis window, we compute the fre-
quency of each heatwave-intensity group by counting the number of
scenarios in that group. To capture how frequencies evolve, we pro-
gressively extend the window from a ﬁxed start year (1980) to a cutoff
year τ and recompute class shares as τ increases from 2000 to 2070,
effectively appending projected years to the dataset. This yields time-
evolving probabilities for heatwave scenarios with different high-
temperature intensities.

Health-aware decarbonization model for urban power grid
To address the excess deaths resulting from blackouts, we have developed an
expansion planning model that integrates heat wave-induced social health
risks. The objective function aims tominimize the total annual cost, which
includes investment, generation, loa d-shedding, economic losses from
excess deaths, and other operational expenses. The decision variables during
the investment stage encompass potential capacities for solar, offshore wind,
gas turbines, electrolyzers, and hydrogen storage systems. In the operational
phase, decision variables include th e hourly power outputs for various
generators, hydrogen production, and storage plans, as well as load-
shedding and electricity purchases.
We establish power and hydrogen ﬂow constraints to accurately
simulate the operation of a hydroge n-integrated urban power grid. The
effects of heat waves on both the power system’s operation and social health
risks are quantiﬁed using the temperature-power imbalance model and the
heat exposure-mortality model, detailed in Section 1.2 of the supplementary
material. In the operational phase, we consider the geographical distribution
of renewable energy sources and the optimal placement of gas turbines to
ensure the feasibility of the proposed planning schemes.
By incorporating these elements, we present a comprehensive frame-
work that optimizes both the operation and investment in the power grid
while addressing the critical challenges posed by heat waves. This frame-
work supports informed decision-making for energy planners and policy-
makers, enhancing system resilienc e, minimizing economic losses, and
improving urban health outcomes in the context of rising temperatures and
increasingly frequent extreme heat events.
