Achieving higher upper-critical- ﬁeld (μ0Hc2(0)) superconductors is of great interest for both
fundamental science and practical applications. While reducing the thickness of two-dimensional
(2D) bulk crystals to a few layers signi ﬁcantly enhances μ
0Hc2(0) with accompanied potential
unconventional pairing mechanisms, further dimensional reduction to 1D compounds rarely exceeds
the Pauli limit. Here, we report the synthesis of a 1D granular Ta-Te nanowire that becomes
superconducting under high pressure, with a maximum critical temperature ( T
c) of 5.1 K. The μ0Hc2(0)
reaches 16 T, which is twice the Pauli limit. Our proposed strategy may have potential applications in
high magnetic ﬁelds, and the granular Ta-Te nanowire serves as an ideal platform for further
investigations of the mechanisms between dimensionality and μ
0Hc2(0).
Since the discovery of the Meissner effect, efforts for pursuing super-
conductors with higher upper critical ﬁelds ( μ0Hc2(0)) have never been
paused. In recent years, the mechani cal exfoliation of 2D van der Waals
materials, such as transition metal c halcogenides (TMCs), leads to the
discovery of superconductivity with exceptionally largeμ0Hc2(0). Few-layer
NbSe2 is reported to be an Ising superconductor and exhibits orbital Fulde-
Ferrell-Larkin-Ovchinnikov (FFLO) states under large magnetic ﬁelds1–5.
The strong Ising spin orbital coupling (SOC) causes the in-planeμ0Hc2(0) of
few-layer NbSe2 to be about 1.5 times of Pauli limit ( μ0Hp). Similar beha-
viors are also observed in MoS2
6,T a S2
7 and gated WS2
8.
Recent advancements in achieving large upper critical ﬁelds in 2D
nanomaterials inspire that similar or even higherμ0Hc2(0) may be realized
in 1D superconducting nanowires. Theoretically, the further conﬁnement of
superconductivity to 1D could result in shorter coherence lengths, thus
permitting high orbital limit of μ0Hc2(0). Experimental studies on nano-
wires support this trend. For exampl e, Bismuth (Bi) nanowires exhibit
superconductivity properties that vary with morphology9–11: Single crystal Bi
nanowires have aTc of 1.3 K andμ0Hc2(0) of 4 T10, while granular nanowires
of Bi reachTc of 8.3 K andμ0Hc2(0) of 4.5 T, resembling the behavior of the
bulk high-pressure Bi-V phase9. Similarly, boron (B) nanowires, typically
insulating at ambient pressure, become superconducting under 84 GPa with
a μ
0Hc2(0) of 2.5 T and Tc of merely 1.5 K 12. In both single-crystal Bi
nanowires and pressurized B nanowires, the upper criticalﬁelds are close to
the Pauli limit due to quantum con ﬁnement. Apart from high pressure,
proximity effects or ion beams can also induce superconductivity in
nanowires13–15. However, despite these advances, the Pauli limit has rarely
been signiﬁcantly exceeded in superconducting nanowires up to now.
In this work, we synthesized a TMC nanowire composed of Ta 3Te4
granules. The nanowires show semi-metal behavior at ambient pressure and
superconductivity under high pressure. The upper critical ﬁeld reaches
about 16 T at 21 GPa, which signi ﬁcantly surpasses the Pauli limit
(1.84∙Tc = 7.6 T), corresponding to the rather low Tc (4.1 K). Theμ0Hc2(0) -
Tc ratio is the highest among all the reported superconducting nanowires.
Strong Rashba SOC induces ﬁnite susceptibility in the superconducting
state, which permits the Zeeman limit of Ta-Te nanowires to far exceed the
conventional Pauli limit. We have successfully combined the advantages of
TMC compounds and nanowires, realizing a large upper critical ﬁeld in
compressed Ta-Te nanowires.

few-layer NbSe2 is an Ising superconductor, with large in-planeμ0Hc2(0).

diagram. However, no signals of upturning FFLO states are observed

pairing mechanisms play important roles in enhancing μ0Hc2(0). In
short, further investigations on the Ta-Te nanowires are required to
unveil the pairing mechanism and its in ﬂuence on the large μ

Apart from the pairing mechanisms of large μ0Hc2(0) observed in these
materials, there might be some unknown origin in the Ta-Te nanowires.
We also note that the anisotropy of the μ

applied from different directions is not as large as expected, considering
the 1D appearance of the nanowires. As shown in Supplementary Fig. S5,
the in-plane μ

We suggest that the granular nature of the nanowires suppresses the
anticipated strong anisotropy caused by the 1D structure. In addition,
due to the large upper critical ﬁeld (16 T) and the moderate T
c (4 K)
among known superconducting nanowires, application potentials of Ta-
Te nanowires can be expected, considering that the nanowires are easy to
synthesize and may exhibit excellent mechanical properties.
In summary, we have successfully synthesized the Ta-Te nanowires
and induced superconductivity under high pressure. Superconductivity
emerges at 10.6 GPa, with a T
c of about 3.5 K, exhibiting a dome-like
behavior during compression. The upper criticalﬁeld (16 T) is twice that of
the Pauli limit. The granular Ta-Te nanowires can serve as a candidate for
applications in high magnetic ﬁelds and also offer an ideal platform for
investigating the exotic mechanisms of large upper critical ﬁelds of
superconductors.
Methods
Ta-Te nanowires are synthesized by the chemical vapor transport (CVT)
method34. A Stoichiometric amount of 99.999% tantalum and tellurium
powder (Alfar Aesar) is mixed and sealed in evacuated silica ampoules. The
starting powder in each ampoule weighs 1 g and 20 mg of iodine is used as
the transport agent. The size of the ampoules is 10 cm (length) × 1.5 cm
(diameter). The sealed ampoules are heated to 1100 °C in 10 h, and annealed
for 24 h in a box furnace. The small temperature gradient in the box furnace
is enough for the growth of the nanowires. The acquired nanowires are
black, ﬁber like, with typical dimensions of 100 nm × 100 nm × 100 μm.
These nanowires are densely tangled into bundles, as shown in Supple-
mentary Fig. S1.
The samples for TEM are prepared by dispersion of nanowires into
ethanol using ultrasonic methods. The dispersed nanowires are transferred
to copper grids with carbon membranes.
High-pressure in-situ electrical transport property is measured in
Physical Property Measurement System (PPMS-9T) and a cryostat with
3He
module and 14 T magnet using a nonmagnetic diamond anvil cell (DAC). A
cubic BN/epoxy mixture is used as an insulating layer between BeCu gaskets
and electrodes. In run 1 and 4 the nanowires are pre-densiﬁed by slightly
compressing the sample in the sample chamber before attaching Pt elec-

may cause different behavior of normal state resistance in Supplementary
