α-Oxy carbonyl compounds,characterized by their distinct electronic and
topological properties, represent highly versatile building blocks and modi-
ﬁers for functional molecules. Despite their potential, the synthetic challenges
associated with these compounds have limited their explora t i o ni nc h e m i c a l
biology and drug discovery. In this study, we address this limitation by intro-
ducing a general Cu-catalyzed three-component carbonyl 1,2-transposition
reaction, which proceeds via a P
V-oxirene intermediate. Our method employs
IIII/PV-hybrid ylides as carbeneprecursors to generateα-PV-Cu-carbenes, which
subsequently facilitate the formation of PV-oxirene species. This intermediate
undergoes regioselective ring-opening by carboxylate anions, followed by
acylation of a third nucleophile, enabling the modular assembly of α-oxy
carbonyl compounds. This strategy provides efﬁcient access to α-oxy esters,
amides, and thioesters, exhibiting broad tolerance to various functional
groups. Furthermore, these compounds exhibit remarkable chemical versati-
lity, as demonstrated in the synthesis of depsipeptides and late-stage drug
modiﬁcation, underscoring their applicability across diverse research areas.
Our ﬁndings not only signiﬁcantly expand the structural diversity of accessible
α-oxy carbonyls but also introduce a versatile and biocompatible synthetic
linkage for chemical synthesis.
α-Oxy carbonyl compounds are privileged motifs in organic synthesis
with essential roles in medicinal chemistry, food chemistry, and poly-
mer science (Fig. 1A)1–3. For example, due to their structural similarity
to α-amino acids,α-hydroxy acids (AHAs) are frequently incorporated
into peptides and proteins by selectively replacing speciﬁc amino acid
residues4,5. The introduction of an ester linkage into the peptide
backbone offers a straightforward method to modulate intra- and
intermolecular hydrogen bonding interactions, which, in turn, in ﬂu-
ences the conformation and bioactivity of the peptides
6–9. Therefore,
numerous studies in chemical biology, peptide chemistry, and drug
discovery have employedα-oxy carbonyls as powerful molecular tools.
The traditional synthesis ofα-oxy carbonyls mainly relies on four types
of strategies: (1) Ready transformation of cyanohydrins originated via
cyanation of aldehydes and ketones
10,11; (2) Pre-functionalization onα-
position of carbonyl derivatives followed by nucleophilic attack from
O-nucleophiles12; (3) Selective oxidation of 1,2-diols 13, enolates 14 or
selective reduction of α-keto carbonyls15; (4) Multi-component Pas-
serini-type reactions which generally assembleα-acyloxyamides from
highly reactive isocyanide reagents. Despite these advances, it is still
highly desirable to develop modular synthetic strategies that could
afford structurally diverse α-oxy carbonyl compounds to meet diver-
siﬁed demands from multiple disciplines
16,17.
The progression of organic synthesis and the discovery of novel
chemical transformations are often propelled by the rational design of
bond-forming mechanisms that leverage highly reactive inter-
mediates. Among these, oxirene— an anti-aromatic, strained ring sys-
tem— has been identiﬁed as a pivotal, yet elusive, species that exists as
multivalent isomers and mediates a range of intriguing processes
18–21.

Our investigation focused on three deﬁning characteristics of oxirene
intermediates: (i) their pronounced electrophilicity and strain-driven
reactivity, (ii) their low selectivity between the two adjacent carbon
centers, and (iii) their inherent propensity to undergo ring-opening to
form carbonyl derivatives (Fig. 1B). However, exploiting oxirene
intermediates in synthetic methodology has been impeded by chal-
lenges in controlling their extreme reactivity and a scarcity of efﬁcient
synthetic precursors
22,23. Typically, nucleophilic attack on an oxirene
intermediate occurs non-selectively at either carbon center, yielding a
mixture of regioisomeric carbonyl products
24. To modulate this high
reactivity, we proposed the introduction of a substituent with tunable
electronic and steric properties. This strategy aims to establish a syn-
thetic paradigm for carbonyl generation via the catalytic formation of
these underexplored oxirene species. Quaternary phosphonium salts,
as i g n iﬁcant class of organophosphorus compounds, are widely
employed in synthetic chemistry due to their utility in diverse
transformations
25. We postulated that integrating these P V salts into
the oxirene framework would leverage their combined electrophilicity

26. This approach is anticipated to signiﬁcantly
enhance regioselectivity during nucleophilic attack while facilitating a
versatile three-component modular synthesis ofα-functionalized car-
bonyl compounds.

Discussion
In summary, we present a modular method for the convergent
synthesis ofα-oxy carbonyl compounds via a copper-catalyzed, highly
regioselective three-component coupling of I III/PV-hybrid ylide
reagents, carboxylic acids, and diverse nucleophiles. The reaction
proceeds ef ﬁciently under mild conditions, exhibiting excellent
regioselectivity and broad functional group compatibility, even with
complex drug-like substrates. Notably, this approach enables the
synthesis of medicinally relevant depsipeptides that are otherwise
challenging to access through conventional methods. Mechanistic
investigations suggest the involvement of a highly reactive α-P
V oxi-
rene intermediate, with 1,2-carbonyl transposition playing a key role in
the copper-catalyzed process. While the proposed mechanism remains
tentative at this stage, this proof-of-concept study establishes a cost-
effective strategy for synthesizing α-oxy carbonyl derivatives and
advances the ﬁeld of substituted oxirene chemistry.
Methods
General procedure for the modular synthesis ofα-oxy carbonyl
compounds
IIII/PV-hybrid ylide reagents1 (0.13 mmol, 1.3 equiv.), carboxylic acids2
(0.1 mmol, 1.0 equiv.), CuCN (0.5 mg, 0.005 mmol, 5 mol%), base
additives (0.22 mmol, 2.2 equiv.) and 3 Å MS (20 mg) were added to a
dry 10 mL reaction tube, successively. The vial was sealed, degassed,
and ﬁlled with nitrogen. Then nucleophiles 3 (0.12 mmol, 1.2 equiv.)
dissolved in dry 1,2-dichloroethane (1 mL) were added to the reaction
tube. The resulting reaction mixture was stirred at 26 °C for 12 h. Sol-
vent was removed under vacuum, and the crude mixture was puriﬁed
by ﬂash column chromatography on silica gel to affordα-oxy carbonyl
products.
