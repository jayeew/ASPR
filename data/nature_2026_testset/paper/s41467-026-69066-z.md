Grain boundaries (GBs) in insulating crystalﬁlms generally create lattice
reconstruction and excess mid-gap states, which may form the transport
pathways of charge carriers and lead to serious degradation of insulating
properties. Here, we reveal the inert electrical behaviors of GBs in poly-
crystalline inorganic molecular dielectricﬁlm attributed to its special van der
Waals structure. Temperature-dependent electrical measurements andﬁrst-
principles calculations uncovered that such GBs are free of mid-gap states,
which explains the unexpectedly low leakage current of Sb
2O3 polycrystalline
thin ﬁlm. Through systematic measurements by conductive atomic force
microscopy, we further verify the undistinguishable electrical behaviors within
grains and at GBs of the polycrystalline ﬁlm. Our ﬁndings may lay a solid
foundation for the applications of inorganic molecularﬁlm as a compatible
gate dielectric in advanced two-dimensional devices.

Fabrication of PIMF and electrical measurements
The PIMF of Sb 2O3 can be fabricated via a thermal sublimation eva-
poration deposition method18,20. The deposited molecules remain in
the cage-like structure and grow into a polycrystallineﬁlm on any given
substrate, as illustrated in Fig. 2a. We ﬁrst demonstrate the poly-
crystalline structure of the Sb2O3 ﬁlm with a thickness of 10 nm grown
on substrates of few-layer graphene (see Methods for details). This is
the minimum thickness necessary to prevent the direct tunneling of
charges
18.A ss h o w ni nF i g .2b, the selected area electron diffraction
(SAED) pattern conﬁrms the single-crystalline nature of the underlying
graphene, whereas the multi-oriented Sb 2O3 domains give rise to
polycrystalline diffraction rings. From the high-resolution transmis-
sion electron microscopy (HR-TEM) images (Fig. 2c), we can also
clearly identify the polycrystalline structure and identify its average
grain size of around 9.6 nm (see Figure S1), close to theﬁlm thickness
of 10 nm. This result implies that there may exist high-density trans-
verse GBs (crossing from the top of theﬁlm to the bottom) within the
ﬁlm. For electrical characterizations, the Sb
2O3 ﬁlm was directly on a
degenerately doped Si substrate, which serves as a ﬂat bottom elec-
trode. We tried to observe the Sb2O3 grains GBs from the cross-section
HR-TEM image (Fig.2d). We can clearly identify the Sb2O3 grains as well
as the transverse GBs. Such an insulating ﬁlm with a large number of
continuous pathways for charge carrier transport is theoretically
expected to possess a poor current-blocking property. In contrast, it
exhibits a low leakage current in our device measurements at room

Impact of vdW GBs on DOS by calculations
We now try to gain insights into the impact of GBs on the electronic
band structure of Sb
2O3 crystals. As the molecular structure of Sb2O3 is
preserved intact in the sublimation-deposition process, we reasonably
assume that all the GBs in Sb
2O3 are of vdW structure, as schematically
s h o w ni nF i g .2a. In order to investigate the impact of such GB presence
on the insulating properties, we performedﬁrst-principles calculations
to study the band structure of the Sb 2O3 polycrystalline ﬁlm (see
details in supplementary materials). We constructed several typical
crystal models with low-index crystal planes of (100), (110), (111), and
(210), which have the lowest formation energy. A large enough vacuum
layer is constructed to create the surface. In these crystal models, the
complete molecular structure of Sb
2O3 is maintained on the exposed
surfaces (Fig. 3a). Since the possible excess mid-gap states in
polycrystallineﬁlm originate from the breakage of the periodic crystal
structure at the GBs, the band structure of our crystal models can
represent the scenario of polycrystallineﬁlm
47. Their calculated results
of DOS are compared with those of bulk single crystal (Fig. 3b). The
results show that they possess exactly the same band gap and intro-
duce no excess mid-gap states for all the constructed crystal models,
thanks to the termination of co mplete molecules. We speci ﬁcally
indicated that the DOS contributed from the top-layer exposed
molecules, and all of their electronic states also remain out of the band
gap (Fig. 3b). More explicitly, we constructed some representative
crystal models containing GBs and investigated their DOS (see details
in Supplementary Materials). For a face-centered cubic (FCC) crystal of
Sb
2O3, Σ3a n d Σ5 twin GBs are preferentially formed in experiments
due to their relatively low GB formation energies, which are chosen as
two typical bi-crystal models for studying the effects of GBs. The Σ3
out-of-plane twist twin boundary (denoted as GB1, Fig. 3c) and the Σ5
in-plane tilt twin boundary (denoted as GB2, Fig. S3) were therefore
selected as representative computational models due to their rela-
tively small number of atoms. They can effectively balance computa-
tional ef ﬁciency and the accurate description of their physical
properties. Their crystal structures were optimized, and electronic
structures were calculated (Fig.3d). The two bi-crystal models possess
very similar band gap, which is slightly smaller than the bulk crystal
calculated via the same approximation method (see detailed analysis
in Supplementary Materials). No excess mid-gap states are created
despite the presence of GBs. Such theoretical results imply that the
presence of vdW GBs in Sb
2O3 PIMF will not result in any mid-gap
states, in clear contrast to conventional oxide dielectric materials30,31.
The PIMF Sb 2O3 possesses an identical band gap to the single-
crystal ﬁlm.

Discussion
The photo-electronically inert behaviors of GBs in polycrystallineﬁlms
have indeed been investigated in photovoltaic semiconductors such as
Sb
2Se3
47 and CuInSe 2
55,56. Such works revealed that polycrystalline
semiconductor ﬁlms can possess a similar photovoltaic performance
as their single-crystal counterpart and lower-cost PV panels based on
polycrystalline semiconductors could be manufactured. In this work,
we revealed the electrical inert behaviors of typical vdW GBs in
dielectric crystals for the ﬁrst time, which explains the unexpectedly
excellent insulating properties of PIMF. Some organic molecules have
been widely used as gate dielectrics in organic electronic devices
57,58,
which were either fabricated via deposition or a self-assembly method.
We expect that the possible vdW GBs in such organic molecular
dielectric may exhibit similar inert electrical behaviors. Our ﬁndings
provide a deeper insight into the insulating properties of inorganic
molecularﬁlms and can probably relax the stringent requirements for
gate dielectrics in 2D electronic devices. Since the PIMF is fabricated
via CMOS-compatible approaches, our work may lay a solid foundation
for the application of PIMF as a compatible gate dielectric in large-scale
integrated 2D devices
18,20,59.
Methods
Thermal evaporation deposition of PIMF Sb2O3
The deposition of Sb 2O3 was carried out in a standard thermal eva-
poration deposition system (Nexdep, Angstrom Engineering) in a high
vacuum (10
−6 torr). Sb 2O3 powder was sublimated by heating and
deposited on substrates. The deposition rate was precisely controlled
by an in-situ quartz crystal oscillator. To maintain the ﬂatness of the
obtained Sb2O3 ﬁlm, the deposition rate in this work was 0.06 Å s−1 for
the entire fabrication process.
Sample preparation for TEM characterizations
For plan-view TEM samples, we exfoliated a few-layer graphene
ﬂakes onto SiO
2/Si substrate and then transferred them onto a TEM
grid assisted by a PMMA membrane. Then the PMMA membrane on
the TEM grid is dissolved in acetone. Afterwards, the TEM grid with
graphene ﬂakes is annealed in an Ar atmosphere to remove the
PMMA residues on graphene ﬂakes, which is then loaded into the
thermal evaporation deposition system, and Sb 2O3 is deposited. For
the cross-section TEM lamella, 10 nm of Sb 2O3 was deposited onto a
degenerately doped silicon substrate, and then the lamella was
fabricated by a dual focused ion beam system (Helios Nanolab G3
UC, FEI). TEM images and SAED were obtained using an FEI Tec-
nai G2 20.
Preparation and characterization of capacitor devices
10 nm of Sb 2O3 was deposited onto a degenerately doped silicon
substrate. A shadow mask with a predeﬁned size of 200 μm×200 μm
was placed over theﬁlm, followed by the deposition of 40 nm of Au via
thermal evaporation to form the topmetal electrode. In such a way, the
devices for electrical characterizations can be maintained free of
contamination resulting from the fabrication processes.
