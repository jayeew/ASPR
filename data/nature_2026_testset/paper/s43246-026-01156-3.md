Helium is a ﬁnite but essential resource with important applications in medicine, research, and
aerospace. Conventional He-upgrading from natural methane gas (CH 4) is done by cryogenic
distillation. This study presents energy-ef ﬁcient He-upgrading by membrane separation using the
“dense” metal-organic framework (MOF) MIL-116(Ga), which enables high precision molecular sieving
of non-adsorptive gases. MIL-116(Ga) membranes were synthesized on alumina disks after
developing a method to homogeneously intergrow the MOF into a polycrystalline ﬁlm. Single gas
permeation experiments reveal exceptional ideal selectivity of α
ideal(H2/CH4) = 122 and αideal(He/
CH4) = 87. In mixed gas permeation, the separation selectivity surpasses αideal(He/CH4) = 207 GPU.
Mixed-gas tests at realistic 4:96 He/CH 4 feed gas composition reached a selectivity of α(He/
CH4) = 31.9 and permeance for He of P(He) = 227 GPU despite the low chemical potential. Electron
microscopy uncovered a complex, grain-boundary microstructure, which limits perfect molecular
sieving but still enables superior separation performance. This work demonstrates that dense MOFs,
at the example of MIL-116(Ga) enables high performance He-upgrading, setting a new benchmark
amongst reported MOF-based membranes. We highlight the potential of dense MOFs for the
separations of small, non-adsorptive gases and the need to address grain boundary diffusion in
polycrystalline MOF membranes.
Helium, a rare and non-renewable resource, has become a requisite part in
our daily lives by steadily gaining importance in modern technological
ﬁelds, such as in medical, industrial, and research-related devices and
applications. He is widely used and not replaceable, for example, in medical
imaging, aerospace technologies, electronic devices, chromatography, and
cryogenics
1,2. While He is present in the air but in very low concentration
(5 ppm), it is a non-adsorptivespecies, making its capture– other than that
of CO2 – from the atmosphere impossible2. The main source to collect He is
through extraction from natural gas reserves, where it accumulates over
time of millions of years
3. In these He accumulating natural gas reserves, the
concentration can be up to 4% He, allowing cost-efﬁcient upgrading with
cryogenic distillation. The increased exploitation of fossil fuels is associated
with a gradual increase in atmospheric He levels, which is an alarming fact
for He waste4.
When He is released into the atmosphere it will escape into space and is
lost forever3. However, there is no other way than radioactive decay to
produce He, which is not possible in largeq u a n t i t i e s .T h ei n c r e a s i n gd e m a n d
for He has resulted in soaring prices and created a need for more attractive
and low-cost separation technologies for He-upgrading. Commercially, He-
upgrading from natural gas is performed by cryogenic distillation, which is
highly energy-intensive and therefore costly. Membrane technology has been
identiﬁed as a more promising alternative, which separates gases by

Polymeric membranes were the ﬁrst ones to be explored for He
separation in 1960s8. Recently, Polyimide-based SEPURAN® Noble hollow
ﬁber membranes have been commercialized for He-upgrading from natural
gas. Although polymeric membranes are relatively easy to fabricate, they
inherently exhibit a trade-off betweenpermeability and selectivity due to the
solution-diffusion transport mechanism
5. To avoid this limitation, materials
with pore sizes comparable to the kinetic diameter of He have been devel-
oped, facilitating separation through molecular sieving. Molecular sieving
materials such as silica
9, zeolites10, graphene and carbon nanotubes11, as well
as metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) have shown signiﬁcant advantages
over conventional polymeric membr anes by offering enhanced perme-
ability and selectivity2.
MOFs are crystalline, porous materials consisting of metal nodes,
interconnected by organic linkers. MOFs are generally characterized by high
surface area, structural versatility, a nd tailorable porosity, offering sig-
niﬁcant potential for adsorption-based separation. Regardless of the fact that
He has a kinetic diameter similar to H
2, literature data on He separation for
MOF membranes are very limited. Moreover, the reported membranes also
exhibit low selectivity values for He/CH
4. Their performance is especially
limited by pore size and breathing effect, resulting in compromised mole-
cular sieving capabilities12–22. In recent years, the real molecular sieving
capabilities of MOFs became questi onable because of their soft porous
nature23. This discussion originates from the ﬂexibilities, gate-opening
effects, and such24, which have been proven to lead to imprecise molecular
sieving25. He is considered a non-adsorptivegas due to its low polarizability
and inert nature. Therefore, kinetic separation through diffusion limitation
is highly favored, therefore Wangs’ group was adding fullerenes into the
cavities of MOFs to sharpen their molecular sieving abilities. They were able
to achieve the best results so far by a combination of ZIF-8 (Zeolitic Imi-
dazolate Framework 8) and fulleren es, for He-upgrading, achieving a
selectivity α(He/CH
4) = 9.2 with a permeance of 210 GPU at 3.5 wt.%
loading of C70
20. In another approach, MXene-assisted ZIF-67 membranes
on Al2O3 disks were reported for the He/CH4 separation. These membranes
were able to achieve selectivity of 17.9 with 825.3 GPU He permeance at
120 °C
21. While these studies helped to improve supported membranes,
dense MOF-supported membranes have not yet been explored for the
separation of small molecules like He and H
2.
This study uses “dense” MOFs for He separation (Fig.1), which were
characterized with basically no porosity, but gates and channels in the range
of the kinetic diameter of He (2.6 Å). We investigate neat MIL-116
(Matériaux de l′Institut Lavoisier 116) (Ga) as a membrane material, which
does not show porosity in stand ard gas sorption experiments
26,b u ti s
optimally suited for the separation of the smallest gases due to its diffusional
properties. MIL-116(Ga), however, shows a water sorption curve compar-
able to LTA27 and demonstrates uptake behavior of very small molecules in
highly conﬁned pore space.

Analysis of the grain boundary microstructure
Since polycrystalline MOF membranes are usually suffer from grain
boundary diffusion, we have conducted a detailed analysis with the SEM
of our ﬁlm. The MIL-116(Ga) membrane consists of a lot of spherically
shaped particles (with a slight habitus showing, as the crystals form
facets) that consist of smaller, needle-like particles, as visible in the SEM
in Figs. 2 and 3. The high-resolution SEM in Fig. 3a reveals how MIL-
116(Ga) grows on the α-Al
2O3 support. We therefore propose that an
underlying seed layer without distinguishable morphology forms in a
ﬁrst step, which is mediated by the surface functionalization with APTES.
Each of the MIL-116(Ga) seeds tends to grow into a needle-shaped
crystal, forming a polycrystal during ripening that consists of a lot of
these MIL-116(Ga) needles
30. Afterwards, these polycrystals start the
intergrowth into a polycrystallineﬁlm, following crystallization kinetics31.
We could capture exactly a part, in which a crystal broke out of the layer
and reveals the “broccoli”-like crystal growth. Energy dispersive X-ray
spectroscopy (EDXS) mapping has been conducted to prove that MOF
and support have intergrown as shown in the mixed map (Fig. 3b) and
the single-element maps (Fig. 3c–f). The proposed mechanism of poly-
crystalline growth and grain boundary generation in the ﬁlm is
demonstrated in Fig. 3g. This schematic image shows that, originating
from the underlying seed layer, spherical polycrystals form, which then
attach with a larger extent of grain boundaries into a polycrystalline ﬁlm.

Conclusion
Polycrystalline MIL-116(Ga) membranes were synthesized, offering a very
fast and selective permeation of He and H2 in mixed-gas experiments for
He/CH4 and H2/CH4 withα ≥ 200. In a 4:96 He/CH4 mixture, the selectivity
is still α(He/CH4) = 31.9, considering the lower chemical potential for He,
which is a very successful outcome. That the membrane is not completely
selective for He and shows gas permeation of larger gases originates from the
complicated crystal growth of MIL-116(Ga) and grain boundary structure,
as we have shown with an extensive SEM analysis. While this study
demonstrates the concept of using“dense MOFs” for H2 and He separation,
the typical problems of MOF membranes, such as competitive diffusion,
adsorption, and inherent defects andgrain boundaries, have been shown to
limit the performance. Further work is necessary to understand diffusion,
surface barrier phenomenon, and grain boundary diffusion in dense MOFs
with respect to He, H
2, and adsorptive gases for further performance
improvements. Nevertheless, the data generated is highly promising and
demonstrates potential as competitive membranes for industrial separation
processes. For the future, starting with the herein presented MIL-116(Ga),
techno-economic analysis is required and should compare existing tech-
nology to these membranes. However, the herein presented MIL-116(Ga)
membranes break the benchmark for polycrystalline MOF membranes in
He/CH
4 separation by far and are thus a well-performing MOF membrane
for He-upgrading at the moment.
