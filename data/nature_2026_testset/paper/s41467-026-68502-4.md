The advancement of bioorthogonal bond-breaking chemistry requires precise
spatiotemporal control over chemical reactions. In this study, we introduce a
click-release strategy based on the reaction between mono-alkyl-
hydroxylamine and cyclooctyne (COT), enabling rapid and nearly complete
payload release. We demonstrate that mono-alkyl-hydroxylamine and hydro-
xylamine react with COT to form nitrone and oxime, respectively, facilitating a
versatile and efﬁcient release mechanism. By conjugating mono-alkyl-
hydroxylamine with responsive cleavable groups, we convert its inherent
reactivity into an on-demand activation system. In vivo, this strategy is applied
in a 4T1 mouse breast tumor model, showing signiﬁcant tumor inhibition
compared to the parent anticancer drug.Additionally, in local anesthesia, the
anesthetic effect could be modulated bylight exposure, allowing for repeated
activation. This click-release system combines fast kinetics, high release efﬁ-
ciency, and precise spatiotemporal control, offering promising applications in
chemical biology and drug delivery.
Click chemistry has fostered an unprecedented synergy between
chemistry and biology, revolutionizing research paradigms in the life
sciences through its exceptional reaction selectivity and efﬁciency
1–6.
Initially celebrated for its robust, bioorthogonal covalent bond for-
mation, click chemistry has empowered precise biomolecule labeling,
visualization, and manipulation within complex biological systems
7–9.
More recently, the advent of the click-release strategies —wherein a
rapid click reaction is directly coupled to a subsequent bond-cleavage
event—has expanded the toolkit for in situ payload liberation
10–13,
driving transformative implications in bioimaging14, regulation of the
activity of nucleic acids and enzymes 15–19, drug delivery 20–26,a n d
beyond27–30. In particular, the tetrazine (Tz) —triggered decaging of
trans-cyclooctene (TCO)—caged molecules has attracted extensive
attention owing to its extraordinary reactivity and biocompatibility31,32,
fueling applications ranging from tumor imaging 33,34 and
immunomodulation35–37 to prodrug therapies 38–43,o n eo fw h i c hh a s
advanced into phase II clinical trials44.
Despite these remarkable achievements, a critical need persists
for bioorthogonal platforms that deliver not only rapid reaction
kinetics and high release efﬁciency but also exquisite spatiotemporal
control. The intrinsic always-on reactivity of traditional bioorthogonal
handles limits their potential for precise activation and localization.
Recent efforts—such as Wang’s concentration-promoted click—release
strategy that enriches reactants at de ﬁned sites

In pursuit of a more versatile solution, recent work by Kim and

In this work, we expand the chemistry of ring strain –assisted

directly with cyclooctyne (COT) to form a hydroxyaminooctene
intermediate. When a leaving group is installed at the propargyl posi-

releasing the payload and generating a nitroneoctene—a bioorthogo-

demand local anesthesia controlled by 405 nm light. Together, these
results establish a versatile click –release system for spatially and
temporally controlled bioorthogonal chemistry (Fig.1c).

Bioorthogonal mono-alkyl-hydroxylamine/COT bond-cleavage
chemistry
Following the establishment of the reactivity of our click reaction, we
focused on its potential application in triggered molecular release. As
previously noted, the click reaction between mono-alkyl-
hydroxylamine and COT results in the formation of an N-alkyl-hydro-
xyl-enamine intermediate. We hypothesize that this intermediate
facilitates a spontaneous 1,4-elimination process upon introduction of
a leaving group at the propargylic position, ultimately leading to the
development of a bioorthogonal cleavage chemistry.
To validate this concept, we synthesized COT 3a using p-nitroa-
niline as a model caging molecule and examined its reaction with
mono-alkyl-hydroxylamine 2c in a 20% ACN/PBS solution at room
temperature. As expected, this reaction led to the successful release of
p-nitroaniline, with a release yield of up to 97.9%. In contrast to the
reduction of N-oxides formed from N,N-dialkylhydroxylamine and
COT by boric acid to release caged molecules, ourﬁndings reveal that
mono-alkyl-hydroxylamine can directly function as a bioorthogonal
scissor to cleave caged molecules tethered to COT, eliminating the
need for an additional reduction step (Fig. 3a, b).
To further validate this mechanism, we monitored the reaction
progress using
1H NMR spectroscopy. To capture the reaction mid-
point, we selected the less reactive mono-alkyl-hydroxylamine deri-
vative 2e (Fig. 3c). Within 10 minutes of mixing 25 mM of substrate 3a
with 50 mM of 2e in DMSO- d
6, the formation of nitrone 3 d was
observed by 1H NMR spectroscopy, which captured the alkene proton
generated from the elimination reaction (two addition isomers were
observed), along with the quantitative release of the payload,
p-nitroaniline (3c). As the reaction progressed, the gradual
disappearance of 3a corresponded with the increasing release of p-
nitroaniline. After 1 h, only trace amounts of 3a remained, and by 12 h,
complete consumption of 3a was observed with full payload liberation.
Notably, the elimination intermediate 3b was not detectable, sug-
gesting that once formed, it rapidly eliminates to releasep-nitroaniline.
Furthermore, the absence of any non-releasing byproducts con ﬁrms
the excellent regioselectivity of the click reaction, ensuring nearly
complete payload release.

Spatiotemporally controllable click-release chemistry
In Fig. 2f, we demonstrate that acylation of the nitrogen atom in mono-
alkyl-hydroxylamine effectively shields its reactivity. Exploiting this
property, we linked various responsive bond-breaking groups to
hydroxylamine via carbamate bonds to block its clickability (i.e.,
Clickability OFF). Upon exposure to a speci ﬁc stimulus, the blocked
clickability is restored (Clickability ON), thereby enabling precise
control over the timing and location of the click–release event. To this
end, we developed a controlled activation strategy by designing and
synthesizing three hydroxylamine derivatives with distinct activation
mechanisms: thiol-responsive 6a, photo-activated 6b, and nitror-
eductase (NTR)-responsive 6c. In each case, the clickability of these
compounds was effectively masked until triggered.
To demonstrate speci ﬁcity, we ﬁrst activated compound 6a by
incubation with 2 mM glutathione (GSH) and subsequently performed
a click reaction with probe 4b. Under these conditions, more than 98%
of product 4 d was released after 2 h, whereas in the absence of GSH,
less than 5% of 4 d was generated (Fig. 6a). Similarly, to verify that 6c
could be activated, we treated it with an aqueous solution of sodium
sulﬁte as a mild reducing agent. The results con ﬁrmed that the click-
ability of 6c was restored, thereby enabling the release of the caged 4 d
from COT 4b.

To directly conﬁrm that the bioorthogonal click–release reaction
occurs at the tumor site, we conducted a pharmacokinetic (PK) study
comparing free DOX (2.0 mg/kg) with a prodrug system consisting of
compound 5c (2.4 mg/kg DOX equivalent) and the GSH-responsive
activator 6a (17 mg/kg), both administered via intravenous injection.
Doxorubicin concentrations in plasma, tumor, heart, and liver were
quantiﬁed using UPLC-MS/MS at multiple timepoints. In contrast to
free DOX, the 6a + 5c group showed markedly reduced drug levels in
non-target tissues (e.g., heart and liver) and in the plasma, while
maintaining comparable tumor concentrations across all time points
(Supplementary Fig. 14). These results provide compelling evidence
that the click-release reaction is preferentially triggered within the
tumor microenvironment by elevated endogenous GSH, thereby
effectively reducing off-target exposure. While this study establishes
spatially selective prodrug activation in vivo, further validation in
expanded cohorts and extended therapeutic studies will be essential
to conﬁrm long-term safety and efﬁcacy.
Photo-triggered bioorthogonal prodrug for local anesthesia
In contrast to the GSH-activated system that relies on endogenous
stimuli, this section explores the use of an exogenous trigger to
demonstrate the conceptual versatility of our click–release strategy. As
a proof-of-concept, we designed a photo-triggered prodrug system for
local anesthesia. We note that the anesthetic effect could, in principle,
be achieved through more straightforward direct uncaging approa-
ches; however, the present design serves to illustrate the modularity of
the platform and the decoupling of the activation trigger from the
payload structure. In this system, the conventional local anesthetic
tetracaine was conjugated to COT via a carbamate bond to mask its
anesthetic activity, yielding the prodrug 7a. In vitro studies under
405 nm LED irradiation, in the presence of the photoactivator 6b,
showed that 7a released tetracaine with an 89.4 ± 2.4% yield within
30 min (Fig. 8a).
To facilitate in vivo application, 7a and 6b were incorporated into
a thermosensitive hydrogel formulation (P407-(6b + 7a). This for-
mulation remains liquid at room temperature for ease of injection and
gels at body temperature, thereby prolonging drug retention at the
administration site.
For in vivo evaluation, the P407-(6b + 7a) formulation was injected
into the plantar surface of rat paws. The local anesthetic effect was
quantiﬁed by stimulating the footpad with a Touch Test sensory eva-
luator, with subsequent measurement of withdrawal responses to
calculate the maximum peak effect (MPE) and duration of nerve block
(see “Methods” for details). Notably, administration of 7a alone pro-
duced no anesthetic effect, conﬁrming that the activity of tetracaine
was effectively masked. However, upon exposure to 405 nm LED light
10 min post-injection, the prodrug was activated, yielding an anes-
thetic effect comparable to that of free tetracaine (Fig. 8b).

euthanized at 4 and 14 days following the administration of free tet-

arms, thus supporting the favorable safety pro ﬁle of this delivery

These ﬁndings collectively demonstrate that our photo-triggered
bioorthogonal prodrug system provides precise temporal control over
the delivery of local anesthetics using an exogenous light stimulus.
This approach not only distinguishes itself from endogenous glu-
tathione (GSH)-triggered systems but also presents a tunable method
for on-demand pain management with potential clinical applications.
Discussion
Over the past decade, signi ﬁcant advances have been made in the
development of click–release chemistries for controlled drug delivery.
However, conventional platforms remain limited by their inherent
always-on reactivity, which limits spatiotemporal precision and
increases the risk of off-target activation. In this study, we introduce a
distinct bioorthogonal bond-cleavage strategy based on the reaction
between mono-alkyl-hydroxylamine and COT derivatives. By structu-
rally masking the hydroxylamine moiety with stimuli-responsive
groups, we achieved conditional activation triggered by either endo-
genous signals (e.g., elevated GSH) or exogenous stimuli (e.g., light),
establishing a versatile and modular platform for spatiotemporally
regulated bioorthogonal activation.
The system was validated across multiple in vivo models. In the
tumor-targeted prodrug delivery study, we demonstrated GSH-
triggered activation within the tumor microenvironment, leading to
enhanced doxorubicin accumulation and reduced systemic exposure,
as conﬁrmed by UPLC-MS/MS-based pharmacokinetic analyses. In the
local anesthesia model, we showed that photocaged hydroxylamine
(6b) enabled repeatable, on-demand drug release when activated by
light, without observable toxicity. This two-pronged validation high-
lights the platform ’s dual capability for both endogenous- and
exogenous-triggered therapeutic control.
Despite these promising results, several challenges remain. First,
the reaction kinetics of the COT/hydroxylamine system are slower
than those of the fast Tz/TCO system. Future efforts will focus on
enhancing its reactivity to expand its applicability and practical utility.
Second, although our prodrug design confers biological inertness
prior to activation, the inherent reactivity pro ﬁle of hydroxylamines
underscores the need for detailed long-term safety and biodistribution
studies, which constitute key priorities for further translational
development.
Looking forward, this modular activation strategy may be inte-
grated with bioorthogonal cascade ampli ﬁcation systems —such as
those previously developed by our group
54—wherein a limited exo-
genous trigger can initiate a self-propagating activation loop for
enhanced signal or drug release in deep or heterogeneous tissues.
Critically, our system serves as a complementary alternative to the
established Tz/TCO pair, founded on a fundamentally different
mechanism and unique operational pro ﬁle. Unlike Tz, which suffer
from instability in highly reducing environments such as those with
high GSH concentrations
55–57, our hydroxylamine-based chemistry
exhibits stability and even beneﬁts from GSH 49. This inherent advan-
tage positions our platform as highly suitable for deployment in
reductive tumor microenvironments. Furthermore, the demonstrated
orthogonality to Tz/TCO reactions
49 provides a foundation for future
multiplexed therapeutic or diagnostic strategies in complex biological
settings. Together with recent advancements in dual-click systems
in vivo (e.g., Staudinger and Tz/TCO pairs)48, our work underscores the
growing importance of chemically diverse and orthogonal activation
platforms for the development of next-generation chemical biology
tools and drug delivery systems.

Preparation of P407-(6b+ 7a)
At 0 °C, dissolve 4 g of P407 in 16 mL of PBS to prepare a 20%
P407 solution. Dissolve compound 7a in a small amount of ethanol, then

rat. At speci ﬁed intervals, neurobehavioral assessments were con-
ducted by applying stimuli to the rat’s footpad using Touch Test sen-
sory evaluators (ﬁlaments designed to exert a target force of 180 g),
while observing the rat’s motor reactions, such as foot withdrawal. No
withdrawal after six trials were de ﬁned as complete nerve block or
100% MPE (Maximum peak effect). The duration of the nerve block was
deﬁned as the period during which the nerve block exceeded 50% of
the MPE. In photo-triggered anesthesia experiments, the hind paw of
the rat with injected formulations were irradiated by the LED at
designed intensity for 2 –5 min under brief iso ﬂurane–oxygen
anesthesia. The LED light source had a diameter of 2.5 cm, allowing it to
fully cover the entire footpad.
Histopathological analysis
Upon completion of the antitumor ef ﬁcacy study, mice from all
experimental groups were euthanized. Major vital organs (heart, liver,
spleen, lungs, and kidneys), along with tumors, were collected for
histopathological evaluation. In the local anesthesia study, rats were
euthanized at 4 and 14 days following the administration of free tet-
racaine, prodrug 7a, or the combination treatment (7a + 6b), and the
injected hind footpads were harvested for analysis. All histopatholo-
gical processing and staining were performed by Nanjing Youmeng
Biotechnology Co., Ltd.
Statistics and reproducibility
All quantitative data represent the mean ± SD of ≥3 independent
replicates. Statistical analyses were performed in GraphPad Prism 9,
with signiﬁcant differences evaluated by one-way ANOVA followed by
Tukey’s test. Data visualization was done using Origin 2022 and
GraphPad Prism 9.
