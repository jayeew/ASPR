Non-Hermitian physics expands our understanding of topological phases and open quantum
systems. In practice, solving non-self-adjoint boundary problems for differential equations and their
physical correspondences is a challenging research topic for both mathematics and physics.
Physically, the reciprocal in ﬂuence of intrinsic boundary conditions on the global properties of
otherwise Hermitian bulk systems remains underexplored. Here, we use α-T
3 nanoribbons as a
platform to demonstrate that stable anomalous dangling bonds from speci ﬁc lattice termination
provide such non-Hermitian boundary conditions. By rigorously deriving an equivalence boundary-
localized complex absorbing potential, we demonstrate that these atomic con ﬁgurations induce
anomalous ﬂat edge states, exceptional points, and imaginary Landau levels. These boundary effects
alter bulk transport, leading to a breakdown of the quantized Hall conductance within a speciﬁc energy
window. Our ﬁndings establish boundary engineering as a distinctive mechanism for manipulating
quantum transport and topological states in both solid-state materials and synthetic quantum
simulators.
The exploration of emergent phenomena in non-Hermitian systems1 has
signiﬁcantly advanced our understanding of fundamental physics and its
potential applications. A pivotal development in this ﬁeld involves
extending Hermitian systems to parity-time ( PT ) symmetric non-
Hermitian systems 2, which has stimulated extensive theoretical
investigations3– 14 and experimental reali z a t i o n sa c r o s sv a r i o u s
platforms15– 28. The observed non-Hermitian characteristics originate from
diverse physical mechanisms, including: (1) gain-loss engineering in elec-
tronic circuits, photonic, optical or mechanical systems
29– 37,( 2 )ﬁnite qua-
siparticle lifetimes in condensed matter systems 38,39, (3) environmental
coupling in open quantum systems40– 42, and (4) disorder effects in metallic
systems43– 45. These characterized systems exhibit fundamentally distinct
spectral properties compared to theirHermitian counterparts, with energy
eigenvalues extending from purely real values to complex conjugates. Such a
spectral complexity gives rise to distinctive phenomena of branch singula-
rities on non-Hermitian eigenvalue manifolds, known as exceptional points
(EPs)
46,47, where both eigenvalues and eigenvectors coalesce. The singula-
rities are classiﬁed as second-order or higher-order EPs based on degeneracy
multiplicity48– 52, with their geometric manifestations potentially evolving
from discrete points to continuous lines or surfaces under speciﬁc parameter
constraints. Their enhanced sensitivity to perturbations has enabled appli-
cations in high-precision sensing53– 55. It has been shown that the funda-
mental nature of EPs originates from the topology of the analytic system’s
complex energy spectrum 56,57. Beyond EPs, non-Hermitian topology
manifests also through two complementary aspects: spectral topology
characterized by generalized gap structures (point gaps, line gaps) and
wavefunction topology governed by rede ﬁned symmetry classi ﬁcations.
Especially, the symmetry classiﬁcation of non-Hermitian systems extends
beyond the conventional 10 Altland-Zirnbauer classes
58 to the 38 Bernard-
LeClair classes59– 61, achieved through systematic symmetry ramiﬁcation and
uniﬁcation. According to the modi ﬁed bulk-boundary correspondence
principle, nontrivial bulk topology corresponds to anomalous edge states
(AESs), which is a manifestation now recognized as the non-Hermitian skin
effect62– 68. Such features are fundamentally characterized by exponentially
localized wavefunctions at system boundaries under open boundary con-
ditions. The wave-functional accumulation has been rigorously conﬁrmed
through experimental observations. While current understanding primarily
attributes edge phenomena to bulk non-Hermitian properties, an intriguing
open question emerges: could edge-induced non-Hermitian characteristics
reciprocally modify the global physical properties of the entire system?
Inspired by the mathematical fou ndations of non-self-adjoint
operators
69,70 and self-adjoint extensions 71– 74, we investigate non-self-
adjoint quantum systems where non-Hermiticity arises exclusively from
boundary conditions. A fundamental challenge resides in establishing
physical realizations of such syst ems while developing quantitative
descriptors for emergent phenomena governed by non-Hermitian bound-
ary constraints. This dual requirement necessitates: (1) constructing
material platforms with precisely en gineered non-Hermitian boundary

ADBs exhibit a fundamentally distinct and stable character. Their physical
origin is rooted in the lattice termination: when a fully coordinated atom
(e.g., from sublattice A or C) resides atthe physical edge, it leaves a nearby
atom (from sublattice B) with a bond truncated by the boundary. This
conﬁguration creates a high-energy, unpassivable virtual site (denoted by
black empty circles) adjacent to the edge. Crucially, the resulting boundary
condition, where the electronic wavefunction does not vanish (ψ
edge ≠ 0), is

non-reciprocal hopping between physical sites and these virtual sites, the
system can preserve real-valued energy eigenvalues for a subset of states, a

Hermitian systems host exotic features beyond their Hermitian counter-

points, and imaginary Landau levels. Consequently, the edge currents
generated by the AESs disrupt the canonical quantized Hall plateaus when
the Fermi energy lies near the excep tional points. The hallmark Hall

from this anomalous regime.

Conclusions
In summary, we have demonstrated that intrinsic non-Hermiticity inα-T3
nanoribbons arises from bond truncation at speciﬁc zigzag edges, leading to
the formation of ADBs. These ADBs impose non-Hermitian boundary
conditions, which generate a spectrum featuring exceptional points with
fractional winding number ±1/2,ﬂat and tilted anomalous edge states, and
imaginary Landau levels. Crucially, this boundary-induced non-Hermiticity
manifests in bulk transport by disrupting the quantized Hall conductance
within the anomalous-state energy window.
Our theoretical framework is grounded in microscopic boundary
conditions andﬁnds rigorous justiﬁcation through non-self-adjoint exten-
sion theory, which is equivalent to provide a formal basis for the complex
absorbing potential description o f the boundary. The predictions are
directly applicable to a range of experimental platforms. These include
material realizations of the α-T
3 lattice, such as (111)-oriented SrTiO 3/
SrIrO3/SrTiO3 trilayers (dice lattice) and Hg1−xCdxTe quantum wells (Kane
fermions), where zigzag edges can be engineered. Moreover, ourﬁndings are
highly relevant to synthetic quant um simulators, including photonic
waveguides and metasurfaces with boundary-localized loss, cold atoms in
optical lattices with selectively addressed edge dissipation, and non-
Hermitian RLC circuits with tailored boundary impedances.
