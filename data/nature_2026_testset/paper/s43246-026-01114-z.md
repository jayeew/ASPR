Two-dimensional (2D) van der Waals (vdWs) heterostructures have rapidly become promising
platforms for advanced optoelectronic devices, mainly due to their extraordinary capability to control
electronic and optical properties at atomically abrupt interfaces. However, realizing high-performance
optoelectronics strongly relies on precise engineering at these interfaces to optimize charge carrier
generation, transport, and extraction efﬁciencies. In this work, we present a comprehensive theoretical
and experimental study of VP/PdSe
2 vdWs heterostructure targeting photodetector. Our ﬁndings
indicate that VP/PdSe 2 vdWs heterostructures exhibit a type-I band alignment, enabling
photodetection across the visible (VIS) to near infrared (NIR) spectral regions. By introducing graphene
as a contact layer (Gr/VP/PdSe
2), we signi ﬁcantly enhanced device performance, achieving a
remarkable responsivity of 111.3 AW −1 and an external quantum ef ﬁciency of 26001%, representing
an enhancement of about three orders of magnitude compared to the bare VP/PdSe 2 devices.
Moreover, the engineered photodetector demonstrated superior stability, maintaining consistent
performance over 100 operational cycles, and an exceptionally fast photoresponse time of
approximately 10 ms. Additionally, the device showed robust polarization-sensitive detection
capabilities with an impressive dichroism ratio across a broad spectrum. This work paves the way to
realize innovative 2D heterostructure for high-performance, multifunctional optoelectronic
applications.
Over the two past decades, two-dimensional (2D) materials have attracted
substantial attention due to their unique optical, electronic, and mechanical
properties arising from their atomically thin layered structures 1,2.T h e s e
atomically thin 2D materials are featured with semiconducting, metallics,
insulating and topological behaviors which provide a versatile platform for
developing the advanced optoelectronic devices with multiple
applications
3,4. Unlike traditional bulk semiconductor materials 5,6,2 D
materials exhibit strong light-matter interactions, high carrier mobilities,
and signiﬁcantly reduced defect densities, enabling devices to achieve high
sensitivity and fast response times in photodetectors research regime 7,8.
Moreover, due to the absence of dangling bonds, van der Waals (vdWs)
heterostructures composed of these 2D materials typically possess atom-
ically clean interfaces without conventional interface state problems 9,10.
Consequently, band alignment engineering in these 2D vdWs hetero-
structures can profoundly enhance optoelectronic performance. Usually,
2D vdWs heterostructures are classi ﬁed into type-I and type-II band
alignment structures
11,12.

Results and discussion
Theoretical calculations
To shed light on how the VP and PdSe2 layers interact at the atomic level, we
carried out DFT calculations. These theoretical investigations allowed us to
explore the band structure in detail, observe how charge redistributes across
the interface and determine how the energy bands align when the two
materials are stacked together. The insights gained from these calculations
provide a solid foundation for understanding the electronic behavior of the
VP/PdSe
2 heterobilayer and help explain the performance trends observed
in our experiments.
We began by optimizing the geometry of the isolated monolayers. As

1.32 eV. VP, on the other hand, crystallizes in a monoclinic phase with space

calculated gap of approximately 1.75 eV (PBE), as presented in Supple-
mentary Fig. 1c, d.

The magnitude of the band offsets plays a crucial role in determining
carrier conﬁnement at the heterointerface. In type-I vdWs heterostructures,
conduction and valence band offsets act as energetic barriers that restrict
electrons and holes within the narrower-gap layer. In the VP/PdSe

offsets of several hundred meV are widely reported to be suf ﬁcient for
effective carrier conﬁnement and to govern recombination and transport
processes in 2D vdWs heterostructures. Similar offset magnitudes have been
experimentally demonstrated to control carrier localization and optoelec-
tronic performance in a broa d range of 2D semiconductor

positioned between those of the Au and VP layers, effectively minimizes the

carriers, as depicted schematically in the energy band diagram (Fig.6d). The

promotes rapid carrier collection wit hout disrupting the band structure,
whereas the Cr/Au contact de ﬁnes the potential reference and assists in
extracting electrons through interfacial band bending. Consequently, the
balanced conﬁnement and transport dynamics in this type-I conﬁguration

Experimental section
Device fabrication . Few layers of VP and PdSe 2 ﬂakes were obtained
through mechanical exfoliation from high-purity bulk crystals
(~99.999% purity). The PdSe
2 bulk crystals were commercially purchased
from Shanghai Onway Technology Co., Ltd. VP crystals synthesis by
CVT technique and then obtained few layers by mechanical exfoliation.
For device assembly, VP ﬂakes were ﬁrst exfoliated onto pre-cleaned p-
type SiO
2/Si substrates using the conventional scotch tape method. The
SiO2 substrates were thoroughly cleaned by sonicating in acetone and
isopropyl alcohol for 15 min each, then dried using a gentle stream of
nitrogen gas. Following this, few-layer PdSe
2 ﬂakes were exfoliated onto a
PDMS stamp and accurately transferred onto the VP ﬂakes using a dry-
transfer approach, creating the VP/PdSe 2 vdWs heterostructure. Gra-
phene ﬂakes were prepared using the scotch tape exfoliation method and
then transferred onto the VP side of the device using a PDMS stamp. The
transfer and stacking of 2D materials were carried out using a transfer
station system supplied by Shanghai Onway Technology Co., Ltd. This
process enabled the assembly of the Gr/VP/PdSe
2 vdWs heterostructure.
Ultraviolet maskless photolithography was carried out using a Litho-
ACA Pro system (TuoTuo Technology) to deﬁne the electrode areas, and
Cr/Au (10 nm/50 nm) electrodes were deposited by thermal evaporation
to complete the fabrication process.
Characterization. The thickness of the VP/PdSe2 vdWs heterostructure
was characterized using an Atomic Force Microscope (Bruker Dimen-
sion Icon, Germany). Surface potential mapping was performed using the
same instrument in scanning probe mode. Raman spectra were recorded
with a Horiba LabRAM HR Evolution from Japan, employing a 532 nm
excitation laser. For Photoluminescence (PL) mapping, Dalian Chuan-
grui multifunctional laser scanning confocal ﬂuorescence imaging
system-FLIM 300 was employed. For photoelectrical measurements,
including broadband photodetection, an NBET Wavelength Mono-
chromatic Tunable Light Source System (Model: Omno 150300, Beijing
NBET Technology Co., Ltd.) was used in conjunction with a Keithley
2638B source meter. Polarization-dependent measurements were carried
out using a photocurrent measurement setup from TuoTuo Technologies
(Suzhou Co., Ltd.), equipped with a Keithley 2612B. The intensity of the
incident light was continuously monitored by a Thorlabs PM101 power
meter. All fabrication steps and device measurements were performed
under ambient conditions at room temperature.

Theoretical calculations . All ﬁrst-principles calculations in this work
were performed using density functional theory (DFT) as implemented
in the Vienna ab initio Simulation Package (VASP)
54– 56. The projector-
augmented wave (PAW) method57 was employed in conjunction with the
Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional within the generalized gra-
dient approximation (GGA) 58 to describe exchange – correlation inter-
actions. To accurately capture interlayer vdWs interactions in the VP/
PdSe
2 heterostructure, the Grimme DFT-D3 dispersion correction
method was applied 59.
The plane-wave energy cutoff was set to 450 eV, ensuring accurate total
energy and force convergence. Brillouin zone (BZ) integrations were carried
out using a Γ-centered Monkhorst-Pack grid of 5 × 5 × 1 k-points. Atomic
positions were fully relaxed until the residual forces on each atom were
below ~0.01 eV/Å. To obtain an accurate estimation of the band gaps, the
HSE06 was used in selected electronic structure calculations. Charge density
difference plots were obtained by subtracting the self-consistent charge
densities of the individual monolayers from that of the combined
heterostructure.
