Ferroptosis, an iron-dependent, lipidperoxidation-driven programmed cell
death, holds substantial promise for cancer therapy, yet its translational
potential is hindered by widespread intrinsic resistance. While glutathione
peroxidase 4 (GPX4) is a well-established ferroptosis suppressor, the epige-
netic circuitry coordinating GPX4-related mechanisms remains elusive. Here,
via genome-wide screening, we identify ten-eleven translocation 1 (TET1)—a
key mediator of DNA 5-hydroxymethylation—as a master controller of cancer
cell ferroptosis susceptibility. In acute myeloid leukemia (AML), TET1 enhan-
ces 5hmC deposition at the glutamate-cysteine ligase catalytic subunit (GCLC)
promoter to activate glutathione/γ-glutamyl-peptide metabolism, fortifying
GPX4-dependent defense. Concurrently, TET1 activates NFκBs i g n a l i n gt o
upregulate GTP cyclohydrolase-1 (GCH1), conferring GPX4-independent fer-
roptosis resistance. Critically, co-targeting TET1/GCLC/GCH1 with low-dose
ferroptosis inducers exhibits potent therapeutic effects against both
ferroptosis-sensitive and -resistant AML. Our work pos i t i o n sT E T 1a sap i v o t a l
epigenetic hub governing ferroptosis surveillance, and provides a translatable
strategy to overcome ferroptosis resistance in cancer, with AML as a paradigm.
Ferroptosis, a non-apoptotic form of programmed cell death
driven by iron-dependent lipid peroxidation, is closely associated
with cancer. Dysregulation of ferroptosis-related pathways,
including the System Xc
--GPX4 axis, iron metabolism, and lipid
peroxidation, contributes to cancer progression and offers
optional therapeutic vulnerabili ties for aggressive malignancies
1.
However, cancer cells exhibit heterogeneous susceptibility to
ferroptosis 2,3. Although ferroptosis induction has emerged as a
promising approach to treat responsive cancers (e.g., originally,
RAS-mutated cancer cells), ferroptotic resistance of irresponsive
cancer cells (e.g., some breast and colon tumors) restricts its
clinical applicability
4–8. Therefore, it is important to identify the
key mechanism that determines the disparity of cancer cell vul-
nerability to ferroptosis.

Cancer cells rely on sophisticated surveillance mechanisms to
maintain cellular homeostasis and evade ferroptosis. One of the major
ferroptosis surveillance mechanisms is mediated by glutathione per-
oxidase 4 (GPX4)—the sole enzyme in mammalian that reduces phos-
pholipid peroxides to corresponding alcohols
9,10. Its upstream cystine
imports and glutathione (GSH) synthesis form the primary step to
suppress ferroptosis. GSH, whose synthesis is initiated by the
glutamate-cysteine ligase (GCL) complex via the ligation of cysteine
with glutamate, is utilized by GPX4 as an essential cofactor to detoxify
lipid peroxidation. Intriguingly, under cystine-deprived conditions, the
GCL complex catalyzes γ-glutamyl-peptides accumulation instead
11.
Despite these insights, the genetic and epigenetic regulation of GSH
and γ-glutamyl-peptides biosynthesis, and more importantly, the
precise activation mechanisms of ferroptosis defense systems remain
unclear
12.
Emerging evidence reveals that mammalian cells employ distinct
mechanisms to combat oxidative damage13. Such GPX4-independent
surveillance pathways are often mediated by enzymes that generate
metabolites with radical-trapping antioxidant (RTA) activity, including
ferroptosis suppressor protein 1 (FSP1), dihydroorotate dehy-
drogenase (DHODH), and GTP cyclohydrolase-1 (GCH1), etc
10,14–16.E a c h
of these systems operates as an independent, parallel defense
mechanism. Up till now, only a handful of regulators have been iden-
tiﬁed as critical modulators of multiple surveillance pathways
17,18.
Therefore, a deeper understanding of the crosstalk between GPX4-
dependent and -independent ferroptosis surveillance systems is
essential. Identifying master regulators that orchestrate these path-
ways could uncover therapeutic targets for ferroptosis induction in
cancer treatment.
DNA methylation is an epigenetic mechanism essential for the
regulation of gene expression, and the demethylation process can be
catalyzed by ten-eleven translocation (TET) dioxygenases. TETs,
comprising TET1, TET2, and TET3, catalyze the oxidative conversion of
5-methylcytosine (5mC) to 5-hydroxymethylcytosine (5hmC),
5-formylcytosine (5fC), and 5-carboxycytosin (5caC) in an Fe (II)/a-
ketoglutarate-dependent manner. While 5mC generally promotes
gene silencing, 5hmC is often associated with active demethylation
and can serve as a distinct epigenetic mark that in ﬂuences gene
activation
19–22. Accumulating evidence underscores the critical invol-
vement of TET proteins in diverse biological processes, including
regulation of gene expression, cell growth, differentiation and
death
23,24. TET1, the founding member of TET family, has been identi-
ﬁed as an oncogene in multiple cancers 19,21,25–27. Our recent study
revealed that Tet1 deﬁciency disrupted cellular responses to iron
overload28. Despite these advances, the role of TET-mediated DNA
modiﬁcation in ferroptosis remains poorly understood.
Here, we show the role and regulatory mechanism of TET1 in
determining cancer cells vulnerability to ferroptosis. Higher expres-
sion of TET1 was found in ferroptosis-resistant cancer cells than in
sensitive cells. Strikingly, the ferroptosis surveillance function of TET1
is partially independent of GPX4. On one hand, TET1 epigenetically
modulated its target gene, the glutamate-cysteine ligase catalytic
subunit (GCLC), which encodes the key enzyme catalyzing the initia-

accumulation; on the other hand, TET1 activated NF κB signaling,
promoted GCH1 transcription, and thus, induced ferroptosis resis-
tance in a GPX4-independent manner. Moreover, using acute myeloid
leukemia (AML), the most common acute hematopoietic malignancy
in adults, as a study paradigm, we demonstrate the anti-cancer ther-
apeutic potential of the combinational administration of ferroptosis
inducer, TET inhibitor, GCH1 inhibitor, and/or GSH synthesis inhibitor.
Collectively, our ﬁndings demonstrate the pivotal role of TET1 in
controlling cancer cell susceptibility to ferroptosis, with particular
signiﬁcance in AML. TET1 orchestrates this process through both
GPX4-dependent and -independent mechanisms. These results
highlight the therapeutic potential of targeting TET1-mediated fer-
roptosis surveillance pathways for the treatment of AML and other
malignancies that employ similar protective systems.

Role of the TET1/GCLC axis in GSH and γ-glutamyl-peptide
synthesis
The GCL complex has dual functions: Under normal conditions with
adequate cystine supply, it catalyzes the canonical ligation of cysteine
with glutamate and initiates GSH synthesis; conversely, during cystine
deprivation, it switches to a non-canonical function by conjugating
glutamate with alternative amino acids, thereby conferring ferroptosis
resistance through glutamate scavenging
11. To comprehensively
understand the role of TET1 in this dual-pathway regulation, we sys-
tematically investigated its impact on both canonical GSH production
and non-canonical γ-glutamyl-peptides accumulation. In primary BM
cells obtained from 9 AML patients (designated AML1-9), we observed
a positive correlation betweenTET1 expression levels and intracellular
GSH content (Supplementary Fig. 7a). In order to investigate the effect
of TET1 on GSH synthesis, we detected cellular GSH levels. We found
that TET1 knockdown signiﬁcantly reduced GSH level, which could be
exacerbated by BSO co-treatment (Supplementary Fig. 7b). To the
opposite, overexpression of TET1 increased intracellular GSH, which
could be reversed by BSO treatment, while TET1-HxD showed no
obvious effects (Supplementary Fig. 7c). TET inhibitor Bobcat339 sig-
niﬁcantly reduced GSH levels (Supplementary Fig. 7d, e). Combined
treatment with BSO and Bobcat339 further strengthened GSH reduc-
tion (Supplementary Fig. 7d, e). In addition, such combinational
treatment also resulted in more dramatic suppression in GSH synthesis
induced by the System Xc

Ex vivo studies validated that inMA9-o r AE9a-expressing AML cells of
Mx1-Cre+/-Tet1ﬂox/ﬂox mice, induced depletion of Tet1 signiﬁcantly
inhibited GSH synthesis, especially in MA9-AML cells; co-treatment
with BSO even more dramatically suppressed GSH synthesis (Supple-

results indicate the role of TET1 in promoting GSH synthesis.

axis wherein both TET1 and NFKB2 critically modulate GCH1 expres-
sion and/or function, thereby unveiling the existence of the TET1/NFκB
signaling/GCH1 axis in cancer ferroptosis regulation. Intriguingly, we
observed a reciprocal regulatory relationship between GCH1 and
GPX4: Genetic inhibition of GPX4 triggered compensatory BH4 accu-
mulation through GCH1 activation, while GCH1 ablation conversely
enhanced GPX4 activity (Supplementary Fig. 10n, o). This feedback
regulation suggests an intricate crosstalk between these two major
ferroptosis defense systems, potentially maintaining redox home-
ostasis in cancer cells under certain conditions. Genetic ablation of
GPX4 resulted in a more pronounced suppression in cell viability
compared toGCH1 knockout, indicating the predominant role of GPX4
in AML ferroptosis surveillance (Supplementary Fig. 10p). Taken
together, we show the pivotal role of TET1 in controlling both GPX4-
dependent and -independent ferroptosis surveillance systems through
TET1/GCL and TET1/NFκB/GCH1 axis (Fig. 5l).

Discussion
In summary, ourﬁndings demonstrate thatTET1 plays a pivotal role in
modulating cancer cells' susceptibility to ferroptosis. Mechanistic
investigations revealed that TET1 regulates two key ferroptosis-related
genes: GCLC and GCH1. On one hand, TET1-mediated transcriptional
activation of GCLC, a critical subunit in GSH synthesis, enhances GSH
production while simultaneously promoting glutamate scavenging via
γ-glutamyl-peptide accumulation, thereby initiating the TET1/GCL/
GSH synthesis cascade. On the other hand, TET1 also activates the
transcription ofGCH1, conferring cancer cell resistance to ferroptosis
independently of GPX4. These results establish TET1 as a central reg-
ulator of ferroptosis through dual, complementary mechanisms.
In recent years, epigenetic modi ﬁcations, including KDM4A
mediated DNA methylation, P53 mediated histone modi ﬁcations,
METTL14, YTHDC2 mediated RNA modiﬁcations, DUB mediated deu-
biquitinating and noncoding RNAs, etc., have been shown to be
involved in regulating ferroptosis
54–56. However, the precise roles of
major DNA epigenetic modulators, such as DNMTs, in cancer cell fer-
roptosis are still controversial
57–60. Whether TET-mediated DNA
demethylation directly in ﬂuences ferroptosis remains mostly
unknown. Here we show that TET1 expression is negatively correlated
with ferroptotic sensitivity in cancer cells. Genetic depletion or phar-
macological inhibition of TET1 signiﬁcantly enhanced ferroptosis sus-
ceptibility, whereas TET1 overexpression conferred resistance.
Importantly, combined treatment with a TET1 inhibitor and a ferrop-
totic inducer exhibited potent therapeutic ef ﬁcacy against AML,
especially ferroptotic resistant AML, both in vitro and in vivo. These
ﬁndings establish TET1 as a key epigenetic regulator of ferroptosis
resistance in cancer cells.
In the present study, we demonstrate that TET1 transcriptionally
activates GCLC. It has been reported that GCLC expression is under
sophisticated transcriptional control. While NFE2L2 serves as a core
transcription factor
41,42, other regulators —including NFκB, Sp-1, acti-
vator protein-1 (AP-1), metal response (MRE), and antioxidant response
(ARE)/electrophile responsive (EpRE) elements, etc.,—have also been
shown to bind the promoter region of GCLC, and collectively regulate
its transcription
61. For instance, in gastric cancer cells, actin-like pro-
tein 6A (ACTL6A) cooperates with NFE2L2 to promote GCLC expres-
sion and confer ferroptosis resistance 62. Here, we demonstrate that
TET1 restores GCLC expression and partially rescues its function even
under NFE2L2-deﬁcient conditions. While it remains unclear whether
TET1 is essential for NFE2L2-mediated transcriptional regulation, our
ﬁndings indicate that TET1 may serve as a previously unrecognized key
regulator of GCLC, acting at least in part independently of NFE2L2.
These results further suggest potential crosstalk between TET1 and the
established NFE2L2/GCLC axis, highlighting the need for further
investigation into the precise role of TET1 within this axis, as well as in
the broader transcriptional network governingGCLC expression.
Conceptually, our ﬁndings provide signi ﬁcant insights into fer-
roptosis by identifying TET1 as a master regulator that orchestrates
both GPX4-dependent and -independent surveillance mechanisms.
While GPX4 has been well-established as a central regulator of fer-
roptosis through its ability to detoxify lipid hydroperoxide
9,63,i t s
activity depends critically on the presence of its cofactor GSH and a
selenium atom within the catalytically active selenocysteine
residue
64,65. GPX4, its upstream cyst(e)ine import and GSH synthesis
were recognized as the primary mechanism to suppress ferroptosis9,66.
Over time, cells have evolved additional, sophisticated defense
mechanisms mediated by RTA-producing enzymes such as FSP1,
DHODH, NOS2, and GCH1
14–16,50,67, which operate independently of
GPX4 to terminate phospholipid peroxidation. Notably, GCH1 has
emerged as a key player in ferroptosis resistance, catalyzing the pro-
duction of BH4/BH2 antioxidants that selectively protect certain
polyunsaturated fatty acyl (PUFA)-PLs from peroxidation
16. Although
both the GCLC/GSH and GCH1/BH4 systems are known to regulate
ferroptosis susceptibility9,16, how these distinct pathways are coordi-
nated remained unclear. Our work resolves this question by demon-
strating that TET1 serves as a unifying regulator that simultaneously: (1)
modulates the GSH metabolic pathway through GCLC activation, and
(2) promotes GPX4-independent ferroptosis resistance via GCH1
transcriptional upregulation. This discovery reveals an unexpected
cross-talk between two of the major ferroptosis surveillance systems
and identi ﬁes TET1 as a pivotal node in the ferroptosis regulatory
network. Our ﬁndings not only advance our fundamental under-
standing of ferroptosis regulation but also highlight TET1 as a potential
therapeutic target for modulating ferroptosis sensitivity in cancer
treatment.
Cysteine serves as an essential component in maintaining redox
homeostasis. As the rate-limiting precursor for GSH synthesis, cystine
deprivation induces ferroptosis
68. While the dual regulatory systems
governing GSH and γ-glutamyl-peptide synthesis in ferroptosis have
only been identiﬁed very recently11, their underlying genetic and epi-
genetic mechanisms remain poorly understood. Here, we show TET1-
mediated epigenetic modiﬁcation plays a pivotal role in coordinating
both GSH biosynthesis and glutamate scavenging pathways. These
ﬁndings complement our previous discovery of Tet1’s involvement in
iron overload response
28, collectively establishing TET1 as a master
regulator of multiple facets of iron metabolism and ferroptosis. While
our current study focuses on the major established pathways, we
acknowledge that TET1-mediated DNA modiﬁcations may potentially
regulate additional, yet-to-be-identiﬁed ferroptotic defense mechan-
isms—an intriguing possibility that warrants future investigation.
While ferroptotic inducers have demonstrated signiﬁcant poten-
tial for ferroptosis- sensitive cancers, e.g., ﬁbrosis carcinoma
9,t h e i r
broad clinical application remains controversial 69,70 due to several
challenges: (1) potential toxicity concerns, particularly regarding HSC
maintenance
71; and (2) the heterogeneous responses of cancer cells to
ferroptosis induction2,3,9. To address these limitations, we propose a
therapeutic strategy that enhances cancer cell susceptibility to fer-
roptotic inducers through targeted inhibition of TET1.O u r ﬁndings
demonstrate that combination therapy using ferroptosis inducers
together with TET1 inhibitors—or inhibitors targeting its downstream
effectors GCL and GCH1 —shows remarkable ef ﬁcacy against both
ferroptosis-sensitive and -resistant cancers. Importantly, this approach
may extend beyond oncology, as targeting the TET1 regulatory axis
may also hold therapeutic promise for various ferroptosis-related
diseases, e.g., neurodegenerative disorders
72.
