Results and discussion
Identiﬁcation of a steroid hydroxylase P450stri
Bacterial P450s are typically expressed in water-soluble form, making
them suitable for further engineering and appilications24.A m o n gd i f -
ferent P450 systems, reactions catalyzed by the self-suf ﬁcient P450s
(SSPs) with a heme domain and a CPR domain containing FAD and FMN
are particularly operable and active
12,25, but their substrates are usually
less bulky, such as the case of P450BM3. Therefore, it would be valuable
to ﬁnd new P450 enzymes naturally using bulky steroids as substrates.
To simplify the screening efforts on obtaining steroid hydro-
xylases with high efﬁciency on electron transfer, we discovered a SSP,
namely P450stri, fromStreptomyces triculaminicusby genome mining
using heme-associated nucleotide sequence as a probe. As shown in
Fig. 1A, P450stri located at a distinct evolutionary clade compared to
P450
BM3 (41.4% amino acid sequence identity) and is classi ﬁed into
CYP102G subfamily. Notably, CYP102G subfamily has been demon-
strated to differ in substrate scope from P450
BM3
26,27, suggesting that
P450stri may possess unique catalytic properties due to divergent
evolutionary paths.
Next, we heterologously expressed P450stri inE. coliBL21 (DE3) as
a soluble protein with good yield (Fig. S1). The puri ﬁed enzyme was
characterized by CO binding assay, which displayed the typical cyto-
chrome P450 spectrum with a characteristic absorbance peak at ~
450 nm (Fig. 1B).
According to previous notion
12, CYP102 family members possess
remarkable activity toward fatty acids such as myristic acid (MA).
Indeed, when MA was used as a substrate, P450stri could catalyze the
ω-hydroxylation of MA at positions from ω−1t o ω−4( F i g .1Ca n d
Supplementary GC-MS data), and ω−2 position in MA was more
favorable to be hydroxylated. Subsequently, testosterone (TST), a
typical steroid, was served as the substrate to explore the possibility of
hydroxylating steroids by P450stri. Under optimized conditions
(Figs. S3 –S5), we observed that P450stri preferentially utilizes less
expansive NADH for catalysis of steroid substrates (Fig. S6), which is
more advantageous for synthetic application compared to the use of
NADPH by P450
BM3

hydroxylated products with a NADH regeneration system. After pur-
iﬁcation and structural elucidation, the products were identiﬁed as C-
15β,C - 1 6β and C-2β in a ratio of 35: 54: 11 (Fig. 1D and Supplementary
NMR data), suggesting the nature of multisite hydroxylation by this
enzyme. Given the native steroid hydroxylation activity of P450stri, it
could be served as a starting enzyme for regio- and stereo-selective
hydroxylation of steroids.
Construction of a H2O2-driven biocatalytic system using
P450stri
The applications of P450 enzymes typically suffer from poor selec-
tivity, low catalytic ef ﬁciency, and reliance on expensive NAD(P)H
cofactor. Alternatively, recent success on the conversion of P450s to
peroxygenases paves a new way for C-H activation in a cost-effective
manner
18. However, the heme group in P450s is easy to be oxidized by
high concentrations of H2O2, leading to a rapid enzyme inactivation.
For instance, single mutation of P450BM3_F87A could convert a P450
enzyme to peroxygenase, but the H2O2 concentration typically had to
be kept below 40 mM regardless of different reactions28–31, suggesting
that high H 2O2 tolerance for these enzymes is a key factor to decide
their activity. Meanwhile, to avoid the direct use of H 2O2,a l t e r n a t i v e
methods, including employing an equimolar concentration of urea
hydrogen peroxide or using H
2O2 regeneration system, have been
developed to slow down the heme oxidation rate for better perox-
ygenase activity
20,32,33. Nevertheless, the inherited H2O2 sensitivity has
been a major issue for practical utility of the peroxygenases. Given the
lower expression level of the oxygenase domain of P450stri, we chose
full-length enzyme for the investigation. As previously implicated to be
a crucial residue
28,34,35, we changed Phe88 to a smaller Ala. The resulting
mutant P450stri_F88A (M1) showed steroid hydroxylation in the pre-
sence of H 2O2, indicating that same situation in P450 BM3 is also
applicable to P450stri. In our case, the highest product formation by
M1 was achieved in the range of 120 –200 mM H
2O2 (Fig. 2A), which
could be a collective outcome from the decrease of steric hindrance
and hydrophobicity within the heme environment. In addition, com-
pared to natural peroxygenases as well as other engineered
peroxygenases

hydroxylation of steroids, the tolerance of P450stri_F88A against H2O2
was much stronger, re ﬂected by signi ﬁcantly increased product for-
mation. Thus, 150 mM H2O2 was chosen for subsequent investigation.
Compared to NADH-mediated reactions, M1 exhibited favorable
properties to pH and temperature in the H 2O2-diven system
(Figs. S7–S9), which is beneﬁcial to application.
Next, the performance of M1 mutant on TST hydroxylation was
evaluated by H 2O2-mediated peroxygenase. Compared to the use of
NADH regeneration system, 15β-selectivity was improved from 35% to

product distribution of 15β-OH was changed from 81% to 94% by H2O2-
diven hydroxylation, partially due to the reduction of further oxidation
under NADH-dependent system (Figs. 2D and S11). Taken together,
P450stri could be converted to a peroxygenase to show high H

Engineering to improve peroxygenase activity based on a Round
Flask model
In conventional enzyme engineering, the generation of a large number
of mutants is a prerequisite to obtain desired enzyme mutant for
various purposes, which is laborious and screening-dependent
44.
Recently, semi-rational engineering of residues around the substrate
binding pocket or substrate tunnels
18,45,46, namely Focused Rational
Iterative Site-speciﬁc Mutagenesis (FRISM)47, has become an advanced
approach to produce a smart library of mutants. However, FRISM
usually focuses on a particular region for the increase of the efﬁciency
on the engineering efforts. In our case, after generating the M1 variant
with strong H
2O2 tolerance and high regioselectivity, we had to deal
with another important issue of increasing its peroxygenase activity.
Based on the substrate entrance tunnels of M1 and the rigid cyclic
structures of steroids, a model of Round Flask was introduced as a way
for the semi-rational engineering of this enzyme, in which substrate
binding pocket and substrate entrance tunnels were separately yet
cooperatively engineered to achieve simultaneous increase of selec-
tivity and activity in a “Two-dimensional” fashion. Meanwhile, MD
simulations suggested that, compared to the binding pose in wild-type
P450stri (Fig.3A), TST is located closer to the substrate binding pocket
in M1 (Fig.3B). By eliminating unfavorable steric hinderance at position
88, the D ring was positioned above heme cluster, which pulls C15
forward to the CpdI center. This altered substrate binding pattern in
M1 was likely to account for the improved 15 β-selectivity. Although
substrate binding site associated with heme environment is critical for
determining regioselectivity, the substrate tunnels are also associated
with the activity
18,48. To simultaneously increase regioselectivity and
activity, our focus was then centered around the substrate accessing
tunnels in M1 variant. As shown in Fig.4A, preliminary inspection of the
substrate accessing tunnels of M1 by CAVER 3.0 plugin

almost all the mutants maintained high 15 β-selectivity derived from

suspected positions positively affected peroxygenase activity. By

Expansion of two-dimensional engineering approach to
“P450stri branch”
Since P450 enzymes ubiquitously occur in nature, it was important to
address the distribution and evolutionary relationship of P450stri-
associated enzymes. Therefore, based on approximately 250 homo-
logous sequences, we performed EFI-SSN analysis
55 for P450stri-
associated enzymes. According to Fig. 7A, most of the enzymes are
located in the phylum of Actinomycetota,f o l l o w e db yBacillota and
Pseudomonadota, suggesting that the homologous proteins of
P450stri generally occur in bacterial kingdom. According to evolu-
tionary distinctions, the “P450stri branch” is composed of twelve
potential P450 enzymes. Given their sequence homolog, we specu-
lated that the enzymes in this branch might generally possess the
capability of catalyzing steroid hydroxylation. To test this hypothesis
and to verify the feasibility of our engineering approach, we randomly
selected three P450s among this branch, namely P450sabi, P450sdsm,
and P450sgri, for further investigation (Figs. S1, S2, S38 –S42). These
enzymes share about 41% sequence identity with P450
BM3 and all
belong to the less-studied CYP102G subfamily26.
To test whether the Round Flask model could be applied to the
engineering of other enzymes in the same branch with a two-
dimensional fashion, the bene ﬁcial information on M1 and M4 var-
iants of P450stri was then transformed to P450sabi, P450sdsm, and
P450sgri at their corresponding positions, generating six mutants for
peroxygenase comparisons under optimal H
2O2 concentrations
(Figs. S43 and S45). As a result, all variants using M1 as the template,
including P450sabi_F87A (P450sabi_M1), P450sdsm_F86A
(P450sdsm_M1), and P450sgri_F88A (P450sgri_M1), indeed acquired
peroxygenase activity with higher 15 β-selectivity. This strongly sug-
gested that on the change of conserved Phe in the region of “round
bottom” is sufﬁcient to convert a typical P450 monooxygenase as a
peroxygenase with higher regioselectivity (Fig.7Ba n dT a b l eS 4 ) .F o r
example, the construction of mutant P450sgri_M1 successfully turned
P450sgri to a 15 β-hydroxylase, along with the enhancement of
regioselectivity from 28% to 87%. Moreover, when this residue was
combined a bene ﬁcial residue located in the substrate tunnel for
mutagenesis, the conversions by resulting variants with double
mutations were profoundly increased (Fig.7C), further indicating the
broad utility of the present approach on the improvement of perox-

After determining the kinetic parameters of the variants gener-
ated from the Round Flask model (Fig. S46), the catalytic efﬁciencies of
these variants with double mutations, exempliﬁed by P450sabi_F87A/

“P450stri branch”, demonstrating the rationality of the Round Flask
model for steroid peroxygenase engineering and the versatility of this
engineering approach.
