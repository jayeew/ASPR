Ferroelectrics hold signiﬁcant promise for a wide range of applications owing
to their spontaneous polarization characteristics. Despite exhibiting multiple
polarization mechanismsthat demonstrate signiﬁcant potential for electro-
magnetic functional materials, the practical deploymentof ferroelectric
polymers has been inhibited by the lack ofprecise control over polymer chains
at the atomic scale and the relatively low stability of the polar phase. Here, a
procedure of fortifying the ferroelectric polyvinylideneﬂuoride phase is pro-
posed by the facet modulation, achieving stable ferroelectric polymer through
engineering the interaction between the inorganic rigid crystal facets and
organic ﬂexible molecular chains at the atomic scale. The constructed polar
ferroelectric polymers composite systems exhibit a broad distribution of
relaxation times along with multi-polarization characteristics from megahertz
to terahertz frequencies. The composite system mitigates the apparent loss-
bandwidth trade-off, thereby achievingbroadband polarization properties
across multiple frequency bands while maintaining a dissipation efﬁciency
above 99.9%. The demonstrated approach presents a breakthrough in
achieving the stable ferroelectric polymers through facet-induced stabiliza-
tion, providing deep insights for the development of high-performance elec-
tromagnetic functional materials.

Through precisely controlled synthesis to selectively expose the

atomic scale. The mediation of polymer interfaces by speci ﬁc plane

stability of the ferroelectric phase of PVDF. This work establishes
the crystal facet control of inorganic ﬁllers as a variable for
manipulating the ferroelectric phase and molecular dipole dis-

tunable electromagnetic attenuation, operating from the MHz to
GHz range and extending into the THz band. These properties
originate from their enhanced polarization intensity along with
multiple polarization mechanisms accompanied by a broad dis-
tribution of relaxation times. Concurrently, we propose an elec-
tromagnetic modulation mechanism based on polarons in
ferroelectric polymers, enabling multi-band operation, which
breaks the trade-off between high loss and wide bandwidth.

On the {111} facet, the coordination environment of surface S
atoms is near complete saturation, whereas on the {100} facet, both
surface Ni and S atoms possess a high density of unsaturated coor-
dination sites, resulting in a higher surface energy (Fig. S7). These
unsaturated sites form high-density local polarization centers, mak-
ing the {100} facet more polar and signi ﬁcantly enhancing interfacial
electron coupling and charge transfer capabilities (Figs. S8 –S10,
Supplementary Tables S1, S2). The higher polarity implies that the
highly polar {100} facet readily undergoes strong dipole-dipole
interactions with β-PVDF molecular chains, which themselves exhibit
relatively high surface energy and polarity. This, in turn, makes the
{100} facet key to inducing and stabilizing the strongly polar β-phase
of PVDF.

Polarization mechanisms are the core for regulating the EM
response of P/N ferroelectric samples. Beneﬁting from their abundant
molecular chain rotational freedom and spontaneous polarization
characteristics, organic ferroelectric polymers can leverage intrinsic
multi-relaxation mechanisms to recon ﬁgure the distribution of
extreme values in electromagnetic parameters. This broadens the
relaxation time, thus facilitating highly ef ﬁcient broadband electro-
magnetic energy response
51. In multi-band EM loss, it plays a crucial
role through the synergistic effects of interfacial, spatial, and orien-
tational dipole polarizations. The functions of each polarization
mechanism in different frequency bands are as follows: As shown in
Fig. S65, in the MHz frequency band, the loss mode of P/N ferroelectric
samples is dominated by interfacial dipole polarization loss. The dif-
ference in permittivity between the ferroelectric phase PVDF and the
inorganic ﬁller induces interfacial dipoles. Under the action of an
external EM ﬁeld, these interfacial dipoles undergo rotational polar-
ization. Through the dipole relaxation process, the EM energy in the
MHz frequency band is converted into heat energy and dissipated,
achieving EM loss in this frequency band
52. When entering the GHz
frequency band, the polar groups of the ferroelectric phase PVDF are
affected by EM ﬂuctuations, resulting in changes in dipole moment in
the spatial dimension. The spatial distribution state of molecular
chains directly affects the polarization ef ﬁciency, enabling targeted
dissipation capability for EM waves in the GHz frequency band. At this
time, the dipoles dissipate the EM waves in the GHz frequency band
through vibration and rotation
53.
As the frequency increases, the orientational rotation of dipoles in
the alternating EM ﬁeld becomes increasingly intense. In the THz fre-
quency band, since the ferroelectric phase PVDF is a semi-crystalline
polymer, the synergistic effect of the crystalline and amorphous phase
structures in the sample promotes the orientational adjustment of
dipole molecules under the THz high-frequency EM ﬁeld (Fig. S66).
Factors such as the characteristics of the crystal-amorphous interface
and the degree of molecular chain orientation determine the polar-
ization relaxation characteristics, which can precisely match the EM
loss requirements in the THz frequency band, and realize energy dis-
sipation through the orientational adjustment process of dipoles
54.I n
summary, P/N ferroelectric samples utilize the synergy of interfacial,
spatial, and orientational dipole polarizations. Different polarization
mechanisms jointly regulate the EM response of the material across
multiple frequency bands to achieve multi-band EM loss (Fig. S67).
Compared to ferroelectric EM loss materials reported in recent
studies, the P/N samples proposed in this work maintain superior loss
capability across various frequencybands. The evaluation metrics for
material performance vary with electromagnetic frequency bands: in
the MHz band, it is determined by the dissipation factor; in the GHz
band, it is based on effective bandwidth and reﬂection loss; and in the
THz band, the shielding effectiveness per unit thickness is employed
(Fig. S68, Table S10)
50,55–68. This performance stems from the diverse
polarization types generated by the ferroelectric phase transition of
PVDF and the broad distribution of relaxation times in PM samples.
These provide valuable insights for developing advanced broadband
EM compatibility materials.
In summary, this study demonstrates a facet-induced strategy for
modulation of stable and uniform polymer ferroelectric phase,
revealing the pivotal regulatory role of intrinsic polarization properties
in the ferroelectric polymer matrix for governing multi-band efﬁcient
electromagnetic responses. By the combination of ﬁrst-principle cal-
culations and AFM experimental procedures, it is demonstrated that
the {100} crystal facet of NiS
2 stabilizes the ferroelectricβ-phase PVDF
by reducing its energy below that of the non-ferroelectric phase and
increasing its energy difference, thereby achieving a stabilized ferro-
electric PVDF system and multi-band EM dissipation performance. This
effect arises from the spontaneous polarization induced by the ferro-
electric phase β-PVDF, leading to a broad distribution of relaxation
times and enhancing multiple types of polarization mechanisms, with
relaxation frequencies across megahertz, gigahertz, and terahertz
frequency ranges. As a direct manifestation of these phenomena,
enhanced polarization loss and a systematic increase in absorption
coefﬁcient are observed. These ﬁndings not only elucidate the
mechanism of crystal facet engineering-driven PVDF ferroelectric
phase transition evolution but also establish a synergistic principle
linking the ferroelectric polarization of organic PM, multi-band fre-
quency response expansion, and enhanced EM wave loss.

The morphology of the resulting nanoparticles was studied by scan-

equipped with a copper target at a wavelength of 1.5418 Å. The ele-
mental composition was characterized by X-ray photoelectron spec-
troscopy (Thermo Scientiﬁc K-Alpha). The U-I curves of the samples
were measured by a semiconductor analyzer (Keithley 2400). AFM-IR
measurements were carried out on a Bruker Anasys nanoIR3 system.
Both topography and chemical images were simultaneously collected
in the tapping mode. The local IR spectra were obtained by placing the
atomic force microscope tip on top of the sample. The HRTEM images
were captured on FEI Talos F200X transmission electron microscope
at an accelerating voltage of 200 kV. The ﬁlm was precisely sectioned
using a Leica EM UC7 ultramicrotome. After that, the sectioned sam-
ples were placed on the copper carrier network support carbonﬁlm for
transmission electron microscopy imaging. Kelvin probe force
microscopy (KPFM) and Piezoelectric reaction force microscopy
(PFM) were performed on a commercial atomic force microscope
(OXFORD-MFP-3D Origin+). High-stiffness conductive probes
(k ≈ 20 N/m) were used in the experiments. To obtain a local piezo-
electric response loop, the DC switch is switched from −30 V to 30 V.
The ferroelectric P-E hysteresis loops were measured using a Radiant
Precision LC II tester (USA). The temperature-dependent dielectric
constant was measured using an Agilent 4294 A impedance analyzer
(USA) over a temperature range of 20 °C to 150 °C at frequencies of
10 kHz, 100 kHz, 1 MHz, and 2 MHz.

used k-point grids of 10 × 6 × 3 for PVDF, 2 × 2 × 1 for NiS
