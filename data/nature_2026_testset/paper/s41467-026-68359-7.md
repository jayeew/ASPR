Current research in photoelectrochemical (PEC) systems focuses on designing
photoelectrodes with light-enhanced charge transfer efﬁciency at the solid-
liquid interface, which leads to the oxidation of various reductant molecules at
the anode, posing a challenge in terms ofselectivity. In this study, we introduce
an original concept of dark-enhanced PEC system (dark-PEC) based on bis-
muth oxybromide (BiOBr)-photocathode. The reversible bismuth reduction
causes the passivation and regenerati o no fo x y g e nr e d u c t i o ns i t e so n
the photocathode, thereby leading to an unconventional reverse photocurrent
(|I

binding and charge transfer between glutathione and BiOBr, the reversibility
of bismuth reduction is increased, with a 10-fold enhancement of reverse
photocurrent. Compared to traditional PEC systems, the dark-PEC system
enables the selective recognition of reductant molecules by photocathodes,
unveiling an original PEC reaction mechanism. This study will offer valuable
insights into device design and redox processes in the PEC ﬁeld.

Reverse photocurrent characteristic of BiOBr
First, the morphology and structure of BiOBr were investigated. The
transmission electron microscopy (TEM) image reveals that the as-
synthesized BiOBr presents a nanosheet morphology with a size of
~50 nm (Fig. 2a). The diffraction peaks of BiOBr can be recognized in
the XRD result (Fig. 2b), which are assigned to the standard lattice
planes (PDF#78-0348) of BiOBr
36,37. Further, high-resolution transmis-
sion electron microscopy (HRTEM) and selected-area electron dif-
fraction (SAED) images verify the crystalline structure of BiOBr
nanosheets, with the (101) crystal plane exposed (Supplementary
Fig. 1). The corresponding energy-dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) ele-
ment mapping images of BiOBr present that the Bi, O, and Br elements
are uniformly distributed (Supplementary Fig. 2).
Then, the systematic research on photocurrent transients of the
photocathodes modiﬁed by BiOBr in electrolytes was studied in dif-
ferent atmospheres. When the BiOBr-photocathode is measured in air-
electrolyte, the photocurrent signal displays a positive direction
(Fig. 2c), indicating a non-traditional reverse photocurrent (|I
dark |> |
Ilight | ). We refer to this PEC process as Dark-PEC system. Afterward,
nitrogen (N 2)a n do x y g e n( O2)-saturated electrolytes were used to
examine the photocurrent response. Interestingly, when O2 is absent
or saturated in the electrolyte, the photocurrents of the BiOBr pho-
tocathode show a negative direction (Fig. 2d), that is, the traditional
cathode-photocurrent characteristic (|I
dark |< | Ilight |) . M o r e o v e r , w e
observed that the surface color of the BiOBr photocathode undergoes
varying degrees of change during the conduct of the PEC test in
electrolytes saturated with N
2,O 2, or air atmosphere (Fig.2e). In an N2-
saturated electrolyte, the color change of the BiOBr photocathode is
most pronounced, shifting from white to brownish. However, during
testing in the O

undergo any color change and remained white. In contrast, under the
air condition, a slight color change can be observed at the edge of the
tested photocathode, falling between the conditions of N
2 saturation
and O 2 saturation. This indicates that the unconventional reverse
photocurrent phenomenon is related to the O 2 concentration in the
electrolyte. Irrespective of whether the O2 c o n c e n t r a t i o ni se x c e s s i v e l y
high or low (Fig. 2f), the dark-PEC process does not take place in the
BiOBr photocathode. Therefore, to gain insight into this distinct dark-
PEC characteristic in the BiOBr photocathode, we seek answers by
exploring the color-changing mechanism and the ORR of the electrode
surface.

Dark PEC mechanism of BiOBr photocathode
The BiOBr photocathode was directly exposed to light to investigate
the dynamic changes in its surface coloration. As illustrated in Fig.3a,
the surface color of the electrode progressively darkens over time
under continuous illumination. Notably, upon cessation of light
exposure, the electrode ’s surface color gradually diminishes, ulti-
mately reverting to its initial white state. This phenomenon indicates
that BiOBr possesses excellent photochromic reversibility. Based on
the color changes of the electrode in different atmospheres as
described above in Fig.1e, it can be inferred that O
2 in the air is capable
of restoring the color state of BiOBr. Subsequently, to explore the
inﬂuence of reversible color changes on the electronic structure of
BiOBr, we utilized XPS technique to analyze the binding energies of
elements before and after exposure to light. In the Bi 4f (Fig. 3b), O 1 s
(Supplementary Fig. 3a) and Br 3 d (Supplementary Fig. 3b) spectra,
noticeable negative shifts of binding energy were observed under light
conditions, suggesting the accumulation of photogenerated electrons.
It is worth noting that after illumination, an additional characteristic
charge transfer (CT) satellite peak
38 appears in the XPS of the Bi ele-
ment at around 157.5 eV, between the characteristic peaks of Bi-metal
and Bi
3+. BiOBr nanosheets undergo photoreduction after illumination,
leading to the formation of Bi(3-&)+ that stores electrons39.F u r t h e r m o r e ,
electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) measurements were
employed to investigate the reversible behavior of BiOBr under alter-
nating light and dark conditions. In comparison with the sample in the
dark state, BiOBr after illumination exhibits a markedly enhanced EPR
signal at g = 2.01 (Supplementary Fig. 4). This enhancement can be
attributed to an increased concentration of oxygen vacancy defects
under photoexcitation, which is consistent with the appearance of the
CT satellite peak in the Bi 4f XPS spectrum. To elucidate the nature of
the dark-PEC phenomenon, cyclic voltammetry (CV) was employed to
investigate the interfacial behavior between the BiOBr photoelectrode
and the electrolyte. Obviously, under the saturated conditions of O
2
and N2, the CV curves of BiOBr exhibit the same interfacial reaction
before and after light exposure ( Supplementary Fig. 5a, b). In an O 2
atmosphere, the reduction of Bi3+ is unlikely to occur when the BiOBr
photocathode is adequately surrounded by O 2. Consequently, the
photogenerated electrons facilitate the interfacial ORR, thereby
enhancing the cathode current density. In a N
2 atmosphere, an oxi-
dation characteristic peak at approximately 0.3 V (vs. Ag/AgCl) is
observed under both light and dark conditions. This peak can be
ascribed to the oxidation behavior of Bi
(3-&)+ on the BiOBr surface due
to the anaerobic environment. The increased intensity of the oxidation
peak after illumination suggests that the Bi
3+ reduction on the BiOBr
surface is substantially enhanced under light exposure. Meanwhile, the
initial potential of the cathode reduction reaction exhibits a negative
shift to approximately −0.3 V, and the current intensity under light
exposure remains signiﬁcantly higher than that in the dark. This phe-
nomenon can be attributed to the transfer of photoelectrons to H
2Oi n
an O2-free environment. However, under air conditions, the CV curves
of BiOBr show different interfacial behavior before and after light
exposure. Speciﬁcally, the formation of Bi
(3-&)+ still occurs during light
exposure, but the oxidation peak associated with Bi(3-&)+ vanishes in the
dark (Fig. 3c). Furthermore, upon illumination, the initial potential of
ORR on the BiOBr photocathode exhibits a negative shift, and the
reduction current diminishes. This behavior aligns with the reverse
photocurrent observed in the above mentioned dark-PEC process.

(3-&)+, ORR and the reverse pho-
tocurrent under air conditions. The i-t curves of the BiOBr photo-
cathode under varying light durations were measured by controlling
the dark state duration to 40 s. Supplementary Fig. 6 shows that
extending the illumination time from 5 s to 100 s increased reverse

During illumination, Bi3+ in BiOBr undergoes photoreduction to form
Bi(3-&)+ (a new energy band marked by the blue line), which induces
surface passivation and decreases the interfacial ORR efﬁciency. Upon
cessation of illumination, Bi(3-&)+ is re-oxidized by O2, facilitating surface
recovery and enhancing the ORR performance at the photocathode/
electrolyte interface. In contrast, under O
2 and N 2 conditions, since
BiOBr is incapable of achieving reversible bismuth reduction reactions
during the alternation between light and dark cycles, it exhibits a single
bandgap characteristic (Fig. 3f). Speciﬁcally, under O
2-saturated con-
ditions, photoelectrons of pure Bi 3+-O-Br facilitate the surface ORR,
thereby increasing the current density. Under N2-saturated conditions,
the photoelectron transfer from pure Bi(3-&)+-O-Br to H2O enhances the
current density.
Generally, the majority of researchers focus on enhancing the
ORR to boost the traditional cathodic photocurrent, as O2 serves as an
efﬁcient natural electron acceptor. However, in this dark-PEC system,
the reverse photocurrent at the cathode is determined by reversible
bismuth reduction occurring at the electrode-electrolyte interface.
Introducing an excessive number of electron acceptors would only
attenuate the intensity of the reverse photocurrent, a phenomenon
that has been corroborated by the direction of the photocurrent under
prior O
2-saturated conditions. It was unexpected to observe that the
reductant GSH could signiﬁcantly enhance the reverse photocurrent
signal, while ascorbic acid (AA) failed to exhibit a comparable
enhancement effect (Fig. 4a). In addition, in the N
2 atmosphere, nei-

that the presence of GSH results in the most signiﬁcant color change,
with the slowest recovery rate after illumination ceases.
Subsequently, GSH and AA were individually mixed with BiOBr.
The resultant samples were collected after centrifugation and further
characterized using FTIR (Supplementary Fig. 12). The FTIR results
reveal that the BiOBr treated with GSH exhibits prominent character-
istic peaks at approximately 1640 cm
−1 and 1500 cm−1, which can be
attributed to the vibrational modes of the C = O and N-H functional
groups in GSH. Furthermore, the characteristic peak positioned at
approximately 417 cm
−1 exhibits a shift towards the high-frequency
region, which is indicative of the formation of the Bi-S bond. This
observation was further corroborated by Raman spectroscopy, which
veriﬁes the binding behavior between BiOBr and GSH. As depicted in
Fig. 4e, two distinct vibrational peaks were observed within the range
of 300-400 cm
−1, and these peaks can be ascribed to the vibrational
characteristics associated with the Bi-S bond 42– 44. To further investi-
gate the state of the photoelectrode after PEC processing, XPS char-
acterization was conducted on different samples following a 1600 s
photocurrent test (Supplementary Fig. 13). Theﬁndings reveal that the
BiOBr photocathode treated with GSH exhibits Bi
(3+&)+ and increased
Bi(3-&)+ characteristic peaks in the high-resolution spectra of Bi elements
(Fig. 4f). Due to the reversible redox reaction of GSH on the BiOBr
photoelectrode surface, under illumination, GSH is oxidized by Bi3+ to
form ox-GSH. When the light is turned off, ox-GSH is subsequently
reduced back to GSH during the regeneration of high-valent bismuth
from its low-valent state. Comparative colorimetric analysis of the
electrolyte solution under light and dark conditions reveals a decrease
in absorbance after light exposure (Supplementary Fig. 14), further
conﬁrming the reversibility of the redox interconversion between GSH
and ox-GSH on the BiOBr photocathode. Therefore, it can be con-
cluded that in the dark-PEC system of BiOBr photocathodes, Bi species
serve as recognition sites for GSH molecules (forming Bi
(3+&)+ ~G S H
complexes). Under illumination, it functions as the pseudo anode to
obtain electrons from GSH molecules to generate increased Bi
(3-&)+.
This process signiﬁcantly reduces the surface ORR activity efﬁciency.
Upon cessation of light exposure, increased Bi(3-&)+ was re-oxidized and
facilitated the interfacial reduction reactions (ORR, ox-GSH reduction),
thereby enhancing the reversibility of bismuth redox reactions and
reverse photocurrent (Fig.4g). Nevertheless, AA molecules possessing
identical reducing properties are unable to be recognized by the
pseudo anode and thus cannot enhance the reversibility of bismuth
reduction (Fig. 4h). These two distinct redox pathways elucidate an
original molecular recognition mechanism based on the dark-PEC
pattern. The photocathode is also capable of detecting and quantify-
ing reductant molecules with high sensitivity and accuracy, owing to
the reversible nature of the valence state transformation of the Bi
element.
Dark PEC sensing for GSH
Finally, we investigated the feasibility and practical application of the
molecular recognition mechanism for GSH quantitative analysis in the
dark-PEC system. By comparing the signal responses of various
reductant molecules, including glucose (Glu), procyanidins (CY), uric
acid (UA), mannitol (MA), dopamine (DA), and AA, our results
demonstrate that GSH detection exhibits great selectivity. In addition,
photocurrent response tests conducted on various thiol-containing
compounds revealed that, apart from ox-GSH (which is capable of
undergoing reversible redox reactions with GSH), the other thiol
compounds failed to achieve reversible transformation during the
light-dark cycles. As a result, they were unable to effectively facilitate
the interconversion between high-valent and low-valent bismuth spe-
cies, thereby further substantiating the high selectivity of this
approach toward GSH molecules (Fig.5a, b and Supplementary Fig. 15).

Discussion
In summary, we developed an original dark-PEC mechanism using the
BiOBr semiconductor. This mechanism harnesses the reversible
photochromic properties of BiOBr to generate a reverse photocurrent
(|I
dark |> | Ilight | ) in the cathodic system. Building on this concept, we
designed a selective PEC sensing platform for GSH detection. Unlike
traditional PEC systems based on anodic oxidation/cathodic reduction
reactions, the dark PEC system achieves speci ﬁc recognition of
reductant molecules at the photocathode surface. Under illumination,
the uncontrollable reconstruction of the Bi valence state in the BiOBr-
photocathode leads to a reduction in surface ORR activity. After the
illumination ceases, the electrode structure recovers, thereby enhan-
cing the interfacial ORR performance. Furthermore, by adjusting the
electrolyte atmosphere and BiOBr concentration, it was demonstrated
that the dark PEC behavior of the BiOBr photocathode depends on
whether O
2 in the electrolyte can fully suppress the photoreduction of
BiOBr. This work offers an original perspective for engineering inter-
facial redox processes in PEC applications.

Synthesis of BiOBr
In a typical synthesis, Bi(NO 3)3·5H2O (1 mmol), mannitol (3.3 mmol)
and PVP (200 mg) were dissolved in 30 mL of deionized water under
vigorous stirring for 10 min. Then, KBr (1.5 mmol) was added to the
above solution, and the mixture was stirred for 1 h. The resulting
solution was then transferred to a 50 mL Teﬂon-lined autoclave, sealed
and heated at 160 °C for 3 h. Finally, the products were collected by
centrifugation, washed multiple times with deionized water, and dried
in a vacuum oven at 60 °C for 10 h. During the synthesis of BiOBr, PVP
functions as a structure-directing agent and dispersant, guiding the
formation of two-dimensional sheet-like morphologies and effectively
preventing particle agglomeration. Mannitol acts as a reaction kinetics
modulator and a mild coordinating and reducing agent, enabling
controlled crystal growth by retarding the crystallization rate, thus
facilitating the production of nanosheets with high crystallinity and
well-deﬁned morphology. The synergistic interaction between PVP and
mannitol allows for precise control over the microstructural char-
acteristics of the ﬁnal product.
