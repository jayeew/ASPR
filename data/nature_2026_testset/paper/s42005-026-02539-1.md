Spin-orbit torques (SOTs) are essential for electrically controlling magnetic order in spintronic devices.
Platinum (Pt) is ubiquitous for SOT generation due to its strong bulk spin Hall and interfacial Rashba-
Edelstein effects. Here, we revisit this established viewpoint by investigating ultrathin Pt ﬁlms
interfaced with a typical magnetic insulator, terbium iron garnet. We ﬁnd that few-atom-thick,
nanogranular Pt exhibits exceptionally ef ﬁcient SOT-induced switching that cannot be explained by
these conventional mechanisms. This enhancement is attributed to the granular morphology of
sputtered Pt, which activates two complementary mechanisms: enhanced spin-orbit scattering at
grain boundaries, leading to an increased effective spin Hall angle, and localized current density
ampliﬁcation due to non-uniform conduction paths. Furthermore, adding a titanium (Ti) or manganese
(Mn) overlayer to thin Pt enhances the switching efﬁciency, indicating an active contribution from light
metals via orbital current generation. These ﬁndings uncover key SOT pathways in ultrathin
heterostructures and provide insights for optimizing spin-orbitronic device performance and enabling
energy-efﬁcient magnetic switching.
The spin Hall (SHE) and Rashba-Edelstein effects (REE) have been at the
center of spintronics research over the past two decades due to their ability to
generate transverse spin accumulation in response to a charge current 1,2.
This spin accumulation can exert spin -orbit torques (SOTs) on adjacent
ferromagnetic (FM) layers3–5, allowing for the electrical control of their
magnetization6–8. Despite the bulk and interfacial origins of the SHE and
REE, respectively, they give rise to SOTs with identical symmetry 9.C o n -

electron gas systems such as SrTiO3/Ta interfaces20. More recently, the spot-

We therefore turn to alternative, previously unexplored mechanisms
within the framework of SOT generation. TEM analysis reveals that ultra-
thin Pt ﬁlms are laterally inhomogeneous, consisting of thick grains inter-
connected by thinner regions. These grains can locally exceed λ
SD,Pt,
enabling conventional SHE-driven spin current generation. However, this
scenario alone would only lead to a partial switching restricted to TbIG
regions beneath the thick grains, contradicting our experiments, which
clearly show complete switching (seeSupplementary Note 6). In the mor-
phological environment presented in ultrathin Pt, we postulate that the
enhanced scattering at grain boundaries may increase the effective spin Hall
angle, thereby boosting the charge-to-spin conversion ef ﬁciency. Within
this framework, the three observed regimes can be interpreted as follows: in
thick Pt ﬁlms, bulk SHE dominates; in ultrathin Pt, concerted action of
scattering-driven spin conversion and local contributions from thick grains
enhance SOTs; and in the intermediate regime, neither mechanism is fully
optimized, thus leading to relatively lower SOT switching efﬁciency. Unlike
interfacial torques or any other interface-related mechanisms (e.g., magnetic
proximity effect), this morphology-driven enhancement is not thickness-
independent. Instead, it emerges under speci ﬁc conditions where grain
boundary scattering signi ﬁcantly reduces the electron mean free path,
creating more spin current generation,and ultimately lowering the critical

Granular structure of ultrathin Pt
The substantial enhancement of SOTs in ultrathin Pt requires further
analysis. To this end, we simulated a plausible growth scenario for
ultrathin Pt ﬁlms using a custom MATLAB script, in light of the electrical
and structural characterization performed above. The simulation models
a 100 × 100 nm
2 matrix, initially populated with a quasi-uniform ultra-
thin Pt background. Two key observations justify this continuous back-
ground layer. First, the current-voltage (I-V) characteristics of the device
with the thinnest Pt layer exhibit strictly Ohmic behavior (see Supple-
mentary Note 9), indicating the presence of a continuous metallic path
between contacts spaced approximately 300 μm apart. This rules out
conduction via hopping or tunneling between isolated Pt islands through
the insulating garnet. Second, the MOKE contrast change induced by
current pulses matches that induced by an external magnetic ﬁeld,
implying that the entire probed region switches uniformly. Therefore, the
SOTs must be (quasi-)uniformly distributed across the device. In the
simulation, this background Pt layer is assigned a mean thickness of
0.2 nm (corresponding to approximately a monolayer of Pt), with a
Gaussian distribution truncated between 0.1 and 0.3 nm and a standard
deviation of 0.04 nm.
Subsequently, Pt grains were randomly inserted into the matrix. Their
radii followed a normal distributionwith a mean 3 nm, a standard deviation
0.8 nm, and were truncated within 1 and 5 nm. Grain heights were sampled
from a Gaussian distribution centered at 1.7 nm, with a standard deviation
of 0.4 nm, and truncated within 0.2 and 2 nm. To emulate physical sput-
tering conditions, partial overlap between grains (up to 40% of their radius)
was allowed. Grains were iteratively added until the total integrated volume
of the simulatedﬁlm matched the target nominal thickness (e.g., 0.6 nm for
the thinnest sample).

Given the observed morphology, two additional competing scenario
can be considered. In ultrathin Pt layers with non-uniform thickness, the
granular structure implies a highly non-uniform spatial distribution of
current density. The current preferentially ﬂows through the more con-
ductive regions, resulting in locally higher current densities that may be
sufﬁcient to induce efﬁcient SOT switching. If most of the current is con-
ﬁned to the grains, the effective current density in these regions would be
higher than that estimated using th e nominal thickness. From this per-
spective, the granular morphology plays a key role, not by introducing new
scattering mechanisms, but by enabling localized regions with high current
density where conventional SHE mechanisms can operate efﬁciently. Once
domain nucleation occurs beneath t hese grains, full switching can be
achieved either via domain propagation or by applying a higher overall
current density. To examine this scenario andﬁnd its signature in the data,
we analyzed the switching behavior with nanosecond pulses, which mini-
mizes thermal contributions and provides clearer insight into the switching
process. Here, we assume that if the global switching is mediated by local
switching occurring beneath the thick Pt islands, then the switching would
be highly progressive and inhomogeneous due to different lateral sizes and
thickness of Pt grains, which would result in different current densities in
different grains for a given input current. This would naturally lead to a
broadening of the switching curve. Weestimated the relative current density
delay (see Supplementary Note 10), and we ﬁnd that this quantity pro-
gressively increases when the Pt thickness is decreased. This behavior sug-
gests that the granular morphology likely leads to more disordered
switching, characterized by multiple nucleation sites followed by domain
wall expansion. This could be interpreted as different current requirement
for different grain size, hence more granular structure resulting in broader
switching delay. The last plausible scenario arises if the lateral dimensions of
the current/thickness non-uniformity (a few nanometers) are smaller than
the domain wall width of TbIG. In such a case, the structure may be forced to
switch fully. We estimated the domain wall width for TbIG at this thickness
to be approximately 15 nm, which is comparable to the average spacing
between Pt grains obtained from our MATLAB simulations. Therefore,
both scenarios are plausible and support the conclusion that the granular
morphology enhances SOT switching efﬁciency in ultrathin Pt.
Conclusions
We have shown that ultrathin Pt ﬁlms, down to sub-nanometer nominal
thickness, can efﬁciently switch the magnetization of a magnetic insulator,
challenging conventional understanding of SOT generation. Our results
reveal that the nanogranular morphology of Pt plays a central role in
enhancing SOT ef ﬁciency, with two competing mechanisms previously
discussed: one based on morphology-induced scattering, and the other on
non-uniform current distribution arising from the granular structure.
These ﬁndings suggest that the structure itself, not just the material
properties, governs the efﬁciency of spin-charge conversion in ultrathin Pt.
The grain-like growth of ultrathin Pt is a well-known phenomenon when
Pt is deposited on oxide substrates, due to poor wetting and the Volmer-
Weber (island) growth mode. This behavior is not unique to TbIG, but is
expected to occur across a wide range of REIGs and other oxide-based
magnetic insulators, such as BiFeO
3,C o F e2O4, and EuO. In fact, similar
granular morphologies have been reported in Pt ﬁlms grown on non-
magnetic oxide surfaces such as TiO 2 and SiO 2,s u g g e s t i n gt h a t
morphology-driven scattering effects could be engineered by selecting
appropriate substrates. Therefore, we anticipate that the morphology-
enhanced spin Hall effect observed in our study could be explored in a
broader range of material systems, potentially enabling the development of
energy- and cost-ef ﬁcient spintronic devices. Furthermore, we demon-
strate that capping Pt with light metals, such as Ti or Mn, introduces
additional orbital torque contributions, which signi ﬁcantly reduce the
switching current and power consumption. These ﬁndings expand the
design space for spin-orbitronic devices, highlighting the critical role of
nanoscale morphology and orbital transport in enabling energy-ef ﬁcient
magnetic switching. Looking ahead, engineering grain structure and
leveraging orbital currents offer promising strategies for advancing next-
generation spintronic technologies.

150 mm. Substrates were held at 800 °C during growth, followed by
