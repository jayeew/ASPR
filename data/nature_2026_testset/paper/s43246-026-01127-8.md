Controlling material phases using gate voltage offers a promising alternative to traditional temperature
and pressure-based methods. However, despite the widespread use of ionic liquid gates, they suffer
from slow and complex response, limiting speed and accuracy that were originally expected from
voltage control. In this study, we evaluate the speed of the VO
2 phase transition by exploiting a solid-
state three-terminal device with an ultra-high- k gate dielectric, leading to the construction of Time-
Temperature-Transformation diagram in metal-insulator transition systems. Remarkably, the
transition speed is modulated by three orders of magnitude through gate voltage although the
transition temperature is affected by only 0.6 K. This effect on transition speed is 1000 times larger than
thermodynamic predictions, revealing the collectivity that underlies the energy barrier during phase
transition. The results indicate such collective behavior could enable high speed with minimal external
stimuli overcoming the power-speed trade-off in conventional electronics.
Field effect transistors that use phase transition materials as the channel can
induce a phase transition by modulatingthe interfacial electron density with
ag a t ev o l t a g e1,2. This gate-induced phase transition is expected to enable
highly accurate and fast control, making these transistors an effective tool for
studying the physical properties of strongly correlated electron systems
3,4.
Early attempts at gate-induced phas e transitions used solid-state gate
dielectrics such as SiO2
5,6,HfO2
7,8 and Al2O3
9. However, solid-state gating
was unable to accumulate enough elect rons to induce the transition. In
recent decades, ionic liquids have en abled the accumulation of a huge
number of electrons through the electr ic double layer, thereby enabling
voltage control of various types of phase transitions10–15. However, due to the
slow response of ionic liquids, it was not possible to systematically evaluate
the speed of gate-induced phase transitions. Despite the inherent high
precision and high speed of voltage control, ionic liquid gating cannot fully
leverage these advantages.
Recently, it has been realized thatthe metal-insulator transition in VO2
can be controlled even with solid-stategating by utilizing an epitaxial TiO2
gate dielectric16–18. This achievement is attributed to an ultra-high dielectric
constant of TiO2
19, which enables electron accumulation on the order of
1×1 0 14 cm-2, similar to that of ionic liquids. This solid-state gating is
expected to realize fast and accurate measurement of phase transition speed,
which cannot be achieved with ion liquid gating. Regarding the speed of
metallic phase transition, fast transitions have been observed with laser
irradiation that excites a large nu mber of carriers (sub-picosecond
timescale)
20–22, and with joule self-heating that causes thermal runaway
(sub-nanosecond timescale)23–27. However, both methods are highly
nonequilibrium processes, making it difﬁcult to extract information about
the rate-limiting processes or the energy barriers that is necessary for device
design. The intrinsically high-speed transition in VO 2 has made its eva-
luation difﬁcult, hindering the construction of a temperaturevs.t i m ep h a s e
diagram (so-called TTT diagram), which is typically used for understanding
phase transitions with much slower timescales28.
In this study, we systematically evaluate the speed of both the metallic
and insulating phase transitions in VO2 by leveraging solid-state gating and
present the TTT diagram for theﬁrst time to the best of our knowledge in
metal-insulator transition systems. The results show that an increased
number of accumulated electrons expo nentially increases the transition
speed, and this increase is much largerthan thermodynamic prediction due
to the collective behavior in phasetransitions, where more than 1000 VO2
unit cells are estimated to get involved in the energy barrier of the rate-
limiting process. Theseﬁndings suggest that the high-speed response driven

In-situ observation of metallic & insulating domains
To investigate the transient characteristics of the gate-induced phase tran-
sition in more detail, the VO2 channel was in-situ observed by a transmitted
optical microscope during the electrical measurements in Fig.2c, f (Fig. 5a,
b). When the light is transmitted from the backside of the Nb:TiO2 substrate,
the metallic VO 2 region is darker than the insulating region due to the
different optical transmittance34,35 leading to a contrast image that reﬂects
metallic and insulating domains in the VO2 channel.
Given the gate voltage modulates the number of accumulated electrons
at the interface, it is highly possible that the gate-induced phase transition
progresses in the out-of-plane dir ection from the interface toward the
surface. On the other hand, such simple out-of-plane domain creation and
growth should be suppressed to some extent given that the large area of the
in-plane domain boundary would require a signiﬁcant amount of positive
energy
32,36,37. The optical microscope images corresponding to the transient
characteristics in Fig.5a, b show that the heterogeneous domains are created
and grow in the in-plane direction. However, this domain boundary motion
is also a repetition of microscopic phase transitions and is a thermally
activated process. Therefore, the heterogeneous transition does not affect
the discussion. This nucleation and domain growth in the in-plane direction
is more clearly observed in Fig. 5c, d by focusing on the square regions in
Fig. 5a, b, respectively. Furthermore, it is also con ﬁrmed in Fig. S3 (Sup-
plementary Information 4) that the local brightness change is much steeper
than the averaged brightness change.These results indicate that the time-
scale of out-of-plane domain growth at each local region is much faster than
that of the in-plane domain growth, and therefore, the observed transient
characteristics is almost dominated by the nucleation and domain growth in
the in-plane direction. Although a speciﬁc description of the rate-limiting
process of the transition is beyond the scope of this paper, it may be inferred
that the nucleation and domain growth in the in-plane direction determine
the energy barrier. These discussions suggest that the collective behavior of
the VO
2 transition in TTT diagram is primarily related to the in-plane
domain boundary motion.

This unique switching mechanism, originating from the collectivity of the
phase transition, may be applicable to other materials systems with metal-
insulator transitions and martensitic transformations, where a similar free-
energy-based analysis will show the universal applicability of the
methodology.

Conclusion
A solid-state three-terminal device with a VO 2 channel was exploited to
demonstrate an accurate and systematic evaluation of the phase transition
delay for both metallic and insulating transitions. The ability to control the
gate voltage over a small rangeﬁnely enables a systematic evaluation of the
transition speed with respect to overshoot, allowing us to construct a TTT
diagram for theﬁrst time to the best of our knowledge in VO2. This has also
enabled the extraction of information on the thermally activated energy
barrier. As a result, the collective behavior of the transition in VO2 leads to a
signiﬁcant modulation of the energy barrier, and hence the initial rate of the
phase transition, even for a small change in thermodynamic variables. In

The result that the metal-insulator transition dramatically speeds up
with small external stimuli suggests t he possibility that phase transition
materials can be used to achieve both high speed and low power, which are
inherently trade-offs in conventional electronics, facilitating the design of
next-generation sensors and neuromorphic devices.
