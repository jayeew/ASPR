Superstructure with emitting Quantum Dots (QD) as building blocks can bring new avenues to rational
design in the synthesis of desired QD based functional materials with novel optical properties. Here we
report one pot colloidal synthesis of self assembled ﬂower like superstructures from InP/ZnSe/ZnS
QDs having yellow emission with photoluminescence quantum yield (PLQY) of 87 %. Such highly
emissive quantum dot-based superstructures were achieved through meticulous selection of surface-
capping ligands and engineering of surface chemistry. Here, tri-octyl-phosphine (TOP) plays a key role
in formation of the ﬂower like superstructures and improving the optical properties, which was further
investigated theoretically through ﬁrst principle calculations. The superstructures obtained are
structurally and optically stable for long periods of time. The heavy metal free highly emitting InP based
QD's size and composition tunability and their ability of forming superstructures will be highly
beneﬁcial for development of new solution processable building blocks for QD based superstructures
with superior and well controlled physical, optical and chemical properties. The synthesized InP based
yellow emitting nanostructures, will certainly be a promising candidate for the development of non-
toxic QD based devices in optoelectronics, plasmonics and catalysis.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the ﬁrst report of a one-pot
colloidal synthesis of InP/ZnSe/ZnS quantum dot assembled ﬂower like
superstructures exhibiting extraordinary optical properties. These bright
yellow emissive InP/ZnSe/ZnS self-assembled superstructures have absolute
photoluminescence quantum yield (PLQY) of 87%. In our synthesis pro-
cess, it was observed that TOP plays a major role in the formation of
superstructures as well as in improving their optical properties. To under-
stand the formation of InP/ZnSe/ZnS QD superstructures and their
dependence on synthesis parameters, the InP QDs with varying TOP
amounts, reaction temperature and growth time were synthesized. The
superstructures were investigatedby TEM, HRTEM, XRD, FTIR, SAXS and
DLS techniques. Optical propertieshave been assessed by absorption and
photoluminescence (PL) spectroscopy. The optical as well as structural
properties of InP QDs are affected by the nature of surface capping ligands
(Olylamine and TOP) present. We validated the experimental observations
with theoretical studies and showed that binding of TOP to the InP QD
surface is energetically more favourable than binding Olylamine to the
surface and triggering the self-assembled superstructures by regulating the
interparticle interaction forces. The most intriguing part of the study is
coating a ZnSe/ZnS shell over the InP superstructures, leading to extra-
ordinary enhancement in their optica l properties without altering their
structural novelty. The superior optical properties of the synthesized QDs
are again cross veri ﬁed by density functional theory (DFT) study. The
optical and structural properties haveshown long term stability, making it
suitable for optical applications.

Conclusion
This study demonstrates the potential of synthesizing ﬂower like super-
structures using super emissive quantum dots (QDs) as fundamental units,
opening new pathways for the deliberate design and synthesis of functional
QD-based materials with superior opti cal characteristics. We have suc-
cessfully established a one-pot colloidal method to synthesize self-assembled
superstructures composed of InP/ ZnSe/ZnS QDs, exhibiting strong
yellow emission with absolute photoluminescence quantum yield (PLQY)
of 87%. The formation of these superstructures critically depends on
the precise control of surface chemistry and the selection of appropriate
surface ligands, with tri-octyl-pho sphine (TOP) playing a pivotal role
in fostering nanoﬂower morphology and enhancing optical performance.
These observations were further supp orted by theoretical insights from
ﬁrst principle calculations. Importantly, the resulting superstructures
display remarkable structural and optical stability over extended periods.
The tunable size and composition of heavy metal-free InP-based QDs,
combined with their capacity to assemble into size tunable superstructures,
present signiﬁcant advantages for developin g new, solution-processable
building blocks with ﬁnely controlled physical, optical, and chemical
properties. Overall, the synthesize d yellow-emitting InP-based nanos-
tructures represent a promising, non-toxic 0D candidate for future optoe-
lectronic, plasmonic, and catalytic applications based on QD
superstructures.
