Cell-cell adhesion is crucial for maintaining cell functions and the integrity of
tissue structure in organisms. However,cell-cell adhesion cues have not been
effectively replicated in biomaterials and the associated mechanisms that
enhance neural regeneration remained largely unexplored. Here, we present a
diffusive N-cadherin functionalized hydrogel system, which provided cell-cell
adhesion cues to modulate intercellular communications to signiﬁcantly
promote the formation of active neural network via thrombospondin-1
mediated neural communication and activation of TGF-β/Smad pathway. The
dynamic assembly of N-cadherin at cell-hydrogel interface driven by adhered
neurons effectively facilitated the reshaping of membrane protrusions to
initiate intercellular adherens junctions. Further, this hydrogel system pro-
misingly promoted neurological function recovery in rats following traumatic
brain injury. Our study provides the principle of replicating diffusive cell
adhesion molecules to mediate cell-cell adhesion in hydrogels, which may
have broad applications in developing engineered biomaterials aimed at

Besides cell-extracellular matrix (ECM) interactions, cell-cell adhesion
and communication play critical roles in maintaining cellular functions
and the structural integrity of tissues in organisms
1– 3. Cell-cell adhe-
sion, mediated by membrane proteins, such as adhesion proteins and
receptor-ligand pairs, is essential for initiating intercellular commu-
nication that sustains tissue homeostasis and drives key physiological
processes, including cellular proliferation, survival, migration, and
differentiation
4– 7. Particularly in the central nervous system, cell-cell
adhesion-dependent neuron-neuron and astrocyte-neuron interac-

networks, which are signi ﬁcant for brain functions, including motor
function, memory, and learning

cell adhesion cues that enhance neural regeneration remain largely
unexplored.

Preparation and characterization of lipid modiﬁed hydrogel
To prepare lipid-modiﬁed hydrogel (L@SFMA), 1 mL solution of 1,2-
dioleoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine (DOPC) and 1,2-dipalmitoyl-sn-
glycero-3-phosphothioethanol (DPTE) lipid mixture was mixed with
2 mL SFMA solution (15% w/w) containing photoinitiator (0.5% w/w).
Upon UV irradiation (365 nm, 30 mW/cm

DPTE and methacryloyl moieties on silkﬁbroin chain. DOPC and DPTE
self-assembled within the hydrogel. To demonstrate the rapid process

vesicles forming lipid bilayer coating on hydrogel, which enhanced the
hydrophilicity of the L@SFMA hydrogel (Fig.2C). The gentle decrease

Diffusive N-cadherin in NL@SFMA hydrogel
Cell-cell adhesion signi ﬁcantly relies on free diffusion of adhesion
molecules based on dynamicﬂow of the membrane27.I no u rL @ S F M A
hydrogel system, we hypothesized that the diffusivity of lipids within
the hydrogel could be modulated by altering the ratio of DPTE (non-
mobile lipids, conjugated to the hydrogel network) and DOPC (mobile
lipids). Therefore, lipid samples were labeled by Texas Red-DHPE and
the diffusivity of lipids with various ratios (DPTE/(DPTE + DOPC): 0%,
5%, 50%, 100%) in the hydrogels was evaluated by ﬂuorescence
recovery after photobleaching (FRAP) assay. As shown in Fig.3Aa n dB ,
hydrogels containing 0% or 5% DPTE exhibited desirableﬂuorescence
recovery after photobleaching within 20 min, indicating that lipids
maintained diffusive in hydrogels containing 5% DPTE. As the content
of DPTE increased to 50% or 100%, the diffusivity of lipids in hydrogels
was decreased dramatically, due to the covalent binding of DPTE to the
silk ﬁbroin chain, which restricts lipid movement. Because the inter-
action between the lipid bilayer and the hydrogel network within the
hydrogel system containing 0% DPTE was unstable, the L@SFMA
hydrogel containing 5% DPTE was selected for further study.
Next, in order to equip hydrogel with cell-cell adhesion cues, N-
cadherin, a transsynaptic adhesion protein, was linked to 1,2-dioleoyl-
sn-glycero-3-[(N-(5-amino-1-carboxypentyl) iminodiacetic acid succi-
nyl] (DGS-nta) via nickel-coupled His tags and introduced to L@SFMA
hydrogel system through self-assembling to obtain a diffusive
N-cadherin functionalized hydrogel (NL@SFMA hydrogel). Within the
membrane, N-cadherin monomer can freely diffuse with the dynamic
ﬂow of the interfaces and form intercellular adhesion junctions in
intercellular interactions
15. Therefore, N-cadherin was labeled by Alexa
Fluor™ 488 and the diffusivity of N-cadherin in NL@SFMA hydrogels
was evaluated by FRAP assay. Compared to N-cadherin directly con-
jugated in hydrogel network (N-SFMA group), N-cadherin was freely
diffusive in NL@SFMA hydrogels, which may further facilitate N-
cadherin-mediated adhesive roles and intercellular interactions
(Fig. 3C and D). To further evaluate the stability of this coating,
NL@SFMA hydrogels were immersed in PBS for 7 days followed by
ﬂuorescence bleaching recovery assays. It was found that N-cadherin
in the NL@SFMA hydrogels still maintained effective diffusivity even
after 7 days, indicating the high stability of this hydrogel supports
subsequent studies in biological environments, including those in the
presence of cells (Supplementary Fig. 1A, B).
Cell-cell adhesion cues in hydrogel promote neural growth
N-cadherin plays a crucial role in neural development and regeneration
by facilitating processes such as neuronal migration, axonal elonga-
tion, and synapse formation
28. Diffusive N-cadherin on the cell surface
can assemble side-by-side to form clusters via cadherin-cadherin
interaction to induce cell-cell adherens junction and initiate cell-cell
contact (Fig. 3E), which allows cells to migrate, differentiate, mature,
and ultimately to form neural circuits
29. Therefore, to demonstrate
whether the diffusive N-cadherin in NL@SFMA hydrogel can form
clusters at membrane protrusions to promote neuronal growth,
N-cadherin in NL@SFMA hydrogel was labeled by Alexa Fluor ™ 488
and PC12 cells were seeded on the hydrogel. As shown in Fig.3Fa n dG ,
N-cadherin at the cell membrane-hydrogel interface assembled into
clusters. Within 30 min of incubation, dynamic formation and dis-
assembly of N-cadherin clusters occurred, coinciding with the
reshaping of membrane protrusions that facilitate neuronal migration,
due to free diffusion of N-cadherin in NL@SFMA hydrogel (Fig. 3H, I
and Supplementary Movie 1). Furthermore, to investigate the capacity
of NL@SFMA hydrogel on promoting axonal elongation, primary cells
from cerebral cortex of E16 Sprague-Dawley rat pups were seeded on
SFMA, L@SFMA, N-SFMA or NL@SFMA hydrogels respectively for
3 days, and the neural cytoskeleton was labeled with NF200 antibody
at 1, 2, 3 days. Attributed to cell-cell adhesions mediated by N-cadherin,
neurons cultured on the N-SFMA and NL@SFMA hydrogels showed a
signiﬁcant increase in neurite length, which was more rapid than pre-
viously reported
30,31. More importantly, compared to neurons in the
N-SFMA groups, the NL@SFMA group exhibited signi ﬁcantly
enhanced axonal elongation and improved intercellular connections
between neurons during 3 days of incubation (Fig. 4A– C), indicating
that the hydrogel with diffusive N-cadherins can better promote axo-
nal elongation and neural communication. In addition, neurons in the
NL@SFMA group migrated farther with a wider range of direction
(Fig. 4D, E and Supplementary Movie 2– 5). To further demonstrate the
validity of NL@SFMA hydrogel in enhancing synaptic activity and
neuronal communication, electrophysiological recordings were
employed to investigate synaptic transmission between neurons
32.
Spontaneous excitatory postsynaptic currents (sEPSCs) were observed
in autaptic cultures of cortical neurons grown on NL@SFMA hydrogels
for 14 days (Fig. 4H– I). The frequency (Fig. 4J), rather than the ampli-
tude (Fig. 4K), of sEPSCs was signiﬁcantly increased compared to that
of neurons cultured on N-SFMA hydrogels. This indicates that the
NL@SFMA hydrogel, which incorporates diffusive N-cadherin, can
effectively enhance synaptic activity and neuronal communication
primarily by increasing either the presynaptic release probability or
the number of synapses, rather than altering postsynaptic receptor
function. It has been reported that the initial neurite outgrowth is
typically determined by N-cadherin localization following cell division,
and increased concentration of N-cadherin at one pole of the neuron
can result in neurite formation
33. Therefore, the enrichment of diffu-
sive N-cadherin in NL@SFMA provided cell-cell adhesion cues that
enhance neuronal migration, neurite sprouting, and neural commu-
nication, thereby bene ﬁting the formation of the neural network.

limited ability to regenerate after injury, due to the pathophysiological
challenges in axonal regeneration and the reconstruction of functional
connections between neurons
34. Therefore, to verify the effect of
this hydrogel system (NL@SFMA) on promoting the axonal
regeneration of cortical neurons, SND450 neural culture plates were
used to isolate neuronal cytoplasm and axons to mimic the scenario of

Biological mechanisms of NL@SFMA hydrogel in promoting
neural growth
Next, the underlying biological mechanisms by which NL@SFMA
hydrogel promotes neurite growth and neural communication were
investigated using RNA-Seq analyses (Supplementary Fig. 3). A Gene
Ontology (GO) Chord plot for clustering enrichment analysis revealed
signiﬁcantly upregulated biological processes, including synapse
organization, neuron migration and cell-cell adhesion, as well as
downregulated process of mitochondrial apoptosis in primary cells
from the cerebral cortex of E16 Sprague-Dawley rat pups cultured on
NL@SFMA hydrogel after 24 h of incubation (Fig.5A). The proteins Bax
and Bcl-2, members of the Bcl-2 family, play critical roles in regulating
apoptosis by modulating mitochondrial membrane permeability
35.B a x
is a pro-apoptotic protein and Bcl-2 is a protein inhibiting apoptosis36.
As i g n iﬁcantly reduced ratio of Bax to Bcl-2 expression was observed in
cells of the NL@SFMA group, indicating that NL@SFMA, with diffusive
N-cadherin, effectively inhibited cell apoptosis by regulating mito-
chondrial activity (Fig. 5B and C). To further explore mitochondrial
activity, mitochondrial membrane potential ﬂuorescence staining
assay and ATP content measurements were conducted. The results
demonstrated that cells in the NL@SFMA group exhibited more intact
mitochondria and produced greater amounts of ATP compared to
other groups, suggesting that NL@SFMA hydrogel inhibits cell apop-
tosis by sustaining mitochondrial function (Fig.5D– F).
Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment
analysis indicated the activation of the transforming growth factor-β
(TGF-β) signaling pathway via thrombospondin-1 (THBS-1) in cells
cultured on the NL@SFMA hydrogel for 24 h, compared to those cul-
tured on the N-SFMA hydrogel withﬁxed N-cadherin (Fig.5A). THBS-1,
a member of the thrombospondin family, can act as an activator of
latent TGF- β by the interaction of a speci ﬁc sequence in THBS-1
(K412RFK415) with the latent TGF- β complex

through quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qPCR)
assays (Fig.5G). To verify whether the effect of NL@SFMA hydrogel on
promoting neurite growth is mediated by activating THBS-1-associated
cell-cell communication, primary cortical cells were cultured on the
NL@SFMA hydrogel with different concentrations of Gabapentin (an

neurites in the siTHBS-1 group was signiﬁcantly reduced, which further

on the NL@SFMA hydrogel; these effects were attenuated by Gaba-
pentin (Fig. 5H– J). Furthermore, it is well known that the AKT/mTOR
signaling pathway plays a crucial role in axonal regeneration

KEGG enrichment analysis also indicated the activation of the AKT/
mTOR signaling pathway in cells cultured on the NL@SFMA hydrogel
for 24 h. Therefore, to further explore the underlying biological
mechanisms by which NL@SFMA hydrogel promotes axonal regen-

this signaling axis. AKT is a serine/threonine-speci ﬁcp r o t e i nk i n a s e

Thr309, and Thr305 sites in the kinase domain, as well as Ser473,
Ser474, and Ser472 sites in the regulatory domain, can lead to com-
plete activation of AKT. Western blot results showed that compared to
the SFMA, L@SFMA, and N-SFMA groups, the expression levels of AKT
and pAKT in the NL@SFMA group were signiﬁcantly upregulated, with
the phosphorylation level of Ser473 site (Fig. 5K) being signi ﬁcantly
higher. mTOR (mammalian target of rapamycin protein) is one of the
important downstream targets of the AKT signaling pathway. Acti-
vated AKT further phosphorylates mTOR, which participates in bio-
logical events such as neuronal development and differentiation,
axonal regeneration, and synaptic plasticity by regulating overall or
local protein synthesis. While studying the phosphorylation level of
mTOR, we further investigated activation of its downstream signaling
molecule, ribosomal protein kinase S6K. Western blot results showed
that compared to the SFMA, L@SFMA, and N-SFMA groups, the
pmTOR/mTOR ratio (Fig. 5L) and pS6K/S6K ratio (Fig. 5M) were sig-
niﬁcantly upregulated in the NL@SFMA group. These results indicated
that NL@SFMA hydrogel can activate AKT/mTOR signaling pathway to
promote axon regeneration. These results indicate that NL@SFMA
hydrogel with diffusive N-cadherin can provide cell-cell adhesion cues
to facilitate cell vitality and promote THBS-1 mediated neural com-
munication and neuronal growth by activating TGF-β/Smad and AKT/
mTOR pathway.
NL@SFMA hydrogel exhibits favorable biocompatibility
Prior to in vivo application, the cytocompatibility and hemocompat-

PC12 cells were seeded on hydrogels, respectively, and the cell viability

exhibited normal proliferation and good cell viability during 3 days of
incubation. Meanwhile, all of these hydrogels displayed a hemolysis
ratio of less than 1% (Supplementary Fig. 6A– C). These results indicate
that these hydrogels held g ood blood compatibility and
cytocompatibility.

Methods
Preparation of vesicle solutions
Solutions of 25 mg/mL DOPC (1,2-dioleoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocho-
line, Avanti, AL, USA), 25 mg/mL DPTE (1,2-dipalmitoyl-sn-glycero-3-
phosphothioethanol sodium salt, Avanti, AL, USA), and 0.01 mg/mL
DGS-nta (1,2-dioleoyl-sn-glycero-3-[(N-(5-amino-1-carboxypentyl)imi-
nodiacetic acid)succinyl] nickel salt, Avanti, AL, USA) were prepared in
chloroform. The solutions were combined in varying quantities to
achieve DPTE: (DOPC + DPTE) at molar ratios of 0%, 5%, 50%, or 100%.
The lipid mixtures were dried under nitrogen gas, and excess
chloroform was removed by vacuum drying. The lipid mixtures were
then rehydrated in 1 mL of rehydration buffer (1 mM sucrose-sodium
bicarbonate solution) to a concentration of 1 mg/mL. The mixture was
allowed to swell at 75 °C for 2 h and subsequently extruded through a
50 nm polycarbonate membrane (Whatman® Nucleopore Track-
etched membrane, Avanti Polar Lipids, AL, USA).
Preparation of hydrogels
Raw silk ﬁber was obtained from Xianjida Cocoon Silk Products Co.,
Ltd. (Nantong, China). Following previously established procedures,53
the silk ﬁber was degummed in a sodium carbonate (Na2CO3) solution
(0.5% w/w) (Macklin, China) for 30 min, repeated three times to
effectively remove sericin proteins. A total of 10.0 g of degummed silk
was dissolved in 50 mL of lithium bromide solution (9.3 M, Meryer,
China) at 60 °C and stirred for 30 min. Subsequently, 2.0 mL of
methacrylic anhydride was gradually added to the mixture, which was
stirred for an additional 3 h and then dialyzed against distilled water
for 3 days at 4 °C to yield a methacrylated silkﬁbroin (SFMA) solution.
SFMA hydrogel: The SFMA solution was concentrated in air at 4 °C
to a concentration of 10% w/w, and then mixed with 0.5% w/w photo-
initiator (2-hydroxy-1-(4-(hydroxyethoxy)phenyl)-2-methyl-1-propa-

Scanning electron microscope observation
A ﬁeld emission scanning electron microscope (SEM, Hitachi S-3400N
II, Tokyo, Japan) was employed to observe the internal architecture of
the hydrogels. A representative freeze-dried sample from each group
(SFMA and L@SFMA) was selected for imaging. After gold sputtering
at a working voltage of 30 kV, images were captured. The pore dia-
meter was calculated using ImageJ software (National Institutes of
Health, Bethesda, MD, USA).
Rheological properties
The photocuring times of the hydrogels were assessed by rheologically
determining the sol-gel transition, also referred to as the gel point,
which is de ﬁned as the moment when the storage modulus (G ’)
exceeds the loss modulus (G”). Time sweeps were conducted at 20 °C
using a Haake MARS rotational rheometer (Thermo Fisher Scientiﬁc)
with P20 parallel plates and a 1.0 mm gap (CD mode), at a strain of
150%, a frequency of 1 Hz, and under UV irradiation (365 nm, 180 mW/
cm²). Theﬁnal moduli of the hydrogels were also determined based on
the results of the rheological tests.
Photo-induced formation of lipid bilayer within the hydrogel
To demonstrate the rapid photo-reaction of DPTE with silk ﬁbroin
chain and the self-assembling of the small vesicles forming lipid bilayer
on hydrogel, contact angles of single-distilled water and a lipid solu-
tion droplet on SFMA hydrogel with/without UV irradiation were
recorded using a JY-PHA instrument (Chengde Jinhe Instrument Man-
ufacturing Co. Ltd., Chengde, China). The contact angle for each
sample was determined as the average value from three independent
experiments.

A total of 80 Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats (females, aged 8 – 9w e e k s ,

Traumatic brain injury (TBI) model
Traumatic brain injury (TBI) rat model was established using a free-fall
striking device (ZH-ZYQ, Zhenghua Biological Instrument Equipment
Co., Ltd, China) based on a modi ﬁed version of the Feeney free-fall
method
38.B r i eﬂy, the rats were immobilized on a brain stereotaxic
apparatus, and the cranial bone was exposed after After anesthetized

dow with a diameter of 5.00 mm was created at the center of the right
parietal bone (2 mm lateral to the sagittal suture and 3 mm posterior to
the coronal suture) to expose the complete dura mater. The impact
parameters were set as follows: a depth of 2 mm, a weight of 40 g, and a
height of 20 cm. Following the impact, the rats were randomly divided

the scalp was sutured and disinfected with iodophor. All procedures
were conducted on a heated pad to maintain the rats ’ normal body
temperature. The rats were then housed in separate cages for feeding
over a period of four weeks.
Modiﬁed neurological severity score (mNSS)
The motor function of SD rats was assessed on days 1, 3, 7, 14, 21, and
28 following treatment using the modiﬁed Neurological Severity Score
(mNSS). Basic neurocognitive functions, including motor, sensory, and
reﬂex capabilities, were quanti ﬁed on a scale from 0 to 18 points,
where a higher score indicates more severe brain damage.

same duration. If a rat fell off the rod during training, it was promptly
