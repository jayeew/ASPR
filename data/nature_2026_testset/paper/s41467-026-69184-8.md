Nav1.7 is a voltage-gated sodium channel (VGSC) subtype predominantly
expressed in sensory neurons, amplifying threshold currents. Here, we identify
that Uvarigranol D (UGD) suppresses human (h) Na
v1.7 with a much greater
maximal inhibition than other VGSC subtypes, despite having similar apparent
afﬁnities. We demonstrate that Thr1398 determines the greater inhibitory efﬁ-
cacy of UGD, the leftward shift in voltage-dependence and faster inactivation
kinetics of hNav1.7. UGD binds to the inactivated state, with Gln360,I l e394,L y s1395,
Phe1737,a n dT y r1744 being critically involved. Moreover, while UGD suppresses
action potentials in both rat dorsal root ganglion neurons and human induced
pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes, its ~60-fold greater sensitivity
in neurons demonstrates that differences in maximal inhibition can translate
into functional selectivity across excitable cells. We conclude that Thr1398 is
critical to the unique function of hNav1.7 as a threshold current generator, and
the lower voltage-dependence can be exploited for developing selective Nav1.7
inhibitors.
Voltage-gated sodium channels (VGSCs) are responsible for the
initiation and propagation of action potentials (APs), which regulate a
plethora of physiological processes1,2. Based on the coding sequences
of the α-subunits, nine VGSC subtypes (Na v1.1-Nav1.9) are found in
mammalian species, with some degree of tissue-speciﬁc expression for
each subtype, e.g., Nav1.2 in the central nervous system (CNS), Nav1.4 in
skeletal muscle, and Na v1.5 in cardiac muscle 3.R e m a r k a b l y ,N av1.7 is
preferentially expressed in peripheral nociceptors, a subset of dorsal
root ganglion (DRG) and trigeminal ganglion (TG) neurons that sense
noxious stimuli. Na
v1.7 is known to contribute to “threshold currents”
and amplify small depolarizations, thereby controlling action potential
(AP) ﬁring in nociceptors4,5. The contribution of Nav1.7 to the threshold
current likely stems from its more hyperpolarized voltage-dependence
on activation and subsequent inactivation than other VGSC
subtypes
6– 9. However, the molecular determinants for the hyperpo-
larizing shifts of Na v1.7’s voltage dependence remain entirely
unknown.

various pain conditions10,11. Gain-of-function mutations in the SCN9A
gene, which encodes Na v1.7, cause primary erythromelalgia and par-
oxysmal extreme pain disorder11,12, while loss-of-function mutations of
SCN9A result in congenital insensitivity to painful stimuli 13,14,a

vated states than hNav1.2. If UGD selectively bound to the inactivated
states of VGSCs, then it would be more ef ﬁcacious at modulating
hNav1.7 than hNa v1.2. To test this hypothesis, we examined the state
dependence of UGD on hNa v1.7 activity. First, cells were held at

inactivation33. Under these conditions, UGD did not alter hNa v1.7
activity even with repeated stimulations (Fig.5a), suggesting that UGD
does not bind to the resting/closed and open states of the hNa v1.7
channel. Second, voltage dependence was assessed in the absence and
presence of UGD (10 µM). While UGD minimally affected voltage-
dependent activation of hNa
v1.7, it shifted voltage-dependent inacti-
vation towards more hyperpolarized potentials by ~ −11.7 mV, from
−68.3 to −80.0 mV (Fig. 5b), suggesting that UGD binds to the inacti-
vated state of hNav1.7. To further conﬁrm this point and illustrate the
consequence of this selective binding, the kinetics of recovery from

Discussion
In the current study, using a pore-blocking mechanism-biased rapid
throughput potentiometric assay, we discovered a class of poly-
oxygenated cyclohexene analogs that inhibited hNa
v1.7. UGD, the
most potent compound, although displaying comparable apparent
afﬁnities at all VGSC subtypes tested, exhibited a much greater max-
imal inhibition on hNa
v1.7 than on other VGSC subtypes, thus exerting
selectivity to hNav1.7 by efﬁcacy. While UGD completely inhibited the
currents of heterologously expressed hNav1.7, the maximal inhibition
of UGD topped at ~50% for other heterologously expressed TTX-
sensitive human VGSC subtypes, as well as the native TTX-R VGSCs
(Na
v1.8 and Na v1.9) in acutely isolated rat DRG neurons 36. Thus, UGD
represents a unique Na v1.7 selective antagonist with its selectivity
based on the inhibitory ef ﬁcacy, which differs from the current
Nav1.7 selective inhibitors that have all been developed based on afﬁ-
nity differences37,38.
We further demonstrate that a single amino acid residue, Thr1398,
in the P-loop between S5 and S6 of Domain III is critical for the stronger
inhibitory effect of UGD on hNa v1.7. Since UGD exhibited similar
apparent afﬁnities across all VGSCs as well as the T1398M mutant of
hNav1.7, Thr1398 is most likely not directly involved in UGD binding.
Thr1398 is unique in that it is only found in hNav1.7, while all other human
VGSC subtypes have a conserved Met at the equivalent position 39.
Importantly, we show that Thr 1398 is a critical determinant for the
hyperpolarizing shifts of voltage-dependent activation and inactiva-
tion of hNa
v1.7. It has long been known that Nav1.7 displays left-shifted
voltage-dependent inactivation curves compared to other VGSC sub-
types, and Na
v1.7 is activated at more hyperpolarized voltage than
Nav1.86,7. It is remarkable that in human VGSCs such shifts almost
exclusively result from having a smaller Thr instead of the bulkier Met
in the boundary between the selectivityﬁlter and the second pore helix
of Domain III. This area deﬁnes the narrowest part of the ion conduc-
tion pathway in the upper gate. The signiﬁcance of Thr1398 here is not
only demonstrated by the reciprocal mutations, in which the swap of
Thr and Met between hNa
v1.7 and hNa v1.2 almost completely
phenocopied the voltage-dependence of the counterparts, but also the
presence of Thr at this position only in hNa v1.7, the channel that
exhibits the hyperpolarized vol tage dependence among VGSCs.
Notably, Thr1398 is only present in species above Saimiri boliviensis.I n
non-primate species, including rats, Nav1.7 still contains a methionine
at this position. Yet, rNa v1.7 is also completely inhibited by UGD and
exhibits left-shifted voltage-dependent activation and inactivation.
Which amino acid(s) is responsible for these properties of rNa
v1.7
remains a question that warrants future investigation.
The smaller size of Thr than Met creates a broader bottleneck
along the ion conduction pathway in the selectivity ﬁlter region, as
shown by the larger pore radius of hNav1.7 than Nav1.7T1398M predicted
using HOLE and Pymol programs. This should reduce the energy bar-
rier for the transitions of the channel between different states. Indeed,
hNa
v1.7 exhibits faster activation, faster inactivation, and quicker
recovery from fast inactivated state than hNav1.2, and these differences
were swapped upon exchanging Thr and Met between these two VGSC
subtypes. The faster kinetics of channels harboring Thr at position
1398 than those having Met are in line with the lower energy barrier for
state transitions due to the presence of Thr at this critical location. As a
result, the coupling between VSD movement and conformational
transition in the pore domain is more ef ﬁcient in channels harboring
Thr, which can translate into increased open probabilities at more
hyperpolarized membrane voltages compared to VGSCs that contain
Met at this position. Supporting this view, mutations in the selectivity
ﬁlter region of the VGSC have been previously demonstrated to affect
not only selectivity but also gating properties of the channel
32.
Nav1.7 has long been recognized to produce“threshold currents”
in DRG and TG neurons, likely owing to its more hyperpolarized vol-
tage dependence on activation. By reducing the energy barrier for
activation, Thr
1398 serves as the main determinant for the low-voltage
dependence of hNav1.7, underscoring the molecular mechanism of its
contribution to the “threshold currents”. The hyperpolarizing shift
renders Na v1.7 more likely to open in response to weak or small
depolarizations. On the other hand, due to its hyperpolarized voltage-
dependence in inactivation, most Nav1.7 channels are in the inactivated
state under physiological conditions (~ −60 mV resting potential 40),
limiting the number of available channels (those at resting/closed
state) that can contribute to the upstroke of APs. Consequently,
compared to channels that have relatively depolarized activation vol-
tages, the contribution of Na
v1.7 to the AP upstroke phase is relatively
small. Our data demonstrating that Thr1398 is critical for the hyperpo-
larizing voltage-dependent activation and inactivation of hNa v1.7,
offering structural insights into the unique function of Na v1.7 as a
threshold current generator and a minor component in the upstroke of
APs of nociceptors.
