Time reﬂection and refraction are among the most canonical phenomena
emerging from the burgeoningﬁeld of time-varying materials. However, their
connection with topologyremains largely unexplored. Here, we experimen-
tally report that temporal scattering processes exhibit two distinct topological
characteristics: eigenstate topological braiding and dynamical topological
phase transition. By developing approaches to implement Schrödinger
dynamics and engineer temporal boundaries, various temporal scattering
processes are implemented in circuit metamaterials. Through measurements
of scattering coefﬁcients, we observe diverse topological braiding and linking
structures, whose linking numbers are governed by the difference in topolo-
gical winding numbers before and after the temporal boundary. Furthermore,
we reveal that these processes exhibit dynamical critical behavior and host
dynamical topological phase transitions, evidenced by quantized jumps at
critical time points in the measured dynamical topological invariants. Our
study demonstrates fundamental links between temporal scattering and
braiding and dynamical topology, opening avenues for exploring the topolo-
gical aspects of time-varying and nonequilibrium phases of matter.
Time-varying materials have recently emerged as a transformative
platform for wave control1– 5. An ultrafast and spatially uniform mod-
ulation of material properties creates temporal boundaries 6. Unlike
their counterparts under the space-time duality, namely spatially
modulated systems, temporal boundaries or interfaces cause wave
reﬂection and refraction in a fundamentally distinct manner by altering
energy while conserving momentum. This distinctive mechanism
unlocks wave-engineering capabilities, including broadband fre-
quency translation
7,8, nonreciprocal wave propagation 9 and reconﬁ-
gurable wavefront shaping10– 15.I nt h ep a s td e c a d e ,t i m er eﬂection and
refraction have been observed in a wide range of systems, including
water waves
16, photonics17– 20,a c o u s t i c s21, microwaves22,m e c h a n i c s23,
and quantum matter waves24.
Recently, it has been discovered that abruptly changing para-
meters in a topological lattice model can provide us a discrete lattice
platform to study both time reﬂection and time refraction24– 26.A l o n g
this line, theoretical study further revealed a connection between
topology and temporal scattering processes, predicting that the time
reﬂection and refraction coef ﬁcients exhibit topological braiding
structures that can be utilized to probe the differences in topological
invariants across a temporal boundary

braiding associated with eigenvalues in non-Hermitian systems 28– 40,
this braiding arises from eigenstates and their dynamics in time-
varying Hermitian systems. Despite these timely advances, the intrin-

In this work, we experimentally bridge this gap by exploring the
dynamical topological aspects of time reﬂection and refraction in time-
varying circuit metamaterials. A dual-circuit-lattice architecture is
implemented to encode and simulate the Schrödinger dynamics of
both the real and imaginary components of the wavefunction. Fur-
thermore, a technology is introduced to create and control temporal
boundaries for realizing temporal interfaces and manipulating tem-
poral scatterings. By measuring the time re ﬂection and refraction
coefﬁcients across various temporal scattering scenarios, we observe a
rich variety of topological braiding and linking structures, arising from
the intertwining of these coef ﬁcients throughout the Brillouin zone
(see Fig. 1a, b). Beyond this geometric-topological link, we reveal the
emergence of dynamical criticality and dynamical topological phase
transitions inside the time re ﬂection and refraction processes. Intri-
guingly, the dynamical topological phase transition cannot be descri-
bed by conventional static topological invariants, but rather by
dynamical topological invariants (see Fig. 1c), which are de ﬁned
through the time evolution of Bloch states. Experimentally, we observe
the dynamical criticality at critical time points through the nonanalytic
behavior in the dynamics of rate functions associated with the tem-
poral scattering coef ﬁcients. At precisely the critical time points,
quantized jumps are detected in the measured dynamical topological
invariants, providing direct evidence for the occurrence of dynamical
topological phase transitions. Together, these results offer fresh
insights into the dynamical topological aspect of temporal scattering
in time-varying metamaterials.

Discussion
In summary, we have experimentally reported two topological aspects
associated with the temporal scattering in the time-varying metama-
terials. We demonstrate that time reﬂection and refraction coefﬁcients
harbor intricate topological braiding and link structures, with their
linking numbers determined by the difference in topological winding
numbers across the temporal boundary. Moreover, we reveal that
temporal scattering processes exhibit dynamical critical behavior and
host dynamical topological phase transitions, manifested through
quantized jumps at critical time points in the dynamical topological
invariants.
Looking ahead, our experiment is expected to inspire further
research into both the topological aspects of time-varying metama-
terials and the out-of-equilibrium aspects of topological metamater-
ials. This direction is particularly important, as implementing time-
dependent control or observing Schrödinger dynamics remains highly
challenging in both solid-state topological systems and conventional
classical-wave platforms. These limitations signi ﬁcantly hinder the
exploration of time-varying topological phenomena and non-
equilibrium topological phases there. Such investigations could also
motivate novel paradigms that exte nd beyond conventional topolo-
gical protection in the spatial domain, enabling capabilities for con-
trolling wave dynamics in the time domain. Lastly, our developed

Methods
Sample fabrications and circuit temporal boundary
Our electric circuits, including the PCB composition, stack-up layout,
internal layer and grounding settings, are designed through PAD
program software (see Supplementary Note 5 for more details). To
implement the ideal INIC realized long-range SSH model coupling, we
employed the LT1363 operational ampliﬁer, which features high speed
and a high slew rate. The operational ampliﬁers were powered by ± 5 V
supply voltages. Two chip resistors R
a =1 0 kΩ were used as auxiliary
components in the positive and negative feedback loops of the
operational ampliﬁer.
To create a temporal boundary in the system, we employed
high-speed CMOS analog switches (model COS12A12511) to abruptly
change the system parameters. These switches, powered by ± 5 V
supplies, exhibit a typical switching time of less than 70 ns, so the
switching can be regarded as instantaneous compared to the sys-
tem’s millisecond-scale (~ ms) dynamics. The analog switches are
digitally triggered and can be programmed to activate at precise time
points, enabling controlled manipulation of the circuit ’s temporal
boundary. To initialize the circuit before each experimental run, the
grounding capacitance C
0 of each node was momentarily connected
to predeﬁned voltage sources via CMOS analog switches to set the
initial conditions. The voltage dynamics of all nodes are then mon-
itored using a Rigol digital oscilloscope with multi-channel input and
high temporal resolution. This setup enables time-resolved mea-
surements of the node voltages, thereby allowing us to extract
dynamical features.
