While extensive efforts have been devoted to enhancing electron transfer
efﬁciency through metal valence cycling in heterogeneous Fenton-like reac-
tions, the potential catalytic improvement induced by dynamic structural
stretching remain unexplored. Here, weintroduce a homointerpenetrated Fe-
based metal‒organic framework (BUC-95) featuring a dynamic stretchable
structure that signiﬁcantly boosts the heterogeneous Fenton-like catalytic
performance. BUC-95’s unique stretchable structure achieved effective per-
oxydisulfate activation for degradingvarious micropollutants via Fe(IV) = O
species, facilitated by a reduced energy barrier for Fe(IV) = O formation
through modulation of the electron density at Fe sites. DFT calculations sug-
gest that, compared with the isostructural analogue with hydrogen bond-
restricted stretching, theﬂexible dynamic stretching in BUC-95 overcomes the
inherent electron transfer limitations from Fe sites to peroxydisulfate,
enhancing the oﬂoxacin degradation performance. Practically, BUC-95
demonstrated effective continuous-ﬂow degradation and detoxiﬁcation of
micropollutants. This work establishes dynamic stretching as a crucial design
principle for advancing environmental remediation materials and
technologies.

catalytically active sites
10–13. Nevertheless, the inherent complexity of
interfacial reaction dynamics frequently induces transient structural
evolution in catalysts, a phenomenon often overlooked in conven-
tional studies but capable of exerting profound implications on the
overall catalytic oxidation mechanisms
14–16.
A few recent research efforts have focused on elucidating the
critical role of dynamic structural distortion in modulating the local
coordination environment of metal sites during catalytic processes.
For example, it was reported that the interaction between host and
guest molecules might induce the stretching vibrations of O‒Sr‒Oa n d
Co/Ti‒O bonds within the Co/La-SrTiO
3 catalyst to increase electron
transfer efﬁciency from PMS to metal sites 16. However, the current
understanding remains constrained by two fundamental limitations:
(1) the catalytic performance improvement achievable through
stretching vibrations of metal ‒oxygen bonds is inherently limited
because of the insuf ﬁcient structural adaptability of conventional
catalysts; and (2) the development of synthetic strategies for engi-
neering catalysts with programmable dynamic stretching archi-
tectures presents technical challenges, particularly in advanced
oxidation processes. More importantly, the relationship between the
structural dynamic stretching of catalysts and the resulting catalytic
activity remains poorly deﬁned.
Interpenetrated metal‒organic frameworks (MOFs) offer a pro-
mising paradigm for addressing these challenges. Characterized by
mutually entangled yet noncovalently bonded identical networks
17,18,
these architectures might exhibit intrinsic structuralﬂexibility derived
from the tunable spatial separation between constituent frameworks.
This unique structural feature enables reversible deformation of the
entire framework in response to external stimuli like chemical activa-
tion. Unlike the localized pore breathing in conventional MOFs or the
ligand-based ﬂexibility in ﬂexible MOFs (Supplementary Fig. 1), the
large-scale dynamic adaptability of interpenetrated MOFs makes them
ideal model systems for studying whole-framework dynamics in
Fenton-like reaction mechanisms.
In this work, a homointerpenetrated MOF (denoted BUC-95,
CCDC: 2352385) with a dynamically stretchable framework was syn-
thesized, which exhibited exceptional degradation ef ﬁciency for
diverse organic contaminants through a high-valent iron-oxo
(Fe(IV) = O)-dominated oxidation pathway. Experimental character-
izations and density functional theory (DFT) calculations revealed that
the presence of hydroxylated BUC-95 not only regulated thed-orbitals
electron density of Fe but also greatly reduced the activation energy
barrier, promoting the formation of Fe(IV) = O in the BUC-95-OH/PDS
system. Compared with the homointerpenetrated BUC-96 counterpart
with restricted dynamic stretching, theﬂexible dynamic stretchability
of the homointerpenetrated BUC-95 framework overcame the limita-
tion of weak electron transfer performance in PDS activation processes
to ultimately improve the catalytic o ﬂoxacin (OFC) degradation per-
formance. Furthermore, the construction of a ﬁxed-bed continuous-
ﬂow catalytic reactor and tests with multiple toxicity assessment sys-
tems conﬁrmed the stable long-term catalytic activity and exceptional
detoxiﬁcation capabilities of the BUC-95/PDS system. This work pro-
vides insight into the design and preparation of interpenetrated MOFs
with dynamic stretching features for highly efﬁcient water puriﬁcation
via Fenton-like reactions.

Characterization and structural analysis
BUC-95 was synthesized from FeSO 4·7H2O, 4,4′-bipyridine (bpy) and
1,4-benzenedicarboxylic acid (H2bdc) via a facile microwave-assisted
method (Supplementary Fig. 2). According to scanning electron
microscopy (SEM) (Supplementary Fig. 3) and high-resolution
transmission electron microscopy (HR-TEM) (Fig. 1b) analysis, BUC-
95 exhibited a rod-like morphology, with particles sized ~15.0 μm×
1.0 μm×0 . 3 μm. The small size of the particles rendered the conven-
tional single-crystal X-ray technique unsuitable, so microcrystalline
electron diffraction (microED) was employed to acquire single-crystal
data
19,20. The crystal structure was determined ab initio from the
microED dataset using the SHELX software package21 (Supplementary
Tables 1 and 2). The 3D reconstructed reciprocal lattice (Fig. 1a)
obtained via microED revealed that BUC-95 (molecular formula
Fe
4(bpy)4(bdc)4) crystallizes in a triclinic unit cell witha = 11.490 (2) Å,
b = 1 6 . 4 6 0( 3 )Åa n dc = 21.183 (4) Å and belongs to space group P/C221.
BUC-95 features a homointerpenetrated structure consisting of a pair
of identical 3D frameworks. The framework contains paddle-wheel-
type binuclear Fe units, where each Fe atom is coordinated by four
oxygen atoms from COO
– groups of three deprotonated bdc2− ligands
and two nitrogen atoms from two bpy ligands, forming a 3D frame-
work (Fig. 1c and Supplementary Fig. 4).
The experimental powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) patterns
showed strong agreement with the simulated pro ﬁles derived from
crystallographic data determined via microED, thereby conﬁrming the
phase purity of the as-synthesized BUC-95 (Fig. 1e). This ﬁnding was

Fig. 7)

Mechanistic insights into Fe(IV)= O formation

spectroscopy and ﬁrst-principles modeling were employed. HVIO
species can oxidize methyl phenyl sulfoxide (PMSO) into methyl
phenyl sulfone (PMSO2)29,30. PMSO oxidation tests revealed the pro-

individual PDS system), with akobs of 0.31 min−1 (103-fold increase over
that of the individual PDS system ) using the BUC-95/PDS system

BUC-95 revealed a characteristic quadrupole splitting shift of
−0.17 mm s

predominant precursor for HVIO formation, with OFC degradation

revealed a 15.78% decrease in the Fe(II) content after the reaction
(Supplementary Fig. 37), which is generally consistent with the HVIO

Discussion
BUC-95, a homointerpenetrated MOF with a dynamic stretching
structure, was synthesized and applied for the ef ﬁcient degradation
of various organic pollutants through PDS activation. The presence
of dynamic stretching in BUC-95 was con ﬁrmed through multiple
characterizations, like in situ PXRD, Raman, FTIR and XPS, which
together indicated a 184.0-fold increase in the OFC degradation rate
constant compared with that of the homointerpenetrated BUC-96
with ﬁxed framework. Fe(IV) = O species were identiﬁed as the major
oxidative species in the OFC degradation process. Their formation
mechanism was investigated via experiments and DFT calculations.
The dynamic dual Fe-Fe’ sites in BUC-95 adsorbed -OH, regulated the
electron density of Fe 3 d orbitals and greatly reduced the energy
barrier, facilitating Fe(IV) = O species formation. Unlike BUC-96,
whose framework stretching is restricted by hydrogen bonding, the
ﬂexible dynamic stretching in BUC-95 facilitates electron transfer
from Fe-Fe ’ sites to PDS, thereby signi ﬁcantly enhancing the ef ﬁ-
ciency of OFC degradation. In addition, BUC-95 achieved excellent
continuous-ﬂow degradation efﬁciency ( > 99.99%, up to 110.0 h) and
superior detoxi ﬁcation performance. These outcomes, validated
through integrated software simulations and plant (mung bean) and
bacterial tests, underscore the substantial practical potential of the
BUC-95/PDS system. This work provides insights for the rational
design and development of MOF catalysts with dynamic stretchable
structures for water puri ﬁcation via Fenton-like reactions, offering
transformative potential for sustainable water puri ﬁcation
technologies.

analyze the residual concentrations of different pollutants are pro-
