Breast cancer diagnosis using magnetic resonance imaging remains limited by
high false-positive rates and substantial inter-reader variability, especially for
lesions classiﬁed as Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System (BI-RADS)
category 4, often leading to unnecessary biopsies. Here we show that the BI-
RADS 4 Lesions Analysis System (BL4AS), an artiﬁcial intelligence system
powered by foundation models and leveraging the rich spatiotemporal
information of dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI, addresses these diagnostic
challenges. Developed on a multicenter dataset of 2,803 lesions from 2,686
female patients, BL4AS demonstratesrobust performance with areas under
the curve of 0.892-0.930 and signiﬁcantly outperforms radiologists in speci-
ﬁcity (0.889 versus 0.491). BL4AS-assisted interpretation signiﬁcantly
improves diagnostic accuracy for both senior and junior radiologists, reducing
inter-reader variability by 24.5% and decreasing false-positive rates by 27.3%.
BL4AS further stratiﬁes lesions into subcategories (4 A, 4B and 4 C) for reﬁned
risk assessment, offering a practical tool for precision breast cancer
management.

The interpretation of breast MRI is highly subjective, with sig-

reported that the positive predictive value (PPV) of MRI-guided biop-

innovative solutions to improve diagnostic accuracy, consistency, and

Recent advances in deep learning have shown promise in breast
cancer detection across imaging modalities20–23. However, existing AI
systems face critical limitations, including lack of robust external
validation, limited prospective evaluation, and insufﬁcient interpret-
ability, which hinder clinical adoption
24–28. To address these gaps, we
developed the BI-RADS 4 Lesion Analysis System (BL4AS), a foundation
model-powered AI system

information of dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI (DCE-MRI). Unlike
previous studies that rely on single images or maximum intensity
projection images

inﬂow, and outﬂow phases) to capture comprehensive lesion dynam-
ics, enabling more accurate characterization.
In this work, we present the BL4AS, a foundation model-powered
artiﬁcial intelligence system that leverages spatiotemporal informa-
tion from multiphase DCE-MRI to enhance diagnostic accuracy and
reduce unnecessary biopsies for BI-RADS category 4 lesions. Rigor-
ously validated on multicenter external and prospective test sets,
BL4AS demonstrates robust generalizability and outperforms radi-
ologists, particularly in speciﬁcity and negative predictive value (NPV).
A reader study involving both senior and junior radiologists demon-
strates BL4AS’s ability to support clinical decision-making. Addition-
ally, interpretable visualizations of model focus areas foster clinician
trust and facilitate adoption. By improving diagnostic consistency and
efﬁciency, BL4AS has the potential to signi ﬁcantly enhance patient
outcomes and optimize resource utilization in breast cancer care.

Models’ performance on retrospective data
BL4AS demonstrated robust performance in lesion segmentation,
achieving Dice similarity coefﬁcients of 0.851, 0.834, 0.825 and 0.837
in the training, validation and external test sets, respectively (Supple-
mentary Table 2). For lesion classi ﬁcation, we conducted three abla-
tion experiments: (1) Pretraining Impact: Vision Transformer (ViT)
models with the pre-trained foundation models outperformed non-
pretrained models across all metrics (Fig. 2a); (2) Multiphase Fusion:
ViT models integrating multiphase DCE images (C
peak,C sub1,C sub2)
performed signiﬁcantly better than single-phase models (Fig. 2b–d);
(3) Feature Integration: Among various feature integration methods,
our image-based fusion approach (ImageConv) performed better than
other methods in most scenarios (Fig. 2e–g).
To establish the superiority of our approach, we compared BL4AS
against multiple baseline methods adapted for breast lesion diagnosis.
These included traditional machine learning algorithms utilizing
handcrafted radiomics features, as well as state-of-the-art deep learn-
ing architecture. Our results demonstrated that BL4AS outperformed
other methods across the majority of evaluation metrics (Supple-
mentary Data 1), with particularly notable improvements in speciﬁcity
for BI-RADS 4 lesions. Ultimately, BL4AS achieved AUC values of 0.896
(95% Con ﬁdence Interval [CI]: 0.832 –0.949) and 0.930 (95% CI:
0.906–0.950) in external test sets A and B, respectively (Fig. 3a,
Table 2).
To evaluate model robustness and generalizability across diverse
clinical scenarios, we performed subgroup analyses based on patient
demographics, lesion characteristics, and imaging protocols. The
ﬁndings indicated that BL4AS exhibited satisfactory predictive per-
formance across combined validation and external test sets when

Interpretation and visualization
To enhance clinical credibility, BL4AS incorporates heatmaps high-
lighting regions critical to its predictions (Fig.5, Supplementary Fig. 4).
These visualizations aided readers in reﬁning diagnoses by identifying
key imaging features, such as irregular margins and abnormal
enhancement patterns. However, some misclassi ﬁcations occurred,
particularly for lesions with peripheral enhancement or overlapping
benign-malignant features (Supplementary Fig. 4). Our detailed ana-
lysis of false negative cases revealed that these misclassiﬁcations pre-
dominantly involved small lesions, circumscribed margins, and BI-
RADS 4a subcategory (Supplementary Table 3). Pathologically, BL4AS
misclassiﬁed ductal carcinoma in situ and mucinous carcinoma cases
more frequently, likely due to their less invasive imaging character-
istics that closely mimic benign lesions. Notably, incorporating addi-
tional sequences particularly DWI showed potential to assist in better
characterizing these morphologically ambiguous cases (Supplemen-
tary Figs. 5–8).
Clinical management strategies
BL4AS demonstrated potential to optimize biopsy decision-making.
Alternative hypothetical operating points could be selected based on
speciﬁcc l i n i c a ls i t u a t i o n s( F i g .6a). At a high NPV operating point,
BL4AS prevented 123 unnecessary biopsies per 1000 patients while
maintaining 100% sensitivity in external test set B. Conversely, at a
high-PPV operating point, BL4AS ensured prompt intervention for
high-risk lesions.
Furthermore, BL4AS could stratify BI-RADS 4 lesions into sub-
categories (4A, 4B and 4 C) with high accuracy (Fig.6b). In external test
set B, malignancy rates were 2.2%, 35.6%, and 86.2% for 4 A, 4B, and 4 C,
respectively. Similarly, external test set A showed corresponding rates
of 9.8%, 44.1%, and 84.8%. BL4AS identi ﬁed more accurately low-risk
lesions (4A malignancy probability: 2.2% vs. 9.2%) and reduced false-
negative rates (6.1% vs. 30.0%) for high-risk categories (4B/4 C) com-
pared to radiologists (Fig. 6c and d). This improved risk stratiﬁcation
was reﬂected in a signiﬁcant Net Reclassiﬁcation Improvement (NRI) of
17.45% (95% CI: 9.15 –25.75%, P < 0.001), highlighting the potential of
BL4AS to individualize follow-up strategies and optimize resource
allocation.
Discussion
In this study, we developed and va lidated BL4AS, an innovative AI-
driven system for segmentation and classiﬁcation of BI-RADS 4 breast
lesions using multiphase DCE-MRI. BL4AS leverages a foundation
model pretrained on 17,149 volumes with 2.5 million DCE-MRI slices,
demonstrating robust performance across multicenter external test
sets and a prospective test set. This system addresses critical chal-
lenges in breast MRI interpretation, including high false-positive rates
and inter-reader variability, while providing interpretable AI visuali-
zation to enhance clinician trust. By subdividing BI-RADS 4 lesions
according to the Sixth-edition ACR BIRADS guidelines, BL4AS offers
actionable insights for personalized clinical management.
The evaluation of BI-RADS 4 lesions remains challenging due to
high false-positive rates and signiﬁcant variability in inter- and intra-
reader reproducibility
18,35–38. Both clinicians and engineers have made
efforts to breast MRI examinations 39–42. Previous AI-based studies,
including works by Daimiel Naranjo et al. 25.a n dS u ne ta l . 24, have
explored CNN-based classiﬁers and radiomics approaches for breast
MRI diagnosis, but were limited by small sample sizes (<600 lesions),
retrospective designs, and inclusion of all BI-RADS categories rather
than focusing on the most challenging BI-RADS 4 subgroup. Other
strategies, such as using an ADC cut-off of ≥1.5 × 10
−3 mm2/s to down-
grade lesions classiﬁed as BI-RADS 4, showed poor diagnostic perfor-
mance, with an AUC of 78.4% and speci ﬁcity of 32.6% 40.C u ie ta l .41
proposed a radiomics model based on 92 patients to classify BI-RADS 4
lesions and achieved an AUC of 92%, but was not externally validated.
In contrast, our study comprises the largest sample size to date with
2803 BI-RADS 4 lesions, including 774 from external multicenter test
sets and 92 from a prospective test set. Unlike other studies that pre-
dominantly employ radiomics or CNN classiﬁers, our research levera-
ges a foundation model approach. This approach beneﬁts from large-
scale pre-training and enhanced feature representation capabilities,
which enables it to better capture complex imaging patterns and
improve generalizability across different imaging protocols and insti-
tutions. Our comparative experimental analysis demonstrates that the
foundation model-based approach achieves superior and more stable
performance compared to traditional CNN and radiomics methods
across different data sets. BL4AS achieved AUCs of 0.896, 0.930 and
0.892 in external test set A, external test set B and prospective test set,
respectively, demonstrating superior generalizability and diagnostic
performance.
A key innovation of BL4AS lies in its ability to capture the temporal
dynamics of lesion enhancement patterns by concatenating multi-
phase DCE-MRI at the channel level using a pre-trained foundation
model. This approach outperforms both single-phase models and
feature-based fusion methods, highlighting the value of comprehen-
sive temporal information integration in improving diagnostic accu-
racy. Early enhancement rates and time-intensity curve patterns
derived from multiphase DCE-MRI are critical imaging features for

taining comparable sensitivity to senior readers. The interpretation of
breast MRI is often subject to signi ﬁcant interobserver variability
46,
particularly in resource-limited settings. BL4AS could address this
critical need by providing consistent second opinion that could bridge
expertise gaps and offer consistent risk strati ﬁcation regardless of
radiologist experience. This is evidenced by the substantial improve-
ment in junior readers’ performance, whose accuracy improved from
0.745 to 0.870, sensitivity from 0.900 to 0.915, and speci ﬁcity from
0.370 to 0.759. This improvement was maintained even in challenging
cases, such as breasts with moderate or marked BPE and non-mass
enhancement lesions, where interpretation is typically more difﬁcult.
Furthermore, BL4AS signiﬁcantly increased inter-observer agreement,
with the kappa coefﬁcient rising from 0.501 to 0.746. Such improve-
ment suggests that BL4AS has the potential to serve as a valuable
decision support tool, particularly in environments where specialized
breast imaging expertise may be limited, potentially offering more
standardized breast MRI interpretation and enhance diagnostic relia-
bility. However, further validation through larger multicenter studies
across diverse clinical settings would be necessary to fully establish its
effectiveness and generalizability.
BL4AS-assisted clinical management strategies could reduce
unnecessary invasive biopsy. According to the ACR BIRADS guide-
line, the lesions scoring ≤3 points do not require biopsy
10. Based on
our strategy, some of the BI-RADS 4 lesions could also be selected to
forgo surgery initially in favor of close monitoring without compro-
mising NPV. Furthermore, this strategy allows us to identify high-PPV
lesions requiring prompt intervention. This dual operating point
strategy enables BL4AS to optimize the trade-off between reducing
unnecessary biopsies and ensuring timely intervention for high-risk
lesions.
The updated ACR BIRADS guideline (6th edition) introduced
subdivisions (4A-C) for MRI-detected lesions. Previously, these sub-
divisions were only necessary for ﬁndings from mammograms and
ultrasounds. This change has put more demand on radiologists ’ pro-
fessional expertise in addition to greatly increasing their workload.
Therefore, we further analyzed the ability of BL4AS to subdivide BI-
RADS 4 lesions. BL4AS showed robust performance in stratifying these
lesions, with malignancy probabilities closely matching expected risk
ranges across both external test sets. Remarkably, it reduced the false
negative rate by 23.9% compared to radiologists when classifying 4B
and 4 C categories as malignant, highlighting its superior sensitivity in
detecting malignant lesions and mitigating missed diagnoses. BL4AS’s
precise risk stratiﬁcation aligns closely with the expected risk ranges,
offering a valuable tool for clinicians to navigate the complexities of BI-
RADS 4 lesion management.

Model development and training
BL4AS is a deep learning system speciﬁcally designed to perform two
primary tasks: lesion segmentation and lesion classiﬁcation (benign vs.
malignant). Foundation models are large-scale, pre-trained neural
networks trained on diverse datasets that can be adapted for various
downstream tasks through ﬁne-tuning. To enhance generalizability
and performance, both tasks utilized a pretrained foundation model as
their architectural foundation. The backbone of BL4AS is a Vision
Transformer (ViT)
53 architecture initialized with weights from a foun-
dation model pretrained on 17,149 volumes (2.5 million DCE-MRI sli-
ces) using the Momentum Contrast (MoCo)-V3 self-supervised
learning algorithm
54,55. This pretraining enables the model to capture
robust feature representations from DCE-MRI data, improving per-
formance on downstream tasks.
For lesion segmentation task, we implemented a UNETR model
architecture that utilizes the pretrained foundation model backbone.
The model accepts input volumes with dimensions of 1 × 48 × 48 × 48
and generates corresponding output volumes of 2 × 48 × 48 × 48,
where the two channels represent background and tumor regions
respectively (Supplementary Fig. 10). Given that the original medical
images typically exceed the 48 × 48 × 48 input size constraint of the
model, we employed a sliding window approach for segmentation.
This technique enables processing of larger volumetric data by divid-
ing them into overlapping patches of the required input size, per-
forming predictions on each patch, and subsequently aggregating the
results to reconstruct the complete segmentation map of the entire
volume. Additional implementation details are provided in the Sup-
plementary Methods. The predicted tumor regions were cropped
using bounding boxes. To ensure segmentation accuracy across all
datasets, an experienced radiologist reviewed and manually corrected
all model-generated segmentations (both training and test sets) when
necessary before cropping the volumes of interest. The correct
volumes of interest were subsequently used for further classiﬁcation
analysis.
For lesion classiﬁcation task, the foundation model is extended
with a multi-temporal fusion module and a patch-based discrimination
module (Supplementary Fig. 11). The multi-temporal fusion module
captures lesion enhancement patterns by integrating three phases of
DCE-MRI: C
peak (images with peak contrast enhancement), Csub1 (sub-
traction images between C peak and pre-contrast phase images), and
Csub2 (subtraction images between C peak and delayed post-contrast
images). These temporal phases are integrated through 3D convolu-
tional blocks to generate spatiotemporal representations. The patch-
based discrimination module enhances the ViT architecture by gen-
erating lesion-speciﬁc tokens, including a classi ﬁcation token that
captures global contextual information and a series of patch tokens
that preserve local spatial details for ﬁne-grained discrimination.
Lesion masks were used to differentiate between lesion and back-
ground regions, reducing background interference and improving
focus on lesion features.
The model is trained using the AdamW optimizer with a learning
rate of 1e-4, and the loss function incorporated Dice loss for segmen-
tation and cross-entropy loss for classi ﬁcation. All experiments were
conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU, with detailed hyper-
parameters and implementation provided in the Supplement
Methods.
Comparative analysis with existing methods
While previous studies have proposed various AI models for breast
lesion classi ﬁcation, none speci ﬁcally address BI-RADS 4 lesion
malignancy prediction. To provide comprehensive performance
benchmarks, we implemented multiple baseline approaches, including
traditional machine learning with handcrafted radiomics features and
state-of-the-art deep learning architectures. All models were trained
and evaluated using identical datasets, preprocessing pipelines, and
protocols for fair comparison. Detailed training parameters, hyper-
parameter settings, and implementation details for all comparison
methods are provided at GitHub ( https://github.com/
zhenweishi/BL4AS).
Subgroup analyses
To evaluate the robustness of our ﬁndings across different clinical
scenarios and technical parameters, we conducted subgroup analyses
in the combined validation and external test sets, including age ( ≤50
years and >50 years), lesion size ( ≤2 cm and >2 cm), lesion enhance-
ment patterns (mass and non-mass), BPE levels (minimal or mild and
moderate or marked), magnetic ﬁeld strength (1.5 T and 3 T) and
scanner types (GE, Philips and SIEMENS).
Reader study
Eight readers independently reviewed the images in the prospective
test sets, including four senior readers (R1-R4: 5 –15 years of experi-
ence) and four junior readers (R5-R8: 2–4 years of experience) in breast
imaging interpretation. All readers were blinded to each other, to the
original interpretation and to the pathological results. When readers
made the judgments of benign and malignant classiﬁcations, reading
time was also recorded. The performance of BL4AS was compared to
visual assessment by readers using the maximum Youden index of the
training set as the cutoff value.
AI-assisted reader study
To assess the utility of BL4AS in aiding radiologists ’ clinical decision-
making, an AI-assisted reader study with a fully crossed design was
conducted. The same 92 breast lesions in the prospective test set were
presented again to the same eight readers 4 weeks after the end of the
ﬁrst assessment. For each lesion, together with the original MRI ima-
ges, the corresponding AI prediction on malignancy risk probability
were provided to the readers at the same time. Besides, all readers
were blinded to the AI model ’s performance in order to reduce
expectation bias and ensure an unbiased assessment. Additionally,
Cohen’s kappa value was used to calculate the agreement between all
pairs of readers with and without BL4AS assistance. Additionally, we
analyzed the performance of BL4AS and readers across various sub-
groups, including magnetic ﬁeld strength, MRI scanner type, age,
lesion size, the degree of BPE and lesion enhancement pattern.
BL4AS interpretation and visualization
To improve model interpretability, the Grad-CAM approach was
employed to investigate and visualize which areas in the image are
important for prediction. By calculating the weighted sum of the gra-
dients ﬂowing into the last convolutional layer of the network, Grad-
CAM generates a heatmap that highlights the image regions most

Clinical management and risk stratiﬁcation analysis
We evaluated BL4AS performance using dual operating point strategy
optimized for different clinical priorities. The high-NPV threshold was
designed to maintain 100% sensitivity while maximizing identiﬁcation
of truly benign lesions potentially suitable for surveillance. The high-
PPV threshold was optimized to minimize false positive predictions,
thereby ensuring that lesions ﬂagged as high-risk truly require
immediate intervention.
Additionally, we conducted risk stratiﬁcation analysis to evaluate
whether BL4AS could effectively subclassify BI-RADS 4 lesions (4A, 4B,
and 4 C) according to the ACR BI-RADS guideline. We assessed the
actual malignancy rates in each subcategory across both external test
sets. To assess the potential clinical impact, we compared the perfor-
mance between BL4AS and radiologists’ assessments in external test
set B. The NRI was calculated to quantify the improvement in risk
stratiﬁcation.
Statistics and reproducibility
The performance of all models used for the classi ﬁcation tasks was
evaluated in terms of AUC, accuracy, sensitivity, speci ﬁcity, PPV and
NPV. The primary outcome was the diagnostic performance of BL4AS
for classifying BI-RADS 4 breast lesions, with secondary outcomes
including (1) improvement in radiologists’ diagnostic performance and
reading efﬁciency when assisted by BL4AS and (2) assessment of the
model’s potential for clinical management and routine practice utility.
The DeLong test

AUC values. 95% CIs were calculated based on 1000 bootstrap repli-
cates. Differences between the performance of BL4AS and readers with
or without the BL4AS aid were calculated using McNemar’s χ
2 test or
Wilcoxon rank sum test. Continuous variables were compared by
Kruskal-Wallis test, while qualitative variables were analyzed by two-

effects between AI assistance and subgroup variables were assessed
using generalized estimating equation models. All experiments and
analyses were performed following standardized protocols to ensure
reproducibility. Inter-observer and intra-observer segmentation con-
sistency were assessed and demonstrated robust annotation repro-
ducibility. Statistical analyses were conducted using R (version 4.4.2)
and Python (version 3.9). A two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statis-
tically signiﬁcant.
