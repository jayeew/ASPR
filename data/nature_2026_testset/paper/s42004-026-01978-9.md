Recent advances in molecular diagnostics have highlighted the transformative potential of multi-
protein response logic gates in enhancing sensitivity and speci ﬁcity for cancer detection. However,
logic gates based on multi-type nucleic acid responses remain underexplored. In this study, we
present a dual-biomarker response platform that utilizes nucleic acid logic gates targeting miR-21
(microRNA-21) and lncRNA MALAT1 (metastasis-associated lung adenocarcinoma transcript 1 ).
Compared to traditional single-target methods, our platform effectively overcomes common
challenges, such as expression variability and cross-reactivity, which are inherent in single biomarker
approaches, thereby signiﬁcantly improving both diagnostic sensitivity and speciﬁcity. This platform is
based on rolling circle ampliﬁcation (RCA) technology and incorporates two key components: a primer
switch and a padlock probe switch. When miR-21 is recognized, a hairpin probe undergoes a shape
change, releasing a primer that starts the RCA process. Simultaneously, a MALAT1-speciﬁc padlock
probe forms a closed circular structure, which is necessary to activate RCA and increase signal
detection. Furthermore, the RCA products contain G-quadruplex sequences that can bind to hemin,
forming DNAzymes that produce an electrochemical signal. This strategy signi ﬁcantly enhances
diagnostic sensitivity and speci ﬁcity, demonstrating strong potential for early cancer detection and
precision oncology.

Validation of rolling circle ampliﬁcation and the HRP-mimetic
activity of RCA products

initially conducted in vitro experiments, including sodium dodecyl sulfate
polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis(SDS-PAGE), Atomic Force Microscopy
(AFM), and ultraviolet-visible (UV-Vis) analyses. The SDS-PAGE results
revealed distinct bands for the raw materials, located at anticipated posi-
tions, conﬁrming the high purity of the components (Fig.1Aa n dS u p p l e -
mentary Fig. S1). When all probes were present simultaneously, a
prominent band of large molecular weight with limited migration was
observed, indicating the formation of rolling circle ampli ﬁcation (RCA)
products with heterogeneous molecular sizes. A band around 110 bp cor-
responded to the MALAT1–padlock hybridization product (track e). In
homogeneous reactions, the MALAT1–padlock product coexists with the
RCA product, while in interface reactions, this by-product is ef ﬁciently
washed away. Furthermore, the padlock binds exclusively to MALAT1,
while hairpin probe (HP) binds only tomiR-21, ensuring speciﬁcity for the
two nucleic acid inputs (Supplementary Fig. S2). AFM analysis showed that
the RCA products primarily consisted of long, linear, or circular structures
with a height variation of ~7.5 nm, lik ely due to molecular intertwining
during ampliﬁcation (Fig.1B). Theseﬁndings conﬁrm that RCA effectively
produced high-molecular-weight products, validating the robustness and
feasibility of our experimental approach.
In the UV-Vis analysis, the TMB (3,3 ’,5,5’-tetramethylbenzidine)
chromogenic assay evaluated the HRP-mimetic activity of the RCA pro-
ducts, a well-established and extensively employed detection technique

The ﬁndings demonstrated a signiﬁcant increase in absorbance exclusively
in the presence of RCA products, thereby conﬁrming the catalytic activity of
the HRP-mimetic enzyme (Fig.1C, D). This observation suggests thatmiR-

production of a substantial quantity of G-quadruplexes. These

Electrochemical characterization of layer-by-layer modiﬁed
electrodes for biosensing applications
To conﬁrm the effective construction of the electrochemical interface, we
evaluated the electrochemical performance of various modiﬁed electrodes
using cyclic voltammetry (CV) and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy
(EIS) (Fig.1E, F). Initially, the CV analysis of the unmodiﬁed gold electrode
revealed a pair of reversible redox peaks with substantial peak currents, indi-
cating excellent conductivity and electrochemical activity. Following the sur-
face modiﬁcation with HP, a marked reduction in current response was
observed, alongside a further decrease inthe reversibility of the redox peaks.
This phenomenon is attributed to the negative charges on the DNA backbone,
which repel the negatively charged ferrocyanide, conﬁrming the effective
binding of the HP to the gold electrodethrough thiol groups. Subsequently,
incorporating mercaptohexanol (MCH) led to a further reduction in CV peak
current and a decrease in the reversibility of the redox peaks. This suggests that
MCH effectively blocked nonspeciﬁc adsorption, thereby enhancing the spe-
ciﬁcity of subsequent steps. After further modiﬁcations withMALAT1, miR-
21, padlock, and T4 RNA ligase, the CV peak current continued to decline, and
the redox peak reversibility weakened,demonstrating the successful attach-
ment of these molecules to the electrode surface. The negative charges they
carry enhanced the electrode’s electrochemical impedance. Following the
introduction of phi29 and dNTPs, the CV results showed a marked reduction
in peak current and diminished reversibility, signifying the successful forma-
tion of RCA products and their signi ﬁcant impact on the electrochemical
characteristics of the electrode. Simultaneously, EIS resultsindicated a gradual
increase in electrode impedance as the layer-by-layer modiﬁcation progressed,
consistent with the trends observed in the CV data, further conﬁrming the
successful execution of the modiﬁcations. Collectively, theseﬁndings illustrate
the successful development of a stepwise modiﬁed electrode.
Optimization of experimental conditions
To enhance the electrochemical performance of the sensor, we optimized
various experimental parameters. We systematically evaluated the effects of
different reaction conditions, identifying reaction temperature, incubation
time, and buffer selection as key factors in ﬂuencing sensor performance.
Regarding reaction temperature (Supplementary Fig. S3A, S3B), the current
response increased with rising temperature up to 37 °C, and then decreased as
the temperature exceeded 37 °C. Therefore, 37 °C was selected as the optimal
reaction temperature for our electrochemical platform. For incubation time
(Supplementary Fig. S4A, S4B), the current response gradually increased with
longer incubation times, but plateaued after 2 h. Thus, 2 h was chosen as the
optimal incubation time. In buffer sele c t i o n( S u p p l e m e n t a r yF i g .S 5 A ,S 5 B ) ,
commonly used biocompatible buffers, including PBS, HEPES, TBS, and Phi
29 buffers, were tested. We found that Phi 29 buffer provided the highest
current response, so it was selected as the optimal buffer for our platform.

single biomarker methods. Meanwhile, the ROC curve (Fig.5I) revealed an

Conclusion
In this study, we developed a dual molecular response platform utilizing
rolling circle ampliﬁcation (RCA) and orthogonal nucleic acid logic gates to
improve the speciﬁcity and sensitivity of molecular detection for potential
cancer diagnostics. By designing a hairpin-structured probe responsive to
microRNA-21(miR-21) and a padlock probe speciﬁct o lncRNA MALAT1
(metastasis-associated lung adenocarcinoma transcript 1 ), the platform
activates signal ampliﬁcation only when both biomarkers are present. This
dual-response system signiﬁcantly reduces the likelihood of false-positive
and false-negative results, ensuring high diagnostic accuracy. Furthermore,
the integration of G-quadruplex sequences within the RCA products and
the formation of G-quadruplex DNAzymes enhances detection sensitivity,
enabling precise signal ampliﬁcation even at low target concentrations. This
platform also demonstrates signi ﬁcant potential for clinical translation,
particularly for point-of-care (POC) applications. Its RCA strategy exhibits
inherent compatibility with miniaturised electrochemical or optical detec-
tion devices, enabling implementation in bedside testing scenarios. Despite
these advantages, several practical challenges remain for clinical deploy-
ment, such as optimising RCA conditions to reduce incubation times and
validating performance through large-scale clinical cohorts. Moving for-
ward, our research will focus on expanding the platform ’s capabilities to
enable multiplex detection of biomarkers across multiple cancer types,
thereby providing innovative approaches and concepts for molecular
diagnostics.
