Utilizing a computational approach to quantify DR-related mole-
cular activities 13, previous analysis of patient data from TCGA
indicates that DR-related therapeutic targets hold signi ﬁcant
potential for the treatment of GBM. We applied this method sepa-
rately to CDKN2A subtype and TP53 subtype GBM, and observed that
TP53 GBM patients exhibit higher DR scores compared to CDKN2A
group patients, indicating that TP53 group GBM are more suscep-
tible to the effects of DR (Fig. 1a). Then, we developed Cdkn2a and
Tp53 GBM mouse models, respectively recapitulating the human
CDKN2A subtype and TP53 subtype (See the “Methods” section for
details). For Cdkn2a and Tp53 GBM mouse models, we randomly
divided the mice into IF and ad libitum (AL) diet groups for different
intervention (Fig. 1b). Experimental ﬁndings indicated that IF did
not confer bene ﬁts to Cdkn2a GBM mice (Fig. 1c). Conversely, mice
with Tp53 GBM in the IF group exhibited a markedly improved
prognosis compared to those in the AL group (Fig. 1d), consistent
with the conclusion of previous studies that IF contributes to the
prognosis of GBM
7. The results of hematoxylin and eosin (H&E)
staining indicated that in Tp53 GBM model, there was a downward
trend in tumor size, but a lack thereof in the Cdkn2a GBM model
(Fig. 1e, f and Supplementary Fig. 1).
Subsequently, differentially expressed genes (DEGs) analysis
of bulk transcriptomics on Tp53 GBM mice revealed 1443 up-
regulated genes and 1952 down-regulated genes in the Tp53-IF
group, compared to the Tp53-AL group (Fig. 1g). Notably, Mettl3,a
well-known RNA-binding protein that regulates RNA methylation,
was one of the down-regulated genes (Fig. 1g). Moreover, KEGG
analysis revealed that these DEGs were enriched in several tumor-
related pathways, such as the TGF- β signaling pathway, MAPK sig-
naling pathway, and proteoglycans in cancer, among others
(Fig. 1h). Notably TGF-β is widely acknowledged as a core pathway in
tumor progression, with studies demonstrating that inhibition of
TGFB2 can suppress GBM
22. Enrichment analysis of differential gene
sets between CDKN2A and TP53 GBM patients, revealed that the
TGF-β signaling pathway was signi ﬁcantly altered between CDKN2A
and TP53 groups, suggesting differential activation of TGF- β sig-
naling in patients with Tp53 versus Cdkn2a subtypes (Supplemen-
tary Fig. 2a, b). And Tgfb2 is core regulator in TGF- β pathway
situated upstream in cancer 23 (Supplementary Fig. 3b). Further-
more, differential analysis between Tp53-IF and Cdkn2a-IF groups
revealed a signi ﬁcant decrease of Mettl3 in Tp53-IF group (Supple-
mentary Fig. 3c). Enrichment analysis further demonstrated sig-
niﬁcant involvement of the TGF- β signaling pathway among the
DEGs (Supplementary Fig. 3d, e), suggesting that Cdkn2a and Tp53
GBM model respond differently to the METTL3-mediated TGF- β
signaling pathway.
Moreover, we analyzed human transcriptomic data from 41 indi-
viduals following IF and controls (GEO DataSets: GSE196335)

conducted metabolomic analysis of blood samples from Tp53 GBM

ﬁles between theTp53-IF and Tp53-AL groups (Supplementary Fig. 5a),
with the majority of metabolites showing signiﬁcant downregulation in
the Tp53- I Fg r o u pc o m p a r e dt ot h eTp53-AL groups (Fig. 3a), notably
methionine and S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) (Fig. 3b). Furthermore,
differential metabolites could be enriched in methionine metabolism-
related pathways (Fig.3c). Methionine and SAM serve as the substrate
for m

inhibiting GBM. Additionally, su pplementation with methionine has
been reported to raise SAM levels in various regions of the central
nervous system 32. These results indicated that the reduction in
methionine caused by IF can inhibit the TGF- β pathway through the
regulation of SAM-m6Aa x i s .

such as TGFB2 in GBM, thereby inhibiting the progression of
GBM (Fig. 6).

The signiﬁcance of this study lies in several aspects. Firstly, while
extensive researches have elucidated the downstream effects of m

on gene expression and disease phenotypes, the dynamic regulation of
m

pletely understood. The current research reveals one such regulatory
mechanism of m

between dietary patterns and gut microbiota, providing a perspective
for understanding the upstream regulation of m
6A. Additionally, pre-

microbiota of mice lead to signiﬁcant changes in m

modulate gene expression

molecular mechanisms 42. And Li et al. describes the regulatory
mechanism of methionine de ﬁciency on m 6Ai n Escherichia coli and
also indicates that the impact of methionine deﬁciency on organisms is
complex and diverse 43. Moreover, this study holds additional sig-
niﬁcance in addressing the challenges faced by patients with under-
lying conditions or elderly individuals suffering from GBM, where
surgical procedures and conventional treatments involving radiation
and chemotherapy often entail signi ﬁcant side effects and bodily
harm. IF emerges as a promising treatment option due to its low cost
and minimal adverse effects, extending patient survival.
This study, elucidates its mechanisms from the perspective of
microbiota-metabolism-m
6A axis, laying a foundational theoretical
framework for the widespread application of IF. The gut is often
referred to as the “second brain” in humans

the impact of diet and gut microbiota on human metabolism is more
signiﬁcant than innate genetics
45. With the introduction of the ﬁrst
fecal microbiota product, REBYOTA, approved by the FDA in 2022, the
ﬁeld of fecal microbiota therapy has been rapidly advancing. The
theoretical framework established by this study on the regulation
mechanism of gut microbiota and m
6A provides possibilities for the
development of methionine-related microbial drugs or metabolic
drugs, offering avenues for the treatment of GBM. Moreover, research
has indicated that fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) can be uti-

However, this study also has limitations. Due to the signi ﬁcant
decrease in the abundance of many candidate bacteria regulating
methionine, we did not pinpoint which speciﬁc species played a larger
role. Considering the complexity of the gut microbiota, it is challen-
ging to attribute the decrease in methionine to a single gut bacterium.
We speculate that multiple methionine-related gut microbial species
have decreased, but which speciﬁc gut microbial species has a greater
impact requires further exploration in future studies. Anyway, this
study revealed that decreased m

Further in-depth research will be conducted in the future to investigate
this aspect.
