Precise three-dimensional arrangements of atoms and functional groups
govern molecular reactivity and biological function, making stereocontrol a
central goal in modern organic and medicinal chemistry. Yet reconciling broad
substrate generality with high enantioselectivity remains a core limitation of
enantioselective catalysis, motivating the pursuit of privileged chiral catalysts.
Here we design a new-to-nature photoenzyme that unlocks enzymatic dera-
cemization of structurally diverse allenes, an entropically disfavored process
inaccessible to natural biocatalysts. Structure-guided design created
substrate-speciﬁc interaction networks, delivering high enantioenrichment for
axially chiral allenic carboxylic acids, esters, and amides via triplet energy
transfer under aerobic conditions. X-ray crystal structures of
enzyme–substrate complexes reveal tailored chiral pockets that accom-
modate distinct substrates and direct enantiocontrol, rationalizing the
breadth and selectivity observed. This work reconciles generality with high
selectivity on an enzymatic platform, establishes deracemization as an evol-
vable route to axially chiral molecules, and broadens activation strategies for
stereochemical editing.

To overcome the inherent substrate limitations of asymmetric
catalysis and natural enzymes, engineering or repurposing an enzyme
and scaffold protein has emerged as a promising strategy. These
approaches enable tailored biocatalysts with broadened substrate

the application of non-natural biocatalytic strategies to chiral allene
synthesis, particularly enzymatic deracemization, remains under-
explored. This is largely due to the thermodynamically disfavored of
deracemization, constraints imposed by microscopic reversibility, and
the need of an external driving force
20,59–61. Although several chemical
deracemization methods have been established by Bach 60,62,63 and
others64–67, enzymatic deracemization is currently absent from the
catalytic repertoire of natural enzymes.
To access structurally diverse axially chiral allenes via enzymatic
deracemization within a conﬁned enzyme active site, we endeavored to
design an engineered photoenzyme that harnesses light as an external
driving force to enable a new-to-nature synthetic pathway with high
enantioenrichment. Guided by this rationale, we incorporated the
photoactive noncanonical amino acid 4-benzoylphenylalanine (BpA)
into a protein scaffold, generating variants that harvest light and
accommodate diverse allenes. Through structure-based rational design,
we achieve oxygen-tolerant deracemization of allenic acids, esters, and
amides with high enantioenrichment via triplet-energy transfer (EnT)
under aerobic conditions (Fig.1d). Furthermore, X-ray crystal structures
of photoenzyme–substrate complexes reveal tailored chiral pockets
that rationalize the observed enantioselectivity across axially chiral
allenes. Collectively, this establishes a new-to-nature photoenzymatic
deracemization strategy for constructing chiral molecules.

Photoenzymatic deracemization of allenic carboxylic acids
We began by targeting the deracemization of allenic carboxylic acids, a
transformation that, to our knowledge, has not been realized by che-
mical or enzymatic methods. After screening our previously con-

Photoenzymatic deracemization of allenamides
Given the differing activities of the engineered photoenzymes on
allenic acids versus esters, we asked whether they would retain activity
toward allenamides. However, experiments showed that the engi-
neered PEC variants exhibited a nearly complete loss of catalytic
activity toward the model amide substrate rac-3a (Supplementary
Table 17). This suggests that differences in polarity, charge state, and
hydrogen-bonding capacity between amide and carboxylate groups
disrupt the substrate-speciﬁc interaction networks, necessitating their
redesign for allenamides. To minimize the experimental effort, weﬁrst
screened our previously established, compact CTB10-based photo-
enzyme library rather than the full set of CTB10-W13BpA variants. Nine
variants from this focused library displayed catalytic activity toward
rac-3a (Fig. 4a, Supplementary Table 17). Among them, CTB10-
M40BpA (designated PEA1.0) delivered the highest performance,
producing ( S)-3a with 50% e.e (Fig. 4b and Supplementary Fig. 1,
Supplementary Table 18). However, subsequent conventional protein
engineering, including structural simulations of PEA1.0 (Supplemen-
tary Fig. 11), molecular docking of PEA1.0 with (R)-3a (Supplementary
Fig. 11), and alanine scanning of residues within 4 Å of the substrate
(Supplementary Table 19), failed to improve the stereoselectivity. The
likely reason for this is from inaccuracies modeling the noncanonical
amino acid BpA during simulations.
To resolve ambiguities from modeling, we solved the crystal
structure of PEA1.0 at 1.77 Å resolution (PDB: 9IR1; Fig. 4c, and Sup-
plementary Fig. 12, Supplementary Table 13). The asymmetric unit
contains a dimer, and BpA adopts distinct conformations in the two
monomers (Fig. 4c, and Supplementary Fig. 12), which may explain
why conventional engineering failed to improve activity. Structural
analysis indicates that the conformational dynamics of BpA40 are
modulated by proximal residues M86 and H111 (within 4 Å), which
adopt different rotamers across chains (Fig. 4c and Supplementary
Fig. 13). We hypothesized that mutating M86/H111 could restrict
BpA40 ﬂexibility and stabilize a de ﬁned chiral pocket to enhance
enantioselectivity. Guided by considerations of steric effects, π–π
interactions, hydrogen bonding, and polarity, we substituted M86 and
H111 with Leu, Phe, His, Gln, Asp, Arg, Thr, or Ile (Supplementary
Table 20). Among these, M86I delivered high performance, achieving
91% e.e. Accordingly, PEA1.0 ‑M86I (PEA2.0) was advanced for
substrate-scope evaluation.
We next evaluated PEA2.0 across a broad panel of allenamides
under optimized conditions (Fig.4d, Supplementary Tables 21–23). It
showed that para-substituted phenyl substrates bearing methyl or
halogen groups (F, Cl, Br, I) generally afforded high stereoselectivity,
giving (S)-3b to ( S)-3f with up to 96% e.e. Introducing a bulky para-
triﬂuoromethyl group moderately reduced stereocontrol, providing
(S)-3g with 78% e.e. Meta-substituted substrates (( S)-3h-(S)-3j) also
delivered high enantioselectivity, up to 93% e.e. As expected from
steric effects, both mono-ortho-methyl and di-ortho-chloro substituted
substrates slightly reduced the deracemization efﬁciency, yielding 86%
e.e. (( S)-3k) and 71% e.e. (( S)-3l), respectively. PEA2.0 also accom-
modated heteroaromatic substrate, producing the thiophene-
modiﬁed allenamide (S)-3m with 86% e.e. Replacing tert-butyl with a
smaller isopropyl maintained high stereoselectivity ((S)-3n, 95% e.e.),
whereas a rigid cyclohexyl group severely reduced stereoselectivity
((S)-3o, 2% e.e.). To mitigate steric encumbrance around the cyclohexyl
group, residues I15, V17, and M117 were downsized based on docking
analysis (Supplementary Fig. 14, Supplementary Table 24).
PEA2.0‑V17A was the most effective variant, increasing the stereo-
selectivity of (S)-3o to 19%. Additionally, the deracemization ofrac-3b
remained effective using PEA2.0 cell lysate, affording ( S)-3b with a
comparable 81% e.e. Notably, scale-up reactions retained comparable
performance, delivering (S)-3b with 90% e.e. and 85% isolated yield.
Overall, these results demonstrate the broad substrate generality and
high enantioselectivity of the engineered photoenzyme platform.

axially chiral allenes via initial enantioselective discrimination between
(R)- and ( S)-substrates (Fig. 5d). Following this chiral discrimination,
selective EnT occurs from photoexcited BpA to the ( R)-substrate, as
supported by quenching with triplet quenchers 2,5-dimethyl-2,4-hex-
adiene and perylene (Supplementary Table 27). Upon energy absorp-
tion, the (R)-substrate undergoes double-bond cleavage to form a 1,2-
diradical intermediate
20–22, which then dissociates from the
photoenzyme promoted by the excess energy that results from
EnT20–22. The free intermediate recombines in solution to a racemic
mixture of ( R)- and ( S)-enantiomers. The (R)-enantiomer is funneled
back into the catalytic cycle and continuously consumed, while the (S)-
enantiomer accumulates, driving progressive enrichment of the S-
conﬁguration. Through this stereoselective cycle, the designed pho-
toenzymes deracemize structurally diverse allenes with high ef ﬁ-
ciency. Overall, structure-based rational design allows the engineering
of substrate-speciﬁc interaction networks, enabling ef ﬁcient derace-
mization of structurally diverse allenes.
Discussion
In this work, we report a new-to-nature, photoenzymatic deracemiza-
tion platform for constructing axially chiral allenic carboxylic acids,
allenic esters and allenamides. Through structure-guided engineering,
we created substrate-speciﬁc interaction networks that can accom-
modate structurally diverse allenes, delivering high enantioenrich-
ment of single enantiomers. Crystallographic and kinetic analysis
elucidated the mechanism of enzymatic deracemization across diverse
allenes and enabled the design of tailored chiral pockets within a
conﬁned photoenzyme active site. Our ﬁndings reconcile selectivity
with generality in enzymatic deracemization and provide design
principles for new deracemization reactions to access high-value chiral
molecules.
