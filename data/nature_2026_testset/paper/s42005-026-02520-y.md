Integrating efﬁcient second-order ( χ(2)) optical nonlinearity into topological integrated photonic
systems presents a fundamental challenge: frequency-dependent topological bandgaps inherently
impede simultaneous, robust edge states at cross-octave frequencies required for second-harmonic
generation (SHG). We overcome this limitation by introducing, based on theoretical modeling and
numerical simulations, dual-frequency topological bandgap engineering in a square lattice of
nonlinear microresonators with synthetic magnetic ﬂuxes. Our design achieves unidirectional edge
states for both fundamental and second-harmonic frequencies. It enables ef ﬁcient SHG with ﬂux-
programmable chirality, a unique consequence of χ
(2) nonlinearity-induced topology transitions in the
system. The topological array yields an SHG ef ﬁciency enhancement of two orders of magnitude
compared to a single resonator. Our design can be readily implemented by using χ(2) integrated
photonic platforms like thin ﬁlm lithium niobate, which would unlock novel functionalities such as
nonreciprocal SHG diodes, optical logic gates, and a pathway to topology-protected entangled
photon sources.
Topological photonics has emerged as a transformative paradigm for
designing robust optical systems 1, leveraging unidirectional,
backscattering-resistant edge states analogous to those in electronic
topological insulators. These photonic analogs of electronic quantum
phenomena
2,3, such as the integer quantum Hall effect, quantum spin
Hall effect and fractional quantum Hall effects, have unlocked unpre-
cedented control over light propagation and localization without external
magnetic ﬁeld. Recent advances have further expanded this ﬁeld by
exploring the interplay between topological physics and optical
nonlinearities
4,5. Different platforms such as dielectric meta-surfaces 6,
and silicon-based waveguide arrays7 and coupled ring resonator arrays5,8
have been demonstrated for third-order ( χ(3)) nonlinear processes,
including four-wave-mixing, third harmonic generation and parametric
ampliﬁcation. Among these systems, silicon-based two-dimensional
(2D) coupled ring resonator arrays exploit strong light con ﬁnement and
high-quality resonances to achieve enhanced nonlinear ef ﬁciencies and
robustness against fabrication disorders, enabling the exploration of
practical topological photonic devices, such as topological optical delay
lines
9, topological lasers 10, and topological single-photon 11 and
entangled-photon sources12. Recently, 2D coupled ring resonator arrays
have also been demonstrated for applications in topological frequency
combs13 and programmable topological photon chips 14.
Second-order (χ(2)) nonlinearity serves as a cornerstone for numerous
classic and quantum photonics applications.χ(2) processes exhibit orders of
magnitude higher efﬁciencies at equivalent pumppower levels, compared to
χ(3) processes. Moreover,χ(2) nonlinearity allows the indistinguishable single-
photon rate and the deterministic photon-photon gates for scalable quan-
tum photonic computing15. Therefore, integrating of opticalχ(2) nonlinearity
into topological Hall effects would transform topological photonics into a
playground for recon ﬁgurable topology, frequency engineering and
enhanced light-matter control; however, it has remained a challenge. For
example, the absence of intrinsic χ
(2) nonlinearity in silicon photonic
materials has conﬁned investigation of nonlinear topological phenomena
primarily to χ(3) nonlinear effects. Previously proposed χ(2) topological
designs have relied on magneto-optical photonic crystals that are difﬁcult to
experimentally implemented 16. Fortunately, recent breakthroughs in
nanofabrication of χ(2) m a t e r i a l ss u c ha st h i n -ﬁlm lithium niobate17,18 and
other materials19,20 have enabled promising χ(2) integrated photonic plat-
forms. These advancements have positioned χ(2) ring resonators as inter-
esting building blocks for topological photonic systems21; nevertheless, their
theoretical and experimental potential in this context remains unexplored.
Here, we present a broadband topologicalχ(2) photonic system via dual
frequency topological bandgap engineering in a 2D nonlinear micro-
resonator array. Our system overcomes the fundamental challenge of

Conclusions
We have established for the ﬁrst time, to the best of our knowledge, a
theoretical framework for broadbandtopological SHG in a 2D coupled ring
microresonator array. By designing a dual-band topological lattice with
synthetic magnetic ﬂuxes, we resolve the critica l incompatibility between
topological protection andχ(2) nonlinearity across an octave frequency gap.
We demonstrated simultaneous topological protection for the fundamental
and SH ﬁelds, enabling efﬁcient SHG with ﬂux-programmable chirality, a
unique consequence ofχ(2) nonlinearity-induced topology transitions in the
system. Theoretical analysis indicated that the topological SHG could have
over two orders of magnitude higher conversion ef ﬁciency than that in
single resonators via topology-en hanced coherent buildup. Our work
bridges topological photonics and second-order nonlinear optics, demon-
strating that χ
(2) processes inherit topological protection and exhibit ﬂux-
programmable SH chirality. The topological design can be readily imple-
mented by using existing low-lossχ(2) integrated photon platforms like thin
ﬁlm lithium niobate. One could further explore the incorporation of electro-
optic tuning in lithium niobate 21, which may enable real-time recon ﬁ-
guration of synthetic ﬂuxes and programmable topological χ(2) photonic
chip14 and high speed quantum processors30.
We note that during the revision of our manuscript, an experimental
observation of topological harmonic generation via relaxed phase-matching
condition in 2D silicon nitride ring arrays is published very recently in
Science by Hafezi’sg r o u p31. Distinctly, our work offers a rational design
guideline for topological optical second harmonic generation in 2D non-
linear resonator arrays such as lithium niobate.
