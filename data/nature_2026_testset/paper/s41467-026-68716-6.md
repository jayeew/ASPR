Chondrosarcoma is a rare malignant bone tumor characterized by the
production of cartilage matrix by tumor cells1,2. Chondrosarcomas are
the second most common form of bone tumor, accounting for over
20% of primary malignant bone tumors
3. Conventional chon-
drosarcoma (cCS) is the most common subtype, developing
centrally in the medullar cavity of bone
1. Other rarer subtypes of
chondrosarcoma include dedifferentiated, mesenchymal, and clear
cell
1,4– 6.
Treatment options for people with chondrosarcoma are very
limited and have not demonstrated impactful ef ﬁcacy. Because con-
ventional chemotherapy is ineffective and non-curative in treating
cCS
6,7, localized disease is managed with surgical resection 1,6,7.

are treated with Ewing sarcoma chemotherapy schedules; however,

Somatic mutations in IDH (isocitrate dehydrogenase) genes are
present in more than 50% of conventional and dedifferentiated
chondrosarcomas
11,14– 17. Mutated IDH proteins produce the metabolite
D-2-hydroxyglutarate (2-HG) fromα-ketoglutarate (α-KG) conversion,
which accumulates and inhibits the function of enzymes dependent on
α-KG, leading to oncogenesis and cancer progression3,6,18. Therefore,
investigating inhibition of mutated IDH in advanced and metastatic
chondrosarcoma is warranted. IDH inhibitors have been evaluated in
patients with advanced chondrosarcoma harboring mutantIDH1,a n d
these studies have demonstrated durable disease control along with
2-HG reduction
14,19.
Olutasidenib (FT-2102) is an oral, highly selective, potent inhibitor
of mutant IDH1 with a low molecular weight (355 g/mol) and a
2:1 stoichiometric binding property
20– 23. The drug selectively targets
the mutant form of IDH1 and does not inhibit the wild-type protein,
which is critical, as inhibition of the wild-type protein may cause off-
target effects leading to oxidative stress and subsequent resistance to
therapy
24. Inhibition of wild-type IDH1 function decreases levels of α-
KG, which can lead to impaired hemoglobin production, resulting in
anemia caused by disruption of erythropoiesis
18,25.
Olutasidenib is FDA-approved for relapsed/refractory (R/R) acute
myeloid leukemia (AML) based on clinically meaningful activity
observed in the registrational phase 2 open-label multicenter trial
21,26.
Five-year data in the pivotal cohort have demonstrated sustained
clinical activity and additional studies have shown efﬁcacy in subsets of
patients with AML or myelodysplastic syndrome
27,28.
Herein we describe the efﬁcacy and safety results from the phase
2 study, which evaluated olutasidenib as monotherapy in a cohort of
patients with IDH1-mutated chondrosarcoma. We report disease con-
trol and overall survival with olutasidenib, and a safety pro ﬁle con-
sistent with prior studies of olutasidenib.
Results
Patients
Twenty-three patients were screened, enrolled, and participated in the
trial between November 14, 2018, and July 20, 2022. Sixteen patients
were diagnosed with cCS, 5 with dedifferentiated CS, and one each
with myxoid or unknown subtype chondrosarcoma (Table 1). The
median age was 57 (range, 30 – 71) years, 74% (17/23) of patients were
male, and the median number of prior regimens was 2 (range, 1 – 5).
Patients had clinical or radiological disease progression prior to trial
entry. In the Response-Evaluable (RE) Analysis set, there were 2 (10%)
patients diagnosed with locally advanced chondrosarcoma, 15 (71%)
with metastatic chondrosarcoma, and 4 (19%) with unknown disease
status. The median treatment duration was 84 days (range, 6 – 827).
Disease progression was the most common reason for discontinuation
(n = 18; 78%). Patient disposition is shown in Supplementary Fig. 1.

Quality of life
The EQ-5D-5L patient-reported questionnaire was used to assess
change from baseline for anxiety/depression, mobility, pain/dis-
comfort, self-care, and usual activities. Modest improvement in the
pain/discomfort scale was reported from Cycle 1 Day 1 to Cycle 3 Day 1,
with an increase in the proportion of patients reporting no problems
(Supplementary Fig. 3).
Pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics
Plasma olutasidenib concentration at steady-state showed that mean
concentrations on Cycle 2 Day 1 were higher than on Cycle 1 Day 1,
indicating accumulation with 150 mg twice daily (BID) dosing (Sup-
plementary Fig. 4). Plasma 2-HG levels decreased from 879 mg/mL at
baseline to 133 ng/mL at Cycle 2 Day 1 for patients in SD and from
182 ng/mL at baseline to 116 ng/mL at Cycle 2 Day 1 for those with PD.
The median percent change from baseline to Cycle 2 Day 1 was−42.3%
(range,−63.3% to −4.7%) for the SD group and−37.1% (range,−38.3% to
−35.8%) for the PD group, demonstrating that olutasidenib effectively
reduced 2-HG levels within the ﬁrst month (Supplementary Fig. 5).
Safety
During the trial, 21 (91%) patients experienced a treatment-emergent
adverse event (TEAE) and 12 (52%) patients reported a treatment-
emergent serious AE (SAE) (Table2). Olutasidenib-related TEAEs were
reported in 19 (83%) patients, with 12 (52%) of these cases determined
to be Grade 3 or higher. Three (13%) patients discontinued olutasidenib
due to a treatment-related TEAE; however, no patient experienced a
DLT. Twelve (52%) patients died during the course of the study, chieﬂy
due to disease progression (n = 8), and no deaths were determined to
be treatment-related.
The most commonly occurring TEAEs, all observed in ≥10% of
patients, were nausea ( n = 15; 65%), decreased appetite ( n =1 0 ; 4 3 % ) ,
fatigue ( n = 8; 35%), constipation ( n = 7; 30%), and liver enzymes
increased (alanine aminotransferase and aspartate aminotransferase,
n = 7 [30%] each) (Table 2). Grade 3/4 treatment-related TEAEs were
reported in 12 (52%) patients: ALT increased (n = 7; 30%), AST and GGT
increased (n = 3 each; 13%), and ALP and bilirubin increased (n =2e a c h ;
9%). Nausea, decreased appetite, fatigue, asthenia, and dysgeusia were
all mild to moderate in severity (Grades 1 and 2). Six (26%) patients
reported TEAEs of disease progression.
Discussion
Chondrosarcoma is usually resistant to conventional chemotherapy;
therefore, without alternative therapies, patients with advanced or
metastatic CS have poor prognoses. AsIDH1 mutations are commonly
detected in cCS, mIDH1 inhibitors such as olutasidenib may be effec-
tive in CS, offering patients new hope for treatment. In this phase 2
trial, olutasidenib conferred disease control, with more than half (52%)
of RE patients achieving SD and 75% of patients surviving for at least 6
months, with a median OS of 16 months. In the cCS patients, the
median OS was 19 months with a 6-month survival rate of 81%. The cCS
patients with SD fared even better, as 80% survived for at least
18 months, and the median OS was not reached. One patient with SD
has remained on treatment for over 4 years via a compassionate use
program due to clinical beneﬁt. Despite the lack of response as deﬁned
by RECIST (CR or PR) observed in the study, durable progression-free
intervals were reported in several patients. Although patient numbers
were small, 82% of patients with SD harbored tumors with the R132C
mutation versus 25% of patients with PD with the mutation. This could
indicate that patients with R132C mutations are more likely to maintain
SD vs those with other IDH1 mutation types. Plasma 2-HG levels
decreased following treatment initiation. Baseline 2-HG levels were
higher at baseline in the SD group compared with the PD group, and
this may be due to the high prevalence of the R132C variant detected in
the patients in the SD group; this mutation is known to be a strong
producer of 2-HG
15.
Olutasidenib had a generally acceptable safety pro ﬁle, with no

decreased appetite, fatigue, constipation, and increases in AST, ALT,
and ALP, consistent with what was reported previously

treat patients with advanced and metastatic chondrosarcoma; some
treatments have been prospectively evaluated, but results have been
disappointing. Retrospective data suggested beneﬁt with pazopanib in
conventional chondrosarcoma
2. However, in a phase 2 trial evaluating
efﬁcacy of regorafenib versus placebo in advanced chondrosarcoma,
the median PFS in the regorafenib arm was 4.6 months compared to
2 months in the placebo arm, but the trial did not meet its primary
endpoint
30. Mixed results have been reported for agents targeting the
hedgehog pathway in chondrosarcoma. In a phase 2 single arm trial of
vismodegib (GDC-0449), the 6-month PFS rate was 28%, which is
similar to the 30% rate observed in cCS patients in the trial results
reported here, while in a phase 2 double-blind randomized study of
saridegib, no improvement in PFS was demonstrated versus
placebo

was 5.5 months, the 6-month PFS rate was 47%, and the OS rate at
2 years was 56%, with 3 patients remaining on treatment for more than
2y e a r s

instead applying Choi response criteria32. Another retrospective study

2.2 months, shorter than the 3.5 months observed in the cCS subset of
this trial
33. The randomized Phase 2 trial of the death receptor 5 agonist
ozekibart (INBRX-109) met the primary endpoint, with a statistically
signiﬁcantly longer median PFS (5.52 months) in the ozekibart arm
compared to placebo (2.66 months). This trial is the ﬁrst positive
randomised trial in advanced conventional chondrosarcoma

Methods
Study design and participants
All patients gave written informed consent before screening and
enrollment and the trial was conducted in accordance with the pro-
tocol, the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, and the Interna-
tional Conference on Harmonisation (ICH) Harmonised Tripartite
Guideline for Good Clinical Practice (GCP). The protocol was approved
by a local Institutional Review Board or Ethics Committee at each site
that enrolled patients (Supplementary Table 1). The study was regis-
tered on Clinicaltrials.gov on September 24, 2018. The protocol is
available on Clinicaltrials.gov ( https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/
NCT03684811), and the protocol and statistical analysis plan are
available in Supplementary Note 2.
This was a multicenter, phase 1b/2,open-label, multi-cohort study
of olutasidenib as a single agent or in combination for the treatment of
multiple advanced solid tumor types, including glioma (Cohort 1),
hepatobiliary cancers (Cohort 2), chondrosarcoma (Cohort 3), intra-
hepatic cholangiocarcinoma (Cohort 4), and other non-central ner-
vous system (CNS) solid tumors with IDH1 mutations (Cohort 5)
(NCT03684811). Cohorts 1– 4 were divided into “a” and “b”, with “a”
evaluating olutasidenib as a single agent and “b” evaluating combina-
tion therapy in the event that clinical responses were not observed. No
patients were enrolled into Cohorts 2 – 4b. The last visit of the last
patient was conducted on June 13, 2022. Results from Cohort 1 were
published previously
29. An analysis of Cohort 3 was presented at the
Connective Tissue Oncology Society 2024 Annual Meeting. Results
from other cohorts have not been published. The analysis presented
here evaluated patients in Cohort 3a, which included those diagnosed
with R/R mIDH1 locally advanced or metastatic chondrosarcoma.

Outcomes
The primary endpoint in phase 2 was to evaluate the clinical activity of
olutasidenib as assessed by the objective response rate (ORR) for each
applicable disease evaluated in this study, which included advanced
solid tumors and gliomas with IDH1 R132X mutations. The key sec-
ondary endpoint assessed safety as measured by the incidence of
treatment-emergent adverse event s (TEAEs). Additional secondary
endpoints included rates of progression-free survival (PFS), duration
of response (DOR), and overall survival (OS). The main exploratory
objective was evaluation of speciﬁc biomarkers indicating response or
resistance. Assessment of health-related quality of life (HR-QoL),
another exploratory objective, was evaluated with the EQ-5D-5L
patient-reported questionnaire, which was administered at Cycle 1
Day 1, Cycle 3 Day 1, and end of treatment. The best percent change in
tumor burden while on study was deﬁned as the maximum percentage
reduction or minimum percentage increase at a single time point of
the patient’st u m o rb u r d e np o s tﬁrst dose of study drug. TEAEs were
assessed at every visit, coded using MedRA version 21.0, and graded
using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI
CTCAE version 4.03). TEAEs potentially associated with liver injury
were speci ﬁed as adverse events of special interest (AESI). These
included elevations in alanine transaminase (ALT), aspartate amino-
transferase (AST), or total bilirubin of Grade 2 or higher in patients
with normal liver function tests (LFTs) at baseline. In those with ele-
vated levels at baseline, speciﬁed events included one Grade-shift or
higher in LFTs. In addition, any hepa tic TEAE (e.g., acute hepatitis,
cholestatic hepatitis, cholestasis, or hepatic insufﬁciency) was speci-
ﬁed as an AESI.
The Safety Analysis set included all chondrosarcoma patients who
received any dose of olutasidenib. The RE Analysis set, used to deﬁne
all efﬁcacy endpoints, included all patients with measurable disease at
baseline who received olutasidenib and had at least 1 post-baseline
response assessment or discontinued the treatment phase due to
disease progression (including death by disease progression) within
8w e e k so ft h eﬁrst dose of study treatment.
Statistics and reproducibility
The trial employed an optimal Simon ’s 2-stage design with a 1-sided
alpha of 0.025, power of 80%, null hypothesis of 5% ORR, and alter-
native hypothesis of 25% ORR. Because this trial was open-label, no
randomization or blinding were used. The ORR was de ﬁned as the
proportion of patients with a best response of complete response/
remission (CR) or partial response/remission (PR) and was summar-
ized by cohort with 95% conﬁdence intervals (CI) estimated using the
Clopper-Pearson method. Summary descriptive statistics were gener-
ated with SAS Enterprise Guide 8.2 (64-bit). The cohort population
demographics and disposition were characterized, and time to event
data and the estimated probability of survival over time, which are
presented with 95% CI unless otherwise speciﬁed, were analyzed with
Kaplan-Meier methods. Conﬁdence intervals were calculated using the
Brookmeyer-Crowley method. PFS was de ﬁned as the time between
the ﬁrst dose of olutasidenib until the start of another anticancer
therapy, disease progression, or death for those patients in the RE
Analysis set, and it is presented with 95% CI. OS (presented with 95%
CIs) was de ﬁned as the time from study entry until death from any
cause and was based on the Safety Analysis set.
