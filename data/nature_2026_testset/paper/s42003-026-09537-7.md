WDR62, a centrosome and microtubule associated protein, regulates mitotic spindle formation and
centrosome integrity in progenitor cells during development. While its role in neural progenitor
differentiation is known, its function in myogenesis remains unclear. Here, we show that WDR62
deﬁcient mice possess smaller skeletal muscles. Following cardiotoxin injury to the tibialis anterior
muscle, WDR62 deﬁcient mice show normal satellite cell activation, but display a higher percentage of
immature myoﬁbers at day 7 post injury, suggesting premature differentiation. In Drosophila larvae,
Wdr62 knockdown in the wing disc increases asymmetric myoblast division resulting in premature
differentiation. In C2C12 mouse myoblasts, WDR62 loss leads to decreased myoblast proliferation
due to increased centriole numbers and centriole cohesion, and a slight increase in myoblast fusion at
day 3 differentiation, which supports premature differentiation. These data implicate WDR62 in
maintaining centrosome integrity that is critical for myoblast proliferation and preventing premature
differentiation during early stages of myogenesis.
WD40 repeat protein 62 (WDR62) is a centrosome and microtubule
associated protein with enhanced localisation to the spindle poles during
mitosis1,2. The N-terminus of WDR62 contains multiple WD40 domains
that typically form beta-propeller structures and are required for micro-
tubule association
3. By contrast, the C-terminus of WDR62 is largely dis-
ordered and contains several serine/ threonine phosphorylation motifs4.
WDR62 interaction and cell-cycle dependent phosphorylation by Aurora
Kinase A and c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK) promote or negatively regulate
association with spindle microtubules, respectively
5,6. The spatiotemporal
regulation of WDR62 mitotic functions is required for normal embryonic
development
3,5,7. WDR62 is the second most commonly mutated gene in
autosomal recessive primary microcephaly8, and studies have shown that
WDR62 deﬁciency results in abnormal spi ndle formation and increased
mitotic delay 9– 12. During brain development, WDR62 deﬁciency leads to
reduced radial glial cell proliferation and premature differentiation into
intermediate progenitors. This combination exhausts the pool of progeni-
tors, leading to thinning of the forebrain cortex and microcephaly
10,12– 14.I n
addition, WDR62 is involved in female meiotic initiation by activating JNK
signalling15, spindle assembly during spermatogenesis 16,a sw e l la sm a n -
chette removal during spermiogenesis 17. These studies reinforce the
important role of WDR62 in co-ordinating cell fate of progenitor cells in
neuronal and non-neuronal contexts.
In addition to brain and reproductive organs, WDR62 is also expressed
in striated muscles
18. Skeletal muscle is comprised of striated, multi-
nucleated myoﬁbers organised into compact bundles known as sarcomeres
that undergo contractions to generate force for movement. Skeletal muscle
stem cells, also known as satellite cells, are marked by PAX7 and the absence
of myogenic factors and reside between the myoﬁber and the basal lamina in
a quiescent (G0) state. In response to injury, satellite cells are “activated”,
express the muscle regulatory family transcription factor, myogenic factor 5
(MYF5), and undergo asymmetric division to generate a myoblast that is
positioned away from the myoﬁber, and a self-renewed satellite cell posi-
tioned on the myoﬁber surface
19,20. Early myogenic commitment in myo-
blasts also requires the expression of myogenic differentiation 1 (MYOD) to

mice plus their control littermates were analysed for each experiment to
ensure reproducibility. Each pair of mice (homozygote and its sex-matched
