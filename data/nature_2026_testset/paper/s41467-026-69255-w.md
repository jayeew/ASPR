The origin and evolution of complex interplay within chemical reac-

biogeochemical cycles, which sustain life by transforming and circu-

processes. At the earliest stages of life’s emergence, synthetic chemical
pathways likely contributed to the construction of self-organized
reaction networks from simpler material forms

chemical cycles, the carbon cycling networks are particularly note-
worthy due to their pivotal role in supporting life and inﬂuencing the
global climate system. Understanding complex carbon cycling reac-

shifting environmental conditions5–7. Organisms use enzymatic path-
ways to construct and regulate carbon cycles, yet the origins of these
pathways prior to life ’s emergence remain elusive, highlighting the
need to explore prebiotic chemistry that may have laid the ground-

Results and discussion
Screening of sulﬁde minerals for CO2RN and analysis of the
reaction pathway
The hydrothermal conversion of NaHCO3 into organics was conducted
in a stainless steel autoclave with an inner volume of 7 mL at 250 °C for
12 h, with 1 MPa H
2 serving as the reducing agent. The resulting pro-
ducts were identi ﬁed through GC −MS and NMR analyses following
acidiﬁcation or derivatization (Supplementary Fig. 3 −63). Drawing
inspiration from the transition metal-sulfur coordination structure of
CO
2-immobilized enzymes, we screened various sul ﬁde minerals for
their potential role in the prebiotic CO2RN. Among the tested minerals
(FeS2,N i3S2,C u2S, CuS, CoS2,C o S ,M n S ,2 H - M o S2,a n d1 Tʹ-MoS2), the
twisted 1Tʹ-MoS2 polymorph, which mimics the distorted geometry of
the carbon dioxide Mo-S2-pterin enzyme23 and Mo-nitrogenase24 that
produce hydrocarbons under certain conditions (Supplementary
Fig. 64), enables the hydrothermal conversion of NaHCO
3 into for-
mate, acetate, glycolate, oxalate, propionate, lactate, 2-
hydroxybutanoate, succinate, and methylsuccinate (Supplementary
Figs. 3−61). Given the instability of pyruvate and other reaction pro-
ducts, the reaction temperature was reduced or the reaction time was
shortened to identify additional intermediates. When the reaction time
was shortened to 3 h, several additional compounds, including α-
ketoglutarate, 3-hydroxybutanoate, crotonate, butyrate, fumarate,
methylmalonate, 4-hydroxybutyrate, 3-hydroxypropionate, and
hydroxyisobutyrate, were detected (Supplementary Fig. 65 −96). At
lower temperatures of 70 °C and 100 °C, further products such as
pyruvate, acetoacetate, acetaldehyde, glyoxylate, methacrylate,
ehtylmalonate, mesaconate, malonate, malate, itaconate and iso-
butyrate were identiﬁed (Supplementary Fig. 97−161).
Using intermediates and end-products of the ancient acetyl-CoA
pathway (formate, acetate, and pyruvate) as substrates, all the afore-
mentioned products of hydrothermal NaHCO
3 reduction were repro-
duced, along with additional compounds β-methylmalate and
citraconate at 70 °C over 12 h during pyruvate conversion (Supple-
mentary Figs. 162−207). This supports the central role of acetyl-CoA in
protometabolic carbon ﬁxation. A total of 32 products were synthe-
sized from NaHCO
3, encompassing 7 out of 11 products from the rTCA
cycle, 9 out of 13 products from the 3HP-4HB pathway, 9 out of 11
products from the DC-4HB pathway, 6 out of the 9 intermediates of the
glyoxylate cycle and all intermediates of the EMCP. The isotopic
labeling studies using NaH
13CO3, combined with GC–MS analysis vali-
dated by authentic standards and spiking experiments, conﬁrmed that
all intermediates originated from a single carbon source (Supple-
mentary Fig. 208−244). Based on the product analysis, a fundamental
chemical autocatalytic network for carbon ﬁxation was constructed
(Fig. 1), enabling a one-step conversion from CO 2/NaHCO3 to multi-
carbon carboxylic acids, speciﬁcally all ﬁve universal metabolic pre-
cursors (Table1 and Supplementary Table 1). This observation of direct
nonenzymatic CO2 conversion surpasses previous attempts to con-
struct rTCA chemical reaction networks, which required both active
pyruvate and glyoxylate as substrates
16.
The variation of Gibbs free energy ( ΔG) of the reactions under
saturated vapor pressure at different temperatures was calculated
based on the existing database (Supplementary Fig. 245; For detailed
datasets, see Supplementary Table 2). Most reactions are thermo-
dynamically favorable (ΔG < 0) under speciﬁc temperature conditions.
For reactions generating malate, fumarate, succinate,α-ketoglutarate,
oxalate, methylmalonate, and hydroxyisobutyrate ( ΔG >0 ) , h i g h e r
pressure from hydrogen replenishment and the consumption of these
organics within reaction networks drive these reactions away from
equilibrium.

Detailed mineral phase structure understanding
To elucidate the origin of the outstanding performance of CO2RN, the
atomic and electronic structure of 1Tʹ-MoS2 was investigated. The 1Tʹ-
MoS2 was synthesized hydrothermally, as the geological conditions,
such as high temperature and pressure or strong stress, can induce the
structural transformation from 2H to 1T phase
30. The XRD analysis
revealed a peak corresponding to the typical 002 plane of the 1T phase,
with a d-spacing of 9.4 Å (Supplementary Fig. 256, Supplementary
Table 4)
31. High-resolution transmission electron microscopy (HRTEM)
analysis conﬁrmed the interlayer spacing and lattice fringes, showing
structures with d-spacings of 0.64 nm (attributed to 2H phase) and
0.94 nm (attributed to 1 T phase) in the 1T ′-MoS2 sample (Supple-
mentary Fig. 257)32. The enlarged HRTEM image clearly displayed the
triangular atomic arrangement of the 1T phase, corroborating the XRD
analysis
33. The 1T phase in 1T ʹ-MoS2 was also con ﬁrmed by photo-
luminescence (PL), ultraviolet–visible (UV−Vis), and Raman spectro-

organics dominated by formate, and the selectivity of C 2+ products
decreased by 8.1-fold (Fig. 4a). To elucidate the mechanism behind
phase-induced CO2RN, diffuse reﬂectance infrared Fourier transform
spectroscopy (DRIFTS) was employed to detect key reaction inter-
mediates (Fig. 4b, c and Supplementary Figs. 264 −265). The C –H
stretching region (3200–2850 cm

characteristics between 1T′-a n d2 H - M o S2 (Fig. 4b). The 1T phase dis-

zation of CH x intermediates, facilitating the formation of long-chain
organics41. In contrast, no peaks corresponding to adsorbed CHx spe-
cies were detected in 2H-MoS2, which aligns with the low selectivity of
C2+ products.

hydrogenation, was observed in the FTIR spectra of both 1T′-a n d2 H -

Synthesis of metallic 1T′-MoS2
1T′-MoS2 in this work was synthesized using a hydrothermal method
similar to previously described procedures 37,49. In brief, 6 mmol of
sodium molybdate and 6 mmol of L-cysteine were dissolved in 60 mL
of ultrapure water (18.25 MΩ, Sichuan UPU Ultrapure Technology Co.
Ltd). The mixture was stirred magnetically for 10 min and then trans-
ferred to a Teﬂon-lined 100 mL stainless steel autoclave reactor (Anhui
Kemi Instrument Co., Ltd). The hydrothermal treatment was con-
ducted at 200 °C for 20 h, after which the reactor was allowed to cool
naturally to room temperature. The resulting black precipitates were
collected by ﬁltration, thoroughly washed three times with ultrapure
water and ethanol, and then lyophilized. Finally, the black solid was
freeze-dried for 12 h.

stirred at a speed of 800 rpm. After the reaction was completed, the
gas, liquid, and solid products were cooled to room temperature by air

Computational details
Spin-polarized electronic structure calculations were performed using
the plane-wave basis set approach as implemented in the Vienna ab
initio simulation package (VASP 5.4.4)
56. The projector augmented
wave (PAW) method was used to represent the ion −core electron
interactions57. The valence electrons were represented with a plane
wave basis set with an energy cutoff of 450 eV. Electronic exchange
and correlation were described with the Perdew −Burke−Ernzerhof
(PBE) functional
58. The DFT-D3 method was used to treat the van der
Waals interaction59. 15 Å vacuum space was included to avoid interac-
tions between surface slabs. A 3 × 3 × 1 Monkhorst −Pack scheme was
used to generate the k-point grid for the modeled surfaces 60.T h e
convergence criteria for the self-consistent electronic structure and
geometry were set to 10
−5 eV and 0.05 eV/Å, respectively. Zero-point
vibrational energy (ZPVE) corrections are calculated by assuming a
quantum harmonic oscillator possessing the calculated vibrational
frequency. Transition state calculations were performed using the
climbing image nudged elastic band (CI-NEB) method, with the tran-
sition state identi ﬁed to have an absolute tangent force below
0.05 eV/Å
