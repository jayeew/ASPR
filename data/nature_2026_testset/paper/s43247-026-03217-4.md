Palaeoceanographic records spanning the Oligocene provide insights into Antarctic ice-sheet
dynamics in a world warmer-than-today, allowing us to improve future climate projections linked to
global warming. Here, we present a high-resolution multi-proxy record from ODP Site 689 (Maud Rise,
Southern Ocean), to investigate the short-term Antarctic ice-sheet variability during the late Oligocene.
Our oxygen isotope composition of sea water record re ﬂects large, obliquity-paced ice volume
ﬂuctuations from 26.2 to 25.2 Ma. These ﬂuctuations would have resulted in changes in the types of
rocks eroded as the Antarctic ice-sheet advanced/retreated. Indeed, the radiogenic isotopic
compositions of neodymium and lead co-vary with the oxygen isotopes for most of the record and
identify changes in sediment provenance and weathering rates linked to advances and retreats of the
Antarctic ice-sheet. At the same time, the stable incongruent lead isotope signal observed con ﬁrms
the presence of a major East Antarctic ice-sheet during the late Oligocene.
Anthropogenic forcings of Northern and Southern Hemisphere ice-sheet
decline represent a major concern for the overall stability of Earth ’s
climate1–4. It is thus essential to gain a deeper understanding of the short-
term dynamics of the Antarctic ice-sheet (AIS) and its relation to climate
change. The establishment of a permanent AIS
5 and its major expansion6
during the Oligocene (33.9–23.3 Ma)7 make this phase of the Cenozoic a key
period to study ice-sheet dynamics. Opening of gateways in the Southern
Ocean led to the establishment of the Antarctic Circumpolar Current
(ACC)
8,9, for a long time inferred to provide a barrier for warm surface
waters to reach the Antarctic continent, resulting in its thermal isolation and
ice-sheet growth10. This interpretation has been challenged by others 11.
Although the Oligocene continental conﬁguration (Fig.1) and consequently
the ocean circulation were somewhat different from today, atmospheric
CO2 concentrations were rather similar to the ones projected for the end of
this century (> 500 ppm)12–14. Even though the available Oligocene atmo-
spheric CO2 estimates are quite variable14, a recent assessment presented
concentrations of ~500–570 ppm during the late Oligocene13, which could
potentially serve as an analogue for modern climate warming, representing a
world similarly warm to the near future. Additionally, the Oligocene ice-
sheet dynamics can provide an ana logy for a future world with a much
reduced (or non-existent) Northern Hemisphere ice-sheet
15.W h i l et h e
long-term evolution of the Oligocene AIS is relatively well constrained16–19,
its short-term dynamics (i.e., orbital to suborbital time scales) and their
underlying forcing factors are still poorly understood due to the scarcity of
high-resolution records.
It is expected that AIS variability has been strongly in ﬂuenced by
orbital forcing, with glacial/interglacial cycles paced by variations in inso-
lation related to changes of the Earth ’s eccentricity, obliquity and
precession
16,20. The AIS has been found to be sensitive to orbital variations
over time21, but existing studies have presented conﬂicting results regarding
the pacing of the AIS dynamics across the Oligocene. Ice sheet-proximal
geological records indicate high-frequency obliquity-paced (~40 kyr) AIS
ﬂuctuations
22–24; while ice sheet-distal benthicforaminiferal oxygen isotopes
(δ18OBF) records are interpreted to re ﬂect AIS oscillations responding to
~1.2 Myr obliquity cycles25–28 as well as to ~400 and 100 kyr eccentricity
cycles16,28,29. More recently, Brzelinski et al. 30 found large oxygen isotope
composition of sea water (δ18OSW) ﬂuctuations at IODP Site U1406 (North
Atlantic; Fig.1) that suggest 40 kyr obliquity forcing of the AIS.
In addition to the dynamics, the size of the Oligocene AIS is also a
matter of debate30. Using a δ18OBF record from Ocean Drilling Programme
(ODP) Site 1264 (South Atlantic, Fig. 1), Liebrand et al.31. indicated large
amplitude changes of the AIS between its modern size and an ice-free
Antarctic continent between 27 and 24 Ma (Supplementary Fig. 1). In
contrast, the δ
18OBF record of ODP Site 690 (Maud Rise, Southern Ocean;
Fig. 1) has been used to suggest the existence of a glaciated Antarctic con-
tinent between 27.8 and 24.5 Ma, withice volumes comparable to or even
larger than the modern ones (Supplementary Fig. 1)32. These inconsistent ice
volume reconstructions, as well as model results 33, reported for the late
Oligocene have led to con ﬂicting interpretations regarding the size and
stability of the AIS during this period.

The differences in size and dynamics of the late Oligocene AIS found in
the literature may partly originate from inherent uncertainties of the
methods used. As mentioned above, most reconstructions of the Oligocene
glaciation history have been based on δ
18OBF records alone, often con-
sidering bottom-water temperature (BWT) to be constant through time.
However,δ18OBF is a function of both BWT andδ18OSW, which itself mainly
reﬂects changes in ice volume and, to a lesser extent, in salinity of bottom
waters34,35. Brzelinski et al.30. reconstructed changes inδ18OSW by combining
the analyses ofδ18OBF with Mg/Ca to obtain a BWT signal. Their results at
Site U1406 feature large δ18OSW changes between 25.94 and 25.78 Ma

are expected to have resulted in a wide variety of rocks being eroded as the
ice-sheet expanded and retreated. Such processes can be tracked using
radiogenic neodymium (Nd) and lead (Pb) isotope compositions, which
vary in adjacent marine sediments as a function of the crustal age and
mineralogy of the weathered rocks
36,37. The marine isotopic composition of
Nd (expressed as εNd = ((143Nd/144Nd)meas/(143Nd/144Nd)CHUR −1) × 104)38
varies according to the mineralogy (Sm/Nd ratio) and the age of the source
rocks39.T h ed e t r i t a lεNd signature of a given marine sediment can thus
inform about its provenance, which has successfully been used to investigate
changes of weathering products introduced by the advances and retreats of
the AIS40–42. The three radiogenic isotopes of Pb (206Pb, 207Pb and 208Pb) are
the decay products of 238U, 235Ua n d 232Th respectively, and are typically
reported as ratios to non-radiogenic 204Pb. Dissolved seawater Pb has a
residence time much shorter (~48 years)43 than the global ocean mixing time
(~1500 years)44, implying that weathering inputs are the main control of its
dissolved or colloidal seawater distribution, rather than inter-basin ocean
circulation45. Authigenic (i.e., seawater-derived) Pb isotopes can be extrac-
ted from marine sediments 46–48 and in turn be used to reconstruct past
variations in weathering inputs and intensity, which are directly linked to
the climate regime (glacial vs interglacial conditions)
49–51. The combination
of detrital εNd and authigenic Pb isotope ratios can thus provide valuable
insights into both the sediment provenance and the weathering intensity on
land related to the advance and retreat of the AIS under different climate
regimes.
Following this approach, the objectives of this study are to (i) recon-
struct changes in AIS volumes using the method of Brzelinski et al.
30 over a
one-million year time interval of the late Oligocene (26.2–25.2 Ma) at ODP
Site 689 (Maud Rise, Southern Ocean; Fig.1) using well-preserved benthic
foraminifera (Supplementary Fig. 2) allowing for the generation of reliable
geochemical records; and (ii) to track regional Southern Ocean circulation
and weathering intensity to obtain new information about the dynamics of
the AIS.
Results and discussion
Oxygen isotopes and BWT
The δ18OBF values measured at ODP Site 689 during the late Oligocene
(~26.2 to 25.2 Ma) range between 2.5 and 3.1 ‰ , showing alteration
between phases of heavier values (~2.9 –3.1 ‰ ) and lighter values
(~2.5–2.7 ‰ ), onto which smaller-scale ﬂuctuations are superimposed
(≤0.3‰ ;F i g . 2A). These δ18OBF ﬂuctuations are consistent with the
δ18OBF reconstruction of Site 69032 which presents values between 2.3 and
3.3‰ over our studied interval (Supplementary Fig. 1). However, these
values are clearly offset compared with the ones of Site 1264 31 (ranging
between 1.4 and 2.2 ‰ ), likely due to differences in BWT at Site 1264
(South Atlantic) compared to Sites 689 and 690 (Maud Rise) (Supple-
mentary Fig. 1).
The Mg/Ca-based BWTs at Site 689 range between 3 and 5 (± 0.53)°C
(Fig. 2B), which is slightly cooler than other Oligocene BWT records from
the North Atlantic

the analysed benthic foraminifera are well preserved (Supplementary

cyclic variations related to obliquity (Supplementary Fig. 3). These varia-

Radiogenic isotopes
The detrital εNd signatures, a proxy of continental erosion and sediment
transport, display values ranging from −8.8 to −12.0 at ODP Site 689
(Fig. 3D). The εNd record exhibits an overall constant signal between ~−9
and −10 over the studied interval, only interrupted by pronounced shorter-
term unradiogenic (i.e., more negative) excursions at around 26.0, 25.9, 25.6
and 25.5 Ma. The authigenic
206Pb/204Pb, 207Pb/204Pb and 208Pb/204Pb com-
positions exhibit the same cyclicity, with markedly more radiogenic (higher)
values during two intervals between ~26.0 and 25.9 and ~25.6 and 25.5 Ma
(Fig. 3E–G).

Implications of the authigenic Pb isotope compositions
The few existing modern sedimentary authigenic Pb isotope observations
around Antarctica66 (Supplementary Data 2) display increasing (more
radiogenic) values from the West to the East (Fig.5B). Similar to the detrital
εNd, the authigenic Pb values display an‘open-ocean baseline’signal for the
entire record (e.g., 206Pb/204Pb ~ 19.10) with more radiogenic values (e.g.,
206Pb/204Pb ~ 19.15) at ~ 26.0 and 25.5 –25.6 Ma characterising a higher
contribution from East Antarctic sources (Fig.3E–G). Together with the co-
variation in εNd and δ18OSW these results allow our interpretation that
advances and retreats of the AIS left imprints in the weathering signal
recorded by both radiogenic isotopic systems.
Sedimentary authigenic Pb isotope compositions have mostly been
used to investigate past weathering inputs and weathering regimes in the
North Atlantic
50,73,78,79, but have also been applied in the Southern Ocean66,80.
The main issue with using authigenic Pb as a palaeoceanographic proxy is
the poorly-constrained inﬂuence of the degree of incongruent chemical
weathering on the oceanic Pb isotope signatures during warm and cold
climate stages. The term‘incongruent’describes the preferential dissolution
or retention of different mineral pha ses in rocks during early chemical
weathering of freshly physically eroded material
49,81. An incongruent
weathering signal will thus be characterised by Pb isotope compositions
different from those of the source-rock. For instance, incongruent weath-
ering of accessory phases rich in uranium will preferentially release a Pb
isotope signal more radiogenic than the bulk rock signature. Since accessory
thorium-rich mineral phases— such as monazite — are not weathered as
easily as other accessory minerals, continental runoff in such settings may be
depleted in
208Pb, while 206Pb is enriched 82,83. Around Antarctica, intense
physical weathering regimes prevail84 and the resulting weathering products
that are subsequently chemically weathered will primarily be accessory
minerals that easily dissolve during i ncipient chemical weathering. This
results in an incongruent signature of the weathering solutions since only a
miniscule fraction of the physically eroded rock debris is also dissolved
(chemically released to seawater), documented here by the substantial offset
between the detrital and authigenic Pb signatures observed for our entire
record (Fig.6 and Supplementary Fig. 5). The close co-variation between the
authigenic Pb isotope and the detrital εNd records (Fig. 4A) suggests that
this incongruent signal was stable th roughout the late Oligocene. In this
context, our authigenic and detrital Pb isotope records provide striking
evidence for the continued presence of an expanded East AIS in the vicinity
of Site 689 that continuously supplieda weathered Pb isotope signature to
the core site that can only be generated during intense subglacial physical
erosion underneath an ice sheet.

Conclusions
This study documents the existence of a highly dynamic AIS during the late
Oligocene (26.2–25.2 Ma), based on the combinedδ
18OSW,d e t r i t a lεNd and
authigenic Pb isotope records of ODP Site 689. The amplitude of variations
in our reconstructed δ
18OSW is comparable to another late-Oligocene
record30 but also to much younger records from the Pliocene and
Pleistocene54,55. Our results illustrate obliquity-paced variations in ice
volume, with an AIS close to its modern size during glaciated intervals of the
late Oligocene and clearly smaller i ce volumes during the corresponding
interglacial periods. TheεNd and Pb isotopes records reﬂect the same gla-
cial/interglacial cyclicity and co-vary withδ18OSW for most of the record,
conﬁrming (i) an obliquity-paced waning and waxing of the AIS and/or (ii)
associated SO regional circulation dynamics, resulting in climatically-driven
changes in the provenance of weathering products. We infer that theεNd
and Pb isotopic records were dominated by an ‘open-ocean’signal during
warmer climates of the Oligocene and bymore local weathering signatures
from East Antarctica during colder climates. Furthermore, the authigenic
and detrital Pb isotopes signatures were clearly decoupled during our stu-
died time interval and systematica lly more radiogenic than across the
EOT
80. Our Pb isotope data provide striking evidence for the presence of a
major East AIS during the entire late Oligocene, as indicated by the sus-
tained incongruent Pb isotope signal supplied to Site 689.
