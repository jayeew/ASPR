Lowering the overpotential of oxygen evolution reaction with electrocatalysts
is essential for efﬁcient renewable-electricity-driven electrolysis. Active noble-
metal catalysts suffer from leaching and scarcity, while non-noble alternatives
face limited intrinsic activity. Here we combine computational guidance with
experimental validation to identify atomically dispersed tungsten within NiFe
oxyhydroxide, namely W1-NiFeOOH, as a promising noble-metal-free oxygen
evolution reaction catalyst. An equivariant transformer-based machine-learn-
ing interatomic potential accelerates out-of-domain adsorption energy pre-
dictions and nominates W1-NiFeOOH from 3,976 single-atom-incorporated
metal oxyhydroxide conﬁgurations. Cyclic-electrodeposited W1-NiFeOOH
achieves a high current density of 13.1 A cm-2 at 2.0 V and remains stable for
500 hours in alkaline exchange-membrane water electrolysis with commercial
membranes. In situ spectroscopy and density functional theory calculations
suggest that subsurface W promoter induces synergistic electron redistribu-
tion at neighboring Ni-O-Fe edge active sites, thereby lowering the proton-
coupled electron-transfer barrier for the deprotonation step and facilitating
transformation into the activeγ-phase. This integrated computational-
experimental workﬂow provides a blueprint for cost-effective catalyst design
for sustainable energy systems.

Iridium-based noble metal oxides are widely regarded as bench-
mark catalysts for OER due to their catalytic activity 6. However, their
scarcity and high expense limit large-scale applications, driving
extensive research into cost-effective alternatives7,8. Discovering non-
noble metal catalysts might cost-effectively replace the demands on
the precious metal, which require hazardous environmental impact
during the mining process.
Among earth-abundant compounds, metal (oxy)hydroxides con-
sisting of 3 d transition metals, particularly Ni and Co, have shown
considerable promise in alkaline OER environments
9,10. Multi-metallic
(oxy)hydroxides, formed by incorporating additional elements, can
tailor the electronic structure and the adsorption energies of reaction
intermediates, enhancing catalytic performance
11,12. For instance,
doping Fe into Ni (oxy)hydroxides yields reactive Ni–Fe layered double
hydroxides13,14. Introducing other 3d transition metals11,15,16, high-valent
4d and 5 d transition metals 17–19 or nonmetal elements 20 has also
effectively tailored the catalytic performance.
Recently, heterogeneous single-atom catalysts (SACs) have revo-
lutionized catalyst design by featuring atomically dispersed metal
atoms on supports, maximizing metal utilization and unlocking
unconventional electronic structures
21,22. The absence of metal-metal
bonds signiﬁcantly inﬂuences the electrochemical microenvironment
through strong interactions between single atoms and support
materials
23,24. Integrating SACs with metal (oxy)hydroxides creates a
synergistic next-generation catalyst platform with the potential to
surpass conventional catalytic performance limits.
Compared to the experimental approach, density function theory
(DFT) calculations have been regarded as ef ﬁcient for assessing can-
didates by acquiring fundamental material properties through elec-
tronic structure calculations
25. However, the extensive combinatorial
space and structural complexity of SACs integrated with metal (oxy)
hydroxides render conventional DFT-based approaches impractical
due to high computational costs. To address these limitations,
machine learning (ML) techniques offer a promising strategy by pre-
dicting material properties directly from atomic conﬁgurations, thus
circumventing computationally intensive processes
26,27.A m o n g
advanced ML models, EquiformerV228, a state-of-the-art graph neural
network with an equivariant transformer model, stands out for main-
taining rotational and translational invariance, enabling accurate and
rapid predictions. Accompanied by recent advances in graphics pro-
cessing unit (GPU) technology for parallel computing, ML models
reduce the time and computational resources requirement, allowing
efﬁcient high-throughput screening of complex catalyst
candidates
29–33.
In this study, we employed a ML-guided screening strategy using
the pretrained EquiformerV2 model to identify NiFe oxyhydroxides
with atomically dispersed W atoms (W 1–NiFeOOH) as the most pro-
mising noble metal-free OER catalyst candidates. The ML-guided cat-
alyst candidates were experimentally prepared via scalable cyclic
electrodeposition, resulting in a substitutional octahedral W atomic
local structure. Electrocatalytic assessments showed that noble metal-
free W
1–NiFeOOH exhibited signiﬁcant OER performance, delivering a
high current density of 13.1 A cm −2 a t 2 . 0V ( 8 0° C ) i n A E M W E

noble metal-free anode catalysts under practical water electrolysis
conditions. In situ spectroscopies and computational calculations
provide insights into the distinct catalytic mechanisms and active
motifs. Anchored Ir atoms directly act as active sites, while substituted
W atoms function as modulators, promoting the original active sites in
the NiFeOOH edge plane. This modulation stabilizes the deprotona-
tion at the active site to lower the energy barrier and promotes the
structural transformation to the active γ-phase at lower potentials.
These combinations of computational predictions and experimental
validation accelerate the discovery of competitive noble-metal-free
catalytic solutions towards sustainable energy conversion with the
renewable electricity.
Results
Machine learning-guided catalyst screening
To accelerate the discovery of efﬁcient noble-metal-free OER catalysts,
we employed a ML framework, EquiformerV2 28, a large-parameter
equivariant graph neural network pretrained on the OC22 dataset. This
ML-guided approach predicts total energies directly from atomic
structures without solving computationally intensive Kohn –Sham
equations, enabling rapid evaluation across large materials candidate
spaces (Fig. 1a).
We constructed single-atom incorporated metal oxyhydroxide
slab models (Supplementary Data 1) within an established K
+-inter-
calated γ-phase oxyhydroxide matrix34, creating a combinatorial can-
didate space from 8 different metal oxyhydroxides (MAMBOOH) with
41 elements of single-atom (M 1) and 13 types of possible adsorption
sites on the edge (01 –12) and basal (0001) plane, resulting in 3976
conﬁgurational candidate models (M 1–MAMBOOH) (Supplementary
Fig. 1). OER intermediates (*V, *OH, *O, and *OOH) were considered for
each conﬁguration, resulting in over 15,000 adsorbed structures and
these geometries represent catalytically relevant systems not included
in the original OC22 training domain. To validate model performance
on this out-of-domain system class, we generated a custom DFT
dataset by selecting structures corresponding to four representative
active motifs illustrated in Fig. 1b, derived from unary NiOOH and
binary NiFeOOH systems, which were subsequently used for DFT
validation and mechanistic investigation (Supplementary Fig. 2).
We benchmarked several state-of-the-art GNN models, including
GemNet, PaiNN, and DimeNet++ on our custom DFT-relaxed dataset.
By leveraging rapid GPU-accelerated predictions, EquiformerV2
exhibited the highest accuracy in adsorption energy prediction while
achieving an approximately three-order-of-magnitude faster adsorp-
tion energy evaluation than conventional DFT calculations (Supple-
mentary Fig. 3). Based on these results, EquiformerV2 was selected as
the backbone model for further screening. To further verify the model
ﬁdelity and enable domain-speciﬁc transferability, we ﬁne-tuned the
backbone architecture using a curated subset of the M
1–NiNiOOH
dataset. This approach signi ﬁcantly reduced total energy prediction
error MAE less than 0.01 eV atom−1 (Supplementary Figs. 5 and 6) and
enhanced accuracy in estimating adsorption energy differences (Sup-
plementary Fig. 7). Notably, theﬁne-tuned model achieved adsorption
energy MAEs below 0.2 eV for unseen binary M
1–NiFeOOH systems
(Supplementary Fig. 8), validating its transferability across composi-
tional domains.
High-throughput screening of all M
1–MAMBOOH candidates was
performed using the original pretrained EquiformerV2 backbone

OER intermediate-adsorbed structures via iterative relaxation.

Discussion
We ef ﬁciently identiﬁed W 1–NiFeOOH as the most promising noble
metal-free OER catalyst candidate by accelerated ML-guided catalyst
screening and experimental validation of its electrocatalytic perfor-
mance with unambiguous catalytic mechanisms. Accelerated adsorp-
tion energy evaluations using the pretrained EquiformerV2 model
pinpointed W
1–NiFeOOH as a promising noble metal-free candidate.
The optimized electrodeposition method enabled the scalable and
rapid growth of W 1–NiFeOOH nanosheets with a consistent high-
loading of atomically dispersed W atoms and validated with compe-
titive OER performance among non-noble metal catalysts.
W
1–NiFeOOH achieved a high current density of 13.1 A cm −2 at 2.0 V
(80 °C) in AEMWE using commercially available membranes, with
robust operational stability exceeding 500 h. These results establish
W
1–NiFeOOH as a state-of-the-art non-noble metal anode catalyst for
scalable green hydrogen production. The role of distinct active motifs
on catalytic mechanisms was revealed through in situ XAS and Raman
spectroscopy, supported by DFT calculations, that substitutional W
atoms promote the original edge active sites of NiFe oxyhydroxide by
facilitating deprotonation at the original NiFeOOH edge active sites
and the active γ-phase transformation at lower potentials, unlike the
direct participation of anchored Ir single atoms in Ir
1–NiFeOOH. This
study bridges computational predictions with experimental validation
to accelerate the discovery of highly active non-precious metal cata-
lysts and pinpoint the underlying mechanism, paving the way for
designing competitive catalytic solutions for sustainable global energy
conversion.

The spin-polarized DFT calculations were performed with the pro-

exchange correlation functional 53, as implemented in the Vienna ab
initio simulation package 54. To correct for self-interaction errors of
d-electrons, we employed the DFT + U method. The effective Hub-
bard U values (Ueff) were taken as 5.20 and 2.56 eV for Ni and Fe from
the previous literatures 10,34, and 6.20 eV for W from the Materials
Project database 55. A functional of DFT-D3 method of Grimme with
zero-damping function was applied to consider van der Waals

for the plane-wave expansion and Monkhorst-Pack (1 × 6 × 4) k-

large vacuum layers were set at least 20 Å in the direction perpen-
