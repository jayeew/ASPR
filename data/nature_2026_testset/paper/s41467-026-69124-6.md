Although cobalt spinel oxide-based catalysts have demonstrated promising
performance for the oxygen evolution reaction in acid, strategies reported
have largely focused on manipulating the binding energies of reaction inter-
mediates through tailoring the inherent electronic structures. The role of
interfacial water structures on the electrode surface in promoting the acidic
oxygen evolution reaction performance through controlled water activation
has been largely overlooked. Here we show that introducing chromium dopant
into cobalt spinel oxide can simultaneously increase the activity and stability
by promoting interfacial water dissociation and dynamic electron transfer. The
chromium dopant can not only dynamically regulate the electronic structure
of cobalt and mitigate the over-oxidation of active cobalt sites at high over-
potential, but also enhance the coverage of hydroxyl species on the catalyst
surface, thereby optimizing the interfacial water structure, accelerating the
reactant free-water supply and promoting subsequent interfacial water dis-
sociation. Consequently, the obtained catalyst demonstrates enhanced per-
formance in both solution electrocatalysis and device electrocatalysis. This
work provides a comprehensive understanding of enhancing the acidic oxy-
gen evolution reaction performanceby promoting interfacial water
dissociation.

Discussion
In summary, we prepared a Cr-Co3O4 electrocatalyst with a Crtet-O-Co
local environment, which demonstrates an overpotential of 358 mV at
10 mA cm−2 and long-term stability for 160 h. Moreover, a PEMWE
device with Cr-Co 3O4 as the anode catalyst can operate steadily for
over 750 h at 1 A cm −2 and 60 °C. Operando electrochemical and
spectroscopic studies reveal that the Crtet-O-Co local structure dyna-
mically modulates the electronic state of Co active sites, preventing
their over-oxidation and thereby enhancing both activity and stability.
Furthermore, combiningoperando ATR-SEIRAS, in situ XPS, and DFT
calculations, we show that the regulation of the interfacial water
structure on Cr-Co
3O4 can promote interfacial water dissociation
through decreasing the connectivity of hydrogen-bond water in the
EDL region, thereby leading to reduced energy barrier of RDS and
accelerated the reaction kinetics. This work offers a promising strategy
to design Co
3O4-based electrocatalysts with good electrocatalytic
performance for acidic OER by promoting interfacial water
dissociation.

Synthesis of Cr-Co3O4 and pristine Co3O4
In a typical synthesis, 1.50 mmol Co(NO3)2·6H2O, a certain amount of
Cr(NO3)3·9H2O, and 120.00 mg C 8H6O6 were dissolved in a mixed

3O4. For compar-
ison, pristine Co3O4 was synthesized using a similar protocol without
the addition of Cr(NO3)3·9H2O.

DFT calculation method
All DFT calculations were performed using the CASTEP code within the
Materials Studio package (Accelrys Inc.). The exchange-correlation
interactions were treated with the generalized gradient approximation
(GGA) and the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional. A plane-wave
cutoff energy of 400 eV and an SCF convergence tolerance of
1×1 0
−6 eV were applied63. The (311) surface was selected to model both
Cr-doped and pristine Co3O4. Geometry optimizations employed the
Methfessel-Paxton (MP) smearing scheme with a width of 0.1 eV. The
Brillouin zone was sampled using Monkhorst-Pack k-point meshes,
with a 4 × 4 × 1 grid for all structural relaxations. A 15 Å vacuum layer
was added along the z-direction to eliminate periodic interactions.
During relaxation, atoms in the bottom layer were ﬁxed at their bulk
positions while all other atoms were fully relaxed. The adsorption free
energy ( ΔG) of reaction intermediates was calculated as ΔG = ΔE +
ΔZPE − TΔS,w h e r eΔE is the DFT-computed adsorption energy, and
ΔZPE and TΔS are the zero-point energy and entropy corrections,
respectively.
