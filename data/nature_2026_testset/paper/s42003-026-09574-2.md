Adipose tissue disturbance underlies the pathogenesis of major depressive disorder (MDD); however,
the underlying neural and molecular mechanisms remain elusive. Utilizing a social defeat stress mouse
model of MDD, we identi ﬁed a paraventricular hypothalamic (PVH) control of susceptibility to social
stress via β3 adrenoceptor ( β3AR) signaling in the brown adipose tissue (BAT). Susceptible mice
exhibited a signi ﬁcantly elevated expression of BAT β3AR. Pharmacological activation of β3AR
increased immobility time in the forced swim test in naïve mice, and decreased social interaction time
in mice subjected to subthreshold defeat. Retrograde tracing revealed a polysynaptic neural
connection from the PVH to BAT. Chemogenetic activation of this projection induced depressive-like
behaviors in subthreshold defeat mice and dysregulated their expression of β3AR downstream
molecules, interleukin-6. Together, these results established a functional role for BAT β3AR in
regulating susceptibility to social stress, which was modulated by neural inputs originating in the PVH.
Major depressive disorder (MDD) is a severe psychiatric disorder that
affects approximately 320 million individuals worldwide and has
emerged as the leading cause of disability globally
1–3. Despite advance-
ments in current therapeutic approaches, one-third to half of individuals
with MDD exhibit inadequate response to existing treatments
4,5. Eluci-
dating the underlying mechanisms is imperative for a comprehensive
understanding of its physiopathology and the development of future
therapeutic strategies. Recently, increasing evidence has demonstrated
that peripheral non-neural organs/tissues anatomically and functionally
connected to the central nervous system to govern the MDD patho-
physiology in laboratory animals, including the liver, skeletal muscles,
adipose and myeloid cells, et al.
6–10. For example, we recently reported a
liver activated intrinsic metabolic pathway mediated by soluble epoxide
hydrolase in regulating depressive-like symptoms in a well-established
chronic mild stress model of depression
7. The alterations in kynurenine
metabolism within the gut or skeletal muscles contribute to the mani-
festation of depressive- or anti-depressive-like behaviors
6,11. The evi-
dence above paved an avenue for future preclinical investigation and
therapeutic strategies development.
The sympathetic nervous system (SNS) plays an essential role in
connecting the central nervous sys tem and the peripheral non-neural
organs/tissues for neuroimmune regulation and in regulating psychiatric
symptoms
12–14. Clinical studies have demonstrated heightened sympathetic
outﬂows in MDD patients and high-risk youths for depression 14–16.T h e
brown adipose tissue (BAT), an important SNS downstream target, has also
been implicated in mediating the lipolytic and thermogenic responses to
environmental stressors
17,18, for example, theﬁght orﬂight response which is
essential for the survival of species19. Preclinical and clinical investigations
consistently support the contribution of BAT dysfunction to psychiatric
disorders. Mice with impaired BA T function, such as those lacking
uncoupling protein 1 or upstream stimulatory factor 1, exhibit anxiety-like
behaviors20,21. Individuals with BAT dysfunction display an exacerbated
development of psychiatric disorders and higher risk for depression and
suicide22,23. Collectively, theseﬁndings suggest that the activity of BAT is a
contributor to the pathogenesis of MDD. However, the molecular
mechanisms underlying the functional role of BAT in mediating MDD
symptoms and how these mechanisms are controlled, especially at neural
circuitry level, remain largely unknown.

Sympathetic outﬂow to BAT induces the release of noradrenaline,
which subsequently activates adrenoceptors. Among these receptors, β3
adrenoceptor (β3AR) is the most abundantly expressed one in BAT com-
pared to other tissues24.F u n c t i o n a l l y ,t h eβ3AR signaling pathway promotes
thermogenesis and lipogenesis in response to cold or dietary stress 25,26.
Moreover, previous study suggests thatβ3AR activation in BAT contributes
to elevated levels of inﬂammatory factors induced by stress19.T h e s eﬁndings
suggest that β3AR plays a crucial role in regulating BAT function and
probably mediate the crosstalk between SNS and BAT. However, how BAT
function to mediate depressive-like symptoms, and especially, how related
maladaptations in the BAT is controlled by the SNS at neural circuitry level
remain unknown.
Here, utilizing the well-established chronic social defeat stress (CSDS)
mouse model of depression
27,28, in conjunction with neuronal retrograde
tracing, pharmacological interventions, and chemogenetic approaches, we
established an anatomical and func tional connection between the para-
ventricular hypothalamus (PVH) and the BAT in mediating the develop-
ment of susceptibility to chronic social stress by targeting localβ3AR.
Materials and methods
Animals
Male C57BL/6J mice (aged 8–12 weeks) were obtained from the laboratory
animal center of Xuzhou Medical University, while retired male CD1 mice
were provided by Charles River Laboratories (Beijing, China). The C57BL/
6J mice were group-housed with a maximum occupancy of ﬁve mice per
cage, whereas the CD1 mice were individually housed with ad libitum access
to food and water. All animals were maintained under controlled conditions
including a 12-h light/dark cycle (lights on from 7:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m.),
temperature set at 24 ± 2 °C, and humidity maintained at 50 ± 10%. All
experimental procedures adhered to theRegulations for the Administration
of Affairs Concerning Experimental Animals (1988) and received approval
from Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee and the Of ﬁce of
Laboratory Animal Resources of Xuzhou Medical University. We have
complied with all relevant ethical regulations for animal use.
Social defeat stress models
Chronic social defeat (CSDS) model was conducted according to the
standard protocols29. In brief, aggressive male CD1 mice were housed in
the defeat cage and allowed to free moving for 24 h prior to the ﬁrst
defeat. An experimental mouse was intruded into the defeat cage of an
unfamiliar CD1 aggressor every day and subjected to a 5 –10-min defeat.
Following the defeat, the experimental mouse and CD1 were separated by
a perforated translucent plastic divider, allowing a continuous social
sensory exposure for 24 h. For each subsequent daily defeat of 5–10 min,
expose the intruder C57BL/6J to a novel resident ’s home cage com-
partment. After this procedure, the experimental mice were individually
housed for 24 h before undergoing social interaction tests to assess social
avoidance behaviors.
Subthreshold defeat (SubD) model was conducted as previously
described
30. In brief, the experimental mouse was defeated by an unfamiliar
CD1 for 2–5 min followed by a 24-h social sensory stress. After 3 days of
repeated defeats, the experimental mouse was transferred to a new cage and
underwent social interaction testing after another 24-h of individual
housing.
For the acute social defeat (SD) p rocedure, experimental mice were
defeated by CD1 mice for 5 min. Then they were separated from their
aggressors using a perforated translucent plastic divider that allowed con-
tinuous social sensory exposure fo r 10 min. Following this period, the
experimental mice were returned to their home cages.
In pharmacological experiments,β3AR agonist (CL316243, Merck&-
Millipore, 5047610001, 1 mg kg
−1, i.p.) injections were administered 1 h
after daily defeats, while β3AR antagonist (SR59230A, TOCRIS, 1511,
5m gk g−1, i.p.) injections occurred 30 min prior to daily defeats. To achieve
chemogenetic activation of PVH, virus injection into PVH was performed
and allowed to infect for 3 weeks. Clozapine-N-oxide (CNO, MCE,
3423369-7, 2 mg kg−1, i.p.) injections were given 1 h after daily defeats in
order to stimulate neurons in PVH.
Social interaction (SI) test
The SI test was consisted of two phases of 2.5 min. In the ﬁrst phases, an
experimental mouse was put into an open ﬁeld arena, which containing a
small animal cage placed on one side in the absence of the social target (no
target), and allowed to explore freely for 2.5 min. In the second phases, a
novel CD1 mouse was put into the animal cage (target) in the interaction
zone, and the experimental mouse was reintroduced back into the arena.
The locomotion of experimental mice was tracked and recorded to deter-
mine the exploratory behavior using SMART V3.0 software (Panlab, Spain).
The SI ratio was deﬁned as [(interaction time, target) / (interaction time, no
target) × 100]. Mice with SI ratio ﹤100 were considered susceptible of
depression and those with SI ratio≥100 were considered resilient to stress.
Open ﬁeld test (OFT)
In the OFT, mice were placed gently in an open ﬁeld chamber (45 cm ×
45 cm × 45 cm), and allowed to explore this new environment freely for
5 min. The locomotion of experimental mice was tracked and recorded by
SMART V3.0 software; the time spend in center area and the total distance
were analyzed.
Forced swimming test (FST)
The forced swim test (FST) was conducted in accordance with our previous
study
7.B r i eﬂy, mice underwent routine handling for 1 week and were
acclimatized to the experimental environment for at least 1 h prior to testing.
The FST was performed between 1:00 p.m. and 5:00 p.m. Mice were placed
into a transparent glass cylinder measuring 25 cm in height and 15 cm in
diameter, ﬁlled with water up to a depth of 20 cm at a temperature of
24 ± 1 °C. The movements of the mice were tracked for a duration of 6 min,
while the immobility time during the last 4 min was recorded.
Sucrose preference test (SPT)
The mice were individually habituatedto a 1% sucrose solution for 4 days.
Subsequently, the sucrose preference test (SPT) was conducted either 24 h
after the last stressor or 2 h after drug injection. During the SPT, each mouse
was provided with two bottles: one containing water and the other con-
taining a 1% sucrose solution. They were allowed to drink freely for a
duration of 24 h, with the positions ofthe two bottles being switched every
12 h. Data analysis involved quantifying the percentage of sucrose solution
consumed relative to total intake.

Results
Increased BAT β3AR expression in CSDS mice
Noradrenaline transmission has been well-established in the function of
the sympathetic nervous system by acting on the postsynaptic
adrenoceptors
32. In this study, we ﬁrst look at the effect of chronic social
stress on adrenoceptor expression in the BAT in mice subjected to the 10-
day CSDS paradigm. As we previously reported, CSDS-susceptible (SUS)
mice spend less time in the interaction zone when the exist of a CD1
target in social interaction (SI) test (Fig. 1A–C) with signi ﬁcantly
decreased SI ratio, shown increased immobility in the forced swimming
test (FST) and reduced preference in the sucrose preference test (SPT),
with an unchanged locomotion activity in the open ﬁeld test (OFT)
(Supplementary Fig. 1). Genome-wide RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) data
revealed BAT expression of adrenoceptor subtypes including β3AR,
β2AR, β1AR, α1DAR and α1AAR (Fig. 1D). Further immunoblotting
observed an increase in BATβ3AR in CSDS mice, especially in those SUS
mice (Fig. 1E). Consistently, our immuno ﬂuorescent staining and
immunoblotting data demonstrated a signi ﬁcant increase in BAT tyr-
osine hydroxylase (TH) expression, a hallmark for sympathetic nerve
activity, in SUS mice when compared to their controls (CTRL) or the
CSDS-resilient (RES) mice (Fig. 1F, G). Norepinephrine (NE), a down-
stream product of TH, is primary neurotransmitter released by sympa-
thetic nerve ﬁbers and also acts as a marker for sympathetic nerves
31,33.
We employed ELISA to measure NE levels in BAT of mice following
CSDS. The results revealed elevated NE levels in both SUS and RES
animals (Supplementary Fig. 2). These ﬁndings collectively demonstrate
an elevated β3AR protein expression and increased sympathetic nerve

Discussion
In this study, we demonstrated that the SNS-β3AR pathway in BAT plays a
pivotal role in the pathogenesis of depression. We observed elevated levels of
TH and β3AR proteins in BAT of susceptible mice following CSDS. Acti-
vation of β3AR is responsible for increased peripheral IL-6 levels and pro-
motes susceptibility to depressive-like behaviors. Additionally, retrograde
tracing data indicates a neural connection between the PVH and BAT.
Excitation of the PVH-BAT axis enhances SNS- β3AR-IL-6 pathway and
social avoidance behaviors. Collectively, these ﬁndings provide direct evi-
dence for a brain-BAT axis that detects social stress, leading to increased
peripheral IL-6 levels and heightened vulnerability to social stress.
BAT is present in humans of all ages and is especially abundant during
adolescence in the cervical-supraclavicular area
18,40,41. And it is increasingly
clear that BAT dysfunction may have great relevance to energy metabolism-
related disorders human health including obesity42,43. The increased pre-
valence of obesity in major depressed patients indicated a functional role of
BAT in regulating related depressive behaviors. In this study, we carefully
removed the interscapular BAT, the largest BAT in mice
44, to demonstrate a
functional role of BAT in regulating depressive phenotypes at behavioral
and molecular levels.
The function of BAT could be regulated by local molecular targets and
neural inputs from the upstream nervous system
35. Here, we demonstrate
that social stress elevated circulating IL-6 levels is associated with the
secretion of BAT through the activation of β3AR signaling pathway.
Increasing clinical and preclinical evidence has shown that peripheral IL-6
and its related signaling pathways participate in the pathological process of
MDD
45,46. For instance, peripheral IL-6 inﬁltrates into the nucleus accum-
bens of stress-susceptible mice through the blood-brain barrier and mod-
ulates the expression of depression-like behaviors 47.A l t h o u g hp r e v i o u s
studies have indicated enhanced release of IL-6 by cultured leukocytes from
social defeated mice upon lipopolysaccharide stimulation in vitro, it remains
unclear the origin of elevated peripheral IL-6 levels induced by social stress
48.
In this study, we found that BAT a signiﬁcant source of peripheral IL-6, as
BAT ectomy blocks the elevation of peripheral IL-6 levels induced by social
stress. Furthermore, we identiﬁed thatβ3AR signaling pathway serves as the
basis for IL-6 production, providing a novel preclinical target for developing
new antidepressant drugs.
In addition to its expression in BAT, β3AR is also expressed in the
central nervous system (CNS) and plays a critical role in the pathophysio-
logical mechanisms of depression. For instance, in our previous study, we
discovered that local infusion ofα1AR and β3AR agonists into the ventral
tegmental area (VTA) for 10 days com pletely reversed social avoidance
behaviors in susceptible mice
49. Other studies have also highlighted the role
of central β3AR in emotion regulation50. For example, activation of central
β3AR enhances the synthesis of serotonin (5-HT) and tryptophan, as well as
increases norepinephrine release in the rat brain, leading to antidepressant-
and anxiolytic-like effects in rodents
51. In this study, we highlight the critical
role of BAT β3AR in the development of depressive-like behaviors. Speci-
ﬁcally, intraperitonealadministration of theβ3AR agonist CL316243 pro-
moted the development of depressi ve-like behaviors. Different
pharmacological effects of β3 A Ra c t i v a t i o ni nt h eb r a i na n dp e r i p h e r y
indicates potentially different roles of central versus peripheral β3AR in

In this study, we did not observe any behavioral effects when β3AR

induced by the inhibition of both peripheral and central β3AR because
SR59230A could penetrate the BBB55. This evidence further suggested the
complicated role of the norepinephrine-β3AR signaling pathway in med-
iating the neuropathology of MDD. I n this case, future studies should
employ more targeted approaches — such as genetic interventions — to
speciﬁcally inhibit β3AR in BAT, minimizing confounding effects arising
from the interplay between CNS and peripheral signaling. Such strategies
may also offer novel insights into gene-based therapies for MDD.
We also identify a neural input from the PVH as a key brain region
target that regulates depressive-like behaviors by targeting BATβ3AR and
its downstream IL-6 signaling. Besides PVH, retrograde tracing data also
suggest that other brain regions, such as LH, DMH, and PAG, are involved
in neuronal connections with BAT and have been shown to functionally
regulate BAT

are part of have also been implicated in the development of MDD. For
example, a study in rats demonstrated that neurons in the PAG send
excitatory efferent signals for BAT thermogenesis
57.A d d i t i o n a l l y ,a n o t h e r

regarding whether there is a functi onal connection between these brain
regions and BAT driving social stress-induced depression besides PVH.
Exploring these topics could providevaluable insights into understanding
the pathology of MDD from a central-peripheral perspective.
The PVH contains very heterogeneous neuronal subpopulations that
project to multiple brain regions and function differently. Given its diverse
downstream projecting targets, the PVH demonstrates substantial cellular
heterogeneity and contains different subpopulations, for example, magno-
cellular neurons projecting to the posterior pituitary, parvocellular neurons
projecting to the external layer of the median eminence, and the pre-
autonomic neurons project to the brainstem and spinal cord

may therefore affect the activity of these neurons to cause other behavioral
phenotypes which need future investigations.

adipose tissueβ3AR in regulating susceptibility to social stress by targeting
the downstream IL-6 signaling, which was controlled by neural inputs from
the paraventricular hypothalamic nucleus. This study thus provided a novel
