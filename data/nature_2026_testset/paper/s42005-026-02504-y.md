Fusing Majorana zero modes (MZMs) leads to multiple outcomes, a property unique to non-Abelian
anyons. The successful demonstration of this nontrivial fusion rule would be a hallmark for the
development of topological quantum computation. Here, we propose that such fusion can be
achieved by simply attaching a fermionic mode to a single Majorana zero mode. By modulation of the
energy level of the fermionic mode (FM) as well as its coupling with MZM in different sequences, we
show that a zero or integer charge pumping can be realized when different fusion loops are chosen.
Such fusion loops are intimately related with the nontrivial fusion rule of Majorana modes and are solely
determined by the crossings at zero energy in the parameter space. Finally, we demonstrate our
proposal in a nanowire-based topological superconductor coupled to a quantum dot. We show that
charge pumping is robust for MZMs in the realistic system irrespective of the initial condition of
FM state.
The realization of Majorana zero modes (MZMs) remains a long-sought
goal in condensed matter physics. MZM-based qubits are considered a
highly promising platform for fault-tolerant topological quantum com-
putation due to their non-Abelian statistics
1,2. Despite the elegance of this
scheme, ﬁnding the right platform poses a serious challenge. A large
amount of evidence in favor of MZMs has been reported over the last
decade
3–23. However, there is still room for other possible interpretations
in regards to experimental results of various studies, such as the existence
of Andreev bound states (ABSs)
24–33. Consequently, demonstrating non-
Abelian statistics experimentally constitutes a crucial test for resolving
these ambiguities
34–37.
The non-Abelian property reveals itself in two distinct but closely
related aspects. First, in a system of MZMs, their quantum state undergoes a
nontrivial rotation when two of them are exchanged, unlike ABS or other
Abelian anyons where the state only acquires a global phase. More
importantly, two consecutive rotati ons in differing sequences result in
totally differentﬁnal states, hence the name non-Abelian. Such rotations, or
braiding operations, constitute the basic logic gates of topological quantum
computations and have been extensively studied theoretically over the past
decades

in terms of their transport processes. During fusion, charge is transferred
via either electron tunneling (TE) or hole tunneling (TH), but never both
(see Supplementary Note 1), with the dominant process determined by the
total parity of the system. Since the total parity is conserved during the
evolution of E

Discussion
With the assistance of a fermionic mode(FM), we reveal the fusion rules of
Majorana zero modes (MZMs). The nontrivial charge pumping serves as a
diagnostic for the fusion rule in nontrivial fusion loops, rendering our
protocol experimentally observable. The FM can be readily attached either
to the end of a 1D topological superconductor (TS) wire or to a vortex in a
2D TS system. Moreover, by controlling the sequence of fusion and splitting
processes among MZMs, one can design both trivial and nontrivial fusion
loops tailored to the speciﬁc platform, as each process can be individually
adjusted. Therefore, our proposal is applicable to a variety of TS systems
without imposing additional constraints. Recent advances in 2D planar
Josephson-junction (JJ) systems have demonstrated a mini-gate-controlled
fusion protocol that involves initialization, fusion operations, and charge-
sensing readout
50. In that scheme, an FM is coupled to the apex of the
V-shaped junction to detect the fusion outcome. In contrast, our approach
employs the FM to actively assist the fusion process itself. In our proposal,
one only needs to tune the energy level of the FM and its coupling to the
Majorana modes via gate voltages, leaving the parameters of the topological
superconductor unchanged. This may avoid potential complications arising
from meddling with TS wires. Finally, we want to stress that the charge
transfer is distinct for different si tuations, and we can expect that other
realistic factors, such asﬁnite temperature or interactions, only play insig-
niﬁcant roles in our setup. The proposed fusion protocol operates effectively
at temperatures K
BTemp ≪ Δ, where quasiparticle excitations are strongly
suppressed. This condition is readily achievable in current experiments,
with the protocol remainingfunctional at temperaturesTemp ≤ 50 mK. The
required energy resolution and noise tolerance, on the order of a fewμeV,
are well within the capabilities of present-day experimental systems. Thus,
our proposal provides a feasible route for showcasing the nontrivial fusion
rules of MZMs.
