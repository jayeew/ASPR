We reexamined hydrated electron motifs in water cluster anions using photoelectron and electronic
absorption spectroscopies under identical ion source conditions. The structural motifs, previously
characterized as four, are now established as ﬁve in the present work, thereby re ﬁning the framework
of hydrated electron isomers. Time-resolved photoelectron spectroscopy revealed motif-dependent
relaxation dynamics, with certain surface-bound species undergoing more ef ﬁcient excited-state
autodetachment than others. Extending the temporal probing window uncovered long-lived
vibrationally excited ground-state anions ( τ ∼ 340 ps), far exceeding prior expectations, while
increasing cluster size enhanced internal conversion ef ﬁciency. These results closely align with
theoretical predictions. More broadly, they demonstrate that excited-state dynamics are highly
sensitive to local structure, giving rise to distinct relaxation pathways under different hydration
environments. The structure – dynamics relationships established here provide a foundation for
extending such studies to other hydrated species, advancing fundamental insight into electron
solvation and relaxation in aqueous systems.
The gas-phase water cluster anion, (H₂O)(n)⁻, has been both intensively and
extensively investigated for many recent decades as a prototypical model
system for elucidating structure and dynamics of the hydrated electron. The
hydrated electron, a key intermediate in electron transfer processes, plays a
pivotal role in redox chemistry, radiation-induced chemistry, photosynth-
esis, water/air interface, and numerous biological reactions
1–6. Dynamics of
hydrated electrons are actually strongly governed by their diverse structural
motifs in various environments. Thes e include geminate recombination,
formation of contact-pairs, or anionic chemical reactions in the gas phase,
bulk medium, or gas/liquid/solid interfaces. For instance, surface-localized
electrons exhibit rapid relaxation at the water/air interface whereas those
undergo relatively slow geminate recombination in the bulk
6. Furthermore,
as the simplest hydrated electron system, detailed study of the water cluster
anions should provide a solid platform for thorough understanding of the
otherwise ambiguous hydration dynamics of electrons in general.
Early investigations of hydrated electrons primarily sought to connect
gas-phase studies of water cluster anions with bulk-phase behavior, focusing
in particular on determining their vertical binding energies (VBEs). In 1990,
Coe et al. measured photoelectron spectra of size-selected (H ₂O)
(n)⁻ and
reported a linear dependence of VBE onn−1/3 7. By extrapolating these data
to the bulk limit (n → ∞), they estimated VBE of the hydrated electron in
aqueous solution. Subsequent studies by Ayotte et al., employing electronic
absorption spectroscopy, demonstrated that water cluster anions may host
surface-bound electrons as well as internally solvated ones
8. This insight
challenged validity of the extrapolation approach, as it was presumed that
the excess electron in the water cluster anion is internally solvated (fully
encapsulated within a water cavity) as in the bulk phase. As a result, research
efforts have increasingly focused on determining whether speciﬁc( H₂O)
(n)⁻
cluster isomers localize the excess electron internally or at the surface.
In this context, it is noteworthy that Verlet et al9. reported photoelec-
tron spectroscopy of water cluster anions generated under various ion
source conditions, identifying three distinct motifs, designated I, II, and III.
Among these, isomer I exhibited the highest electron binding energy and
was ascribed to the so-called ‘cavity isomer’. This assignment particularly
supports the extrapolation method proposed by Coe et al., which yielded a
VBE value of 3.3 eV for liquid water
7. More recently, direct measurements
using liquid microjet photoelectron spectroscopy have reported the VBE in
bulk water, ranging from 3.3 to 3.7 eV 10–13, consistent with the early

Spectroscopic evidence for Ia1 and Ia2 motifs in water
cluster anions
Absorption spectra of size-selected (H ₂O)(n)⁻ clusters, obtained by mon-
itoring the depletion of the parent anion signal as a function of excitation
energy (Fig.2a), reveal two distinct spectral features. These features could be
well reproduced by convolution of twoGaussian line shapes, suggesting the
presence of at least two different isomeric forms. In water cluster anions,
electronic transitions are hardly ac companied by dramatic structural
changes, since the excess electron in mid-sized anionic clusters is expected to
be largely delocalized rather than localized. Accordingly,as above, we assign
the two absorption features to two distinct isomeric forms, each exhibiting
the s → p excitation of the excess electron. Both absorption bands exhibit
systematic blue shifts with increasing the cluster size, and it is quite con-
sistent with the previous report by Herburger et al.
28.

recorded at a photon energy of 1.57 eV to estimate VBEs of corresponding
isomers (Fig. 2b). It should be noted that the noisy feature in the high

error. The VBEs of motifs I and II reported by Verlet et al.
19 are indicated in
Fig. 2b for comparison with our photoelectron spectra though it should be
noted that linear extrapolation was used for several clusters whose VBEs

It should be noted that the cavity isomer (Ib) is not expected to exhibit a
blue shift in the absorption maximu m with the increase of the cluster
size
21,25,26,28. Namely, when the electron is conﬁned within a cavity, its radius

minimal variation in the energy spacing betweens and p quantum states. For
this reason, the same absorption band in the earlier study by Herburger et al.
was assigned to the surface isomers
28. Barnett et al. theoretically predicted
the absorption maximum of the cavity isomer would lie slightly above 2 eV,
without a signi ﬁcant shift with cluster size, whereas that of the surface
isomer would be around 1 eV for n =1 8–64
21. Furthermore, quantum/

cavity isomer is not anticipated to be thermodynamically stable for water

predictions and experimental observations suggest that the absorption and

two variants of the Ia surface isomer. In other words, Ia may encompass two
distinct structural motifs, hereby designated as Ia1 and Ia2.

Taken together with prior theoretical and experimental studies, our
results then support a reﬁned classiﬁcation of water cluster anion isomers. In
addition to the cavity isomer Ib, we identify now four distinct surface iso-
mers: Ia1, Ia2, II, and III. The Ia1 and Ia2 species represent subdivisions of
the previously deﬁned Ia surface isomer. The expected order of the radius of
gyration is Ib < Ia1 < Ia2 < II < III, with smaller values re ﬂecting greater
electron embeddedness. Isomer III was not observed in our experiment,
most likely due to the rapid autodetachment of dipole-bound states
32,41–44,
which would render this species even less populated than isomer II under
our experimental conditions. It should be emphasized again that the two
surface-bound isomers predicted by QM/MM calculations26 should now be
reassigned as Ia1 and Ia2, rather thanthe previously assigned motifs Ia and
II. The Ib and III isomers predicted by theory are consistent with the
experimental observations. On the other hand, the experimentally identiﬁed
II motif does not match the theoretical prediction. Therefore, although only
four motifs were theoretically propo sed, water anion clusters should be
classiﬁed into ﬁve distinct motifs.

therefore most plausibly assigned to GSAD, competing with fragmentation.

previously recognized.

Conclusion
Isomeric motifs of hydrated electrons in water cluster anions were reex-
amined by combining photoelectron and electronic absorption spectro-
scopies under identical ion source conditions. The four previously
characterized isomeric structures are now expanded toﬁve in the present
work, thereby reﬁning the framework of hydrated electron isomers. Nota-
bly, our experimental results corroborate theoretical predictions, under-
scoring the essential role of computational modeling in advancing the
otherwise formidable understandingof hydrated electrons. Time-resolved
photoelectron spectroscopy (TRPES) reveals isomer-speciﬁc excited-state
relaxation dynamics. By tuning the excitation energy, we probed the
dynamics of distinct surface-bound motifs (Ia1, Ia2, and II) across different
cluster sizes. In particular, isomers Ia1 and Ia2 undergo more ef ﬁcient
excited-state autodetachment compared to II, offering an additional struc-
tural property that can be used to dist inguish hydrated electron motifs.
Extending the temporal probing window further reveals that vibrationally
excited ground-state anions persi s tf o rm u c hl o n g e rt h a np r e v i o u s l y
recognized (τ ∼ 340 ps). With increasing cluster size, internal conversion
becomes more efﬁcient for both Ia1 and Ia2, as evidenced by femtosecond
transient measurements. Our ﬁndings highlight a strong consistency
between theoretical predictions and experimental observations, reinforcing
conﬁdence in previous computational approaches and emphasizing their
value for future studies. Moreover, the results demonstrate that excited-state
dynamics are highly sensitive to structural motifs, with distinct relaxation
pathways emerging from different hydration environments. This structural
sensitivity becomes particularly relevant in anisotropic environments such
as aerosols or the water/air interface, where electron localization is strongly
governed by local geometry. Looking forward, the structure –dynamics
relationships established here provide a foundation for systematic exten-
sions to other hydrated species, offe ring a broader platform for under-
standing electron solvation and relaxation in aqueous systems.
Methods
Water cluster anions were generated by expanding water vapor seeded in
neon gas (backing pressure of 5 bar) through a 150μm nozzle using a pulsed
Even-Lavie valve equipped with a ﬁlament ionizer. Thermal electrons
emitted from the ﬁlament ionized the neon gas via electron impact, pro-
ducing secondary slow electrons thatwere subsequently attached to water
molecules to form anions. The resulting water cluster anions were accel-
erated toward the laser interaction region using a Wiley–McLaren time-of-
ﬂight (TOF) mass spectrometer, whic h enabled mass selection based on
arrival time differences. When the desired size of water cluster anions
arrived between the repelling and ext ractor electrodes of a home-built
velocity map imaging (VMI) spectro meter, a laser pulse was applied to
induce photoelectron and/or photofr agment formation. Photoelectrons
were imaged using chevron-type microchannel plates (MCPs) backed by a
phosphor screen and CCD camera. Images were reconstructed into pho-
toelectron kinetic energy distributions using the BASEX method, which
performs an Abel inversion through a basis-set expansion. Using a photo-
multiplier tube, photofragments were recorded in a mass-selective manner
based on their arrival times at the M CP, which varied according to the
electric ﬁeld given in the VMI setup. Especially, the contribution of GSAD
could be separately identiﬁed at the edge of the long tail of photoelectron
signal along the time-of-ﬂight axis
49,50. For the photoelectron spectroscopy,
the voltage ratio of the repeller to that of the extractor was set to 0.71, while it
was adjusted to 1.23 for the photofragment detection. Electronic absorption
spectra were obtained by monitoring depletion of the parent anion signal as
af u n c t i o no fp h o t o ne n e r g yw h i l ei ti snormalized with respect to the laser
intensity. Tunable nanosecond laser pulses were provided by an Nd:YAG
pumped optical parametric oscillator (OPO) system (NT342, Ekspla). All
photoelectron spectra of water cluster anions were recorded using a
Ti:sapphire regenerative ampliﬁer (Legend Elite-P, Coherent) seeded by a
femtosecond oscillator (Vitara-T-HP, Coherent). For time-resolved pho-
toelectron spectroscopy (TRPES), the femtosecond output at 1.57 eV was
split into two parts; one was used as the probe (~200 mW) whereas the other
was converted to 0.84 or 1.09 eV using an optical parametric ampli ﬁer
(TOPAS, Light Conversion) to be used for the pump pulses (~60 mW for
both). All beams were loosely focused to a spot size of approximately 3 mm
in the laser –ion interaction region. The delay between pump and probe
pulses was controlled using a 220 mm long optical delay stage (DDS 220,
Thorlabs) equipped with a retrore ﬂector (UBBR2.5-1S). The instrument
response function (IRF) was determined to be approximately 100 fs from
