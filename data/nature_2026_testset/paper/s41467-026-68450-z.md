Hybrid lead halides have demonstrated great promise for CO2 photoreduction
due to their excellent photophysical properties. However, developing a single-
component hybrid lead halide that combines high intrinsic stability with near-
infrared absorption remains a signiﬁcant challenge. Herein, to address both
issues, we design a series of nine highly stable lead halide frameworks func-
tionalized by bis(terpyridine)-metal complexes, which exhibit strong light
absorption extending into the near-infrared region up to 1150 nm. Unlike their
symmetric chloride and bromide analogues, the charge-polarized lead iodide
sites in bis(terpyridine)-metal-functionalized frameworks serve as highly efﬁ-
cient C– C coupling centers for C
2 production in near-infrared-driven CO2
photoreduction, achieving notable C2 production rates of up to 14.2μmol g-1 h-1
with electron selectivities as high as 86%. These performances match the state-
of-the-art among all reported single-component, near-infrared-responsive
photocatalysts. This work highlights the potential of coordination-driven
assembly of highly stable lead halide frameworks for near-infrared-driven
artiﬁcial photosynthesis.
Massive fossil fuel consumption has sharply increased atmospheric
CO2, driven climate change, and prompting over 130 countries to
commit to carbon neutrality1. Photocatalytic CO2 conversion into high-
value-added products presents a promising pathway to close the car-
bon cycle
2,3. However, this process has two major challenges: (1) the
difﬁculty in effectively utilizing near-infrared (NIR) light, which
accounts for ~51% of the solar energy, and (2) the inef ﬁciency in
selectively producing C2+ hydrocarbons such as ethylene and ethane,
which are major industrial chemical feedstocks 4,5. Most commonly
used semiconductor photocatalysts have a bandgap greater than
1.80 eV, limiting their ability to harvest light from the NIR region
6,7.T o
address this limitation, recent studies have focused on developing
narrow-bandgap photocatalyst systems to extend the light absorption
range into the near-infrared (NIR) region. Although existing catalysts
can achieve highly selective CO2 reduction to C1 products (such as CO
and CH4) under NIR irradiation, a signiﬁcant challenge for all reported
NIR-driven photocatalysts is that the low energy of NIR light leads to
inadequate photon absorption and suppressed charge carrier accu-
mulation, which hinder the multi-electron CO
2 reduction kinetics for
C– C coupling required for C 2+ product formation8– 10.M o r e o v e r ,t r a -
ditional inorganic photocatalysts typically possess active sites with
uniform charge density, which suppresses C– C coupling due to strong
dipole-dipole repulsion between adsorbed *CO intermediates

heterojunction construction have been employed to introduce charge-

Transition metal tripyridine complexes (M(tpy) 2,M = F e 2+/Co2+/
Ni2+) exhibit exceptional photophysical properties due to their poly-
aromatic structures, including long excited-state lifetimes and tunable
redox characteristics, which render them highly promising in photo-
catalysis, molecular sensing, photovoltaics, and biomedicine
29,30.
M(tpy)2 complexes readily form diverse metal-ligand assemblies
through non-covalent interactions, such asπ-π stacking and hydrogen
bonding31. Their intramolecular d– d orbital transitions and cascaded
electron transfer processes endow them with notable NIR absorption
properties
32.
Herein, we report the successful construction of nine lead halide
frameworks via the coordination-driven assembly of M(tpy) 2-based
linkers (M = Fe2+/Co2+/Ni2+) with 0D lead halide clusters (X = Cl– /Br– /I– ).
The highly covalent Pb2+-carboxylate linkage effectively mitigates the
dielectric mismatch between the organic and inorganic component33,
enabling both M(tpy)2 ligands and the lead halide units substantially
contributing to the frontier orbitals. This distinct structural con ﬁg-
uration extends their broad absorption ranges across the ultraviolet-
visible-near-infrared (UV– vis– NIR) region, covering wavelengths up to
1150 nm. Compared to the symmetric Pb
2Cl4 and Pb2Br4 units in the
lead chloride and bromide frameworks, the additional coordination of
DMF molecules in Pb 2I4(DMF) clusters induces asymmetric charge
polarization at the local Pb 2 catalytic sites, achieving photocatalytic
CO2 reduction to C2 hydrocarbons with a selectivity up to 86% (elec-
tron basis) under NIR irradiation. Importantly, the Fe(tpy) 2-functio-
nalized lead iodide framework reaches the highest evolution rates of
35.1 μmol·g
−1·h−1 and 14.2 μmol·g−1·h−1 for C 2 products under
UV– vis– NIR and NIR light irradiation, respectively. Moreover, in situ
spectroscopic studies and DFT calculations reveal that the asymmetric
Pb
2I4(DMF) sites facilitate C– C bond formation by stabilizing the key
*COCOH intermediate, thereby promoting the selective generation of
C
2 products.

migrate from Fe(tpy)
2 orbitals to Pb2+ centers via LMCT process. We
have further performed time-dependent density functional theory
(TD-DFT) calculations to reveal that electrons and holes are clearly
localized in distinct domains: ~95.5% of the electrons reside on the
Pb
2I4(DMF) fragment, while the holes remain largely con ﬁned to
Fe(tpy)2 (Supplementary Fig. 33b). Moreover, electrostatic potential
(ESP) analysis shows that the negative ESP is primarily distributed
around the Pb2I4(DMF) moieties, while the positive ESP is concentrated
on Fe(tpy) 2 (Supplementary Fig. 33c). These calculations strongly
evidence the formation of a strong drive force for directional charge
transfer from Fe(tpy)

carbons over TJU-60(I)-Fe(tpy)2, the Gibbs free energies of reaction

via DFT calculations; however, it is noted that DFT-derived energy
barriers and pathways are based on simpli ﬁed structural models

supplied by TEOA facilitate the activation and conversion of CO2 into
the *CO intermediate. The absorbed *CO undergoes further protona-

has lower energy barriers (0.40– 0.59 eV) than CO desorption. Subse-
quently, partial *CO and the unsaturated *COH are co-adsorbed at

coupling barrier of 0.49 eV. The formation of the *COCOH inter-

production, in agreement with the in situ DRIFTS observation of

*COCOH→ *CCO → *CHCOH→ *CCH → *CHCH

energy transitions occur as H

the pathway toward C 2H6 formation, aligning well with our experi-
