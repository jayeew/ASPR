The inland basins of western China are home to the world ’s earliest deﬁnition of yardangs, which are
streamlined, wind-sculpted landforms. The Qaidam Basin in northwestern China hosts one of the
largest yardang ﬁelds on Earth, which has been regarded as the most Mars-analogue. However, basin-
scale research on the timing and forcing mechanism of yardang formation in the Qaidam Basin
remains lacking. In this study, we used electron spin resonance and optically stimulated luminescence
dating to determine the tectonic-scale (10
6-year) spatiotemporal evolution of yardangs in the basin.
Our results indicate that yardang development in the Qaidam Basin commenced around 0.8 million
years ago, starting in the northwest and gradually expanding southeast around 0.5 −0.3 million years
ago. This stepwise expansion of yardangs correlated well with the remarkable growth of Northern
Hemisphere ice-sheet, which intensiﬁed the Siberian High and enhanced cold, dry prevailing winds in
the region.

Results
Sedimentary facies of the yardang landforms
We studied seven representative yardang proﬁl e sa c r o s st h eb a s i n( F i g .1a),
ranging in morphologies from long-ridge, whaleback, to pyramidal shapes
(Supplementary Fig. 1a −g). Field observations reveal that the studied
yardangs consist of lacustrine deposits, primarily composed of mudstones
and siltstones with occasional sandstone intercalations. Field observations
indicate that the sedimentary rocks are cemented by evaporates, such as
carbonates or gypsum. However, such cementation is often heterogeneous.
In addition, the deposits commonly exhibit bedding structures. When
temperatures fall below freezing (commonly in the Qaidam Basin), water
within the rock undergoes repeated freeze-thaw cycles, which accelerates
rock fragmentation. Meanwhile, inﬁltrated water dissolves soluble mineral
salts; subsequent evaporation leads to salt crystallization, further promoting
rock disintegration through salt wea thering. As for carbonate cements,
although relatively hard, they are susceptible to dissolution by weakly acidic
water containing carbon dioxide (chemical weathering). This dissolution
widens existing fractures and increases porosity, which in turn enhances
physical weathering. Collectively, these factors make the sedimentary rocks
constituting yardangs being far more vulnerable to physical erosion com-
pared to volcanic and metamorphic roc ks. This susceptibility facilitates
ongoing wind deﬂation and the development of yardang landforms.
Based on comprehensive lithological and sedimentological observa-
tions from different parts of the yardangﬁeld (Fig. 2a, b), we identiﬁed ﬁve
distinct sedimentary facies of yardangs: deep lake, shallow lake, evaporite
salt lake, storm-inﬂuenced tidal ﬂat and lakeshore facies (Fig. 2c−h). The
deep lake facies is characterized by grayish-green laminated mudstones
(Fig. 2c). The ﬁne particle size together with grayish-green color indicate
deposition in a low-energy, anoxic deep-water environment. The shallow
lake facies exhibits alternating mudstones and siltstones with horizontal
bedding and current ripples (Fig.2d). Salt lake facies are distinguished by the
presence of evaporite minerals (predominantly halite and gypsum) (Fig.2e),
occasionally with crystal clusters of gypsum (Fig.2f). Storm-inﬂuenced tidal
ﬂats facies is marked by hummocky cross-stratiﬁcation (Fig. 2g), formed
through wind-driven storm wave action. Lakeshore facies is characterized
by the massive to stratiﬁed coarse-sized sandstones with occasional small
gravel inclusions (Fig.2h). These sedimentary facies exhibit distinct vertical
stacking patterns across different yardang locations (Fig.3a−g), reﬂecting
the basin’s complex depositional history.

Our analysis of the lithofacies and sedimentary environment of the
yardang landforms suggests that the Qaidam Basin previously sustained
paleolakes prior to yardang formation. Notably, the visible yardang land-
forms represent only the exposed parts of the lacustrine deposits after wind
sculpting. An integrated analysis of 47,847 data from 146 borehole cores in
the Qaidam Basin indicates the presence of extensive basin-scale lacustrine
deposits during the early Quaternary
32. Further analysis reveals that the
basin hosted a pan-lake environment, comprising multiple discrete lakes
with separate depositional centers, rather than a single, uniﬁed lake32.T h e
ESR and OSL ages obtained from the uppermost yardangs constrain the
timing of lake desiccation. Our integr ated chronostratigraphic analysis
reveals pronounced spatiotemporal variations in the paleolake desiccation
and yardang development (Fig.4).
In the northwestern yardangﬁeld, our chronological evidence reveals
lake desiccation commenced around 0.8 Ma. Although it is uncertain
whether wind sculpting of landform started immediately after the lake dried
up, the intensiﬁed arid and windy climate since the Quaternary
32,33 implies
that the development of yardangs likely did not lag far behind the lake
desiccation (see Discussion Section for details). Furthermore, it should be
noted that some yardang surfaces are protected by caprock layers, such as
calcium-cemented gravels or salt-indurated hard sedimentary crusts, which
effectively shield their tops from wind erosion. Of course, there are also
yardangs lacking such protective caps may have experienced some aeolian
deﬂation. In this context, the age of th e uppermost part of the yardangs
(corresponding to lake desiccation), is close to the initial formation age of the
yardangs or represents their maximum age of yardang development.
In the central yardangﬁeld, our chronological evidence indicates lake
desiccation occurred slightly later than in the northwest, at ~0.8−0.6 Ma.
Further southeast, the lake dried up even later, with desiccation constrained
to roughly 0.5−0.3 Ma (Fig. 4). Therefore, our evidence demonstrates that
the Qaidam Basin not only hosted extensive lakes during the Miocene-
Pliocene
32,33, but maintained substantial water bodies until ~0.8 Ma. The
disintegration of the pan-lake environment was characterized by its gradual
retreat from the northwest to the southeast, eventually being replaced by
diachronous wind-sculpted yardang landforms (Fig.4).

compelling evidence for this relationship. Notably, evaporite salt layers are
direct indicators of enhanced lake evaporation. Borehole records show that
the salt layers dominated by haliteﬁrst appeared sporadically at 2.2 Ma but
became frequent and extensive after 0.8 Ma, eventually constituting up to
one-third of total deposits in the Qaidam Basin

when evaporation exceeds inﬂow, these minerals precipitate in a speci ﬁc

Methods
Electron spin resonance (ESR) dating
In the laboratory, the 125−250 μm size-fraction was isolated by wet-sieved
for each sample, and 10% HCl and 10% H 2O2 were used to remove car-
bonate and organics. Heavy minerals and feldspars were mostly removed
with sodium polytungstate solutionswith densities of 2.75 and 2.62 g/ml,
respectively. Subsequently, the samples were treated with HF (40%) for at
least 60 minutes to eliminate the remaining feldspars. After that, HCl (10%)
was added to remove any solubleﬂuoride. The extracted quartz grains were
evenly divided into 10– 12 aliquots. One aliquot was preserved for measuring
natural ESR signal, while the others were irradiated using a
60Co source at the
Institute of Atomic Energy of China. Gamma irradiation was performed at
two groups of doses.
ESR measurements were conducted using a Bruker-Emxplus-6/1
X-band paramagnetic resonance sp ectrometer coupled with a standard
rectangular ER 4102ST resonator in the ESR Dating Laboratory of the
Institute of Geomechanics, Chinese Academy of Geological Sciences, Beij-
ing, China. To maintain consistent experimental conditions, the tempera-
ture of the coolant circulating through the magnet was controlled and
stabilized at 18 °C using a Zhonghe BLK Ⅱ−2FF-R water chiller. ESR
measurements were performed at low temperature (∼90 K) using a liquid
nitrogen-cooled ER4141VT Digital Temperature control system. All
acquisition parameters, including cavity temperature, were automatically
recorded with each ESR spectrum
59,60.
We observed ESR signals of Ti-Li centers. The used experimental
parameters include: sweep width of 40 mT, modulation frequency of
100 kHz, modulation amplitude of 0.05mT, time constant of 10 s, scans of
10 times, conversion time of 1.25 ms, and a resolution of 8000 points. To
address potential orientation-dependent ESR signal variations caused by
sample heterogeneity, all ESR signal intensities were calculated by averaging
at least three measurements taken at different orientations (with the sample
tube rotated ~120° in the resonator cavity between each measurement). This
approach mitigates the effects of any direction bias on the sample’sm a g n e t i c
properties. Representative ESR spectra are shown in Supplementary Fig. 2.
The ESR intensity of the Ti signal was extracted with peak-to-peak
amplitude measurement following the method of Duval and Moreno
60.F o r
each aliquot, the ﬁnal ESR intensities of Ti centers were determined by
averaging the values obtained from repeated measurements.
The equivalent dose was obtained using the multiple aliquot additive-
dose method61,62. The ESR dose estimates were based on the single saturating
exponential function, which was also used by previous studies61,63.T h ed a t a
ﬁtting was carried out using Microc al OriginPro 2021 software, which
employs an iterative procedure based on the Levenberg-Marquardt algo-
rithm through chi-square minimization64. The data were weighted using the
inverse of the squared ESR intensity61. The goodness ofﬁt was evaluated by
the r-squared (R2) value. The dose growth curves for representative samples
are shown in Supplementary Fig. 3.
The environmental dose rate and the other relevant parameters are in
Supplementary Table 1. The uranium and thorium concentrations were
measured using inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry, and
potassium content was measured usingatomic absorption spectroscopy at
the Research Institute of Uranium Geology, Beijing, China. The water
content was determined by heating the samples to 60 °C for approximately
24 h and calculating the percentage weight loss after drying. It should be
stressed that although yardang landforms originated from lacustrine sedi-
ments with potentially high initial water content, the climate in the Qaidam
