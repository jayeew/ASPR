Human papillomavirus (HPV)-negative head and neck squamous cell carcinoma
(HNSCC) is characterised by hyperactivation of the cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6
(CDK4/6) pathway. As immunotherapy has become theﬁrst-line treatment for
HNSCC, resistance to anti-programmed death-1 (PD-1) agents has emerged as a
pivotal challenge. This prospective, single arm, phase II study (NCT05721443)
evaluated the efﬁcacy and safety of dalpiciclib, a CDK4/6 inhibitor, combined
with cetuximab in patients with anti-PD-1-resistant, HPV-negative recurrent and/
or metastatic HNSCC. Patients diagnosed with p16-negative R/M HNSCC resis-
tant to ﬁrst-line anti-PD-1 therapy without prior cetuximab treatment were
enroled. Patients received oral dalpiciclib (150 mg daily on days 1-21 of each 28-
day cycle) and intravenous cetuximab (400 mg/m
2 on day 1 of cycle 1, followed
by 250 mg/m2 weekly in each cycle). The primary endpoint was objective
response rate (ORR), secondary endpoints were overall survival, progression-
free survival, duration of response, and safety. Between March 2023 and
November 2024, a total of 28 patients were enroled. The ORR was 67.9% (19/28;
95% conﬁdence interval [CI], 49.0%-82.0%), which met our primary endpoint.
With a median follow-up of 9.2 months (interquartile range [IQR], 5.98-14.12),
the median progression-free survival was 7.0 months (95% CI, 4.13- not reached
[NR]), and the median overall survival was 17.0 months (95% CI, 10.75-23.25).
Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) occurred in all patients, pre-
dominantly grade 1-2. Grade 3 TRAEs included neutrophil count decreased (9/
28, 32.1%) and white blood cell count decreased (9/28, 32.1%). No grade 4 or 5
TRAEs were observed. Dalpiciclib combined with cetuximab was well-tolerated

Patients diagnosed with recurrent and/or metastatic (R/M) head and
neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) have poor outcomes, with a
median overall survival (OS) of 8 –10 months
1,2. In recent years,
immunotherapy has signi ﬁcantly improved clinical outcomes in
patients with R/M HNSCC and has increasingly emerged as a ﬁrst-line
treatment for HNSCC. However, pembrolizumab combined with che-
motherapy results in an objective response rate (ORR) of 36% and a
median progression-free survival (PFS) of 4.9 months, revealing that a
considerable number of patients rarely beneﬁt from immunotherapy
3.
The incidence of human papillomavirus (HPV)-negative HNSCC is
greater than that of HPV-related HNSCC. However, compared with
HPV-related HNSCC, HPV-negative HNSCC is often associated with a
lower response rate and poorer survival with immunotherapy
4,5.H P V -
negative HNSCC is characterised by hyperactivation of the cyclin-
dependent kinase 4/6 (CDK4/6) pathway, accompanied by CCND1
ampliﬁcation and CDKN2A deﬁciency. Epidermal growth factor
receptor (EGFR) is highly expressed in more than 90% of HNSCC cases
and can activate the CDK4/6-cyclin D complex and ultimately drive
cancer cell growth by promoting the progression of the cell cycle
through the G1 phase, facilitating the malignant transformation of
cancer cells
6. This provides a rationale for the combination of CDK4/6
and EGFR inhibitors.
Cetuximab, an IgG1 monoclonal antibody against EGFR, binds to
the extracellular domain of EGFR and thereby inhibits the tyrosine
kinase activity of cancer cells
7. Cetuximab is the standard salvage
therapy for HNSCC patients refractory to ﬁrst-line immunotherapy.
However, the ef ﬁcacy of cetuximab monotherapy as a second-line
treatment was modest, with an objective response rate of 13% and a
median PFS of 2.3 months
1. A previous study reported that palbociclib,
a selective CDK4/6 inhibitor, resulted in promising outcomes when
combined with cetuximab in patients with platinum-resistant or
cetuximab-resistant, HPV-negative R/M HNSCC
8. However, the anti-
tumour activity of the combination of CDK4/6 and EGFR inhibitors is
unknown in patients with immunotherapy-resistant, HPV-negative R/
M HNSCC.
Here, we report that dalpiciclib, a selective CDK4/6 inhibitor,
combined with cetuximab has a promising objective response and
considerable toxicity in patients with anti-PD-1-resistant, HPV-
negative HNSCC.

promising antitumour activity and manageable toxicity in patients
with anti-PD-1-resistant, HPV-negative R/M HNSCC. The ORR was
67.9%, meeting the primary endpoint.

might lead to selection bias, and a large-scale, double-blinded, ran-
domised trial is needed to conﬁrm the results of this study. In addition,
the participants in this study had mainly oral squamous cell carcinoma
and oropharyngeal carcinoma, and the primary tumour site of HNSCC
should be more balanced in future studies. In addition, the median
follow-up time should be longer to observe the comprehensive long-

antitumour activity and improved survival outcomes in patients with
anti-PD-1-resistant, HPV-negative R/M HNSCC, with a manageable
safety proﬁle. These ﬁndings require further validation in a rando-

Methods
Study design and participants
The trial protocol was approved by the ethics committee of Shanghai
Ninth People’s Hospital. The trial was conducted in accordance with
the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines. All
the participants signed written informed consent prior to enrolment
(the ﬁrst participant was enroled on March 7

participant was enroled on November 1st, 2024). The study was regis-
tered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05721443,https://register.clinicaltrials.
gov/, ﬁrst posted on February 10th, 2023). In our trial, dalpiciclib and
imaging examinations were provided free of charge for all enrolled
patients.
This study was an investigator-initiated, single-arm, single-centre
phase II trial conducted at Shanghai Ninth People ’s Hospital. The eli-
gible participants were aged 18 –75 years and had histologically con-
ﬁrmed R/M HNSCC; had received at least one cycle of anti-PD-1 therapy
in the R/M setting; had at least one measurable lesion according to the
Response Evaluation Criteria In Solid Tumours (RECIST) version 1.1
35;
had radiographic con ﬁrmation of disease progression or clinical
judgement indicating no further beneﬁt from anti-PD-1 therapy; had
conﬁrmed HPV-negative disease by immunohistochemistry (IHC); had
an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status
of 0 or 1; and had acceptable bone marrow and organ function.
Participants were excluded if they had previously received
cetuximab or tyrosine kinase inhibitors targeting EGFR; were known to
be hypersensitive to recombinant humanised EGFR monoclonal anti-
bodies or CDK4/6 inhibitors; had other types of cancer (excluding
cured skin basal cell carcinoma, skin squamous cell carcinoma, carci-
noma in situ of the cervix, and early prostate cancer) within 5 years;
had received haematopoietic growth factors within one week prior to
the study treatment; had active human immunode ﬁciency virus or
treponema pallidum infection; were unable to swallow the drugs; or
had abnormal coagulation function.
Procedures
Participants received cetuximab intravenously at an initial dose of
400 mg/m² followed by a weekly maintenance dose of 250 mg/m 2.
Dalpiciclib was administered orally at a dose of 150 mg once daily for 21
consecutive days, followed by a 7-day break. Each 28-day period was
considered a treatment cycle. Treatment was continued until disease
progression, unacceptable toxicity, or patient withdrawal, whichever
occurred ﬁrst.
Dalpiciclib treatment was stopped if a grade 2 treatment-related
adverse event (TRAE) occurred and was resumed if the TRAE recov-
ered to grade 0 or 1 within 12 weeks. If a patient experienced a grade 3
or higher TRAE, the dose was reduced to 125 mg/day. If the patient was

Biomarker analyses
Tumour tissue samples (archival parafﬁn-embedded specimens from
the last 2 years or fresh tumour biopsies) and blood samples were
collected from the enrolled patients. HPV status was conﬁrmed on the
basis of p16 expression detected by IHC (CINtec p16 Histology [Ven-
tana, Tucson, AZ]) using formalin-ﬁxed parafﬁn-embedded samples. A
targeted sequencing library was generated using a next-generation
sequencing panel (YouSu
TM, OrigiMed) according to the established
standard operating protocol. DNA (50–250 ng) from each sample was
used for DNA library preparation as follows:ﬁrst, DNA was fragmented
using the M220 focused ultrasonicator Covaris (Covaris, Woburn, MA,
USA) to 150–220 bp; second, the DNA fragments were end-polished A-
tailed and ligated with adaptors; then, the exons of 242 cancer-related
genes and the introns of 43 genes were captured and ampli ﬁed by
polymerase chain reaction. After quality control and quanti ﬁcation
using a LabChip GXII Touch HT (PerkinElmer) and a Qubit 3 Fluo-
rometer (Thermo Fisher), the DNA libraries were sequenced on the
Illumina-Novaseq 6000 Platform (Illumina Incorporated, San Diego,
CA) by paired-end 150-bp sequencing to a mean unique depth of
1000 × coverage.
Single-nucleotide variations were identiﬁed by MuTect (version
1.7; Broad Institute, Cambridge, MA, USA). Insertion deletions were
identiﬁed using PINDEL (version 0.2.5). The functional effects of the
GAs were annotated using SnpEff 3.0. Copy number variation regions
were identi ﬁed using Control-FREEC (version 9.7; Institute Cochin,
Paris, France) with the following parameters: window = 50000 and
step = 10000. Gene fusions were detected using an in-house devel-
oped pipeline. Gene rearrangements were assessed using the Inte-
grative Genomics Viewer. The tumour mutation burden (TMB) was
estimated by counting the number of coding somatic mutations,
including single-nucleotide variations and insertions deletions, per
kilobase of sequence examined for each patient. The visual analysis of
cancer genomics was performed with the “maftools” and “ggplot2” R
packages. Biomarker analysis for tissue samples was approved by the
Institutional Review Board of Shanghai Ninth People ’s Hospital, and
written informed consent to obtain these samples for analysis was
obtained from all participants.
Multiplex immunoﬂuorescence analysis
Tumour-inﬁltrating lymphocytes (TILs) were analysed via multiplex
immunoﬂuorescence using the following primary antibodies: rabbit
anti-human CD4 (Servicebio, cat. no. GB150062; 1:1000), mouse anti-
human CD8 (Servicebio, cat. no. GB12068; 1:500), rabbit anti-human
CD56 (Servicebio, cat. no. GB112671; 1:500), and rabbit anti-human
FOXP3 (Servicebio, cat. no. GB152325; 1:500). Brie ﬂy, tissue sections
underwent deparafﬁnization and rehydration, followed by antigen
retrieval. The sections were incubated in 3% H ₂O₂ in an enclosed
humid chamber for 25 min to block endogenous peroxidase activity,
after which they were washed on a decolourising shaker. The sections
were blocked with 10% goat anti-rabbit serum for 30 min and then
incubated with the primary antibody at 4 °C overnight. After the cells
were washed, an HRP-conjugated secondary antibody was applied for
50 min at room temperature, followed by incubation at room tem-
perature for 10 mins. Antibody stripping preceded repetition of the
blocking-primary-secondary-TSA-stripping cycle for the remaining
three primary antibodies. Finally, the sections were counterstained
with DAPI, quenched with an auto ﬂuorescence quencher, mounted
with anti-ﬂuorescence quenching medium, and imaged via spectral-
based systems with panoramic section scanning.
Statistical analysis
Historical data revealed that th e ORR for second-line treatment
with cetuximab monotherapy was 13%. The study adopted a
Simon’s two-stage design to minimise the number of subjects
exposed to ineffective treatment (Fig. 1A). On the basis of the
expected ORR of 33% for dalpiciclib combined with cetuximab,
assuming an α of 0.05 (two-sided) and a power of 80%, we aimed
to enrol 14 participants in the ﬁrst stage. If the number of
responders (CR + PR) was no more than 1, the study was termi-
nated because of insuf ﬁcient ef ﬁcacy. If more than one partici-
pant achieved a CR or PR, another 12 participants were recruited
in the second stage. A total of 26 participants were enrolled. If the
total number of responders (CR + PR) across both stages was
more than 6, the combination therapy was considered effective.
Efﬁcacy and safety were analysed in patients who received at least
one dose of the study drug. Continuous variables are expressed as
medians (ranges), and categorical variables are expressed as fre-
quencies and percentages. The 95% conﬁdence interval (CI) of the ORR
was estimated using the Clopper –Pearson method. The median PFS,
OS, and DOR were estimated using the Kaplan –Meier method, and
their 95% CIs were estimated using the Brookmeyer–Crowley method
(IBM SPSS Statistics 27.0). Comparisons of the TMB and gene mutation
frequency between responders and nonresponders were performed
using the Mann –Whitney test and Fisher ’s exact test, respectively.
Comparisons of the percentage of lymphocytes during treatment were
performed using the paired t test method. A P-value < 0.05 was con-
sidered to indicate statistical signiﬁcance.
