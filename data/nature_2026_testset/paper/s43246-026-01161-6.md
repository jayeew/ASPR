The distinctly different mechanical behavior of nanocrystalline materials compared to their coarse-
grained counterparts has been extensively studied using atomistic simulations and experimental
works. In-situ TEM deformation has revealed multiple mechanisms, including grain rotation and
deformation twinning. Yet the effect of elastic strain concentrations from mechanical anisotropy or
dislocation pile-ups remains less understood, as precise measurements were previously limited to
speciﬁcally oriented grains. Here, we show that combining precession nanobeam electron diffraction
with advanced orientation and strain-mapping algorithms allows measurement of transient elastic
strain ﬁelds across all grains in a sputter-deposited nanocrystalline Au thin ﬁlm. During nominally
elastic loading, we observe an increase in tensile elastic strain and substantial grain rotations
exceeding 6°, with pronounced heterogeneity among individual grains. Analysis reveals that these
rotations arise from a combination of grain size, initial strain state, and dislocation density. The
importance of the initial microstructural state is further highlighted in annealed samples: reducing
defect density delays yielding and triggers deformation twinning at similar length scales. These results
demonstrate the feasibility of mapping elastic strains with high accuracy and nanometer resolution
across arbitrary grain boundaries, and reveal the critical role of local strain heterogeneities in
determining the deformation behavior of nanocrystalline materials.
Nanocrystalline (nc) materials have attracted great interest due to their
unique properties like high strength and ductility 1,2, tailored magnetic
properties3,4 or superplasticity 5,6. The enhanced mechanical properties
compared to their coarse-grained counterparts can be explained by
dislocation pile-ups through the well-known Hall-Petch relation
7,8.W i t h
decreasing grain size, dislocation pile-ups at the grain boundaries (GBs),
which act as a driving force for dislocation movement in the neighboring
grain, are strongly reduced, thus signi ﬁcantly increasing the yield
strength. However, this relation is no longer valid below a mean grain
size of about 100 nm. At those intermediate grain sizes, the ability to store
lattice dislocations inside grains is reduced, and deviation from the Hall-
Petch relation becomes prominent
9,10. At even smaller grain sizes, around
10– 30 nm and below, dislocation activity is mostly diminished, and grain
boundary-mediated deformation processes become dominant. These
include, among others, GB sliding, GB migration, grain rotation, or the
emission of partial dislocations. Material softening is often observed in
this range of grain sizes, which is often referred to as the inverse Hall-
Petch regime
11– 13.
This drastic change in material behavior has attracted great interest
in the last two decades. Molecular dynamics simulations have provided
atomic-level insight into this size effect and the associated deformation
mechanisms for different metals
14– 18. Experimental evidence for the
described phenomena, though challenging, can be provided by in-situ
transmission electron microscopy (TEM). High-resolution TEM-based
methods allow for direct observation of phenomena like GB migration,
GB sliding, grain coalescence, or grain rotation with sub-ångstrom
resolution
19– 25. Applying geometric phase analysis (GPA) even allows to
map elastic strains at the nanoscale. However, these methods face the
problem of relying on grains perfectly aligned on the zone axis to obtain
meaningful results. This necessity bounds these techniques to individual
grains or, in the best case to speciﬁc grain boundaries like Σ3o r Σ9
26 thus
drastically limiting the ﬁeld of view. A different approach focuses on 4D-
STEM to elucidate the deformation mechanisms in nc metals. Auto-
mated crystal orientation mapping (ACOM) enables determination of
local crystal orientations with high accuracy, allowing quanti ﬁcation of
grain rotations and visualization of grain growth and sliding with spatial

Discussion
To evaluate the effectiveness of this novel technique and rule out any
potential misinterpretations of the von Mises strains caused by errors due to
off-axis tilt, Fig.8a, b illustratesεvM and its standard deviation per grain as a
function of the initial tilt from the <111> zone axis for sample I. The graphs
show no signi ﬁcant correlation; thus, it can be claimed that the strain

In fcc metals, a transition from dislocation slip to deformation twin-
ning can be observed if the characteristic length scale is reduced to a few
10 nm
49,58– 60. In our study, we observe this transition at aﬁxed characteristic
length scale of 50 nm by changing the initial state of the material, rather than
varying the grain size. However, co nsidering the origin of the change in
deformation mechanism, the observed phenomenon can still be explained
by the same principles. As the grain size shrinks, the likelihood of the
appearance of suitable dislocation sources is reduced; thus, a transition from
multiple slip to alternating slip and ﬁnally to single slip can be observed,
leading to higher hardening and strength. This source-limited size depen-
dency of the yield strength was demonstrated, for instance, by Kiener et al.
61
on single-crystalline Cu. As the formation of full dislocations becomes
increasingly more limited, nc fcc met als tend to favor partial dislocation
activity and deformation twinning. In this study, the higher annealing
temperature of sample II resulted in a more relaxed microstructure, healing
out dislocations and dislocation sources, thereby delaying the onset of
plasticity. This manifests directly in the higher yield strength of 1.7 GPa
compared to only 1.1 GPa for sample I. In the moderate-annealed state, the
microstructure contains a high densityof preexisting dislocations and dis-
location sources, which can be activated more easily. As a result, plastic
deformation initiates at relatively low stresses and proceeds mostly through
full dislocation slip. The abundance of dislocation sources prevents local
stress build-ups from forming deformation twins. Thus, although the grain size is
within the regime where twinning is possible, the high initial defect density sup-
presses twin formation in the non-annealed sample. A higher annealing tempera-
ture, as was the case for sample II signiﬁcantly reduces the dislocation and source
density. The deformation becomes increasingly more source-limited, and a higher
applied stress is required to nucleate newdislocations, leading to a delayed onset of
plasticity. When stresses rise sufﬁciently, the emission of partial dislocations becomes
more favorable, leading to the formation of deformation twins. Additionally, the
reduced density of lattice defects in the annealed state lowers the likelihood of twin
interruption by interactions with pre-existing dislocations. Previous studies have
shown that low-defect microstructures allow twins to propagate more easily across
nanocrystalline grainswithout being arrested
62.
Conclusion
In conclusion, we have established a new routine to quantify the deforma-
tion mechanisms in polycrystalline materials and applied it to nc Au. The
novel combination of ACOM with 4D-STEM-based strain mapping allows
for a comprehensive insight into deformation at the nanoscale, where grain
rotations, local transient nanoscalestrain, and grain boundary movements
can be tracked. Crystal orientation and elastic strain maps with a spatial
resolution of 2 nm were recorded during in-situ straining. Their time-
resolved evolution enables quantiﬁcation of unexpected deformation phe-
nomena. The results show an increase in elastic strain in the tensile direction
and substantial grain rotations beyond 6 ° already in the nominally elastic
regime. Deformation varies signiﬁcantly among different grains, a fact that
cannot be explained by the difference in grain orientation. With the help of
the acquired strain data, it was possible to trace the origin of these rotations
to a combination of grain size, strain state, and initial heterogeneities in the
material. Especially the residual strains and dislocations still present after
annealing have a strong inﬂuence on grain rotation. After fracture, global
elastic strains are mostly released, but strong strain concentrations are still
present, and the grains remain rotated relative to the unloaded state. The
importance of the initial state was further showcased by reducing the defect
density to a minimum. Annealing at 410 °C resulted in a microstructure
without noticeable grain growth and o nly a few single dislocations. The
absence of dislocation sources led to a signiﬁcant increase in strength from
1.1 GPa to 1.7 GPa through exhaustion hardening. The higher stress states
then led to a change in deformation mode, by triggering twinning as the
dominant mechanism. These results highlight the importance of hetero-
geneous strain states and defect densities on the deformation behavior of nc
metals, allowing for tuning their mec hanical properties. In addition, the
experiment showcases the feasibility of mapping elastic strains in poly-
crystals via 4D-STEM with high accuracy, even if grains are not perfectly
aligned on the zone axis. The ability to quantify transient nanoscale stress
ﬁelds across grain and phase boundaries, irrespective of local orientation,
opens up new research avenues.
Materials and methods
Sample Preparation
The investigation focused on ananocrystalline gold (Au) thinﬁlm. A 50 nm
thin layer was deposited onto both a single-layer graphene on holey silicon
nitride (Ted Pella, Inc) and on a holey carbon grid (Ted Pella, Inc) using a
DC magnetron sputtering system (Hex Korvus) at a pressure of 1.5 *
10
−3 mbar and an argon ﬂow rate of 20 sccm. To ensure uniform layer
thickness, a sample rotation of 20 rpm was used.
Sample I was subsequently in-situ annealed using a Gatan 652 heating
holder. The annealing temperature was gradually increased from 250 °C up
to 350 °C with a holding time of 10 minutes for every 10 °C step. Continuous
imaging of the sample ensured no unwanted grain growth, as only the
dislocation density should be reduced. The fairly lowﬁnal annealing tem-
perature of 350 °C avoided solid solu tion formation between Au and Si
(eutectic point at 363 °C, immiscibility at lower temperatures
63). Sample II
was ex-situ annealed in a self-built vacuum oven to 410 °C for 10 min.
Using a Zeiss Auriga dual beam FIB, the SiN-supported thinﬁlm was
transferred onto a push-to-pull (PTP) MEMS device (Hysitron, Bruker) and
secured using FIB-assisted chemical vapor deposition of Pt. Both rough
cutting and cutting of the ﬁnal dog-bone structure were performed at an
acceleration voltage of 30 kV and a beam current of 50 pA. Preparation of
the tensile sample II was performedon a ThermoFisher Helios 5 PFIB CXe.
The specimen was secured using FIB-assisted deposition of W, and cutting
was performed at 30 kV and a beam current of 10 pA.
TEM investigation
A Jeol JEM 2200FS operated at 200 kV in NBD mode was used for 4D-
STEM data acquisition. The TEM wa s equipped with a scan generator
(USG, TVIPS) that allowed for beam precession. Using a 1.9 nm spot size, a
condenser aperture #4 (10 μm), and alpha 3, a real space probe size of 2.0 nm
with a semi-convergence angle of 0. 93 mrad was generated. For ideal
ACOM data, a short camera length of 25 cm was used to ensure many Bragg
spots were visible in the patterns. A precession angle of 0.5 ° was used. 4D-
STEM image acquisition was realized with a hybrid pixel direct electron
detector (Merlin EM Quad) with 512 by 512 pixels operated in 12-bit mode.
The precession system operated at an angular frequency of 100 Hz, and a
frame time of 20 ms was chosen. The 4D-STEM maps consisted of 150 by
150 scan points with a step size of 2 nm, resulting in an acquisition time of
7:30 min per dataset.
In-situ nanomechanical testing was performed utilizing a Hysitron
PI95 picoindenter (Hysitron, Bruker) operated in load-controlled mode
with a loading rate of 0.1 μNs
−1. The deformation was paused every 10 μN
for the acquisition of the 4D-STEM datasets, and continued once the dataset
was recorded. These steps were repeated until the sample fractured.
