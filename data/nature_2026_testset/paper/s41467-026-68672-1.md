Conceptual engineering system design faces challenges from traditional
methods and emerging AI tools to fully address its inherently complex,
dynamic, and creativity-driven demands. iDesignGPT is a framework that
integrates large language models with established design methodologies to
enable dynamic multi-agent collaboration for problem reﬁnement, informa-
tion gathering, design space exploration, and evaluation. By incorporating

quantitative insights for early-stage conceptual design. Performance evalua-
tions across six public design challenges show that iDesignGPT achieves
competitive novelty and consistently higher originality and modularity than
GPT-4o zero-shot, GPT-4o chain-of-thought and Deepseek-r1, based on metrics

iDesignGPT as a practical framework for integrating language-model agents

Engineering system design plays a pivotal role in technological
advancement, serving as the bridge between scienti ﬁc research and
practical application 1,2. Conceptual design underpins the earliest
phase of development, where fundamental decisions on system
functionality, architecture, and feasibility are made 3,4. Critically, the
“80/20 rule” indicates that up to 80% of a product ’s lifecycle cost is
determined during conceptual design, with NASA studies showing
that poor early decisions led to 73% of total corrective costs in rocket
engine programs
5,6. Conceptual design demands high levels of crea-
tivity, structured reasoning, and the ability to navigate uncertainty
under incomplete information
7– 9.
Additionally, human-related factors, such as unclear
requirements10, tight deadlines 11, and the varying expertise levels of
designers12,13, complicate early-stage conceptual design process,
echoing recent discussions on democratizing design education and
practice14. Established engineering design methods 15, including
approaches such as Quality Function Deployment (QFD) 16 and the
Theory of Inventive Problem Solving (TRIZ) 17, were developed for
narrower industrial contexts and face limitations in evolving design
spaces as requirements and constraints are revised over time during
early-stage design. Thus, these methods often require high domain-
speciﬁc expertise and struggle to scale to multifaceted, inter-
disciplinary problems
18. Meanwhile, CAD, CAE, and CAM tools mainly
support detailed design and manufacturing, leaving conceptual
design critically under-supported
19. Even in domains such as
advanced air mobility, where recent tools improve early-stage
modeling and trade studies
20,21, exploration remains largely para-
meter driven and grounded in prior data, limiting discovery of fun-
damentally novel concepts. Consequently, engineers are still
required to manually reconcile diverse multimodal early-stage inputs

Core components of iDesignGPT
iDesignGPT, built on the fastGPT platform (Fig.2), integrates four core
components: a. Agents, b. Toolkit, c. Knowledge Base, and d. Web
front-end. Designers interact with the system via the Web front-end,
submitting requests to initiate the design process. The system utilizes
LLM-driven Agents to navigate four key stages: I. problem deﬁnition, II.
information gathering, III. concept generation, and IV. concept eva-
luation. During these stages, the system dynamically identi ﬁes and
reﬁnes constraints and integrates diverse design methods to generate
innovative solutions. At each stage, an Agent cluster collaborates
based on a prede ﬁned process. Powered by LLM ’s intelligence, the
Agents execute tasks by querying the Knowledge Base for local and
online access to relevant domain knowledge and by leveraging the
Toolkit to address complex tasks, ultimately facilitating the generation
of design solutions. This structured work ﬂow operationalizes key
design dynamics, including knowledge ﬂow and integration, through
LLM-powered agents.

Case study with GoAERO design challenge
To evaluate iDesignGPT ’s performance in engineering design chal-
lenges and illustrate its interactive usage, we selected the GoAERO
Design Challenge ( https://www.herox.com/goaero). Sponsored by
Boeing and other leading aerospace companies, this initiative seeks
groundbreaking solutions for a compact, autonomy-enabled rescue
aircraft that is both safe and easy to operate.
iDesignGPT’s design process (Fig. 3) mirrors the conceptual
design phase of engineering development procedures
52, where user
needs are translated into an early-stage Product Design Speciﬁcation
(PDS)53 and a corresponding conceptual design scheme. Initially, the
system enters the Copilot mode, involving users in natural language
dialogue to clarify design requirements and constraints. By supporting
multimodal inputs (e.g., documents and images), iDesignGPT enables
reﬁnement of the problem deﬁnition, incorporating user feedback to
regenerate and prioritize requirements based on their intent. Upon
transitioning to the Agent mode, the Analyst agent cluster employed
three parallel requirement analysis methods: Maslow ’s hierarchy of
needs
54, multidimensional (Performance, Time, Cost, Quality) analysis,
and KANO analysis55. The integrated results produced a comprehen-
sive customer requirements list, with dynamically assigned weights
reﬂecting both user-expressed priorities and system-inferred con-
textual importance. These weighted priorities directly informed the
development of robust evaluation metrics. Subsequently, the system
built a QFD
15 analysis, mapping customer requirements to engineering
characteristics in a matrix. This process quanti ﬁed the priorities of
engineering features and updated evaluation criteria, providing
actionable guidance for addressing complex design tasks.
To gather information, the Information Of ﬁcer agent cluster
retrieved domain-speciﬁc knowledge from multiple sources, including
Google Patents (Patent), Web of Science and Scopus (Articles), and
Red Dot Award-winning design repositories (Projects), while addi-
tionally recommending targeted journals classi ﬁed under JCR cate-
gories to guide further exploration. Retrieved information was
structured into a dedicated literature retrieval report provided to the
user for reference. This report informed the construction of the “2.
Market Identi ﬁcation” section within the evolving PDS, serving to
contextualize external market and technological trends without
directly embedding external content into theﬁnal conceptual designs,
thereby supporting the originality of subsequent outputs. This
ensured the integration of high-quality and relevant external
information.
During the concept generation stage, the Innovator agent cluster
utilized functional decomposition
56 validated by autonomous agents
to address the hallucination problem, ensuring logical consistency.
Designers reviewed and approved agent-validated results before gen-
erating functional decomposition graphs that captured material,
energy, and informationﬂows. Advanced concept generation methods
were then applied sequentially, including morphological analysis
57,
biomimetic design58, brainstorming59,S C A M P E R60,a n dT R I Z16,61 analy-
sis, which produced modular solutions, with each module presenting
three candidates enriched by optimization insights (see Supplemen-
tary Note S1 for detailed generation logic and representative outputs
from each concept method).
Finally, in the evaluation stage, the Evaluator agent cluster asses-
sed candidate solutions against prede ﬁned metrics. The highest-
scoring design was selected and summarized in a PDS, including
schematic diagrams generated through AI-based visualization (see
Supplementary Note S2 for detailed conceptual design scheme and its
PDS). To further evaluate the quality of theﬁnal output, the conceptual

Performance evaluation
To assess the design capabilities of iDesignGPT in comparison to
established workﬂows, we adopted a dual-evaluation strategy com-
bining objective metrics and expert-based assessments. The baseline
systems included GPT-4o in both zero-shot and chain-of-thought
(COT) prompting con ﬁgurations
22,66, as well as the latest reasoning
model Deepseek-r123. Evaluation was conducted across six publicly
available engineering design challenges sourced from organizations
such as NASA and the U.S. Department of Energy. These tasks were
selected to ensure diversity in scale, complexity, technical maturity
requirements, and degrees of design freedom. Full task descriptions
are provided in Supplementary Note S5. All systems received only the
competition guidelines as input, and no human intervention was
applied during the generation of conceptual design solutions.
Table 1 presents a comparative analysis using ﬁve objective
metrics, grounded in established engineering frameworks, to evaluate
design outcomes across the dimensions of Novelty, Originality,
Rationality, Technical Maturity, and Modularity. iDesignGPT con-
sistently demonstrated superior or competitive results. Speciﬁcally, it
achieved a median Level 3 invention on the TRIZ scale, a 0.4 median
patent similarity score, and the lowest component coupling ratio
(28%), indicating strong innovation, distinctiveness, and architectural
modularity. Deepseek-r1 showed the highest rationality score (93% CR
satisfaction), and higher technical maturity, but underperformed in
originality, indicating a trade-off between creativity and feasibility.
Efﬁciency, the sixth evaluation dimension, was excluded from Table1
due to the absence of a widely accepted quantitative metric but was
retained in expert evaluation via Fig. 5.

novice designers.

Methods
Framework supporting multiple agents
The implementation framework of iDesignGPT (Fig. 7), built on
fastGPT ( https://github.com/labring/FastGPT), is built for modular
integration of emerging design methodologies and AI advances. The
workﬂow is orchestrated using a ﬂow-based approach, where the
overall execution logic primarily adopts a programmed sequence with
a small-scale multi-agent design. This balances the orderliness of the
design process while striving to harness the emergent capabilities of
LLMs. The current implementation serves as one possible realization.
For user inputs, iDesignGPT integrates a safeguard agent that
ensures the appropriateness of design intentions. Potentially harmful
or socially risky requests are automatically rejected. Once the input is
deemed reasonable, the system presents an initial mode selection
interface, defaulting to Copilot mode.
In Copilot mode, iDesignGPT operates as a collaborative partner,
actively engaging with designers through a cluster of consultant
agents. These agents assist in clarifying design objectives by prompt-
ing iterative dialogue. Designers specify their requirements, while the
consultants probe deeper into details, encouraging exploration and
reﬁnement of design problems.
In contrast, Agent mode shifts towards automation, streamlining
the design process through four distinct agent clusters: analysts,
information ofﬁcers, innovators, and evaluators. Each cluster corre-
sponds to a key phase of the design process — problem de ﬁnition,
information gathering, concept generation, and evaluation. This
structured, automated approach guides the designer step-by-step by
an intent classi ﬁer and prede ﬁned prompts. Transparency is main-
tained by presenting intermediate analysis results and explaining the
reasoning behind decisions. Designers can intervene at any stage to

Workﬂow enabling design methods
The framework supports different design methods through three
primary workﬂow topologies:
 Star ﬂow is particularly suited for brainstorming scenarios
requiring diverse expert perspectives, emphasizing collaborative
reﬁnement of design insights. A host agent undertakes organiza-
tional and coordination tasks. At the beginning of each topic, the
identities of expert agents are de ﬁned based on the design pro-
blem. During each discussion round, the host provides targeted
questions to each expert agent and collects their responses. The
expert agents share their conversation history, ensuring that
insights are collaboratively reﬁned.
 Loop ﬂow is optimized for tasks requiring systematic validation,
such as functional decomposition, where iterative collaboration is
adapted to handle logical reasoning and ﬂowchart generation
tasks. To ensure accuracy and mitigate hallucination risks, a
generator agent collaborates with a checker agent. The checker
evaluates the generator ’s outputs against prede ﬁned criteria,
sending feedback for iterative improvement when necessary, until
all checks are satisﬁed. This iterative mechanism ensures robust
design outputs while addressing common LLM challenges, such as
overgeneralization or hallucinated reasoning
72.
 Sequential ﬂow supports stepwise methodologies like TRIZ,
where predeﬁned principles are systematically cross-referenced
and applied to resolve conﬂicts. A guider agent ﬁrst formats the
features involved in the design case and sends them to a
JavaScript program, which retrieves conﬂict-resolution principles
from TRIZ. These principles are then cross-referenced with a
principle database, and the resulting solutions are applied by an
analyst agent. This structured workﬂow simpliﬁes the application
of TRIZ principles, offering designers ef ﬁcient and context-
speciﬁc recommendations.
These workﬂow topologies exemplify how iDesignGPT adapts to
diverse design methodologies. A detailed description of agent roles
and workﬂows is provided in Supplementary Note S13 and illustrated
in Supplementary Figs. S19– S21.
Knowledge base
The knowledge base is composed of two main components: online and
local. The online component is designed for large-scale data retrieval
tasks, such as patent and academic literature searches. Patent searches
are conducted using the Google API, with ﬁlters applied to retrieve
only patent-speciﬁc content. Academic literature searches are facili-
tated by the Web of Science and Scopus API, where iDesignGPT can
customize search queries and set publication yearﬁlters.
The local component focuses on content-based semantic sear-
ches, which are critical for tasks where the content itself is central, such
as in design inspiration or biomimetic cases. This component includes
databases like the Red Dot-awarded designs from the past three years
and selected examples from the Ask Nature platform. Local data is
structured using RAG technology, where large text blocks are seg-
mented and indexed using the text-embedding-3-large model
73.S e a r c h
terms are further optimized by GPT-4o-mini to generate related
queries. Semantic searches are performed through vector matching,
with minimum similarity thresholds and text length constraints. The
matched paragraphs are incorporated into the prompts provided to
the agent.
Toolkit
Several specialized tools are inte grated into the system. During the
Web of Science paper retrieval, a Calendar tool automatically captures
the current date, ensuring that the API queries are restricted to rele-
vant publications within a speci ﬁed time range. For TRIZ analysis, a
Script Runner tool executes custom code, enabling matrix-based
searches and the retrieval of solution principles when features for
improvement or deterioration are speci ﬁed. In functional decom-
position, Mermaid generatesﬂowcharts by inputting markdown code
formatted to deﬁne the structure of the design process. Finally, in the
summary phase, key components such as functional modules, solution
approaches, and application scenarios are visualized using DALL-E 3,
which generates conceptual diag rams to complement the textual
content.
LLM integration
The framework is designed to support diverse LLMs, including state-
of-the-art models like GPT-o3 ( https://openai.com/index/o3-o4-mini-
system-card/) and open-source alternatives such as LLaMA 4.T h i s
adaptability ensures that iDesignGPT’s performance can continuously
improve alongside advancements in LLM capabilities. The selection of
speciﬁc models is guided by task requirements, balancing computa-
tional efﬁciency and model capabilities to optimize user experience. In
this study, we utilized two versions of OpenAI ’sm o d e l s :G P T - 4 oa n d
GPT-4o-mini. GPT-4o, with its superior language understanding and
generation capabilities, was primarily employed for complex tasks in
Copilot mode, such as in-depth interactions with designers. Con-
versely, GPT-4o-mini was selected for most tasks in Agent mode that
required processing large amounts of context at higher speeds, with
less stringent logical demands. To balance creativity and consistency
in generated responses, we adjusted the temperature parameter based
on task requirements. Detailed results are presented in Supplementary
Note S14.
At the core of each agent, the LLM is tasked with executing
operations based on four key components: Prompt, History, Data, and
Question, as illustrated in Fig. 7.
Prompt. As the most crucial element in the task execution, prompts
contain the following subcomponents:
Role.D e ﬁnes the identity and function of the agent within the
system. These roles are typically pre-deﬁned at the start of the task, but
in certain scenarios, such as during brainstorming sessions, the host
agent dynamically deﬁnes expert roles from various domains based on
the project’sn e e d s .
Background. Provides global variables and related stage results
necessary for task execution. This component is especially critical in
tasks that involve external API calls and tool usage, as it includes
detailed instructions on tool names, descriptions, invocation timing,
and operational steps.
Thought Process. Utilizes the chain of thought technique to break
down complex tasks into manageable steps, guiding the agent through
the problem-solving process to maintain logical consistency and
ensure accuracy in task completion.
Output.D eﬁnes the structure of the agent’s output to ensure that
the generated information can be ef ﬁciently processed and trans-
mitted, minimizing information loss or ambiguity during the
communication.
History. The History component restricts the number of prior con-
versation rounds accessible to the model, ensuring that the agent
maintains relevant and coherent context when executing the
current task.
Data. The data component is populated with information extracted
from the knowledge base through retrieval-based methods. This
allows the model to access and leverage domain-speci ﬁck n o w l e d g e ,
overcoming the limitations of the model ’s pre-existing training data
and compensating for gaps in design-speciﬁc knowledge or outdated
