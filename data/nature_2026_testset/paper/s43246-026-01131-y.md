kagome lattice as potential magnetic devices. By applying band structure calculations and angle-

The kagome lattice — a two-dimensional network of corner-sharing tri-
angles — has been intensively studied as a fertile platform for realizing
correlated and topological electronic phenomena1–3. Its unique geometry
gives rise to characteristic band featu res: linearly dispersive Dirac bands,
saddle points associated with van Hov e singularities (VHS), and a dis-
persionless ﬂat band. In the two-dimensional limit, the interplay of these
features with spin-orbit interaction under broken time reversal symmetry
can yield quantized anomalous Hall effect4–8. From the topological point of
view, the Berry curvature of the band-crossing point in the kagome magnets
has attracted attention for the emergence of large anomalous Hall effect9–14
and Nernst effect15–17. Not only ferro/ferrimagnets of Co 3Sn2S2
12,13,16 and
RMn6Sn6 (R = Lanthanide elements)14,18–20 but also antiferromagnets of
Mn3Sn10,15 and AV3Sb5 (A =K ,R b ,C s )21 have been found as prototypical
examples to exhibit distinct physical phenomena based on the electronic
band structures and/or characteristic magnetic structures. In particular, Co-
based kagome metals such as paramagnetic CoSn
22 and a Weyl magnet
Co3Sn2S2
12,13,16,23,24 exemplify how kagome-derived bands can mediate
anisotropic charge transport 22, orbital magnetism 23, and large intrinsic
anomalous Hall conductivity mediated by the Berry curvature near Fermi
energy (E
F)12,13,16,24. These enormous responses anticipated from the Dirac
and ﬂat bands have stimulated intensiveefforts to harness kagome metals
for functional devices.

Precise control of the fabrication process facilitates the stabilization of
single-crystalline D0
19-type Co3Mo thin ﬁlms. The kagome-derived ﬂat
bands in the ﬁlm, conﬁrmed by ARPES, agree well with theoretical calcu-
lations. As D019-type materials are abundant but not yet well studied for
magnetism, thin-ﬁlm research will enable us to develop kagome materials in
future material exploration. A room-temperature PMA in Co3Mo and its
further enhancement in Co3Mo1-xPtx (x = 0.17) evidence the characteristic
magnetocrystalline anisotropy in Co-kagome lattice and a promising
strategy for regulating the magnetic properties. Overall, kagome metals are a
valuable platform for engineering exoticﬂat-band-driven phenomena and
for developing spintronic deviceswith topological functionality.

Ab initio calculation
We performed structural optimization and electronic structure calculations
based on density functional theory using the OpenMX code 44.T h e
exchange-correlation functional was treated within the generalized gradient
approximation in the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof form45. Norm-conserving
pseudopotentials were employed, and the Kohn–Sham wave functions were
expressed as linear combinations of pseudoatomic orbitals, with the basis
sets Co6.0H-s3p2d1 and Mo7.0-s3p2d2 selected for Co and Mo atoms,
respectively. Structural optimizationof atomic positions and lattice para-
meters was carried out under a ferrimagnetic con ﬁguration within non-
relativistic calculations, using the quasi-Newton method until the residual
forces were reduced below 0.01 eV/Å per atom. Subsequently, fully relati-
vistic calculations including spin-orbit interaction
46 were conducted to
obtain the electronic structures of b oth ferrimagnetic and paramagnetic
states. A cutoff energy of 1200 Ry was set for the Fourier transform grid, and
the Brillouin zone was sampled using ak-point mesh of 31 × 31 × 31.

Angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES)
After the thin ﬁlms were fabricated in a vacuum chamber using the sput-
tering technique, they were extracted into air and quickly placed in a
vacuum-sealed tube. Theﬁlms were transferred to the Photon Factory of the
High Energy Accelerator Research Organization (KEK-PF) with a vacuum-
sealed tube, attached to the ARPES sample holder in the glovebox, and then
installed into the ARPES chamber without exposure to air. A clean surface
was obtained by annealing the ﬁlm at 800 °C for 3 h. Soft-x-ray-ARPES
measurements were carried out with an Omicron-Scienta SES2002 electron
analyzer with energy-tunable synchrotron light at BL-2A in KEK-PF. We
used linearly polarized light (horizontal polarization) of 250–1020 eV, with
an energy resolution of 100–170 meV. All the ARPES measurements were
conducted at T = 50 K in the ferrimagnetic phase.
Magnetization measurement
Magnetic ﬁeld dependence of magnetization was measured at T = 300 K
by VersaLab (Quantum Design Inc.). Temperature dependence of
magnetization below T = 350 K was measured by a magnetic properties
measurement system (Quantum Design Inc.) with ﬁeld cooling of
μ
0H = 3 T parallel to the c-axis. Above 350 K, magnetization was mea-
sured by a vibrating sample magnetometer (Tamakawa Co. Ltd.). The
magnetic ﬁeld dependence of magnetization in each apparatus was
consistent with each other.
Electrical measurement
Resistivity and Hall effect were measured by VersaLab and Physical Prop-
erties Measurements System (Quantum Design Inc.). The ﬁlms were
manually scratched to a Hall-bar shape with attaching In electrical contact.
X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS) and x-ray magnetic cir-
cular dichroism (XMCD)
X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS)and x-ray magnetic circular dichro-
ism (XMCD) were conducted at BL-7A in KEK-PF. For the XAS and
XMCD measurements, the photon helicity wasﬁxed, and a magneticﬁeld of
±1.2 T was applied parallel to the incident polarized soft-x-ray beam to
obtain signals deﬁned as μ+ and μ− spectra. The beam and magneticﬁeld
were introduced at normal incidence to the sample surface. The total elec-
tron yield mode was utilized to measure the drain currents, which detect
beneath 5 nm with exponential decay. The partial ﬂuorescence yield was
also utilized with a silicon drift detector to capture signals from tens of
nanometers. The measurements were carried out at room temperature and
80 K using a liquid nitrogen cryostat.
