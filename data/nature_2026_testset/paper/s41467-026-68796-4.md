Spin-crossover complexes are promisingcandidates for molecular bits owing
to their magnetic bistability. However, it’s a formidable challenge to turn
densely packed spin-crossover molecules into high-density bits due to coop-
erative switching dynamics inherent in spin-crossover aggregates. Herein,
coordination-ﬁeld engineering is demonstrated by scanning tunneling
microscopy/spectroscopy and density functional theory as an efﬁcient strat-
egy to modulate the cooperative dynamics of surface-conﬁned spin-crossover
chains. Within these chains, Ni centers exhibit collective spin transitions upon
local stimuli. This spin-crossover cooperativity can be disrupted by
coordination-ﬁeld modiﬁcation via hetero-metal/ligand doping, which con-
verts partial switchable coordination centers into non-switchable nodes. These
nodes divide the single-bit-like spin-crossover chain into multiple
independently-switchable segmentsand thereby augment the bit density.
One-by-one erasable write-in of these spin-crossover bits is achieved by
scanning tunneling microscope tip manipulation. These results highlight how
to precisely tweak the cooperative dynamics of the spin-crossover aggregates
to advance their potential applications for high-density memory.
Magnetic bistability underpins modern information storage and
processing technologies1–3. Among various mechanisms that induce
magnetic bistability, molecular spin-crossover (SCO) has attracted
sustained interest since its discovery in the 1930s
4–8. SCO molecules,
primarily 3d metal-organic complexes, can reversibly switch between
low-spin (LS) and high-spin (HS) states upon external stimuli
9–18. This
transition is accompanied by changes in coordination geometry, with
the HS (LS) state exhibiting longer (shorter) metal-ligand
distances
19–21. This magneto-structural correlation introduces inter-
molecular elastic interactions when SCO molecules are densely
packed, driving cooperative spin transitions across SCO
aggregates
22–32. Such SCO cooperativity enables sharp and global
responses of bulk SCO materials to local excitations, making them
promising for high-speed and low-energy-consumption molecular
memory devices.
However, as device sizes shrink toward the single-molecule limit
to meet the demand for ultrahigh-density information storage, the
cooperativity becomes detrimental: It forces collective switching and
hinders independent control of individual molecules
33–38,t h e r e b y
rendering reduced memory densities. Therefore, precise control of the
SCO cooperativity at the molecular level is crucial for advancing SCO-
based data storage.

Here, we employ coordination- ﬁeld engineering as an ef ﬁcient
strategy to suppress collective spin transitions in SCO aggregates and
enhance their memory densities. The SCO phenomena arise from a
balance between the 3d electron paring energy and the ligand- ﬁeld
splitting, which yields comparable energies for different spin states of
the complex and hence its magnetic bistability
9. Once this delicate
balance is perturbed, spin transitions become energetically unfavor-
able, producing a non-switchable ground state. When snugged into
SCO ensembles, these non-switchable centers would introduce rigid
(rather than elastic) interactions with other SCO sites, thereby dis-
rupting the SCO cooperativity. To achieve this, hetero-metal/ligand
dopants can be utilized to modify the coordinationﬁeld of partial SCO
centers, converting them into non-switchable ones.
To conduct proof-of-principle demonstration, a surface-conﬁned
SCO coordination chain reported in our previous works
39,40 is hereby
employed as a model system. In these chains, Ni centers are bridged by
the dehydrogenated 1,2,4,5-tetrahydroxybenzene (THB) ligands
(denoted as the O-ligands) which couple the spin transitions of
neighboring Ni centers and induce their collective SCO dynamics
(Fig. 1a). Combined scanning tunneling microscopy/spectroscopy
(STM/STS) and density functional theory (DFT) studies reveal that
precise coordination-ﬁeld modi ﬁcation, by varying either the metal
center (e.g., Ni to Fe) or the coordinating atom in ligands (e.g., O to N),
converts the SCO Ni centers into non-switchable nodes that disrupt the
SCO cooperativity along the chain (Fig.1b). As a result, the SCO chain is
divided into multiple independently-switchable segments, each of
which individually functions as a binary bit, leading to reduced SCO bit
size and boosted memory density. Afterwards, one-by-one erasable
write-in of these SCO bits is demonstrated by STM-tip manipulation.
These ﬁndings open up a route to ef ﬁciently control cooperative
dynamics of the SCO aggregates with atomic precision, shedding light
on their potential applications in high-density information storage.

In summary, this study demonstrates that coordination- ﬁeld
engineering is an efﬁcient strategy to control cooperative dynamics of
the SCO aggregates. The Au(111)-supported coordination chains con-
taining the SCO Ni
O centers serve as a prototypical scaffold to
experimentally explore cooperative spin transitions triggered by local
STM-tip stimulations. Doped with hetero-metal (Fe) or hetero-ligand
(N-ligand), the coordination ﬁeld of the SCO centers is modi ﬁed,
resulting in non-switchable Fe
O or NiN nodes that divide the NiO chains
into independently-switchable NiO segments. Each segment functions
as a binary bit, which allows multiple bits to be incorporated in an
individual chain and thereby augments the memory density. One-by-
one erasable write-in of these bits can be achieved by STM-tip
manipulation. These ﬁndings exemplify atomically precise control of
cooperative dynamics in surface-conﬁned SCO systems, providing a
promising avenue to improve the performance of molecular memory
devices towards ultrahigh-density information storage.
Methods

vacuum STM at a base pressure below 3 × 10

were afterwards annealed at 420~430 K to initiate the dehydrogenated
coordination reaction. All STM images were acquired in constant
