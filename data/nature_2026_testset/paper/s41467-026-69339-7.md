Activating the type I interferon response in tumor cells and enhancing T cell-
mediated anti-tumor immunity have broad clinical applications in tumor
immunotherapy. However, the detailed mechanisms underlying the antitumor
immune response and type I interferon response in nasopharyngeal carcinoma
(NPC) remain unclear and require further elucidation. In this study, we identify
CD38 in NPC cells as a key mediator impairing T cell antitumor immunity.
Mechanistically, CD38 induces mitochondrial autophagy through PHB2,
enhances the interaction between PHB2 and MAVS, leading to the degradation
of MAVS protein, and inhibits the type I interferon response and CD8
+Tc e l l -
mediated anti-tumor immunity. Importantly, CD38 promotes tumor progres-
sion and reduces the proportion of CD8+T cells and IFNγ+CD8+T cells in vivo via
MAVS. In conclusion, theseﬁndings reveal previously unrecognized roles and
mechanisms of CD38 in regulating anti-tumor T cell immunity, suggesting that
inhibition of CD38 could initiate tumor-targeted immune responses, enhance
anti-tumor immunity in patients, and provide new therapeutic strategies
for NPC.

response in NPC through the type I interferon response. First, we

inhibits the type I interferon re sponse, alters the tumor micro-

interferon responses. Our ﬁndings demonstrate that CD38 mediates
type I interferon signaling responses and tumor-in ﬁltrating T cell-

therapeutic insights and strategies for anti-tumor immunotherapy.

Tumor-intrinsic CD38 promotes autophagic degradation of
MAVS to inhibit type I interferon response
To explore the role of CD38-MAVS interaction in regulating RLR-
mediated type I IFN signaling.We transfected Flag-CD38 into the 5-8 F
and CNE2 cells for Western blot and immunoﬂuorescence analyses. The
results showed that CD38 protein expression was inversely correlated
with MAVS protein levels, with MAVS protein expression decreasing as
CD38 levels increased (Fig. 5A, B, Supplementary Fig. S11A,11B). To
conﬁrm that MAVS downregulation was not due to changes in MAVS
mRNA levels. We performed RT-qPCR experiments in 5-8 F and CNE2
cells and found no signiﬁcant change in MAVS mRNA abundance with
varying CD38 expression (Fig. 5C). Since eukaryotic cells use three
major systems to control protein de gradation: the proteasome, lyso-
some, and autolysosomal pathways
31. To identify the degradation
pathway responsible for CD38-mediated MAVS degradation. We per-
formed Western blot analysis using inhibitors of different protein
degradation systems in 5-8 F and CNE2 cells. The results showed that the
use of autophagy inhibitors 3-methyladenine (3MA), chloroquine (CQ),
and baﬁlomycin A1 (Baf) blocked CD38-mediated MAVS degradation,
while the proteasome inhibitor MG132 did not (Fig.5D). This indicates
that CD38 promotes MAVS degradation via autophagy.
To investigate whether CD38 induces autophagy, we used
Western blot to analyze the protein expression of autophagy markers
LC3II and p62 in 5-8 F and CNE2 cells. The results showed that CD38
overexpression upregulated LC3II and downregulated p62 levels,
and CQ further increased LC3II levels, indicating enhanced autop-
hagic ﬂux (Fig. 5E, F). Given that MAVS primarily functions on the
mitochondrial outer membrane and CD38 is also localized in mito-
chondria (Fig. 4C, D). Meanwhile, laser confocal microscopy revealed
that knocking down CD38 signi ﬁcantly enhanced the localization of
MAVS on mitochondria in 5-8 F cells (Fig. 5G). Therefore we hypo-
thesized that CD38 inhibits MAVS protein expression by inducing
mitochondrial autophagy. To verify this hypothesis, we isolated
mitochondrial proteins from 5-8 F and CNE2 cells for Western blot
analysis. The results showed that CD38 promoted LC3II expression
(Fig. 5H). Meanwhile, laser confocal microscopy experiments in 5-8 F
cells revealed that overexpression of CD38 promoted the colocali-
zation of LC3B (green) and MitoTracker (red) (Fig. 5I). Transmission
electron microscopy further demonstrated an increase in mito-
chondrial autophagosomes following CD38 overexpression in 5-8 F
cells (Fig. 5J). Additionally, overexpression of CD38 also decreased
the expression of mitochondrial outer membrane proteins TOMM20,
VDAC1, and COXIV in 5-8 F and CNE2 cells (Fig. 5K). These results
indicate that CD38 induces mitochondrial autophagy.
To con ﬁrm that CD38 degrades MAVS through mitochondrial
autophagy, we treated 5-8 F and CNE2 cells with the mitophagy inhi-
bitor Mdivi-1. We found that Mdivi-1 treatment reversed CD38-induced
MAVS protein degradation and restored CD38-induced inhibition of
IFN-β promoter activity (Fig.5L, M). These results collectively indicate
that CD38 promotes MAVS degradation through mitophagy, thereby
inhibiting type I IFN responses.
Tumor-intrinsic CD38 niduces mitochondrial autophagy
through PHB2, leading to MAVS protein degradation and inhi-
bition of type I interferon response
Mitochondrial autophagy is initiated by speciﬁc autophagy receptors
located mainly on the outer mitochondrial membrane (OMM) 32.A s
CD38 itself is not an autophagy receptor, we hypothesized that CD38
may interact with a mitophagy receptor to trigger mitochondrial
autophagy and degrade MAVS protein. Mass spectrometry identiﬁed
PHB2 (Prohibitin 2) as a potential interacting partner of CD38. PHB2 is
a mitochondrial inner membrane protein known to function as a
mitophagy receptor in mammalian cells
33–35. Co-IP and Western blot
analysis conﬁrmed the interaction between CD38 and PHB2 in 5-8 F
and CNE2 cells (Fig.6A, Supplementary Fig. S12A). However, CD38 did
not alter the mRNA or total protein levels of PHB2 (Fig. 6B, C, Sup-
plementary Fig. S12B, S12C). Laser confocal microscopy and mito-
chondrial fractionation showed that CD38 enhanced the
mitochondrial localization of PHB2 (Fig. 6D, E) and promoted PHB2
recruitment of LC3II, thus inducing mitophagy (Fig.6F). Furthermore,
interfering with PHB2 blocked the upregulation of LC3II induced by
