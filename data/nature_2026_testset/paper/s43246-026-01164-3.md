Lithium lanthanum titanium oxide (LLTO) is a promising solid electrolyte (SE) for solid-state batteries,
owing to its excellent chemical and thermal stability, wide electrochemical window, and high ionic
conductivity even at room temperature. However, the inﬂuence of lattice dynamics and local structural
features on lithium transport remains poorly understood. In this study, we employ large-scale
machine-learned molecular dynamics (MLMD) simulations alongside inelastic neutron scattering (INS)
measurements to reveal the role of strongly anharmonic soft phonons in Li-ion diffusion. Unlike many
other materials where soft phonons facilitate ion transport, we ﬁnd that in LLTO, these phonons
suppress Li diffusion by inducing tilt or rotational distortions in the lattice. These distortions constrict
the bottlenecks that signiﬁcantly hinder ionic conductivity. Our results show that stabilising these soft
phonon modes and thereby restricting the oxygen dynamics, combined with the inherent
anharmonicity of Li-related vibrations, can substantially enhance Li diffusion. Thus, in perovskite-
based SEs, soft phonon modes and oxygen dynamics emerge as critical descriptors for designing
advanced electrolytes. Additionally, our investigation of lattice thermal conductivity using the Green-
Kubo formalism reveals glass-like thermal transport behaviour, arising from the disordered structure
and extremely anharmonic soft modes.

Besides ionic conductivity perfor mance, effective thermal manage-
ment is critical for solid-state batteries because their inherently low thermal
conductivity hinders heat dissipation, increasing the risk of localised over-
heating and system failure
62– 64. While most research on LLTO has focused
on its ionic conductivity, experimental or theoretical data on its lattice
thermal conductivity are notably absent. Conventional methods for pre-
dicting lattice thermal conductivit y are challenged by the presence of
vacancies and strong phonon anharmonicity in this material. Owing to their
near-DFT accuracy, machine-learning forceﬁelds enable the Green-Kubo
(GK) method to reliably predict the lattice thermal conductivity of strongly
anharmonic solid electrolytes, ev en in the presence of vacancies and
defects40,53,55,65– 73. Therefore, we employed an MLMD-based GK method to
calculate the lattice thermal conductivity of LLTO. Our simulated lattice

genide and oxide-based solid electrolytes, an advantageous characteristic for
SE applications

and transport properties, will prov ide valuable insights for the under-
standing and improved design of solid-state batteries.

Role of Li-vacancies and TiO6 octahedral dynamics on Li diffu-

Anharmonic phonons and lattice thermal conductivity
Thermal management is crucial for solid electrolytes in batteries due to their
inherently low thermal conductivity, which limits heat dissipation and poses
risks of localised overheating 62– 64. Solid electrolytes have low, glass-like
thermal conductivity, leading to suppressed heat transport via atomic
vibrations, which can result in temperature increases and system failure
during operation. Proper thermal m anagement ensures optimal ionic
conductivity by maintaining stable temperatures, as lithium-ion diffusion is
temperature-dependent. It also prevents material degradation and interface
instability between electrodes and electrolytes, which are exacerbated by
high temperatures. Furthermore, effective thermal regulation mitigates
thermal expansion mismatches that ca n fracture brittle perovskite solid
electrolytes and ensures uniform heat distribution during fast charging
cycles.
In crystalline solid electrolytes the lattice thermal conductivity is largely
governed by phonons, and shows inverse relation (~1/T) with temperature
within three phonon scattering framework, whereas in glassy systems the
temperature dependency becomes very weak
63. We should mention that not
only the temperature dependency, butalso the magnitude of lattice thermal
conductivity and associated transport mechanism are very different in
crystalline and glassy systems
40,45,63,113– 115. In general, a solid electrolyte shows
glassy-type lattice thermal conduct ivity due to various reasons such as
strongly anharmonic phonons, vacancies, sublattice disorder, and phonon
breakdown etc54,64,72,116– 118.
In this context, although LLTO is extensively investigated for its ionic
transport, the thermal transport studies are missing. The presence of
vacancies and strong phonon anharm onicity in that system limits the
applicability of perturbative methods to estimate the thermal transport.

Conclusions
Using large-scale machine-learned molecular dynamics simulations and
inelastic neutron scattering measurements, we have comprehensively
investigated the anharmonic lattice dynamics in Li
0.29La0.57TiO3.W e
showed that the spatial variation in crystal structure due to static (i.e.
local structure) or dynamic disorder leads to the distribution of diffusion
bottlenecks of higher and lower area. For long-range diffusion, the for-
mation of a lower-dimensional bottleneck matters the most. Even a tiny
fraction of the presence of smaller bottlenecks signi ﬁcantly hinders the
long-range diffusion. We showed that the presence of soft modes asso-
ciated with TiO
6 octahedral rotational-dynamics and Li vibrations plays
a crucial role in Li diffusion. At low and moderate temperatures, these
modes get destabilised, leading to TiO
6 rotational-distortion and forming
a local structure re ﬂecting as narrower bottlenecks for Li-diffusion.
However, at elevated temperatures, anharmonic stabilisation of these
modes leads to the average structure, minimising the rotational distor-
tions, results in the widening of the bottleneck area and enhances the Li-
diffusion. Further, by selectively constraining oxygen dynamics, we
found that structure distortions can be tuned and show enhanced Li
diffusion. Hence, by suitable chemistry or external stimuli, one can sta-
bilise these soft modes and achieve tuneable ionic conductivity. There-
fore, this knowledge of rotational-distortion, local structural effect and
strongly anharmonic soft phonons will be helpful in designing and
tuning the ionic conductivity of perovskite solid electrolytes. We found
