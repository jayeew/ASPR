Non-Hermitian skin effect (NHSE) is a distinctive phenomenon in non-Hermitian systems, marked by
the accumulation of eigenstates at system boundaries. While well understood in one dimension,
unraveling the NHSE in higher dimensions is challenging due to the diversity of lattice geometries.
Here, we present a geometry-adaptive non-Bloch band theory in arbitrary dimensions, through the
lens of spectral potential. Our formulation precisely determines the energy spectra and generalized
Brillouin zone in the thermodynamic limit, unveiling their geometric dependency. We establish exact
spectral relations that elucidate the geometric dependence of non-Bloch spectra and their connection
to amoeba spectra. Moreover, we show that critical skin modes in higher dimensions exhibit scale-free
localization along the boundary, thereby making the spectrum highly sensitive to the system size and
boundary ratios. We demonstrate that these critical skin modes lead to spectral non-convergence and
instability. Our ﬁndings pave the way toward a uniﬁed understanding of NHSE and non-Bloch bands in
arbitrary dimensions.
Non-Hermitian physics is a rapidly growing ﬁeld1–5 that explores
systems with non-Hermitian Hamiltonians. It spans various classical
wave systems, including photonics
6–12, acoustics 13–15, mechanics 16–19,
and electrical circuits 20–24, and plays a key role in quantum dynamics of
open systems 25–29. The breaking of Hermiticity leads to a myriad of
intriguing phenomena absent in Hermitian systems. Notably, the
energy spectra become highly sensitive to boundary conditions, with
eigenstates pushed towards systems ’boundaries under open boundary
conditions (OBC). This phenomenon, known as the non-Hermitian
skin effect (NHSE)
30, has been extensively studied 11,16,20,21,31–42, due to its
departure from Bloch band theory and its broad promising
applications43–46.
Dissecting the NHSE in one dimension (1D) led to the develop-
ment of non-Bloch band theory 30,47, which predicts the asymptotic
energy spectra (i.e., non-Bloch spectra) under OBC in the thermo-
dynamic limit (TDL) without severe numerical errors. By analytically
continuing the Bloch Hamiltonian H(k) → H(β = e
ik), the skin locali-
zation of eigenstates is described by the generalized Brillouin zone
(GBZ). Yet, in higher dimensions, the diversity of OBC signi ﬁcantly
complicates the NHSE and spectral structures. The skin modes and non-
Bloch spectra may vary with geometry
48–52. In the Amoeba
formulation53, geometric information is not taken into account, and a
geometry-independent non-Bloch spectrum and its corresponding GBZ
are obtained through the minimization of the Ronkin function. To date,
a comprehensive framework governing non-Bloch bands and the NHSE
in different geometries is still lacking. Key questions remain: given a
Bloch Hamiltonian H(k), how can the non-Bloch spectra and skin
modes be determined for speciﬁc lattice geometries? This theoretical gap
has sparked ongoing debates in the literature
52–57. For instance, do non-
Bloch spectra and GBZ depend on geometry? What are the key features
of critical skin modes in higher dimensions, and what physical con-
sequences do they entail? Furthermore, a crucial issue overlooked in
prior studies is spectral convergence and stability. It remains unclear
when non-Bloch spectra exhibit instability and how it relates to the
behavior of skin modes.
In this article, we address these questions by developing a
geometry-adaptive non-Bloch band theory for arbitrary dimensions.
Through a hierarchical formulatio n of spectral potential, our theory
yields the asymptotic non-Bloch spectra and GBZ in the TDL for
regular geometries, showing that these quantities depend on lattice
shapes. In our formulation, geometri c information such as the lattice-
cut directions is incorporated through the choice of momentum
basis, so that the resulting potent ial function varies with geometry
and is always no greater than that in the Amoeba formulation. We
introduce a theorem and a conjectur e to establish spectral relations
across different geometries. The theorem shows that the non-Bloch

Critical skin modes and spectral instability
The localization behavior of skin modes is far more complex in higher
dimensions. For instance, in two dimensions, beyond corner skin modes,
one may also encounter critical skin modes at boundaries for reciprocal
non-Hermitian systems
48. Our geometric adaptive non-Bloch band theory
accurately captures the energy spectrum and localization length for con-
ventional (exponentially localized) skin modes; however, it ceases to apply in
such critical cases, where the boundarylocalization is no longer character-
ized by a single, well-deﬁned exponential decay length. To illustrate this, let
us consider a 2D model under rhombus shape with Hamiltonian48

system size, the wave-packet broadening (obtained via Gaussian ﬁtting

respect to the boundary length is shown in Fig. 4b. The width scales
linearly with L, in sharp contrast to corner modes or plane waves, whose
broadening is size-irrelevant.

dimensions. Unlike scale-free states in 1D

Conclusions
To summarize, we present a uni ﬁed geometry-adaptive non-Bloch band
theory for non-Hermitian systems in all dimensions. Our formulation
determines the spectral density, and GBZ in the TDL for regular lattice
shapes, indicating their geometric dependency. Spectral relations between
different geometries are established through a theorem and a conjecture. We
further show that the critical skin modes feature scale-fee localization along
the boundary and yield spectral non-convergence and instability. Our fra-
mework offers a solid foundation for comprehending non-Bloch bands,
NHSE, their geometry-dependence and stability.
Our results reveal an important distinction from Hermitian systems in
higher dimensions: the geometric shape affects the continuum energy bands
in intriguing ways. Our formulation focuses on dominant skin modes of
order O(L
d)i n dD, while subleading modes like hybrid skin-topological
modes69,70 or higher-order skin modes61,71–73 of order O(Lj)( j < d) are not
explicitly included in the spectral potential. Incorporating these subleading
modes into the non-Bloch band theory remains an open question. Given the
prevalence of non-Hermitian systems in photonic, acoustic, mechanical,

Several remarks are in order. Firstly, our formulation of spectral
potential is conducted hierarchically ford≥2D. No prior assumptions are
made about the higher-dimensional Szegö’s limit theorem74,75, considering
that it cannot be applied to the occupied spectral region. This differs from
the Amoeba formulation
53, where certain minimization of Ronkin’sf u n c -
tion is assumed. Secondly, we note that the spectral potential depends on the
speciﬁc choice of geometry. Through the Poisson equation in Eq. (3), the
spectral density of the system under this geometry in the TDL is obtained.
Different lattice cuts may yield different spectral potentials. Consequently,
different geometries can exhibit distinct spectral ranges and density. This
sharply contrasts with the spectra obtained from the Amoeba formulation,
which is geometry-independent. Thirdly, the only assumption in our for-
mulation is the spectral convergence, i.e, the stabilization of systems ’
eigenspectra and eigenstates for largesystem sizes. Our theory then predicts
that they would converge to the non-Bloch spectra and skin modes in the
TDL. However, this assumption is not valid when the system exhibits scale-
free localization, where the localization length of skin mode scales with
system size. This represents a critical case, where the eigenspectra highly
depend on system size and boundary ratios and exhibit instability in the
presence of perturbations. Thus, the non-Bloch spectra in the TDL are not
well-deﬁned solely with the information on the lattice-cut directions.
