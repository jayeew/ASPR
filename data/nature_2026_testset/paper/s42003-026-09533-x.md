Plasticity is a central mechanism underlying the robust regenerative capacity of the intestinal
epithelium. Two major forms of plasticity have been described: spatial plasticity, in which
differentiated cells revert to crypt base columnar cells (CBCs), and fetal reversion into revival stem cells
(revSCs). However, the relationship among these two stem cell populations and differentiated cells
remains to be clari ﬁed. Here, we demonstrated the bidirectional interconversion between CBCs and
revSCs. Using lineage tracing, injury models and villus culture, we show that absorptive enterocytes
can reprogram into revSCs and regenerate CBCs. These ﬁndings position fetal reversion as an entry
point to spatial plasticity, establishing a regenerative hierarchy where CBCs, revSCs, and enterocytes
collectively orchestrate intestinal repair. Furthermore, we identi ﬁed revSCs as a highly stress-tolerant
stem cell population, whose emergence would preserve the stem cell pool. Our results establish fetal
reversion as a cellular escape mechanism safeguarding epithelial regeneration under in ﬂammatory
conditions.
The intestinal epithelium exhibits a remarkable capacity for regeneration
following injury. Crypt base columnar stem cells (CBCs), located at the base
of the intestinal crypts of Lieberkühn, govern homeostasis in normal
intestinal epithelium
1. These intestinal stem cells are de ﬁned by the
expression of markers such asLgr52 and Olfm42,3. As they migrate from the
crypt base toward the villus tip, CBCs differentiate into the various cell types
that comprise the intestinal epithelium, including absorptive enterocytes
(approximately 90% of epithelial cells) and secretory lineages, such as goblet,
Paneth, enteroendocrine, and tuft cells
4.
In response to epithelial injury thatleads to the loss of CBCs, multiple
differentiated epithelial lineages can replenish the CBC pool5–9 by moving
back along the crypt-villus axis towardthe crypt base and dedifferentiating
to reacquire stemness, thereby regenerating CBCs— a process collectively
referred to as spatial plasticity5,10,11. More recently, revival stem cells (revSCs)
— a population transcriptionally re miniscent of fetal progenitors — have
been identiﬁed as key players in tissue regeneration12. This phenomenon,
now referred to as fetal reversion12–14, is under intense investigation in the
context of in ﬂammatory bowel diseases (IBD) 15,16 and colorectal cancer
(CRC)17,18. The intestinal epithelium is thus emerging as a therapeutic target
beyond immune modulation 19,20 and understanding the molecular and
cellular mechanisms of regeneration is crucial in translational medicine.
Despite recent advances, the relationship between spatial plasticity and fetal
reversion remains to be clariﬁed. In particular, reconstruction of the CBC
pool from differentiated cells has been reported in several epithelial
tissues
5–9, and computational inference suggests that multiple cell types
contribute to regeneration through fetal reversion21,22. Therefore, experi-
mental validation with ﬁne lineage tracing of CBCs, revSCs, and differ-
entiated lineages in physiologicall y relevant models, analyzed within
regenerative hierarchies, is essential for a comprehensive understanding of
the overall mechanisms of intestinal regeneration. Moreover, the biological
advantages conferred by such dynamic cellular transitions during regen-
eration remain poorly understood.

and revSCs, conﬁrming the ability of CBCs to generate revSCs, and vice

Lineage tracing experiments revealed fetal reversion potential of
differentiated enterocytes in vitro
To understand the entire process of fetal reversion, the contribution of non-
CBCs is not negligible as shown abov e. Accumulating evidence has also
demonstrated that various differentiated epithelial cell types can contribute
to stem cell pool restoration via spatial plasticity5–7,9,44. RNA velocity analysis
of our scRNA-seq data predicts a directional shift from absorptive enter-
ocytes toward revSC-like states. Previous studies have also reported that
Alpi⁺ enterocytes can dedifferentiate into CBCs in the context of Lgr5⁺ CBC
ablation. Based on these ﬁndings, we hypothesized that this dedifferentia-
tion process may occur via a fetal-reversion-like intermediate state. To
experimentally conﬁrm this lineageﬂexibility, we performed in vitro lineage
tracing using Alpi
CreER/+;R o s atdTomato/+ mice. Matrigel organoids established
from proximal small intestine were labeled with tdTomato following
4-hydroxytamoxifen (4-OHT) treatment, thereby marking Alpi+ enter-
ocytes localized to the villus domain and their progeny (Fig.3A, B). These
organoids were serially passaged in Matrigel or collagen. While tdTomato+
cells gradually disappeared during passaging in Matrigel, they were stably
retained in collagen organoids, suggesting survival or expansion of lineage-
traced cells (Fig.3B, C). Flow cytometric analysis further revealed that these
tdTomato+ cells in collagen organoids expressed Ly6a, a hallmark of
revSCs, at comparable levels to tdTomato− cells (Fig. 3D), indicating that
revSCs derived from Alpi+ enterocytes are molecularly indistinguishable
from other sources of revSCs. To further examine whether these revSCs
could reconstitute homeostatic intestinal epithelium, tdTomato+ collagen
organoids were replated into Matrigel. As a result, budding organoids re-
emerged, and the entire organoid stru cture, including the crypt domain,
were labeled tdTomato + (Fig. 3B). Immuno ﬂuorescence analysis con-
ﬁrmed the presence of multiple lineages, including Olfm4+ CBCs, Muc2+
goblet cells, Lyz+ paneth cells, Chga+ enteroendocrine cells, Dclk1+ tuft
cells, and Fabp1+/Alpi+ absorptive enterocytes (Fig. 3E), indicating that
enterocyte-derived revSCs restored a fully functional intestinal epithelial
architecture in vitro.
These ﬁndings provide direct evidence that differentiated absorptive
enterocytes can undergo fetal reversion in niche-dependent manner and
subsequently regenerate functional CBCs, implying a lineage plasticity that
contributes to epithelial regeneration.

Fetal reversion renders inﬂammatory stress tolerant
Given that both CBCs and differentiated cells, including Alpi+ enterocytes,
can undergo fetal reversion to become revSCs — a transient and
inﬂammation-associated stem cell state — we hypothesized that revSCs
possess a functional advantage in stresstolerance. To test this, we examined
the response of organoids to inﬂammatory stress induced by 5-FU. Mouse
and human small intestinal organoids were cultured in either Matrigel or
collagen and treated with 40 µM 5-FU from day 4 to day 6 (Fig.6A). In both
species, Matrigel organoids exhibited marked growth inhibition following
5-FU treatment, whereas collagen org anoids retained their proliferative
capacity (Fig. 6B, C). As previously reported, various types of stress —
including irradiation and 5-FU treatment— not only induce fetal reversion
but also selectively expand the revSC population 12, demonstrating their
remarkable stress tolerance. These ﬁndings functionally validate that col-
lagen organoids recapitulate the revSC state. To explore potential
mechanisms underlying this resilien ce, we performed differential gene
expression analysis using scRNA-seq data from mouse organoids. Com-
pared to CBCs (cluster2 and cluster3) , revSCs (cluster1) exhibited sig-
niﬁcant upregulation of genes associated with cellular stress protection,
including glutathione S-transferase family members
49, Bcl2 family genes50,
and heat shock protein family genes 51 (Fig. S5A). Gene set enrichment
analysis (GSEA) was performed to ev aluate pathway-level alterations
between revSCs and CBCs and to capture the global landscape of cellular
states (Supplementary Data 2). This analysis highlighted three major
pathway groups that together outlin e a stress-tolerant phenotype char-
acteristic of revSCs: detoxiﬁcation and oxidative stress pathways, damage
tolerance and protein quality-control pathways, and in ﬂammatory-
response pathways (Fig. 6D). Importantly, these pathway signatures were
consistently enriched in the human dataset as well, where revSCs (cluster 4)
similarly exhibited signiﬁcantly higher activation of these stress-resilience
modules compared with CBCs (cluster 1) (Fig.6E), collectively supporting
the notion that revSCs are transcriptionally primed for in ﬂammatory
resilience.
To further understand the stress tolerance of revSCs, we performed
bulk RNA-seq on Matrigel organoids and collagen organoids treated with
5-FU. Heatmap analysis revealed that Matrigel organoids exhibited broad
transcriptional alterations follow ing 5-FU exposure, whereas collagen
organoids displayed a more restricted response in both mouse and human
(Fig. 6F, G; Supplementary Data 3 and 4). Matrigel organoids responded to
5-FU with strong induction of DNA-replication and mitotic programs,
together with differentiation-associated pathways. These features reﬂect a
damage-driven, proliferation-dependent response characteristic of a stress-
sensitive epithelial state, in which cells attempt to compensate for injury by
accelerating proliferation and progressing toward differentiation. In con-
trast, collagen organoids did not simply upregulate proliferative programs.
Instead, they exhibited controlled modulation of the cell cycle, accompanied
by prominent induction of cytoskeletal-stabilization and metabolic-
rewiring pathways (Fig. 6F, G). Collectively, these responses indicate that
collagen organoids activate a stress-adjusted, injury-tolerant state, enhan-
cing structural robustness and metabolicﬂexibility rather than defaulting to
proliferation.
Discussion

reversion in the intestinal epitheliu m, revealing a stress-adaptive regen-

CBCs and enterocytes— to preserve stem cell function under injury (Fig.7).

differentiated cells and fetal reversion during in ﬂammation, how these

of their transitions, has yet to be fully understood.
