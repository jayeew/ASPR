Discussion
Our results indicate that the historic drought conditions in the US
during the late 1980s were likely an important driver for ﬁsheries
declines during subsequent years via reduction in the Mississippi River
ﬂow, nutrient inputs, and phytoplankton production. Prolonged
extreme droughts anticipated during the 21st century are expected to
result in substantial declines of biomass and catch in the GoM
(Figs. 2 and 3), which are substantially more severe compared to pre-
vious estimates
1,30,31.

a primary driver of ecosystem productivity, such bottom-up forcing is
expected to have particularly broad consequences. While we
acknowledge this distinction as a source of uncertainty when com-
paring the relative magnitudes of drought and ﬁshing effects, the
strong drought effects observed in our simulations remain consistent
with empirical evidence for bottom-up control in the region, lending
conﬁdence to the qualitative direction of our ﬁndings.

The impact of prolonged droughts is especially important when
considering the life cycle of estuary dependent groups, such as Gulf
menhaden and penaeid shrimp, which are short lived (mode <2 yrs)
and are dependent on viable estuaries and delivery of nutrients to
coastal areas. For such species, three consecutive years of poor
recruitment due to unfavorable environmental conditions severely
reduces stock productivity. Moreover, this poses a threat to the entire
ecosystem, given the role of these groups as energy pathways from
primary producers to higher trophic predatory species (Table S1). This
threat brings forward the possibility of a drought-induced regime
shift
48 in species composition, where low trophic level species that are
not estuary dependent, e.g., jelly ﬁsh, become more dominant. Such
jellyﬁsh dominance could have cascading effects, as blooms reduce
prey availability for planktivorous ﬁsh and compete with the larval
stages of many ﬁsh species for zooplankton resources, potentially
impairing recruitment success and altering trophic energyﬂow49.
The declines in biomass and catches projected in our study for the
GoM are more severe than those projected by previous global
ensemble marine ecosystem model simulations, which projected
declines of ~20 – 30% in biomass and catches at the end of the 21st
century50. These were mainly attributed to a rise in SST, which aug-
ments ocean stratiﬁcation, and reduces the availability of nutrients in
the upper ocean 50. It is likely that a combined effect of reduced
nutrient input from the Mississippi River applied in our study, and the
effects associated with upper ocean warming will result in an additive
reduction of productivity in the GoM ecosystem. Other aspects of
climate change, such as sea-level rise and ocean acidi ﬁcation
51,52,a r e
likely to concurrently impact the GoM ecosystem, which may lead to
additional habitat loss and biomass declines
1. The effect of droughts
on the GoM ecosystem, combined with these additional stressors may
lead to negative additive or synergistic impacts53, which may increase
the risk of ﬁsheries collapse in the 21st century50,54.
Other stressors in the GoM are related to the excess rather than
the deﬁcit of nutrient input, which leads to eutrophication and hypoxic
conditions in estuaries and large areas at the mouth of the Mississippi
River
55, causing mortality and deleterious conditions for a wide range
of marine organisms 56. Historical data and previously published
studies57, however, suggest that the positive effect of high nutrient

The exact mechanisms by which drought and reduced river ﬂow
affect the downstream marine ecosystem should be further studied in
the GoM and elsewhere. Similarly, the balance between de ﬁcit and
excess nutrient input, as well as the mechanisms by which they affect
the marine ecosystem, should be examined to support informed
decision-making and policy development. Ef ﬁcient water, land-use,
and ﬁsheries management strategies should be developed to alleviate
this severe threat and maximize the sustainability ofﬁsheries. Adaptive
management strategies, such as harvest limitations during drought
periods and increased harvest during wet periods, should be carefully
considered and tested through simulation studies to evaluate the
potential impacts onﬁsheries biomass and catch. The observed milder
declines and faster recovery of ﬁsheries following the 2006 and 2012
droughts, for example, suggest that reduced ﬁshing effort during
drought periods may play a signiﬁcant mitigating role (supplementary
note S1, Figure. S4). Future work will focus more on other Repre-
sentative Concentration Pathway (RCP) scenarios, emphasizing the
consequences of greenhouse gas (GHG) emission mitigation and
management. Furthermore, the potential effects of future droughts on
marine ecosystems in other regions and their interactions with addi-
tional environmental stressors, such as harmful algal blooms, should
be investigated. This research is particularly vital in areas that largely
depend on ﬁsheries for nutrition and livelihood.

Ecosystem modeling: Ecopath and Ecosim (EwE) analysis and
data sources
We used the US Gulf-wide Ecopath with Ecosim (EwE) model 4,6 to
examine the possible effects of variability in nutrient input and Gulf
menhaden ﬁshing mortality on the GoM ecosystem. EwE
66 is an eco-
system modeling framework that is widely used for exploring eco-
system trophic dynamics, and the effect of environmental and
anthropogenic stressors on the ecosystem, namely ﬁshing pressure.
The modeling framework is based on two main components: (1) Eco-
path, a static mass-balanced snapshot representing the ecosystem ’s
food web and static trophic interactions
66 and (2) Ecosim, a temporal-
dynamic model expressed through a series of differential equations
simulating ecosystem dynamics over time
67. Our analysis was con-
ducted in Ecosim rather than Ecospace because our focus was on
temporal dynamics at the scale of the entire GoM ecosystem, for which
spatially explicit modelling was not required. We also did not include
species-speciﬁc environmental response functions; instead, drought
forcing was implemented through changes in nutrient input and pri-
mary productivity. Future work m ay extend this approach with Eco-
space to explore spatial heterogeneity.
The model area represents all state and federal waters of the US
GoM continental shelf and coastline (down to a bottom depth of
~400 m). The GoM food web model includes 78 trophic groups,
including three marine mammal groups, separate aggregate groups
for seabirds and sea turtles, eight elasmobranch groups, 52ﬁsh groups,

Nutrient input and primary production in EwE
Ecosim accounts for bottom-up drivers on primary production using a
Michaelis-Menten nutrient uptake model. Speciﬁcally, the EwE mod-
eling framework assumes that the total nutrient concentration can be
divided between that which is available (free) and unavailable for
uptake (e.g., bound in biomass). The total nutrient content is com-
puted as the ratio between the total nutrient in ﬂow rate from all
nutrient loading sources, and the total loss rate considering all loss
pathways (e.g., advection, sedimentation, and harvest). The Ecopath
base estimate of total nutrient concentration is calculated by the EwE
software based on the biomass pool value, its ﬁxed nutrient content
per unit of biomass pool, and the free nutrient concentration
68.

Ecosim simulations
Four types of simulations were conducted (Table S6). First, Biomass
and catches comparison hindcast + projections: main runs (33 runs)
examined functional group biomass and catch under varying nutrient
input and ﬁshing pressure levels for historical (1980s drought) and
projected (2050, 2100) drought scenarios. Second, Biomass and cat-
ches comparison hindcast + projections: error bars (258 runs) calcu-
lated the standard deviation in biomass and catch based on the
uncertainty of the CMIP5 climate projections used, derived from the
5th con ﬁdence interval percentile of the CMIP5 PDSI projections.
Third, Biomass and catches comparison: tradeoff analysis (231 runs)
assessed biomass and catch across a range of nutrient input and
menhaden ﬁshing pressure multipliers to investigate trade-offs
between these factors. Finally, Sensitivity analysis of number of
drought years (66 runs) evaluated the inﬂuence of drought duration (1-
year vs. 3-year droughts) on the ecosystem under projected future
climate conditions (supplementary note S5, Fig. S5). All projected
drought and ﬁshing scenarios were implemented as isolated 5-year
simulations layered onto the 2014 – 2016 baseline conditions, with
forcing applied throughout the run and the terminal (ﬁfth) year used
for analyses. This design allowed transitional dynamics under directed
forcing to stabilize while remaining ecologically realistic. Both Gulf
menhaden ﬁshing mortality ( F) and purse-seine ﬂeet effort were
implemented as forcing functions, with their values scaled by multi-
pliers in the scenarios to reﬂect changes in ﬁshing pressure and asso-
ciated technical interactions (e.g., bycatch). Background climate
variability beyond the applied drought and ﬁshing scenarios was not
included.

Model limitations
While the historical and projected ﬁsheries declines are robust and
systematic across the entire trophic range, it is important to note the
limitations and uncertainties of the reference data and the modeling
framework. For example, most groups’ biomass reference data are a
product of stock assessment models, which consider processes such
as age-speci ﬁc natural mortality, growth, recruitment, and ﬁshing
mortality
37. These models, like others, encompass inherent uncertainty
Article https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-69116-6
Nature Communications|         (2026) 17:2409 9

<!-- GEAR_PAGE: 10 -->

and are based on assumptions or best available data concerning the
species biology and life history. However, they do represent the only
source of absolute biomass for the entire GoM. To partially address
this limitation, we cross-checked temporal trends against independent
observational datasets, including standardized CPUE indices from the
Gulf menhaden purse-seine ﬁshery and ﬁsheries-independent trawl-
based juvenile abundance indices from Louisiana estuaries (supple-
mentary note S1). While differences in design and coverage precluded
full quantitative validation, these comparisons provided qualitative
consistency checks. In addition, assessment output data are smoothed
relative to observed data, which may result in the loss of high fre-
quency variability. On the other hand, the thorough revision of data
and model outputs enhances quality control and mitigates unrealistic
ﬂuctuations that may arise from data derived from methods such as
bottom trawl surveys
71. Catch data also harbors uncertainties related to
unreported or misreported catches, as well as uncertain estimations of
recreational landings. Furthermore, factors unrelated to environ-
mental conditions and ﬁshing pressure, such as management regula-
tions, can signi ﬁcantly in ﬂuence population and ecosystem
dynamics
38.
Limitations of the US Gulf-wide EwE model include lack of con-
sideration of spatially explicit processes such as migration or spatially
explicit mortality, for example of harmful algal blooms and hypoxic
zones. In our model, hypoxia was not explicitly implemented, partially
because our domain is Gulf-wide, and the hypoxic zone is localized
near the mouth of the Mississippi River. However, in a previous study
57,
a spatially explicit EwE model for the northern GoM implemented
hypoxia as well as nutrient input 72, and concluded that the positive
contribution of nutrient input on ﬁsheries production outweighs the
negative effect from hypoxic conditions. Consistent with this,
empirical data (Fig. S2e) show that hypoxic area declines sharply
during extreme drought years (e.g., 1988, 2000, 2012) and recovers
once drought conditions diminish, suggesting that lagged effects are
weak or absent. Our simulations used primary productivity anomalies
as a proxy for drought forcing and did not explicitly capture
match– mismatch dynamics with spawning or recruitment. Ecosim can
incorporate seasonal egg production forcing, and future work should
test such interactions to better represent drought effects on early life
stages. In addition, we did not explicitly incorporate salinity – habitat
interactions in our modeling framework. While salinity increases dur-
ing droughts can stress estuarine-dependent invertebrates (e.g.,
oysters, shrimp)
19, the ecosystem-wide declines observed here were
reproduced based solely on nutrient forcing, supporting a parsimo-
nious focus on trophic mechanisms. Part of the uncertainty in our
model is attributed to knowledge gaps with respect to the life history
and traits of functional groups in the GoM ecosystem, limited diet
(stomach content) data, as well as limited capacity in correct char-
acterization of highly migratory species. In addition, limitations exist in
the taxonomic resolution for marine mammals, seabirds, and turtles as
well as in the limited time series available for model calibration for
these groups. Nevertheless, the US Gulf-wide EwE model characterizes
the diverse GoM ecosystem and incorporates extensive reference data
(161 reference and forcing time series), many of which correspond well
with model predictions
6.
