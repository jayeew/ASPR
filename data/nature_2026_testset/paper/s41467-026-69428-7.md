solid oxide fuel cells, ion membranes, sensors and other advanced

Temperature dependent XRD patterns (Fig. 4a) directly reveal the
phase transition behavior in the NCNZ-Na x samples. It is observed
that the NCNZ-Na 0.96 sample transitions from orthorhombic ( Pbma,
Pnmm, Ccmm) to tetragonal ( P4/mbm) and then to cubic ( Pm-3m)

volume changes are related to NbO

sion. Besides, the temperature-dependent dielectric constant and
loss are presented in Fig.4b–g. In the temperature range from 300 °C
to 600 °C, the NCNZ-Na
x samples display two distinct phase transi-

antiferroelectric Pbma (P) phase to the orthorhombic ferroelectric
Pnmm (R) phase, and the tetragonal P4/mbm (T

Pm-3m (U) phase, respectively 43,44. Prominently, intense phase tran-
sition peaks of T2-U (~560 °C) are observed in the NCNZ-Na x samples
with increasing Na-deﬁciency (Fig. 4b–d). The formation of vacancies
caused by Na-deﬁciency results in signi ﬁcant lattice contraction and
oxygen octahedral distortion. These vacancies are highly susceptible
to temperature variations, with signi ﬁcant changes in oxygen octa-
hedral and unit-cell parameters as the temperature increases,
thereby giving rise to distinct phase transition peaks. Differently, the
characteristics of the T

with increasing Na content (Fig. 4f and g). This is because interstitial
Na+ ions compensate for lattice expansion caused by temperature
increase. However, as the temperature increases, the interstitial Na +
ions are thermally activated and a more pronounced behavior of
increased dielectric loss is observed in the T
2-U phase transition
region (Fig. 4f and g).

In this work, the synergistic regulation of Na+/O2− ionic conduction
was effectively achieved in the Na 0.96xCa0.04Nb0.96Zr0.04O3-δ solid-
state ionic conductor by employing an A-site non-stoichiometric
strategy. The correlation between the torsion and distortion patterns
of NbO6 octahedra and the conduction behavior of Na + and O2− ions
was elucidated. With rising Na content, the defects transformed from
vacancies to interstitials. The NbO

from ﬂat to normal and then to twisted andﬂattened, which led to the
expansion of the corresponding interstices of Na-O-Na and Na-O-Nb

effectively regulated by the changes in the NbO6,N a - O - N a ,a n dN a - O -
Nb polyhedral structures. Given this promising insight, a comprehen-
sive re-evaluation of other perovskite-type materials is imperative,
which provides guidance for designing and optimizing functional
materials with tailored ionic and electronic transport properties.
Methods
Preparation of ceramics
Polycrystalline ceramics of Na0.96xCa0.04Nb0.96Zr0.04O3-δ (abbreviated
as NCNZ-Nax, x = 0.96, 0.98, 0.99, 1, 1.01, and 1.02) were prepared by a
traditional solid-state reaction. Carbonate, NaCO3 (99.8%), and CaCO3
(99.8%), and oxide, Nb2O3 (99.9%), and ZrO2 (99.99%) were purchased
from Shanghai Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd., CN, and
used without further puriﬁcation. The raw materials were dried in an
oven at 180 °C for 8 h before weighing to reduce hygroscopic carbo-
nates. Accurately weigh these reagents according to the molar ratio,
and suspend them in ethanol, and then mill in a planetary ball mill for
12h using zirconia balls. The following calcination was conducted in a
covered alumina crucible at 850 °C for 2 h. To guarantee a homo-
geneous distribution, the calcined powders were ball-milled again for
12 h. The prepared powders have been uniaxially compacted into
pellets (diameter 12 mm) with a pressure of ca. 25 MPa and pressed
cold isostatically at 200 MPa. Finally, the pressed NCNZ-Na
x pellets
were covered with the same composition powders and then sintered at
1250 ~ 1350 °C for 2 h at a heating rate of 3 °C/min.

The working electrodes were manufactured by coating and sintering

ﬂat surface, the sample was ultrasonically cleaned several times with
ethanol. Then, Ag or Pt electronic paste was coated on both sides of the
samples and cast at room temperature. Finally, the prepared electro-
des were sintered at 850 °C for 15 min. The dielectric properties were
tested in the temperature range from 25 °C ~700 °C at different fre-
