Computational spectroscopy is a powerful tool across numerous disciplines,
yet its development remains constrained by limited bandwidth and low light
throughput of spectral encoders, which are inherent to optical materials and
design frameworks. Here we overcome these challenges using transition metal
dichalcogenides (TMDCs), leveraging their high refractive index (n >4 )a n d
broadband transparency to achieveoptical manipulation. This enables
broadband spectral encoders that modulate light from the visible to short-
wave infrared while maintaining light throughput above 65%. These char-
acteristics originate from the high-index-contrast interface with optical feed-
back, which extends optical path length and augments the photon density of
states, accelerating multi-beam phase accumulation within TMDCs. By inte-
grating encoders with InGaAs photodetectors, we construct a snapshot spec-
trometer with remarkable performance, featuring a wavelength accuracy of
0.02 nm, a spectral resolution of 1 nm, and a detection limit below 1 nW. We
further demonstrate the application of this spectrometer in material identiﬁ-
cation, highlighting its versatility for real-world deployment.

N-H bonds. To demonstrate our spectral encoder ’s functionality, we
integrate them with InGaAs photodetectors to construct a SWIR
spectrometer.

In summary, we demonstrate spectral encoders operating from
the visible to SWIR with a high light throughput (>65%), achieved
through single TMDC ﬂake. This approach fully exploits the unique
optical properties of TMDCs, particularly their highn across a broad-
band transparency window. The synergistic combination of high-
index-contrast interfaces and low-loss propagation accelerates phase
accumulation within TMDCs, facilitating broadband spectral modula-
tion by merely tuning TMDCs thickness. We further integrate
thickness-variable PdSe
2 ﬂakes with InGaAs photodetectors to develop
a compact spectrometer. This spectrometer achieves an excellent
wavelength accuracy (0.02 nm) and a high spectral resolution (<1 nm),
enabling precise spectral discrimination for material sensing and
imaging applications. In further, the bandwidth of full visible-to-long-
wave infrared coverage can be realized through integration with
ultrabroadband photodetector array.
