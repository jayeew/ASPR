The demand for high-performanceﬁlters in next-generation wireless com-
munication systems underscores the limited electromechanical coupling of
aluminum nitride-basedﬁlm bulk acoustic resonators. Scandium incorpora-
tion enhances piezoelectricity but is hindered by crystallinity degradation and
polarity inversion. Theoretical modeling reveals the polarity inversion inter-
face between aluminum nitride and scandium-doped aluminum nitride as a
key cause of piezoelectric degradation. This work proposes a dual-
optimization strategy that improves crystalline alignment and eliminates
polarity mismatch. A single-crystalline aluminum nitride seed layer promotes
high c-axis oriented scandium-doped aluminum nitrideﬁlms, yielding reso-
nators with a maximum quality factor of 736. Subsequent seed layer removal
eliminates the polarity inversion interface, raising the effective electro-
mechanical coupling coefﬁcient of resonators from 6.0% to 13.2%. Filters
fabricated with this strategy achieve a center frequency of 6.4 GHz, a 3 dB
bandwidth of 740 MHz, and out-of-band rejection above 40 dB, indicating
potential for Sub-7 GHz communication systems.
The rapid development of emerging applications such as the Internet
of Things, smart homes, augmented reality, and virtual reality has
dramatically increased the performance requirements for wireless
communication systems
1,2. These applications require high data
throughput, minimal latency, and improved reliability, which places
considerable pressure on current network infrastructures. In response
to these evolving needs, wireless communication technologies have
progressively shifted toward higher-frequency operation, particularly
within the Sub-7 GHz spectrum. This spectrum offers an optimal
balance between coverage and capacity, making it a key enabler for
next-generation systems, including 5G New Radio, emerging 6 G
networks
3,4, and advanced wireless local area networks such as Wi-Fi
75– 7. To support these systems, high-performance radio frequency
(RF) front-end modules are essential, withﬁlters playing a central role
in maintaining signal ﬁdelity by selectively transmitting desired fre-
quency bands while suppressing out-of-band interference 8.A m o n g
the available ﬁltering technologies, ﬁlm bulk acoustic resonators
(FBARs) have garnered increasing attention for Sub-7 GHz applica-
tions due to their achievable high working frequency, high quality
factor (Q), and low insertion loss ( IL)
9,10.

choice of piezoelectric material, which determines both the acoustic

Both AlN and ScAlN ﬁlms can exhibit either N-polarity ([000-1]
orientation) or Al-polarity ([0001] orientation). Critically, the polarity
behavior of these ﬁlms is fundamentally governed by the arrange-
ment of Al and N atomic layers along the c-axis (Fig. 1b). Al-polarity
corresponds to atomic stacking along the + c direction, whereas
N-polarity corresponds to stacking along the −c direction. In AlN and
ScAlN ﬁlms, especially AlN/ScAlN bilayer structures essential for
FBARs, the piezoelectric response is critically dependent on the
polarity alignment between layers. Polarity mismatch at the stack
markedly degrades performance, therefore, this study speci ﬁcally
investigates the inﬂuence of polarity states in AlN and ScAlNﬁlms. To
accurately predict the material and device performance for sub-
sequent simulations, ﬁrst-principles calculations are employed to
evaluate thin- ﬁlm properties at varying Sc doping concentrations
(supercell models shown in Fig. S1) and the detail simulated results
are listed in Tables S1 and S2. As illustrated in Fig. 1c, these calcula-
tions reveal that the longitudinal piezoelectric coef ﬁcient ( e

details provided in Methods and Supplementary Note 1).
Based on ﬁrst-principles calculations of AlN and ScAlN thin-
ﬁlm materials to evaluate Sc-doping effects on piezoelectric proper-
ties, the polarity-dependent behavior of AlN/ScAlN heterostructures is
further analyzed through a bilayer model. In this framework, the
AlN/ScAlN structure is treated as an equivalent two-phase composite,
with its effective electromechanical properties analyzed using both
the Reuss model and Mori-Tanaka method
32– 34. Detailed calculation
procedures are provided in the Methods and Supplementary Note 2.
The AlN layer is treated as the matrix, while the ScAlN layer, char-
acterized by enhanced piezoelectricity and reduced stiffness, is con-

accurate estimation of the effective electromechanical properties,
particularly when the volume fraction of the inclusion phase is high or
when there is a large disparity in the material properties of the two
phases

Atomic-scale structure and polarity alignment at the MOCVD AlN/
Sc0.2Al0.8N interface are examined by HRTEM and integrated differ-
ential phase contrast scanning transmission electron microscopy
(iDPC-STEM). Cross-sectional HRTEM imaging (Fig.3a) reveals a sharp
interface featuring a thin transition layer between the AlN seed and the
overlying ScAlN ﬁlm. High-magniﬁcation analysis of regions A and B
(Fig. 3b, d) shows well-ordered (002) lattice fringes in both layers, with
measured interplanar spacings of 0.246 nm for AlN and 0.255 nm for
ScAlN. With the latter ’s expansion con ﬁrming lattice distortion
induced by Sc incorporation. Corresponding Fast Fourier transform
(FFT) patterns exhibit clear, symmetric diffraction spots, verifying
good crystallinity in both layers. The sharper reﬂections from the AlN
seed layer indicate its well-deﬁned single-crystalline quality. At atomic
resolution, iDPC-STEM imaging distinctly resolves the stacking
sequences of Al and N atomic columns, clearly demonstrating oppo-
site polarities across the interface. The ScAlN layer exhibits N-polarity
(−c direction) while the AlN seed layer maintains Al-polarity (+ c
direction) (Fig. 3c, e). Signi ﬁcantly, despite this observed polarity
reversal within the AlN/Sc
0.2Al0.8N bilayer, high-resolution TEM ana-
lysis reveals no evident lattice defects or structural discontinuities,
indicating coherent epitaxial alignment and interfacial compatibility.
The observed polarity inversion between the Al-polarity MOCVD
AlN seed layer and the overlying N-polarity PVD ScAlN ﬁlm arises
from inherent growth technique dependencies and scandium-
induced lattice modi ﬁcation. Speci ﬁcally, the MOCVD process
favors Al-polarity AlN formation under the employed deposition
conditions, while PVD deposition intrinsically stabilizes the
N-polarity orientation in ScAlN
28,40– 42. Concurrently, the incorpora-
tion of larger Sc atoms distorts the wurtzite lattice, altering surface
energetics to further promote N-polarity stabilization during ScAlN
growth on the Al-polarity template, and a mechanism requiring
focused investigation in future studies26,40,43. However, as indicated in
Fig. 1, introducing the oppositely polarized AlN seed layer sig-
niﬁcantly compromises key resonator metrics. Despite this draw-
back, the seed layer remains indispensable for improving the
crystalline quality of ScAlN ﬁlms. To resolve this fundamental trade-
off between crystalline quality and polarity alignment, subsequent
sections explore fabrication process optimizations aimed at enhan-
cing FBAR device performance, with particular emphasis on polarity
conﬁguration and AlN seed layer crystallinity control.

Discussion
This investigation integrates theoretical modeling, thin- ﬁlm char-
acterization, and device-level measurements to clarify how the quality
and polarity of AlN seed layers and ScAlN ﬁlms affect microstructural
characteristics and electromechanical performance, as well as how
these elements jointly govern the ultimate operational performance of
FBAR devices. Theoretical frameworks employing the Reuss and Mori-
Tanaka methodologies demonstrate that the conﬁguration of polarity
is pivotal to the electromechanical response of AlN/ScAlN bilayer
systems. Antiparallel polar states at the bilayer structure induce partial
cancellation of internal electric ﬁelds, leading to a signi ﬁcant dimin-
ishment in d

t , k2
ef f . Our theoretical framework (Reuss and Mori-
Tanaka models) quantiﬁes how antiparallel polarity at the AlN/ScAlN
bilayers catastrophically degrades electromechanical coupling (Fig.1d,
e), directly linking polarity mismatch to the observed K2
t reduction.
This cancellation effect stems fro m opposing spontaneous polariza-
tion vectors, disrupting charge transfer ef ﬁciency across the hetero-
interface, as corroborated by STEM imaging (Fig.3).
Critically, the MOCVD single-crystalline AlN seed layer addresses
crystallinity limitations endemic to sputtered polycrystalline seeds.
Its epitaxial template suppresses AOG formation and yields ScAlN
ﬁlms with exceptional orientation and smoothness. This aligns with
reports that low-defect acoustic scattering, explaining the higher Q
in devices using single-crystalline seeds
19,54,55. However, crystallinity
alone is insuf ﬁcient because polarity inversion persists at bilayer
structures (Fig. 3), constraining k2
ef f . Our innovation lies in selective
seed layer removal, a tactic absent in prior ScAlN FBAR studies. This
physically decouples crystallinity and polarity management; the
MOCVD template guides high-quality ScAlN growth but is removed
before electrode deposition to eliminate the polarity-mismatched
structure. The resulting k
2

that the performance loss is due to polarity cancellation, not to Sc
doping or intrinsic material properties. This approach outperforms
polarity-control methods relying solely on doping, which introduce
additional defects
30.
Finally, the optimized FBARs were integrated into lattice-typeﬁl-
ters, yielding impressive results. Relative to prior ScAlN-based ﬁlters,
this work demonstrates signi ﬁcant advancements in bandwidth and
selectivity metrics, stemming from enhanced k2
ef f and Q in the con-
stituent FBAR resonators. While this study emphasizes engineering
innovations and microstructural performance analysis, future studies
will incorporate deeper investigations into underlying physical
mechanisms. These outcomes validate the fundamental linkage
between seed layer engineering and device performance, and further
highlight its potential for high-performance RF components in
advanced communication systems.
In summary, this study demonstrates a dual-optimization strat-
egy for ScAlN-based FBARs that resolves the con ﬂict between crys-
tallinity and polarity alignment. While a MOCVD single-crystalline
AlN seed layer is essential for achieving high-quality ScAlN ﬁlms, it
introduces a polarity inversion that degrades electromechanical
coupling. This limitation is mitigated through post-deposition seed
layer removal informed by theoretical modeling and STEM analysis.
This approach yields FBARs with about 43% higher k
2
ef f than con-
ventional devices. Filters incorporating these FBARs show excep-
tional performance with 6.4 GHz center frequency, −2.6 dB insertion
loss, 740 MHz bandwidth, and >40 dB rejection, which validates our
methodology. Beyond polarity engineering, this work presents an
approach for piezoelectric heterostructures that integrates epitaxial
seeding, post-processing, and polarity-aligned multilayer archi-
tectures to optimize performance in Sub-7 GHz applications such as
ﬁlters, sensors, and energy harvesters.

Film deposition and characterization
Polycrystalline AlN seed layers are deposited by PVD using reactive
magnetron sputtering. The deposition is carried out in an Ar/N 2
mixed atmosphere with gas ﬂow rates of 20/100 sccm at a working
pressure of 2.8 mTorr. The substrate temperature is maintained at
200 °C, and an RF power of 10 kW is applied to sustain a stable
plasma during ﬁlm growth.
Single-crystalline AlN seed layers are epitaxially grown by MOCVD
using trimethylaluminum (TMA) and ammonia (NH3) as the aluminum
and nitrogen precursors, respectively. The growth is performed on Si
substrates at low temperature of 1120 °C and high temperature of
1330 °C under a chamber pressure of 5500 Pa, with TMA and NH
3 ﬂow
rates of 300 and 1000 sccm, respectively, to promote high-quality
epitaxial crystallization.
Sc
0.2Al0.8N ﬁlms are subsequently deposited by reactive magne-
tron sputtering using a Sc-Al alloy target containing 20 at.% scandium.
The process is conducted in an Ar/N
2 atmosphere with gasﬂow rates of
24/120 sccm at a working pressure of 2.8 mTorr. The substrate tem-
perature is maintained at 200 °C, and an RF power of 15 kW is applied
to achieve stable plasma conditions and uniform ScAlN ﬁlm growth.
Detailed deposition parameters are listed in Tables S3 and S4 (Sup-
plementary Note 3).
Surface morphologies are characterized by AFM (Bruker Dimen-
sion Icon) operated in PeakForce Tapping mode. Cross-sectional spe-
cimens are prepared by focused ion beam (FIB) milling using Solaris
GMH and Helios CX G5. Conventional TEM is performed on a JEOL JEM-
F200 operated at 200 kV, while atomic-resolution imaging is carried
out on a spherical-aberration-corrected TEM (Thermo Scienti ﬁc
Spectra 300) operated at 300 kV in iDPC mode. XRD analyses are
conducted on a Bruker D8 Advance diffractometer operated at 40 kV
and 40 mA.
Device fabrication and characterization
FBAR fabrication (Fig. S7) begins with deposition of an AlN seed layer
and ScAlN ﬁlm on a Si substrate. A Mo layer is subsequently deposited
and patterned by inductively coupled plasma (ICP) etching to form the
bottom electrode. An amorphous silicon (a-Si) sacriﬁcial layer is then
deposited and patterned to deﬁne the resonant cavity. A SiO
2 support
layer is deposited and planarized by chemical mechanical polishing
(CMP) to enable wafer bonding. The device wafer is bonded to a cap
wafer through Au-Au interfaces, after which the Si substrate is com-
pletely removed to achieve ﬁlm transfer onto the cap wafer. The AlN
seed layer is selectively removed by ICP etching to expose the ScAlN
layer. Subsequently, via holes are opened by ICP etching to selectively
remove the AlN/Sc
0.2Al0.8N stack (or only the Sc 0.2Al0.8N layer) in
predeﬁned regions. A Mo layer is then deposited and patterned to
form the top electrode. Finally, release holes are etched, and the
sacriﬁcial layer is removed by XeF
2 dry etching to form the resonant
cavity, completing device fabrication. The process details can be seen
in Supplementary Note 4.
Electrical characterization of the fabricated devices is performed
using ground-signal-ground (GSG) and ground-signal-signal-ground
(GSSG) probes on a probe station (Cascade), in conjunction with a
vector network analyzer (VNA, Agilent N5222B) to measure the fre-
quency responses.
