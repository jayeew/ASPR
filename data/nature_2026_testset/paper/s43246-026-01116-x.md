Memristors are nonlinear dynamic devices with applications in memory and neuromorphic computing.
As memristors scale down to sub-2 nanometer thickness, atomic design is crucial for controlling
switching dynamics. Here, we show the design of sub-2 nanometer MgO/Ga
2O3/Al2O3 memristors
with a bilayer structure consisting of a switching layer and an oxygen vacancy layer using in vacuo
atomic layer deposition to control the number and sequence of atomic layers. A switching layer of
intrinsic aluminum oxide provides the best switching speed, increasing by up to two orders of
magnitude as the number of layers decreases from 12 to 5. Introducing oxygen vacancies in the
switching layer offers little beneﬁt and reduces on/off ratio and yield. The oxygen vacancy layer of 5– 7
atomic layers strongly in ﬂuences switching dynamics, with an interposed magnesium oxide/gallium
oxide structure enabling switching faster than 50 nanoseconds. These results show that atomic layer
design critically controls memristor switching dynamics.
Traditional von-Neumann computing architectures1 rely on a shared bus
between the central processing unit or CPU and memory. This creates a so-
called von-Neumann bottleneck which becomes particularly cumbersome
when large amounts of data are required to be processed for tasks such as
image recognition or artiﬁcial intelligence (AI)
2,3. Neuromorphic computing
(NC) as a brain-inspired computing paradigm has gained increased interest
in recent years for its potential to re move this bottleneck through devel-
opment of networks of artiﬁcial neurons and synapses where processing and
memory storage occur in the same location instead of being separate as in
the von-Neumann architecture
4,5. Development of arti ﬁcial neurons and
synapses that can have high switchingspeed, high endurance, high energy
efﬁciency, and scalability is imperative to NC. In addition, neuromorphic
computing speciﬁcally requires devices that support analog tunability with
multi-level conductance states6, uniform performance in large-scale arrays,
as well as spike-timing-dependent pl asticity in spiking neural networks,
enabling faithful synaptic emulation for unsupervised learning as in biolo-
gical neural networks7,8.
This has motivated research efforts in memristors devices 9,10,w h i c h
have been identiﬁed as one of the most promising candidates for artiﬁcial
neurons and synapses8. In memristors, the operations of both non-volatile
memory storage and low-power computing can be integrated in one device,
taking advantage of the computing strengths of a neural system for pattern
recognition and unstructured data sorting 11–14. Memristors offer fast
switching speed, high energy efﬁciency, and excellent scalability15–19.F u r -
thermore, switching speed and scalability can be enhanced through atomic-
layer engineering strategies, such as optimizing the stacking sequence of
oxide layers, controlling oxygen vacancy (Vo) distribution, and minimizing
Vo traps through atomic scale control of defect formation. These approa-
ches enable precise modulation of ﬁlament formation and rupture
dynamics, allowing faster and more reliable switching.

Conclusion
In summary, this work probed the correlation of atomic layer design and
switching dynamics in sub-2 nm memristors consisting of three kinds of
atomic layers of pristine Al
2O3 with negligible Vo, MgO and Ga 2O3 for
insertion of Vo with atomic control. Several critical insights have been
obtained through integration of atomistic simulation of materials based on
traditional and ML-MTP DFT, device fabrication using iALD,a n dc h a r -
acterization using advanced in situ STS/STM, ex situ high-resolution STEM
and transport measurement of device switching dynamics. Firstly, it was
found that the M1 switching layer is vital for the device to maintain a suitable
HRS for high on/off ratio. This means that pristine oxides are preferable for
M1 and its impact on the switching dy namics such as switching speed is
moderate. Speciﬁcally, the pristine oxideM1 affects Vo drift via“tunneling”
mechanism by about two orders of magnitude when the Al
2O3 M1 thickness
is varied from 0.5 to 1.2 nm. In a stark contrast, the M2 Vo-layer plays a
critical role in controlling switching dynamics of sub-2 nm memristors.
While adequate Vo concentration in M2 is essential to enable memristive
switching, the atomic design of the M2, consisting of 5-7 constituent MgO,
Ga
2O3,a n dA l 2O3 atomic layers, can drastically impact the switching
dynamics including switching speed byﬁve orders of magnitude and device
yield from 30% to 100%. The best atomic design of M2 has an interposed
MgO/Ga2O3 structure with <50 nanoseconds switching speed and 100%
device yield. The con ﬁrmation of the atomic layer structure by STEM is
complemented by the atomistic simulation, revealing smaller Vo binding
energy and higher Vo mobility facilitated by the insertion of MgO atomic

Ex situ sample characterization
Electrical measurements of the fabricated memristors were carried out using
25 µm tungsten probes in a probe station in conjunction with an Agilent
B1500 semiconductor analyzer. The Pdelectrode was grounded while the Al
electrode was biased. Initial low voltage-current (V-I) sweeps in the range of
−500 mV to 500 mV were conducted on each memristor to extract the
initial resistance (R
0) before subjecting the memristor to switches. Electro-
forming appeared to require a slightly higher voltage to initiate CF forma-
tion during the ﬁrst switch of a memristor. To test the device yield, 12
memristors were fabricated in each sample. The initial device resistanceR
0
and at least ﬁve DC V-I sweeps were performed on each device. LRS and
HRS resistances were extracted from fullV-I sweeps performed after each
SET and RESET event. No compliance current was applied during these
sweeps; the measured on/off ratios thus re ﬂect the intrinsic switching
behavior of the memristors. The device yield was estimated from the pro-
portion of the memristors made under the same condition that exhibited
successful switches in these V-I sweeps. The V
set (for SET) and Vreset (for
RESET) were determined from the sweeps. A device is considered to have
failed if it either cannot be SET or more commonly RESET at least 5 times in
the device yield evaluation. Memristor switches driven by electrical voltage
pulses were tested to determine the switching speed and endurance of
memristors. The pulse amplitudes were chosen to be slightly above theV
set
and Vreset to ensure consistent switches in the pulsed mode. For the
switching speed characterization, the voltage pulse width was varied from
the smallest pulse width of 50 nanoseconds (Instrument limit) to larger
values until the device was able to switch consistently, which was used to
estimate the switching speed. After the switching speed was determined, a
voltage pulse train was used to switch a memristor repeatedly between HRS
and LRS. To minimize the Joule heating effect, a short pause was adopted
between each READ, SET and RESET pulse during the endurance test. Each
READ pulse width was 100 milliseconds to ensure detection of the switch in
the SET and RESET pulse widths range from 50 nanoseconds (instrument
limitation) to 100 milliseconds. A preliminary endurance test was o con-
ducted on a few selected samples. A device was repeatedly SET and RESET
up to 3000 switches (time consuming) or until the device was no longer able
to switch between HRS and LRS. In order to check the consistency of the
memristor properties, the same characterization, such as HRS, LRS,
switching speed and endurance, was carried out on multiple devices on each
sample (or chip) and the result presented is representative

Density functional theory (DFT) defect calculations
The DFT calculations were performed using the Vienna Ab-initio Simu-
lation Package (VASP)
63,64 and utilized projector augmented wave (PAW)
pseudopotenti65 and the HSE06 hybrid functional 66,67 with the mixing
parameter set to 32%. For the defect calculations, a 120 atom supercell was
created from the corundum conventional unit cell and relaxed using a
2×2×2 k-point grid and a plane-wave expansion cutoff of 400 eV until all
forces were smaller than 10 meV/ Å. The defect formation energies were
calculated according to the methodology outlined in ref. 46. For these
calculations, MgAl
2O4 was used as the limiting phase to calculate the che-
mical potenti and the defect charge corrections were calculated using
SXDEFECTALIGN
68
Machine learning molecular dynamics simulations
Ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations were performed using a
similar code VASP (PAW-PBE) with a plane-wave cutoff of 400 eV.
Supercells containing 160 atoms were equilibrated in the NVT ensemble at
1000–4000 K using a Langevin thermostat. An external electricﬁeld (0.5 eV/
Å) was applied along the b-axis to model ﬁeld-induced oxygen migration.
Over 6000 conﬁgurations were sampled to train Moment Tensor Potentials
(MTP). MTP parameter optimization was conducted via BFGS mini-
mization, ensuring consistency with reference energies, forces, and stresses.
