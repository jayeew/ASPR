Lead-free relaxor ferroelectric ceramics are promising candidates for advanced
pulsed power systems owing to their combination of exceptional power density
and ultrafast charge-discharge capabilities. However, the simultaneous realiza-
tion of ultrahigh recoverable energy density (Wrec) and high efﬁciency (η)
remains a persistent challenge, as strategies to enhance polarization typically
increase hysteresis losses. To address this issue, we propose a strategy actively
constructing a superrelaxor critical state— a crossover from dynamic to static/
frozen relaxor states— through targeted compositional tuning and polarization
conﬁguration control. Guided by phase-ﬁeld simulations andﬁrst-principles
calculations, we introduce BaHfO3 into a Sr0.5Bi0.25Na0.25TiO3 relaxor matrix.
This approach successfully shifted the dielectric maximum temperature to room
temperature and enhanced the strength of relaxor behavior. Atom-scale struc-
tural characterization reveals that thisstructure weakens local domain interac-
tions within 3− 5n mr eﬁned polar nanoregions yet preserving robust polar
atomic displacements, effectively bridging the kinetic advantage of superpara-
electrics with the dipole magnitude of classical relaxors. As a result, the super-

This work establishes a generalizable strategy for engineering critical polariza-
tion states in dielectric oxides toward next-generation capacitive energy storage.

unique dielectric con ﬁguration residing at a crossover between
dynamic and static/frozen polar states. In dielectric spectra, this
unique state appears where the permittivity maximum ﬂattens, at

preserving the polarization stability. Such a con ﬁguration effectively

a SPE-like dynamic regime, yet it retains strong dipoles and large polar
nanoregions relative to the static or frozen polar state. This inter-
mediate polar state, therefore, provides a promising pathway for
advancing dielectric energy-storage performance beyond the inherent
limitations of either the relaxor or superparaelectric phases alone.

Density functional theory calculations
The calculations were performed using the density functional theory
(DFT) formalism, speci ﬁcally implemented in the Cambridge Serial
Total Energy Package (CASTEP). Within the framework of Generalized
Gradient Approximation (GGA), the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE)
function was utilized to deﬁne the exchange-correlation energy. A cut-
off energy of 520 eV was chosen to ensure convergence, with a mini-
mum energy tolerance set at 10
−5 eV per atom and a force tolerance of
0.001 eV/Å. Brillouin zone integration was carried out using the
Monkhorst-Pack scheme, employing a 3 × 3 × 3k-point mesh.
Preparation of dielectric ceramics
(1−x)Sr0.5Bi0.25Na0.25TiO3−xBaHfO3 [(1−x)SBNT−xBH] ( x = 0, 0.1, 0.2,
0.3, 0.35) ceramics were prepared by the solid phase method. Raw
materials of Na 2CO3 (≥ 99.8%), Bi 2O3 (≥ 99%), TiO 2 (≥ 98%),
SrTiO3(≥ 99%), BaCO 3 (≥ 99%), HfO 2 (≥ 98%) (all purchased from
Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co, Ltd, China) were weighed according
to the stoichiometric ratio. Use a ball mill to mix the slurry evenly, then
put it in the oven for treatment. The dry powder and 3 wt% PVA were
mixed and then pressed into pellets with a diameter of 10 mm and a
thickness of 1 mm. PVA is then burned at 500 °C for hours and then
sintered at 1200−1300 °C for 2 h. Finally, the ceramic is polished to a
thickness of 0.06 mm to test its electrical properties.
