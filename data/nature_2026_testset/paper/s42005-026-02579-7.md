Vortex light beams in the vacuum ultraviolet (VUV) range are in high demand for many applications.
Speciﬁcally, the energy range of 10-17 eV is of interest as it enables probing the ultrafast dynamics of
interband transitions and excitonic resonances in solids. However, VUV vortex beams are usually
produced using large-scale facilities or high-order harmonic generation. In this work, we present a
compact, table-top method to generate such beams using near-threshold harmonics (NTHs) in rare
gases, driven by intense infrared vortex lasers carrying orbital angular momentum. Our simulations
reveal distinct spectral and spatial features that vary with the gas-jet position relative to the laser focus.
Using time-frequency analysis at both single-atom and macroscopic levels, we show that the NTH
generation mechanism depends on the harmonic order, governed by the interplay between
multiphoton and recollision pathways. Coherence-length maps and ﬁeld-propagation analysis further
clarify the spatial behavior of NTHs. This work establishes a foundation for compact VUV vortex
sources and their potential applications in the study of ultrafast electron dynamics.
Structured light beams represent a cornerstone of next-generation laser
technologies, opening new frontiers in light-matter interactions1–3.A m o n g
these, optical vortex beams stand out due to their unique physical properties:
they carry well-deﬁned orbital angular momentum (OAM) and exhibit a
helical wavefront phase distribution, which creates a topological charge-
dependent phase singularity in the transverse plane
4–6. This distinct phase
structure gives rise to a characteristic donut-shaped intensity proﬁle and an
azimuthal phase gradient, enabling a wide range of applications, including
optical communications
7,8, optical trapping 9,10, and super-resolution
microscopy11,12. Recent breakthroughs in generating short-wavelength
OAM beams— from the extreme ultraviolet (XUV) to the X-ray regime—
have pushed the spatial resolution of optical vortices to the nanoscale, while
achieving unprecedented temporal resolution in the attosecond domain13–17.
In particular, there has been growing interest in generating vortex beams
with wavelengths ranging from several tens to a few hundred nanometers in
the vacuum ultraviolet (VUV) spectral region (10 –200 nm, 6.2–124 eV),
offering promising applications across variousﬁelds
18. For example, Zhang
et al. 19 demonstrated the generation of a 193-nm vortex beam from a
compact solid-state laser system, wh ich is essential for semiconductor
lithography. Furthermore, VUV vortices with high topological charges have
opened the door to exploring new OAM-dependent effects, such as the
chirality of molecular systems
20 and novel forms of helicoidal dichroism21,22.
To date, several approaches have been established for generating short-
wavelength vortex beams. One primary method involves the use of large-
scale facilities23–27. For example, synchrotrons can provide high-ﬂux VUV
light; however, accessing such sources is not straightforward due to the
complexity, high cost, and limited beamtime associated with these
facilities
28,29. Other approaches include the use of excimer lasers, which
operate only at a few ﬁxed wavelengths30, free-electron lasers 31,32,a n d
supercontinuum generation in photonic crystal ﬁbers33. However, these
methods often suffer from low efﬁciency and complex experimental setups.

Conclusions
In summary, we investigated the generation of near-threshold vortex har-
monics (NTHs) in the vacuum ultra violet (VUV) spectral region via
simulations. We showed that the macroscopic NTH spectra remain largely
unchanged with varying gas-jet positions, in stark contrast to the strong gas-
position dependence observed in high-order harmonic spectra. Using time-
frequency analysis of NTHs via the synchrosqueezing transform, along with
intensity-dependent spectral and phase characteristics, we revealed that the
microscopic generation mechanism of NTHs varies with harmonic order.
Speciﬁcally, the 7th- and 9th-order harmonics (H7 and H9) arise primarily
from the interference between multiphoton and recollision pathways,
whereas H11 originates from a more efﬁcient recollision process. As a result,
the corresponding single-atom phases exhibit distinct dependencies on laser
intensity, resulting in pronounced differences in the coherence-length maps.
We further examined the spatial intensity proﬁles of various vortex NTHs
by varying the gas-jet position. The observed dependence of single-ring and
multiple-ring structures on harmonic order and gas-jet position is again
markedly different from that of high -order harmonics, as discussed in
previous work
64. The spatial proﬁle of H7 shows weak dependence on the
gas-jet position, while H11 consisten tly exhibits a single-ring structure
regardless of position. In contrast, the spatial proﬁle of H9 is highly sensitive
to macroscopic conditions. Finally, we calculated the harmonic coherence-
length maps and analyzed the evolution of the harmonicﬁeld within the gas
medium, which effectively explains t he features observed in the spatial
proﬁles of NTHs. We expect that this workwill deepen our understanding of
vortex beam generation via NTHs in r are gases, offering an alternative
