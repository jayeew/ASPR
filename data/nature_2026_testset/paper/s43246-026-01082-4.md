The rapid progress of arti ﬁcial intelligence has exposed the inherent limitations of the conventional
chip technology, particularly the high energy-consumption, driving the emergence of neuromorphic
chips and ionics. Using K
+ ion-ﬁlled graphene channels, we investigate the mechanism underlying the
graphene-based ion transistors by ab initio molecular dynamics simulations. Here we show that
graphene electrons enable long-range correlation of con ﬁned ions, which provides a basis for the
sensitive responses of transistors to the channel ion density (as modulated by a gate voltage). The ON/
OFF switching effect speci ﬁcally results from the competition between π-π stacking and cation- π
interaction in the channels with different ion- ﬁlling densities. The nonlinear increasing of transport
efﬁciency (i.e., signal ampli ﬁcation effect) is due to the ion density-depended collective oscillation of
channel-conﬁned ions. Additionally, resonance between channel-outside and channel-con ﬁned ions
triggers rapid ion dehydration, enabling the transistor ’s ultrahigh ion diffusivity. These atomic-level
insights as a design principle for the ultralow energy-consumption neuro-morphic chips.
The rapid progress of artiﬁcial intelligence (AI) has exposed inherent lim-
itations in conventional chip technology 1–3. Neuromorphic computers,
inspired by neural systems, are proposed to mimic the neural high-efﬁciency
and ultralow energy-consumption information processing4–7. However, a
challenge in the accurate simulations is that the ultralow energy-
consumption signal transmission in our brain is primarily achieved via
ion channels (but not electrons), leadingto the rise of ionics, i.e., ions serve as
an information carrier
8–11. For integrating ionics into the neuromorphic
chips and simulating the neural functions better, various devices are
designed, e.g., ionic transistors, ionic memristors and ionic switches as well
as their functional applications i ncluding logic gates and soft ionic
systems
12–24. Recently, Xue et al. fabricated an atomic-scale ionic transistor
with reduced graphene oxide, demonstrating gate voltage-modulated ON/
OFF switching at a critical ion density of 1.8 × 1014 cm−2, nonlinear signal
ampliﬁcation, and ultrahigh ion diffusivity 12. However, the atomic-level
mechanisms underlying these remarkable properties remained unclear.
These developments promise to evol ve brain-like neuromorphic chips
and AI.
Advancement in molecular dynamics (MD) simulations has greatly
improved the understanding of ion transport, especially in ionics. Classical
MD (CMD) simulations are widely used in the study of mechanisms under
the high ﬂux, selectivity, surface chemistry, ion-water coupling and
dimension effects of ion channels as well as the pressure and electric ﬁeld
regulations, leading to numerous achievements
25–54. However, for the sys-
tems substantially affected by charge transfer, especially affected by dyna-
mical charge transfer (DCT), the CMD approach is clearly limited. Recently,
ab initio MD (AIMD) simulations have successfully elucidated DCT-
dominant dynamics processes in ion batteries and capacitive storage
devices
55,56, where dynamical charge transfer between electrodes and ions
critically determines device performance. This approach provides a feasible
framework for exploring similar quantum effects in ionic transistors.
Here, employing a potassium ion-ﬁlled graphene channel system and
applying AIMD simulations, we stud ied the underlying mechanism of
graphene-based ion transistors. In our simulations, with an increase in the
ion density of graphene channels (as experimentally modulated by the gate
voltage of transistors), an OFF/ON switching occurs and the ion transport
efﬁciency nonlinearly increases in the ON state (Fig. 1). Assisted by the
graphene electrons, there forms a quantum correlation of the channel-
conﬁned ions, providing a basis for the sensitive responses of transistor to
the ion density of channels (i.e., to the gate voltage). The switching effect is

Signal ampliﬁcation through collective ion oscillations
To study the ampliﬁcation function of graphene-based ion transistors, we
analyzed the collective oscillation modes of conﬁned ions in the graphene
channels of ON state. The oscillation spectra of channel-conﬁned ions were
calculated based on our AIMD simulations, and Gaussian multipeak ana-
lyses were used to distinguish the normalized collective ion oscillation
modes (the details in Supplementary Note 3). The results are shown in
Fig. 4a. For each system at the ion- ﬁlling density D ranging from
1.88 × 10
14 cm−2 to 4.38 × 1014 cm−2, two distinct peaks in the oscillation
spectrum were observed in the regions of 0.7 –1.0 THz and 2.0–2.7 THz,
respectively. There thus exist two kindsof collective ion oscillation modes in
the ON state of the channels, i.e., low-frequency and high-frequency modes,
as labeled LFM and HFM, respectively. Based on the spectra, we studied the
dependence of collective ion oscillation intensity (i.e., peak height) on the
ion density. As the ON-state ion density increased from 1.88 × 10
14 cm−2 to
4.38 × 1014 cm−2, the LFM intensity decreased from 0.98 to 0.37 and the
HFM one increased from 0.32 to 0.66,indicating that the responses of LFM
and HFM to the ion density are opposite (Fig.4b). The relationship of ion
transport efﬁciency with the collective ion oscillation modes was further
studied. The efﬁciency of ion transport increased linearly with the HFM
intensity and decreased exponentially with the LFM intensity, denoting that
the transport efﬁciency of ion-ﬁlled graphene channels highly depends on
the collective oscillation HFM (Fig. 4c). We thus can conclude that the
increase of K
+ ion density in the graphene channel promotes the collective
ion oscillation mode of high frequency, enhancing the ion transport efﬁ-
ciency, which governs the signal ampliﬁcation effect of graphene-based ion
transistors.

Implications for neuromorphic device design
The atomic-scale mechanisms identiﬁed here provide direct guidance for
engineering practical ionic transistors. First, the quantum correlation of
channel-conﬁned ions (Fig. 2) suggests that multi-channel device
architectures should optimize inter-channel spacing to exploit collective
oscillation modes while avoiding destructive interference. The char-
acteristic oscillation frequencies (~2.5 THz, Fig. 4) set the operational
bandwidth for neuromorphic signal processing. Second, device stability
can be enhanced through rational edge functionalization: while our
simulations show hydrophobic C-H edges enable ultrafast dehydration
(<500 fs, Fig. 5), partially hydrophilic functionalization might provide a
controllable trade-off between transport ef ﬁciency and long-term sta-
bility in aqueous environments. Third, the identi ﬁed critical ion density
(1.8 × 10¹⁴ cm⁻²) and the π-π/cation-π structural transition (Fig. 3) offer
quantitative targets for electrochemical gate control. Fabrication proto-
cols can leverage the well-de ﬁned KC ₈ stoichiometry as an endpoint,
monitored via cyclic voltammetry or in-situ Raman spectroscopy.
Finally, the ion-speci ﬁc resonance mechanism (Supplementary Fig. 5)
enables rational design of multi-ion neuromorphic systems by matching
channel properties to target ion oscillation frequencies, potentially
achieving intrinsic selectivity without additional chemical modi ﬁcation.
These insights bridge the gap between fundamental quantum transport
phenomena and the practical requirements of scalable, stable neuro-
morphic devices.

transistor to the channel ion density. The OFF/ON switching effect is spe-
ciﬁcally caused by the competition between π-π stacking and cation- π
interaction in the graphene channels with different ion densities. In the ON
state, the ampliﬁcation effect of transistors is attributed to the collective
high-frequency oscillation mode of channel-conﬁned ions, i.e., increasing
the channel ion density causes a nonlinear increase in this mode, leading to a
nonlinear enhancement of ion transport ef ﬁciency. The resonance of
channel-outside and channel-conﬁned ions causes rapid ion dehydration,
which enables ultrahigh ion diffus ivity of the transistors. These ﬁndings
provide an atom-level understandingfor how ionic transistors work, which
is expected to promote the development of ionics and even ultralow energy-
consumption neuromorphic chipsfor the future AI technology.
Methods
AIMD simulations were performed using DFT with the LDA exchange-
correlation functional for accurate representation of K+ ion-ﬁlled graphene
channel system. We employed 500 eV energy cutoff and the PREC =
Accurate setting. Given the large supercell (10.2 Å × 8.6 Å × 57.2 Å), Γ-
point sampling with a 1 × 1 × 1 Monkhorst –Pack grid was used. AIMD
simulations ran for 2 ps (10000 steps at 0.2 fs) at 300 K using a Nosé-Hoover
thermostat. Further details are provided in Supplementary Methods.
