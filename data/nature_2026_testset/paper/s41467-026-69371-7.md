While age-related changes are commonly viewed as drivers of aging,
speciﬁc alterations may paradoxically represent adaptive counter-
measures against aging pathologies. For instance, elevated systemic IL-
10 levels during aging could constitute a compensatory mechanism to
mitigate chronic in ﬂammation-a well-established aging accelerator
(i.e., inﬂammaging)
1–4.
A typical characteristic of inﬂammaging is the increased circulat-
ing proin ﬂammatory mediators, such as TNF- α, IL-6, and IL-1 β3,5.
Among the major sources of these cytokines, white adipose tissue
(WAT), especially visceral adipose tissue (VAT), has emerged as a key
contributor of aging
6–8. Beyond adipocytes, VAT harbors diverse
immune cell populations with cytokine-secreting capacity, including
macrophages, T cells, B cells, and eosinophils
8,9. During aging, the
composition and functionality of these immune populations undergo
signiﬁcant alterations 10,11. For instance, aging disrupts the M1/M2
macrophage balance in VAT, leading to disturbed proin ﬂammatory
/anti-inﬂammatory cytokine secretion11. Concurrently, the number of
eosinophils in VAT decreases with age, further diminishing their
secretion of anti-inﬂammatory factors such as IL-4
12. Despite the pro-
gressively developing in ﬂammatory milieu, whether VAT harbors
intrinsic mechanisms to counteract this pathological process remains
unclear.
Mounting evidence has established interleukin (IL) −10 expres-
sing B lineage (B-10) cells as critical modulators of in ﬂammatory
cascades in VAT through their immunoregulatory functions
13–15.

B-10 cells are the dominant source of IL-10 in aged VAT
Next, we evaluated the proportional contribution of B-10 cells to VAT
IL-10+ cells. Compared to younger individuals, the proportion of B-10
cells among all IL-10+ cells was signiﬁcantly elevated in aged VAT from
both humans and mice (Fig.2A, B). Notably, B-10 cells emerged as the
predominant IL-10+ cell subpopulation in aged VAT across both species
(Fig. 2A, B).
To directly assess B-10 cells’ IL-10 secretory capacity, we analyzed
IL-10 levels in B cell-speci ﬁc IL-10 knockout (B ΔIL-10) mice. IgM +IL-10+
cells were basically undetectable in B ΔIL-10 mice (Supplementary
Fig. 6A). This genetic ablation resulted in markedly reduced IL-10+ cell
frequencies and protein levels in aged VAT (Fig.2C, D). Systemic IL-10
level was also reduced (Fig. 2E). Similar but smaller reductions were
also observed in 3 M mice (Supplementary Fig. 6B–D).
Collectively, these data establish B-10 cells as the principal IL-10
source in aged VAT.

with 3 and 100 kDa ﬁlters. At same concentration, only the 3-100 kDa
fraction enhanced IL-10 secretion (Fig.4G), implicating proteins as key
mediators.
Analysis of 12 major adipokines/cytokines
29 in explant super-
natants revealed elevated BAFF, TNF-α, IL-6, IL-10, TGF-β1, and leptin,
alongside reduced adiponectin, in 24 M versus 3 M VAT (Fig. 4H).
Absolute concentrations of these proteins in supernatants were shown
in Fig. S11. When administered at concentrations matching 24 M VAT
explant levels, BAFF, IL-6, and leptin enhanced the secretion of IL-10
from IL-10
tdTomato B-10 cells, whereas IL-10 slightly auto-inhibited its
production (Fig.4I). Combinatorial blockage of all three factors (BAFF,
IL-6, and leptin) - but not individual blockade - completely abrogated
the aged VAT-induced IL-10 enhancement (Supplementary Fig. 12B),
revealing their cooperative action. Furthermore, BAFF is known to
promote the survival of B cells. To exclude this effect, we assessed the
impact of BAFF on the survival of B-10 cells. After 1 d culture, BAFF had
no signiﬁcant effect on the survival of B-10 cells, while on day 2, it only
slightly promoted their survival (Supplementary Fig. 12E). This may be
due to the overall low mortality rate of B-10 cells after 2 days of culture
(Supplementary Fig. 12E). We also measured the IL-10 secretion of B-10
cells on day 1 and found that 24 M VAT explants signiﬁcantly increased
IL-10 levels at this time point. These results indicate that the effect of
BAFF on B-10 cell survival is not the primary factor for the increased IL-
10 secretion.
In summary, the aged VAT microenvironment promotes IL-10
secretion by B-10 cells through coordinated actions of multiple protein
factors.

VAT BAFF controls aging-related inﬂammation, IR and lifespan
Finally, we analyzed the effects of VAT-speciﬁc BAFF knockdown and
overexpression on inﬂammation, IR, and lifespan. Bodyweight and lean
mass were not signi ﬁcantly affected by BAFF editing, while BAFF
overexpression signiﬁcantly reduced fat mass in males (Supplemen-
tary Fig. 16A–C). Consistent with VAT B-10 cell changes, BAFF knock-
down reduced IL-10 levels in VAT, while overexpression increased VAT
IL-10 protein (Fig. 6A). Similar patterns were observed in serum (Sup-
plementary Fig. 16D). On the contrary, BAFF knockdown increased VAT
IgG and serum GDF15 levels while overexpression decreased these
indexes (Supplementary Fig. 16E, F). Sirius red staining and insulin
clamp analysis con ﬁrmed worsened ﬁbrosis and IR in BAFF-
knockdown mice and ameliorated ﬁbrosis and IR in BAFF-
overexpressing aged mice (Fig. 6B, C, E, F). Furthermore, BAFF
knockdown shortened lifespan, whereas BAFF overexpression exten-
ded longevity (Fig. 6D, G).
Collectively, these ﬁndings demonstrate that VAT BAFF expres-
sion critically inﬂuences aging-related inﬂammation, IR, and lifespan.
Discussion
Our study uncovers a previously unrecognized regulatory axis cen-
tered on B-10 cells that critically modulates age-related metabolic
inﬂammation and systemic homeostasis. Our ﬁndings demonstrate
that B-10 cells undergo striking expansion in aged VAT, serving as the
dominant IL-10 source to counteract adipose tissue in ﬂammation.
Mechanistically, this age-associated B-10 cell accumulation depends
on microenvironmental BAFF signaling from VAT-resident macro-
phages and adipocytes. Therapeutically, VAT-targeted BAFF over-
expression not only ampli ﬁes B-10 cell populations but also
ameliorates IR and extends lifespan, establishing B-10 cell-mediated
immunometabolic regulation as a promising target for healthy aging
interventions.

The discovery that BAFF drives B-10 cell expansion in aging VAT

Meanwhile, it has been reported that BAFF can directly promote
thermogenesis in adipocytes, thereby improving metabolism

Thus, the direct effects of BAFF on adipocytes may also partially
mediate the metabolic impacts of BAFF knockdown or over-
expression independent of B-10 cells. Similarly, BAFF over-
expression has also been reported to improve atherosclerosis
44.O n
the contrary, whole-body knockout of BAFF or its receptor BAFFR
can improve metabolism
45,46, indicating the metabolism-improving
effect of BAFF is tissue-dependent.
