Non-Abelian geometric phases offer fault-tolerant unitary operations that are
essential for holonomic quantum computation. While the“all-geometric”
approach has successfully enabled robust control of atoms, ions, and electrons
in various quantum systems, its photonic implementations have remained
limited by a lack of tunability—an essential feature for modern classical or
quantum optical information processing. Here, we demonstrate nonvolatile
multilevel tunable generic special-orthogonal (SO) geometric phases on a
multilayer silicon photonic platform incorporating the phase-change material
(PCM) of Sb₂Se₃. By toggling Sb₂Se₃ between crystalline and amorphous
states, we dynamically control the number of degenerate states involved in the
holonomy, enabling switching between different holonomic paths and SO(m)
geometric transformations. Our results bridge the key gap between robust
geometric-phase control and in-memory reconﬁgurable photonics, establish-
ing a paradigm for fault-tolerant, high-dimensional operations in integrated
optical systems.
The non-Abelian geometric phase is well known for its inherent
robustness in realizing unitary transformations. Building upon the
foundational work on the Berry phase

lack of tunability. Traditional geometric phases in photonics are static,
ﬁxed by device geometry at fabrication. This stands in stark contrast to
the high recon ﬁgurability demanded by mainstream programmable
photonic circuits – the engines of modern optical computing plat-
forms like Mach-Zehnder interferometer arrays26,68–71 and micro-ring
arrays72,73 – which require dynamic adaptability for diverse tasks like in-
situ training27 and multi-task processing. Bridging this gap between the
inherent robustness of geometric phases and the essential need for
tunability has remained a major obstacle. Here, we overcome this
fundamental limitation by integrating in-memory non-volatile phase-
change material of Sb ₂Se₃ into a silicon photonic platform. The key
innovation lies in exploiting the distinct optical properties of Sb₂Se₃ in
its amorphous and crystalline states to dynamically recon ﬁgure the
holonomy itself. By optically switching the PCM waveguide state, we
actively control the number of degenerate modes within the system,
thereby enabling the manipulation of the underlying Hilbert space
structure that deﬁnes the geometric phase. This capability allows us to
experimentally realize in-memory nonvolatile, recon ﬁgurable, high-
dimensional non-Abelian geometric phases, speciﬁcally SO(m) matrix-
valued transformations. Crucially, the in-memory non-volatility of
Sb₂Se₃ ensures that the reconﬁgured state persists without constant
energy expenditure, a vital attribute for practical, power-ef ﬁcient
devices. Our demonstration of recon ﬁgurable two-mode braiding
operations, enablingﬂexible switching between‘bar’ and ‘cross’ states,
exempliﬁes the immediate practical impact. This capability directly
addresses a core need in photonic information processing: tunable,
low-loss optical switching. It is worth noting that the stochastic
nucleation characteristics of PCMs usually lead to subtle deviations in
conventional dynamic phase modulation devices, but these deviations
are not an issue in recon ﬁgurable geometric phase devices. Slight
deviations from the fully crystalline or amorphous states do not sig-
niﬁcantly affect the two-level geometric phase exhibited by the device
(Supplementary Fig. 15). The robustness inherent to geometric con-
trol, combined with PCM-based tunability, presents a compelling
solution for building large-scale, fault-tolerant optical switch networks
far beyond the capabilities of static geometric or dynamically phase-
sensitive approaches. Furthermore, the realization of multi-level, large-
tunability-range SO(m) matrices signi ﬁes a leap towards high-
dimensional unitary control purely through geometric means.
To conclude, our work pioneers in-memory non-volatile and
reconﬁgurable non-Abelian geometric phases in photonics by inte-
grating phase-change material (Sb₂Se₃) into a silicon platform, effec-
tively merging the inherent fault tolerance of geometric control with
the essential dynamic programmability demanded by modern optical
technologies. Our demonstration overcomes the critical barrier of
static geometric phases, unlocking practical “all-geometric” approa-
ches: it enables programmable photonic circuits for robust linear
computing and neuromorphic processing, establishes a paradigm for
stable, tunable optical switching networks via braiding operations, and
provides the vital toolset —dynamically controllable degenerate sub-
spaces—for building fault-tolerant photonic quantum processors
based on holonomic quantum computation. Based on the architecture
demonstrated in this work, additional functionalities can be further
developed. For example, the basic two-level tunable SO(2) geometric
phase discussed in the manuscript is achieved by fully switching the
entire PCM waveguide. However, if only a portion of the waveguides
undergoes phase transition, the mapping between the threefold and
twofold degenerate subspaces may lead to population leakage from
the degenerate subspace. This effect enables the system to effectively
realize a singular value decomposition (SVD)-like architecture, thereby
supporting the programmable implementation of general 2×2 matri-
ces. Besides, this work demonstrates tunable SO(m) holonomy. By
incorporating complex-valued coupling coefﬁcients, the active tuning
method of this work has the potential to reach tunable U(2) holonomy.
For higher-dimensional holonomic control, multiphoton Fock-state
injection
20,74 can provide a compatible and complementary route to
reconﬁgurable higher-dimensional holonomic quantum computation
beyond the Givens-rotation-based approach presented in this work.
The synergy of robust geometric control and in-memory non-volatile
tunability marks a pivotal advancement, paving the way for resilient,
adaptable, power-efﬁcient high-performance photonic devices across
classical and quantum information processing, with future work tar-
geting scaled integration, higher-order operations, and enhanced
material performance.

Measurement setup
The light is emitted from a wideband source (Wuhan Leicheng Pho-
tonics Technology Co., Ltd.), which covers a wavelength range of
1150 –1680 nm. Then it propagates through a polarization controller
and is coupled in/out to the integra ted photonic chip. Edge couplers
are utilized to interface the light between a single-mode ﬁber and a
silicon waveguide. The light output is monitored by a power meter
(JW3216, Shanghai Joinwit), and the spectra are analyzed through OSA
(AQ6370C, YOKOGAWA).
Phase transition control of Sb₂Se₃
The transition from the crystalline to the amorphous phase is achieved
via laser direct writing using a DWL 66+ system (Heidelberg Instru-
ments) with a nominal laser power of 15 mW and a writing speed of 3
mm
2/min. The transition from amorphous state to crystalline state can
be realized through annealing (whole chip erasing, 200 °C, 5 min) or
through laser-diode-writting (locally erasing) at a wavelength of
639 nm. Figure 2a illustrates the setup. Laser-diode-induced crystal-
lization can be achieved using two distinct modes: (1) a continuous
scanning mode with a laser power of 40 mW, a scanning speed of
0.01 mm/s, and an energy density of ~80μJ/μm² on the sample surface;
and (2) a single-pulse mode with a laser power of 6.95 mW, a pulse
duration of 300 ms, and the same energy density of ~89 μJ/μm². To
calibrate the time and energy needed for laser-controlled amorphiza-
tion, amorphization was also demonstrated on this system using a
single pulse with a power of 44 mW, a duration of 200 ns, and an
energy density of ~5.7 nJ/μm².
